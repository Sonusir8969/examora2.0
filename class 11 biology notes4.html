NCERT Solutions for Class 11 Biology Chapter 4 Animal Kingdom

NCERT Solutions for Class 11 Biology Chapter 4 Animal Kingdom provides comprehensive explanations of animal classification criteria (symmetry, coelom, germ layers, segmentation, notochord) and detailed characteristics of non-chordate and chordate phyla.

1. Basis of Classification

Short Note:

• Levels of Organisation: Cellular (e.g., Porifera), Tissue level (Coelenterata), Organ level (Platyhelminthes), Organ-system level (Aschelminthes to Chordates).

• Symmetry: Asymmetrical (Sponges), Radial symmetry (Coelenterates, Ctenophores, Echinoderm adults), Bilateral symmetry (Annelids, Arthropods, Chordates).

• Germinal Layers: Diploblastic (Ectoderm + Endoderm e.g., Coelenterates) vs. Triploblastic (Ectoderm + Mesoderm + Endoderm e.g., Platyhelminthes to Chordates).

• Coelom (Body Cavity):

  - Acoelomates: Cavity absent (Platyhelminthes).

  - Pseudocoelomates: Mesoderm present as scattered pouches (Aschelminthes).

  - Coelomates (Eucoelomates): True body cavity lined by mesoderm (Annelids to Chordates).

• Segmentation & Notochord: Metameric segmentation seen in Annelids and Arthropods. Notochord is a mesodermally derived rod-like structure formed on the dorsal side during embryonic development in chordates.

2. Non-Chordate Phyla

Short Note:

• Porifera (Sponges): Cellular level, ostia (pores), osculum, choanocytes (collar cells) line spongocoel; internal skeleton of spicules/spongin fibers. E.g., Sycon, Spongilla.

• Coelenterata (Cnidaria): Cnidoblasts/cnidocytes containing nematocysts. Show alternation of generation (metagenesis) between Polyp (sessile) and Medusa (free-swimming). E.g., Hydra, Aurelia, Obelia, Physalia.

• Ctenophora (Comb Jellies): Exclusively marine, 8 rows of ciliated comb plates, show bioluminescence. E.g., Pleurobrachia, Ctenoplana.

• Platyhelminthes (Flatworms): Dorso-ventrally flattened, acoelomate, flame cells for osmoregulation/excretion. E.g., Taenia (tapeworm), Fasciola (liver fluke).

• Aschelminthes (Roundworms): Pseudocoelomates, complete digestive tract with muscular pharynx. E.g., Ascaris, Wuchereria (filarial worm).

• Annelida: True coelomates, metameric segmentation, nephridia for excretion. E.g., Nereis, Pheretima (earthworm), Hirudinaria (leech).

• Arthropoda: Largest phylum; chitinous exoskeleton, jointed appendages, open circulatory system, Malpighian tubules. E.g., Apis, Anopheles, Palaemon, Limulus.

• Mollusca: Second largest phylum; soft body covered by calcareous shell, mantle cavity with gills, radula (rasping organ). E.g., Pila, Pinctada, Octopus.

• Echinodermata: Spiny-skinned, radial symmetry in adults, water vascular system for locomotion/feeding. E.g., Asterias (starfish), Echinus.

• Hemichordata: Worm-like marine animals; body consists of Proboscis, Collar, and Trunk. E.g., Balanoglossus, Saccoglossus.

3. Phylum Chordata Overview

Short Note:

• Fundamental chordate characters: Presence of a Notochord, Dorsal hollow nerve cord, paired pharyngeal gill slits, and a post-anal tail.

• Subphylum Urochordata (Tunicata): Notochord present only in larval tail. E.g., Ascidia, Salpa.

• Subphylum Cephalochordata: Notochord extends from head to tail region and persists throughout life. E.g., Branchiostoma (Amphioxus).

• Subphylum Vertebrata: Notochord is replaced by a cartilaginous or bony vertebral column in adults.

4. Classes of Vertebrata

Short Note:

• Cyclostomata: Jawless vertebrates (Agnatha), suctorial circular mouth, ectoparasites on fishes. E.g., Petromyzon (Lamprey), Myxine (Hagfish).

• Chondrichthyes (Cartilaginous Fishes): Marine, stream-lined body, cartilaginous endoskeleton, placoid scales, ventral mouth, air bladder absent. E.g., Scoliodon (dogfish), Pristis (saw fish), Torpedo (electric ray).

• Osteichthyes (Bony Fishes): Marine and freshwater, bony endoskeleton, operculum covering gills, ctenoid/cycloid scales, air bladder present (regulates buoyancy). E.g., Exocoetus, Labeo, Betta.

• Amphibia: Can live in aquatic and terrestrial habitats, moist skin without scales, 3-chambered heart (2 auricles + 1 ventricle), poikilotherms. E.g., Rana (frog), Bufo (toad), Salamandra.

• Reptilia: Creeping/crawling mode of locomotion, dry and cornified skin with epidermal scales/scutes, 3-chambered heart (4-chambered in crocodiles). E.g., Chelone, Calotes, Naja, Crocodilus.

• Aves (Birds): Presence of feathers, forelimbs modified into wings, pneumatic (hollow) bones, 4-chambered heart, homoiothermous (warm-blooded). E.g., Corvus, Columba, Psittacula, Pavo.

• Mammalia: Presence of mammary glands, hair on skin, external ears (pinnae), 4-chambered heart, homoiothermous. E.g., Ornithorhynchus (oviparous platypus), Macropus (kangaroo), Panthera leo.

Intext & Conceptual Questions

Q1: What are the primary distinctions between Chordates and Non-Chordates?
Answer:
Feature Chordates Non-Chordates
Notochord Present at some stage of life. Absent.
Central Nervous System Dorsal, hollow, and single. Ventral, solid, and double.
Pharyngeal Gill Slits Present. Absent.
Heart Position Ventral. Dorsal (if present).
Post-anal Tail Present. Absent.

Textbook Chapter End Exercise Questions

Q1: How useful is the study of the nature of body cavity (coelom) in the classification of animals?
Answer:
The body cavity (coelom)—the fluid-filled space between the body wall and the alimentary canal—is a vital diagnostic character in animal classification:

1. Acoelomates: Absence of body cavity (e.g., Platyhelminthes). The space is filled with parenchyma tissue, restricting organ independence.
2. Pseudocoelomates: Body cavity is not lined by mesoderm; mesoderm exists as scattered pouches inside the blastocoel (e.g., Aschelminthes).
3. Coelomates (Eucoelomates): Body cavity completely lined by mesoderm on both sides (e.g., Annelida to Chordata). This permits independent movement and development of complex internal visceral organs.
Q2: Distinguish between Chondrichthyes and Osteichthyes.
Answer:
Character Chondrichthyes Osteichthyes
Endoskeleton Cartilaginous. Bony.
Habitat Exclusively marine. Freshwater and marine.
Mouth Position Ventral. Terminal.
Operculum & Air Bladder Operculum absent; air bladder absent (must swim constantly). Operculum present over gills; air bladder present for buoyancy.
Scales Placoid scales. Cycloid or Ctenoid scales.

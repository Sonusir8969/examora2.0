NCERT Solutions for Class 11 Chemistry Chapter 1 Some Basic Concepts of Chemistry

NCERT Solutions for Class 11 Chemistry Chapter 1 Some Basic Concepts of Chemistry covers the nature and importance of chemistry, laws of chemical combination, atomic and molecular masses, mole concept, and methods of expressing concentration.

1. Laws of Chemical Combination

Short Note:

• Law of Conservation of Mass: matter can neither be created nor destroyed in a chemical reaction; total mass of reactants equals total mass of products.

• Law of Definite Proportions: a pure chemical compound always contains the same elements combined in a fixed ratio by mass, regardless of its source.

• Law of Multiple Proportions: when two elements combine to form more than one compound, the masses of one element that combine with a fixed mass of the other are in a ratio of small whole numbers.

• Gay Lussac's Law of Gaseous Volumes and Avogadro's Law: equal volumes of all gases under the same conditions of temperature and pressure contain equal numbers of molecules.

2. Atomic and Molecular Masses

Short Note:

• Atomic Mass is expressed in atomic mass units (u), based on the carbon-12 isotope as the standard (1 u = 1/12th the mass of one carbon-12 atom).

• Molecular Mass is the sum of atomic masses of all atoms present in a molecule of a substance.

• Formula Mass is used for ionic compounds that do not exist as discrete molecules, calculated similarly using the formula unit.

3. Mole Concept and Molar Mass

Short Note:

• One mole is the amount of substance that contains exactly as many elementary entities as there are atoms in 12 g of carbon-12, i.e. 6.022 × 10²³ entities (Avogadro's number, NA).

• Molar Mass is the mass of one mole of a substance, expressed in g/mol, and is numerically equal to atomic/molecular mass in u.

4. Percentage Composition & Concentration Terms

Short Note:

• Empirical Formula shows the simplest whole-number ratio of atoms of each element in a compound; Molecular Formula shows the actual number of atoms.

• Molarity (M) = moles of solute / volume of solution in litres. Molality (m) = moles of solute / mass of solvent in kg. Mass percent = (mass of solute/mass of solution) × 100.

NCERT Textbook Exercise Questions

Calculate the molecular mass of glucose (C6H12O6).
Answer: Molecular mass = 6(12) + 12(1) + 6(16) = 72 + 12 + 96 = 180 u.
Calculate the amount of water (g) produced by the combustion of 16 g of methane (CH4 + 2O2 → CO2 + 2H2O).
Answer: Molar mass of CH4 = 16 g/mol, so 16 g = 1 mole of CH4. From the equation, 1 mole CH4 produces 2 moles H2O. Mass of 2 moles H2O = 2 × 18 = 36 g.
If the density of methanol is 0.793 kg/L, what is its volume needed for making 2.5 L of its 0.25 M solution?
Answer: Moles required = 0.25 × 2.5 = 0.625 mol. Mass required = 0.625 × 32 (molar mass of CH3OH) = 20 g. Volume = mass/density = 20 g / 793 g/L = 0.0252 L = 25.2 mL.
What is the concentration of sugar (C12H22O11) in mol/L if its 20 g are dissolved in enough water to make a final volume of 2 L?
Answer: Moles of sugar = 20/342 = 0.0585 mol (molar mass of C12H22O11 = 342 g/mol). Molarity = 0.0585/2 = 0.02925 mol/L.
Calculate the mass percent of different elements present in sodium sulphate (Na2SO4).
Answer: Molar mass of Na2SO4 = 2(23)+32+4(16) = 46+32+64 = 142 g/mol. Mass % of Na = 46/142 ×100 = 32.39%; Mass % of S = 32/142 ×100 = 22.54%; Mass % of O = 64/142 ×100 = 45.07%.

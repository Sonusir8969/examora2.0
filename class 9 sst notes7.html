NCERT Solutions for Class 9 Geography Chapter 2 Physical Features of India

NCERT Solutions for Class 9 Geography Chapter 2 covers Plate Tectonics, The Himalayan Mountains, The Northern Plains, The Peninsular Plateau, The Indian Desert, Coastal Plains, and Islands.

1. Major Physiographic Divisions & Plate Tectonics

Short Note:

• Theory of Plate Tectonics: The Earth's crust is divided into seven major and several minor tectonic plates that move, causing folding, faulting, and volcanic activity.

• Plate Boundaries: Convergent (move towards each other), Divergent (move away from each other), and Transform (slide past each other horizontally).

• Gondwanaland: The ancient southern supercontinent from which the Indo-Australian plate drifted northward, colliding with the Eurasian plate to form the Himalayas.

2. The Himalayan Mountains & The Northern Plains

Short Note:

• Three Himalayan Ranges:

  • Himadri (Inner/Great Himalayas): Northernmost, highest range with continuous peaks (average height 6,000m).
  • Himachal (Lesser Himalayas): Middle range, highly compressed and altered rocks; home to popular hill stations.
  • Shiwaliks (Outer Himalayas): Southernmost range made of unconsolidated sediments (height 900-1100m). Longitudinal valleys between Himachal and Shiwaliks are called Duns (e.g., Dehradun).

• Northern Plains: Formed by the alluvial deposits of three major river systems: Indus, Ganga, and Brahmaputra. Divided into Bhabar (pebble belt), Terai (marshy region), Bhangar (older alluvium), and Khadar (newer alluvium).

3. Peninsular Plateau, Coastal Plains & Islands

Short Note:

• Peninsular Plateau: Composed of old crystalline, igneous, and metamorphic rocks. Comprises two broad divisions: Central Highlands and Deccan Plateau.

• Western vs. Eastern Ghats: Western Ghats are continuous and higher in elevation; Eastern Ghats are discontinuous and dissected by east-flowing rivers.

• Coastal Plains: Western Coast (Konkan, Kannad, Malabar) is narrow; Eastern Coast (Northern Circar, Coromandel) is broad with large river deltas.

• Islands: Lakshadweep (coral origin in Arabian Sea) and Andaman & Nicobar (volcanic/tectonic origin in Bay of Bengal).

Q1: Distinguish between Bhangar and Khadar.
Feature Bhangar Khadar
Age & Soil Older alluvial soil found above floodplains. Newer, younger alluvial soil deposited in floodplains.
Fertility Less fertile, contains calcareous deposits (Kankar). Highly fertile, renewed every year by floods.
Agriculture Suitable for less water-intensive crops. Ideal for intensive agriculture and crops like paddy.
Q2: Distinguish between Western Ghats and Eastern Ghats.
Western Ghats Eastern Ghats
Form the western boundary of Deccan Plateau along the Arabian Sea. Form the eastern boundary of Deccan Plateau along the Bay of Bengal.
Continuous range; can be crossed through passes only (Thal, Bhor, Pal Ghats). Discontinuous and irregular, cut through by major rivers.
Higher elevation (Average height: 900–1600m). Highest peak: Anamudi. Lower elevation (Average height: 600m). Highest peak: Mahendragiri.

Textbook Chapter End Exercise Questions

Q1: Choose the correct answer from the options given below:
(i) A landmass bounded by sea on three sides is referred to as:
(a) Coast   (b) Island   (c) Peninsula   (d) None of the above
Answer: (c) Peninsula

(ii) Mountain ranges in the eastern part of India forming its boundary with Myanmar are collectively called:
(a) Himachal   (b) Uttarakhand   (c) Purvachal   (d) None of the above
Answer: (c) Purvachal

(iii) The western coastal strip south of Goa is referred to as:
(a) Coromandel   (b) Konkan   (c) Kannad   (d) Northern Circar
Answer: (c) Kannad

(iv) The highest peak in the Eastern Ghats is:
(a) Anai Mudi   (b) Kanchenjunga   (c) Mahendragiri   (d) Khasi
Answer: (c) Mahendragiri
Q2: Briefly describe how the Himalayas were formed.
Answer:
1. Millions of years ago, the Indo-Australian plate separated from the supercontinent Gondwanaland and drifted northwards.
2. This northward collision resulted in the plate colliding with the much larger Eurasian Plate.
3. Due to this high-impact collision, the sedimentary rocks deposited in the ancient sea known as Tethys folded to form the mountain system of Western Asia and the Himalayas.
Q3: How do the major physiographic units of India complement each other to make the country rich in natural resources?
Answer:
1. Himalayas: Act as major water source for perennial rivers and provide vast forest and wildlife wealth.
2. Northern Plains: Serve as the granaries of the country, providing food security for the nation.
3. Peninsular Plateau: Storehouse of rich minerals playing a central role in India’s industrialization.
4. Coastal Plains & Islands: Provide strategic sites for fishing, maritime trade, and port activities.

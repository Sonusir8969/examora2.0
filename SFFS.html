Ch-13: Gender, Religion and Caste 30 Questions

1. A system in which all work inside the home is either done by the women of the family, or organized by them through domestic helpers, is known as:

  • A. Sexual division of labour
  • B. Gender hierarchy
  • C. Patriarchal division
  • D. Matriarchal structure

Answer: A. Sexual division of labour

Explanation: Sexual division of labour is a system in which all household work is assigned to women while public and economic work is handled by men.

2. A system or society that values men more and gives them power over women is referred to as:

  • A. Patriarchy
  • B. Matriarchy
  • C. Secularism
  • D. Oligarchy

Answer: A. Patriarchy

Explanation: Patriarchy literally means 'rule by father' and refers to a male-dominated social setup where men exercise control over women.

3. What are radical women's movements aiming at equality in personal and family life called?

  • A. Feminist movements
  • B. Suffragette movements
  • C. Human rights movements
  • D. Social reform movements

Answer: A. Feminist movements

Explanation: Movements organized by women demanding equal rights in education, career, political and personal life are known as Feminist movements.

4. Which act provides that equal wages should be paid for equal work to both men and women?

  • A. Equal Remuneration Act, 1976
  • B. Equal Opportunities Act, 1985
  • C. Minimum Wages Act, 1948
  • D. Factory Act, 1952

Answer: A. Equal Remuneration Act, 1976

Explanation: The Equal Remuneration Act of 1976 mandates that equal wages must be paid for equal work across all sectors.

5. In which set of countries is the participation of women in public life exceptionally high?

  • A. Scandinavian countries (Sweden, Norway, Finland)
  • B. South Asian countries
  • C. Middle Eastern countries
  • D. African countries

Answer: A. Scandinavian countries (Sweden, Norway, Finland)

Explanation: In Scandinavian countries like Sweden, Norway, and Finland, gender equality is high and women participate actively in public life.

6. What is the approximate proportion of women representatives in the Lok Sabha of India according to recent parliamentary data?

  • A. Around 12% to 14%
  • B. Around 33%
  • C. Around 50%
  • D. Around 5%

Answer: A. Around 12% to 14%

Explanation: The percentage of elected women members in Lok Sabha reached around 14.36% for the first time in 2019, which remains low compared to international averages.

7. What percentage of seats in local government bodies (Panchayats and Municipalities) in India are reserved for women?

  • A. One-third (33%)
  • B. Half (50%)
  • C. One-fourth (25%)
  • D. Two-thirds (66%)

Answer: A. One-third (33%)

Explanation: By constitutional amendment, one-third of seats in rural and urban local self-government bodies are legally reserved for women.

8. What is the child sex ratio (number of girl children per 1,000 boys) indicator of in Indian society?

  • A. Gender bias leading to female foeticide
  • B. Natural demographic balance
  • C. High female literacy rates
  • D. Matriarchal preferences

Answer: A. Gender bias leading to female foeticide

Explanation: The decline in child sex ratio in many regions points toward sex-selective abortion and preference for male children in society.

9. Laws that deal with family matters such as marriage, divorce, adoption, and inheritance are known as:

  • A. Family Laws
  • B. Criminal Laws
  • C. Civil Procedure Laws
  • D. Constitutional Laws

Answer: A. Family Laws

Explanation: Family laws govern personal matters including marriage, divorce, custody, adoption, and property inheritance across religions.

10. Belief that religion is the principal basis of social community and that followers of a religion form one nation is called:

  • A. Communalism
  • B. Secularism
  • C. Regionalism
  • D. Socialism

Answer: A. Communalism

Explanation: Communal politics is based on the idea that religion is the principal basis of social identity and political grouping.

11. An extreme form of communalism can lead to which of the following outcomes?

  • A. Communal violence, riots, and massacre
  • B. National integration
  • C. Economic equality
  • D. Universal suffrage

Answer: A. Communal violence, riots, and massacre

Explanation: In its ugliest form, communalism leads to inter-religious violence, riots, and targeted conflict as seen during Partition.

12. Which of the following is a feature of a 'Secular State' like India?

  • A. No official religion for the Indian state
  • B. Special status given to Hinduism
  • C. Compulsory religious education in schools
  • D. Tax collection based on religious affiliation

Answer: A. No official religion for the Indian state

Explanation: India has no state religion; the Constitution guarantees freedom to profess, practice, and propagate any religion or none.

13. Which Article of the Indian Constitution prohibits discrimination on grounds of religion, race, caste, sex, or place of birth?

  • A. Article 15
  • B. Article 21
  • C. Article 370
  • D. Article 32

Answer: A. Article 15

Explanation: Article 15 explicitly bans discrimination by the State against any citizen based on religion, race, caste, sex, or place of birth.

14. What constitutional measure was taken to eliminate the extreme age-old social evil based on caste in India?

  • A. Abolition of Untouchability under Article 17
  • B. Creation of separate electorates
  • C. Ban on inter-caste marriages
  • D. Mandatory hereditary occupations

Answer: A. Abolition of Untouchability under Article 17

Explanation: Article 17 of the Indian Constitution outlawed untouchability and forbade its practice in any form.

15. A hierarchy in which all the caste groups are placed from the highest to the lowest castes is known as:

  • A. Caste hierarchy
  • B. Economic ladder
  • C. Political ranking
  • D. Occupational division

Answer: A. Caste hierarchy

Explanation: Caste hierarchy is a ladder-like structure where social groups are ranked above or below others based on birth.

16. Which among the following social reformers worked actively to establish a society free of caste prejudices in India?

  • A. Jyotirao Phule, Dr. B.R. Ambedkar, and Periyar EV Ramasamy
  • B. Lord Cornwallis and Lord Dalhousie
  • C. Robert Clive and Warren Hastings
  • D. Clement Attlee and Stafford Cripps

Answer: A. Jyotirao Phule, Dr. B.R. Ambedkar, and Periyar EV Ramasamy

Explanation: Social reformers like Jotiba Phule, Gandhiji, Dr. B.R. Ambedkar, and Periyar advocated for a society free from caste inequality.

17. Which economic factor has contributed significantly to breaking down traditional caste barriers in modern India?

  • A. Urbanisation and growth of literacy/occupational mobility
  • B. Decline in factory jobs
  • C. Continuation of hereditary village trades
  • D. Isolation of rural communities

Answer: A. Urbanisation and growth of literacy/occupational mobility

Explanation: Economic development, large-scale urbanisation, growth of literacy, and occupational mobility have weakened caste rigidities.

18. The shift from one occupation to another, usually when a new generation takes up occupations other than those practiced by their ancestors, is called:

  • A. Occupational mobility
  • B. Social stagnation
  • C. Caste rigidity
  • D. Secular shifting

Answer: A. Occupational mobility

Explanation: Occupational mobility refers to the ability to switch professions regardless of hereditary caste traditions.

19. When political parties choose candidates in elections, why do they consider the caste composition of the electorate?

  • A. To muster necessary support and win elections
  • B. Because it is mandated by the Supreme Court
  • C. To enforce religious taxes
  • D. To prevent women from voting

Answer: A. To muster necessary support and win elections

Explanation: Parties select candidates keeping in mind the caste demographics of a constituency to consolidate vote banks and secure a majority.

20. Is it true that a single caste group can solely determine the outcome of a parliamentary constituency in India?

  • A. No, because no single caste holds a clear majority in most parliamentary constituencies
  • B. Yes, every constituency in India belongs to one dominant caste only
  • C. Yes, because voters only vote for candidates of their own caste
  • D. No, because caste is banned from elections completely

Answer: A. No, because no single caste holds a clear majority in most parliamentary constituencies

Explanation: No parliamentary constituency has a clear majority of a single caste; every candidate needs to win the trust of multiple communities.

21. What happens when candidates from the same caste are nominated by multiple political parties in a single constituency?

  • A. Caste votes get divided among candidates
  • B. The election gets cancelled
  • C. All votes go to the ruling party automatically
  • D. Minorities lose their right to vote

Answer: A. Caste votes get divided among candidates

Explanation: When multiple parties field candidates from the same dominant caste, the caste vote gets split among rival contenders.

22. What statement best reflects Mahatma Gandhi's view regarding religion and politics?

  • A. Religion can never be separated from politics (referring to moral values of religion)
  • B. Politics should be based strictly on athletic merit
  • C. Religion must be completely destroyed for political progress
  • D. Only one majority religion should control politics

Answer: A. Religion can never be separated from politics (referring to moral values of religion)

Explanation: Gandhiji believed politics must be guided by ethics drawn from all religions, rather than dogmatic or sectarian interests.

23. What did Human Rights groups in India demand regarding religious minorities?

  • A. Special protection measures because most victims of communal riots belong to minority groups
  • B. Ban on all minority educational institutions
  • C. Complete abolition of state welfare programs
  • D. Mandatory military service for minority communities

Answer: A. Special protection measures because most victims of communal riots belong to minority groups

Explanation: Human rights groups highlight that communal violence disproportionately affects religious minorities, requiring state protection.

24. What does the Women's Reservation Act (Nari Shakti Vandan Adhiniyam) passed recently by Indian Parliament provide?

  • A. 33% reservation for women in Lok Sabha and State Legislative Assemblies
  • B. 50% reservation in private jobs
  • C. Free higher education for all girl students
  • D. Equal inheritance of ancestral property only

Answer: A. 33% reservation for women in Lok Sabha and State Legislative Assemblies

Explanation: The Nari Shakti Vandan Adhiniyam reserves 33 per cent seats for women in the Lok Sabha and state assemblies.

25. What term is used for those social groups that have faced social discrimination and exclusion historically and are listed under government schedules?

  • A. Scheduled Castes (SC) and Scheduled Tribes (ST)
  • B. General Category
  • C. High Income Group
  • D. Non-Resident Indians

Answer: A. Scheduled Castes (SC) and Scheduled Tribes (ST)

Explanation: Scheduled Castes (Dalits) and Scheduled Tribes (Adivasis) are constitutionally recognized groups eligible for affirmative action policies.

26. Expressing caste divisions in politics can sometimes yield positive results because it:

  • A. Gives disadvantaged communities space to demand their fair share of power
  • B. Leads to military intervention in governance
  • C. Causes complete destruction of national unity
  • D. Promotes wealth concentration in elite families

Answer: A. Gives disadvantaged communities space to demand their fair share of power

Explanation: Political mobilization along caste lines has allowed historically marginalized groups to seek representation and social justice.

27. Which of the following statements about urbanisation is correct?

  • A. Urbanisation refers to the shift of population from rural areas to urban areas
  • B. Urbanisation means moving from cities to villages
  • C. Urbanisation strictly refers to agricultural modernization
  • D. Urbanisation reduces literacy rates

Answer: A. Urbanisation refers to the shift of population from rural areas to urban areas

Explanation: Urbanisation is the migration of people from villages to towns and cities, leading to social changes and neutral workplace environments.

28. Which form of division is unique to Indian society and not found in most other parts of the world in the same form?

  • A. Caste system
  • B. Gender division
  • C. Class division
  • D. Linguistic division

Answer: A. Caste system

Explanation: While gender and class divisions exist worldwide, the hereditary and endogamous caste system is specific to India.

29. What is meant by the term 'Vote Bank' in Indian political discourse?

  • A. A group of voters who tend to vote together for a specific political party based on caste or religion
  • B. A commercial bank handling election funds
  • C. A government agency collecting ballot papers
  • D. Electronic Voting Machines stored securely

Answer: A. A group of voters who tend to vote together for a specific political party based on caste or religion

Explanation: When a large proportion of a social group votes consistently for a single party, it is described as that party's 'vote bank'.

30. Why is political mobilization on religious lines considered dangerous for democracy?

  • A. It can divide society into hostile groups and weaken national harmony
  • B. It lowers voter turnout in elections
  • C. It reduces government expenditure on infrastructure
  • D. It increases foreign trade competition

Answer: A. It can divide society into hostile groups and weaken national harmony

Explanation: Mobilizing voters purely on religious grounds fuels intolerance, divides citizens, and threatens the secular fabric of the nation.

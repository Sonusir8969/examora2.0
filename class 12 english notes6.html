NCERT Solutions for Class 12 English Chapter 6 Poets and Pancakes

NCERT Solutions for Class 12 English Flamingo Chapter 6 Poets and Pancakes covers Asokamitran's humorous account of the Gemini Studios make-up department, the theme of behind-the-scenes film industry life, and important questions and answers.

1. Chapter Summary

Short Note:

• The chapter offers a witty, satirical glimpse into the workings of Gemini Studios, a prominent South Indian film production house, focusing particularly on its make-up department and the various eccentric personalities who worked there.

• The author humorously describes the make-up department's obsession with using excessive amounts of grease paint and its rigid caste-based hierarchy regarding who could apply make-up to which type of actor, highlighting subtle social commentary through comedy.

• The chapter also narrates an amusing anecdote involving a poet, who was recruited to write a Tamil translation of a Hindi film's story, but ends up working on entirely unrelated administrative tasks, illustrating the often chaotic and unpredictable nature of the studio's operations.

2. Key Characters and Anecdotes

Short Note:

• The Make-up Man: Known as the studio's chief make-up artist, he oversees a caste-conscious system of applying make-up, revealing subtle prejudices ingrained within the industry's practices at the time.

• The Poet (referred to humorously): A talented Tamil poet, hired seemingly for his literary skills, who instead finds himself thrust into unrelated bureaucratic responsibilities within the studio, symbolising the often disorganised and whimsical nature of studio management.

• The chapter's title, 'Poets and Pancakes', humorously juxtaposes the poetic, artistic aspirations of studio staff (poets) with the mundane, superficial world of thick, pancake-like make-up used on film sets.

3. Theme & Message

Short Note:

• The chapter uses gentle satire and humour to critique the superficiality, bureaucratic disorganisation, and subtle social hierarchies present within the film industry, while affectionately capturing the unique atmosphere of Gemini Studios.

• It also reflects broader themes about the unpredictable nature of creative industries, where individual talents are sometimes overshadowed or misapplied due to organisational quirks.

Reading With Insight (Textbook Questions)

Q1: What does the author mean by the term 'a pancake' in relation to make-up used at Gemini Studios?
Answer: The 'pancake' refers to a specific thick, heavy foundation make-up product used generously by the studio's make-up department; the author uses this term satirically to highlight the excessive, almost comical over-application of make-up on actors, regardless of the natural requirements of each scene or character.
Q2: How does the author use humour to describe the office politics and hierarchy at Gemini Studios?
Answer: The author uses gentle, ironic humour to describe various eccentric characters and their exaggerated sense of self-importance, along with the studio's chaotic, informal management style, highlighting absurd situations such as the poet being assigned unrelated bureaucratic tasks, to satirise the often illogical office politics and hierarchy prevalent in such creative organisations.
Q3: What does the incident involving the office boy and the legal adviser reveal about Gemini Studios' work culture?
Answer: This incident reveals a rather chaotic, unstructured work culture at Gemini Studios, where roles and responsibilities were often loosely defined and could shift unexpectedly, reflecting a broader satire on the improvisational and sometimes disorganised nature of the Indian film industry during that era.

Extract-Based / Short Answer Questions

Q4: What subtle social commentary does the chapter offer through its description of the make-up department's practices?
Answer: Through its description of the caste-based hierarchy in assigning make-up artists to actors of different social standing, the chapter subtly critiques the persistence of social prejudices and rigid hierarchical thinking even within a modern, seemingly progressive industry like filmmaking.
Q5: Why is the chapter's title 'Poets and Pancakes' considered fitting?
Answer: The title cleverly juxtaposes the poetic, literary aspirations associated with 'poets' against the superficial, cosmetic world symbolised by 'pancakes' (make-up), effectively capturing the chapter's central irony about talented individuals often being sidelined or misapplied within the often superficial and disorganised environment of the film industry.

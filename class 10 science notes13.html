NCERT Solutions for Class 10 Science Chapter 13 Our Environment

NCERT Solutions for Class 10 Science Chapter 13 Our Environment covers ecosystems, biotic and abiotic components, food chains and webs, energy flow and the 10% law, biological magnification, ozone layer depletion, and waste management techniques.

1. Ecosystem and Its Components

Short Note:

• Ecosystem: A self-sustaining structural and functional unit of nature consisting of living organisms interacting with each other and their non-living physical environment.

• Biotic Components: Producers (autotrophs), Consumers (herbivores, carnivores, omnivores, parasites), and Decomposers (bacteria and fungi).

• Abiotic Components: Non-living factors such as temperature, light, water, soil, minerals, and air.

• Types: Natural ecosystems (forest, lake, ocean) and Artificial/Man-made ecosystems (crop fields, garden, aquarium).

2. Food Chains and Food Webs

Short Note:

• Food Chain: A linear sequence of organisms where energy and nutrients pass from one organism to another as one eats another (e.g., Grass → Deer → Lion).

• Food Web: A complex network of interconnected food chains operating naturally within an ecosystem, reflecting real feeding relationships.

3. Trophic Levels & Energy Transfer (10% Law)

Short Note:

• Trophic Levels: The distinct steps or levels in a food chain where transfer of food energy takes place (Producers → Primary Consumers → Secondary Consumers → Tertiary Consumers).

• 10% Law (Lindeman's Law): Only about 10% of the energy available at a particular trophic level is transferred to the next higher level. The remaining 90% is lost as heat to the environment during metabolism and respiration.

• Flow of energy in an ecosystem is always unidirectional.

4. Biological Magnification

Short Note:

• Biological Magnification (Biomagnification): The progressive accumulation of non-biodegradable harmful chemicals (like pesticides, DDT) at successive higher trophic levels in a food chain.

• Human beings occupy the topmost level in most food chains, resulting in maximum chemical concentration inside human bodies.

5. Environmental Problems & Waste Management

Short Note:

• Ozone Layer Depletion: Ozone (O3) protects Earth from harmful ultraviolet (UV) radiation. Depletion is caused by Chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs) used in refrigerators and fire extinguishers.

• Biodegradable Waste: Waste materials that can be broken down naturally by microorganisms (e.g., vegetable peels, paper, cotton).

• Non-Biodegradable Waste: Materials that cannot be decomposed by natural biological processes (e.g., plastics, polythene bags, metals, synthetic fibers).

Intext Questions - Page Number: 257

Q1: What are trophic levels? Give an example of a food chain and state the different trophic levels in it.
Answer: Trophic levels are the various steps or stages in a food chain at which transfer of energy takes place.

Example of a terrestrial food chain:

Grass → Grasshopper → Frog → Snake

• First Trophic Level (Producers): Grass

• Second Trophic Level (Primary Consumers): Grasshopper

• Third Trophic Level (Secondary Consumers): Frog

• Fourth Trophic Level (Tertiary Consumers): Snake

Q2: What is the role of decomposers in the ecosystem?
Answer: Decomposers (microorganisms like bacteria and fungi) break down complex organic compounds present in dead plants and animals into simple inorganic substances. These inorganic nutrients are released back into soil and water, making them available again for absorption by producers, thereby recycling nutrients in nature.

Intext Questions - Page Number: 261

Q1: Why are some substances biodegradable and some non-biodegradable?
Answer: Decomposers produce specific enzymes that break down natural organic bonds. Substances like paper and plant wastes can be degraded because enzymes can act upon them. Synthetic materials like plastics have man-made chemical structures that specific enzymes cannot break down, making them non-biodegradable.
Q2: Give any two ways in which non-biodegradable substances would affect the environment.
Answer:

1. They persist in soil and water bodies for long periods, causing environmental pollution and choking drainage systems.

2. Toxic non-biodegradable chemicals (like DDT) enter food chains and undergo biomagnification, threatening top-level species including humans.

Textbook Chapter End Exercise Questions

Q1: Which of the following groups contain only biodegradable items?

(a) Grass, flowers and leather
(b) Grass, wood and plastic
(c) Fruit-peels, cake and lime-juice
(d) Cake, wood and grass

Correct Answer: Options (a), (c), and (d) contain only biodegradable items.

Q2: Which of the following constitute a food-chain?

(a) Grass, wheat and mango
(b) Grass, goat and human
(c) Goat, cow and elephant
(d) Grass, fish and goat

Correct Answer: (b) Grass, goat and human.

Q3: Which of the following are environment-friendly practices?

(a) Carrying cloth-bags to put purchases in while shopping
(b) Switching off unnecessary lights and fans
(c) Walking to school instead of getting your mother to drop you on her scooter
(d) All of the above

Correct Answer: (d) All of the above.

Q4: What is ozone and how does it affect any ecosystem?
Answer: Ozone is a molecule formed by three atoms of oxygen (O3). It forms a protective layer in the stratosphere that absorbs high-energy ultraviolet (UV) radiations emitted by the sun. Without this layer, harmful UV rays would reach Earth's surface, causing skin cancer, cataracts, immune system suppression in animals, and damaging plant growth.
Q5: How can you help in reducing the problem of waste disposal? Give any two methods.
Answer:

1. Segregation and Recycling: Separating biodegradable and non-biodegradable wastes at the source and recycling plastic, glass, and metal items.

2. Composting: Converting kitchen wet waste, vegetable peels, and fallen leaves into organic manure using compost pits.

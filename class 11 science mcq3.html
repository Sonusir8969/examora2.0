Phy-3: Motion in a Straight Line 30 Questions

1. The total length of the actual path travelled by an object, regardless of direction, is called:

  • A. Distance
  • B. Displacement
  • C. Speed
  • D. Velocity

Answer: A. Distance

Explanation: Distance is a scalar quantity representing the total path length covered by an object during its motion.

2. The shortest straight-line distance between the initial and final position of an object, along with direction, is called:

  • A. Displacement
  • B. Distance
  • C. Acceleration
  • D. Speed

Answer: A. Displacement

Explanation: Displacement is a vector quantity representing the change in position, considering both magnitude and direction.

3. The rate of change of distance with respect to time is called:

  • A. Speed
  • B. Velocity
  • C. Acceleration
  • D. Displacement

Answer: A. Speed

Explanation: Speed is a scalar quantity defined as the distance covered per unit time.

4. The rate of change of displacement with respect to time is called:

  • A. Velocity
  • B. Speed
  • C. Acceleration
  • D. Distance

Answer: A. Velocity

Explanation: Velocity is a vector quantity representing the rate of change of displacement, including both magnitude and direction.

5. The rate of change of velocity with respect to time is called:

  • A. Acceleration
  • B. Velocity
  • C. Speed
  • D. Displacement

Answer: A. Acceleration

Explanation: Acceleration measures how quickly the velocity of an object changes over time.

6. The SI unit of acceleration is:

  • A. m/s²
  • B. m/s
  • C. m
  • D. s

Answer: A. m/s²

Explanation: Since acceleration is the change in velocity (m/s) per unit time (s), its SI unit is m/s².

7. The first equation of motion is given by:

  • A. v = u + at
  • B. s = ut + 1/2at²
  • C. v² = u² + 2as
  • D. s = vt

Answer: A. v = u + at

Explanation: The first equation of motion relates final velocity (v), initial velocity (u), acceleration (a) and time (t) as v = u + at.

8. The second equation of motion is given by:

  • A. s = ut + 1/2at²
  • B. v = u + at
  • C. v² = u² + 2as
  • D. s = vt

Answer: A. s = ut + 1/2at²

Explanation: The second equation of motion calculates displacement (s) in terms of initial velocity, time and acceleration.

9. The third equation of motion is given by:

  • A. v² = u² + 2as
  • B. v = u + at
  • C. s = ut + 1/2at²
  • D. a = v/t

Answer: A. v² = u² + 2as

Explanation: The third equation of motion relates velocities and displacement without directly involving time.

10. A graph plotting the position of an object against time is called a:

  • A. Position-time graph
  • B. Velocity-time graph
  • C. Acceleration-time graph
  • D. Force-time graph

Answer: A. Position-time graph

Explanation: A position-time graph visually represents how an object's position changes over the course of time.

11. The slope of a position-time graph at any point gives the object's:

  • A. Instantaneous velocity
  • B. Instantaneous acceleration
  • C. Total distance
  • D. Average speed only

Answer: A. Instantaneous velocity

Explanation: The slope of the position-time graph at a given instant represents the object's instantaneous velocity at that moment.

12. The slope of a velocity-time graph at any point gives the object's:

  • A. Instantaneous acceleration
  • B. Instantaneous velocity
  • C. Total displacement
  • D. Position

Answer: A. Instantaneous acceleration

Explanation: The slope of the velocity-time graph represents the rate of change of velocity, i.e., the instantaneous acceleration.

13. The area under a velocity-time graph represents the object's:

  • A. Displacement
  • B. Acceleration
  • C. Speed
  • D. Force

Answer: A. Displacement

Explanation: The area enclosed under a velocity-time graph, between the curve and the time axis, gives the total displacement of the object.

14. If an object moves with uniform velocity, its acceleration is:

  • A. Zero
  • B. Constant but non-zero
  • C. Increasing
  • D. Undefined

Answer: A. Zero

Explanation: Since velocity remains constant during uniform motion, there is no change in velocity, resulting in zero acceleration.

15. If an object is in free fall under gravity (ignoring air resistance), its acceleration is:

  • A. Constant, equal to 'g'
  • B. Zero
  • C. Continuously increasing
  • D. Continuously decreasing

Answer: A. Constant, equal to 'g'

Explanation: Under free fall, an object experiences a constant acceleration due to gravity, denoted as 'g' (approximately 9.8 m/s²).

16. A body's motion is called uniformly accelerated if its velocity changes by:

  • A. Equal amounts in equal intervals of time
  • B. Random amounts each second
  • C. Zero amount over time
  • D. Only in the horizontal direction

Answer: A. Equal amounts in equal intervals of time

Explanation: Uniform acceleration means the velocity increases (or decreases) by the same amount during each successive equal time interval.

17. Displacement is classified as which type of physical quantity?

  • A. Vector quantity
  • B. Scalar quantity
  • C. Dimensionless quantity
  • D. Fundamental quantity only

Answer: A. Vector quantity

Explanation: Displacement has both magnitude and a specific direction, classifying it as a vector quantity.

18. Distance is classified as which type of physical quantity?

  • A. Scalar quantity
  • B. Vector quantity
  • C. Dimensionless quantity
  • D. Complex quantity

Answer: A. Scalar quantity

Explanation: Distance only has magnitude and no associated direction, making it a scalar quantity.

19. The average velocity of an object is calculated as:

  • A. Total displacement divided by total time taken
  • B. Total distance divided by total time taken
  • C. Final velocity minus initial velocity
  • D. Acceleration multiplied by time

Answer: A. Total displacement divided by total time taken

Explanation: Average velocity specifically uses total displacement (not distance) divided by the total time interval.

20. The average speed of an object is calculated as:

  • A. Total distance divided by total time taken
  • B. Total displacement divided by total time taken
  • C. Change in velocity divided by time
  • D. Initial velocity plus final velocity

Answer: A. Total distance divided by total time taken

Explanation: Average speed uses total path length (distance) covered, divided by the total time taken.

21. If a body moves with a negative acceleration, it is also referred to as:

  • A. Retardation or deceleration
  • B. Uniform velocity
  • C. Zero velocity
  • D. Positive acceleration

Answer: A. Retardation or deceleration

Explanation: Negative acceleration, which reduces an object's speed over time, is commonly termed retardation or deceleration.

22. For an object moving with constant velocity, the position-time graph is a:

  • A. Straight line with constant slope
  • B. Parabola
  • C. Horizontal straight line at zero
  • D. Curved exponential line

Answer: A. Straight line with constant slope

Explanation: Since velocity is constant, the rate of change of position remains constant, resulting in a straight-line position-time graph.

23. For an object undergoing uniform acceleration starting from rest, the velocity-time graph is a:

  • A. Straight line passing through the origin
  • B. Horizontal line
  • C. Curved parabola
  • D. Vertical line

Answer: A. Straight line passing through the origin

Explanation: Since velocity increases linearly with time under uniform acceleration starting from rest, the graph is a straight line through the origin.

24. If a car travels 100 m in 5 s at constant velocity, its velocity is:

  • A. 20 m/s
  • B. 500 m/s
  • C. 5 m/s
  • D. 100 m/s

Answer: A. 20 m/s

Explanation: Velocity = displacement/time = 100/5 = 20 m/s.

25. A ball is thrown vertically upward. At the highest point, its velocity is:

  • A. Zero
  • B. Maximum
  • C. Equal to initial velocity
  • D. Undefined

Answer: A. Zero

Explanation: At the highest point of vertical motion under gravity, the ball momentarily comes to rest before falling back, so its velocity is zero.

26. The value of acceleration due to gravity 'g' near the Earth's surface is approximately:

  • A. 9.8 m/s²
  • B. 9.8 m/s
  • C. 10 m/s only
  • D. 1 m/s²

Answer: A. 9.8 m/s²

Explanation: The standard value of acceleration due to gravity near the Earth's surface is approximately 9.8 metres per second squared.

27. In one-dimensional motion, an object can move along:

  • A. A single straight line only
  • B. Any curved path
  • C. Two directions simultaneously
  • D. A circular path only

Answer: A. A single straight line only

Explanation: Motion in a straight line, by definition, restricts the object's movement to a single dimension (one straight-line path).

28. If an object's velocity-time graph is a horizontal straight line, the object is undergoing:

  • A. Uniform velocity with zero acceleration
  • B. Uniform acceleration
  • C. Retardation
  • D. Free fall

Answer: A. Uniform velocity with zero acceleration

Explanation: A horizontal line on a velocity-time graph indicates the velocity remains constant, meaning acceleration is zero.

29. If displacement is zero but distance travelled is non-zero, the object's motion involves:

  • A. Returning to its starting point after moving along some path
  • B. No movement at all
  • C. Constant velocity throughout
  • D. Motion only in a straight line without direction change

Answer: A. Returning to its starting point after moving along some path

Explanation: When an object travels along a path and eventually returns to its initial position, distance is non-zero while net displacement is zero.

30. For a body under free fall starting from rest, the equation used to determine the distance fallen in time t is:

  • A. s = 1/2gt²
  • B. s = gt
  • C. s = ut
  • D. s = g/t

Answer: A. s = 1/2gt²

Explanation: Applying the second equation of motion with initial velocity u = 0 gives s = 1/2gt² for free-fall distance.

Intext Questions - Page Number: 61

Q1: What would be the electron dot structure of carbon dioxide which has the formula CO2?
Answer: Carbon has 4 valence electrons and Oxygen has 6 valence electrons. In CO2, the central carbon atom shares two pairs of electrons with each of the two oxygen atoms to form double covalent bonds.

Structure: :O::C::O: (where each pair of dots represent shared valence electron pairs).
Q2: What would be the electron dot structure of a molecule of sulphur which is made up of eight atoms of sulphur?
Answer: An S8 molecule consists of eight sulphur atoms linked together in a ring-like (puckered crown) structure. Each sulphur atom shares one electron pair with two adjacent sulphur atoms to form a single covalent bond.

Intext Questions - Page Number: 68

Q1: How many structural isomers can you draw for pentane?
Answer: Pentane (C5H12) has 3 structural isomers:

1. n-pentane: CH3–CH2–CH2–CH2–CH3

2. Isopentane (2-methylbutane): CH3–CH(CH3)–CH2–CH3

3. Neopentane (2,2-dimethylpropane): C(CH3)4

Q2: What are the two properties of carbon which lead to the huge number of carbon compounds we see around us?
Answer:

1. Catenation: The unique ability of carbon to form strong covalent bonds with other carbon atoms to form long chains, branched chains, and closed rings.

2. Tetravalency: Carbon has four valence electrons, allowing it to bond with four other monovalent atoms or groups of atoms (such as Hydrogen, Oxygen, Nitrogen, Chlorine).

Intext Questions - Page Number: 71

Q1: Why is the conversion of ethanol to ethanoic acid an oxidation reaction?
Answer: The conversion of ethanol (CH3CH2OH) to ethanoic acid (CH3COOH) involves the removal of hydrogen atoms and addition of oxygen atoms using an oxidising agent like alkaline KMnO4 or acidified K2Cr2O7.

Equation: CH3CH2OH + 2[O] → CH3COOH + H2O

Intext Questions - Page Number: 74

Q1: Would you be able to check if water is hard by using a detergent?
Answer: No, because detergents form lather readily with both soft and hard water without reacting with the Calcium (Ca2+) and Magnesium (Mg2+) ions present in hard water. To test water hardness, soap should be used instead, as it forms insoluble precipitate (scum) in hard water.

Textbook Chapter End Exercise Questions

Q1: Ethane, with the molecular formula C2H6 has:

(a) 6 covalent bonds
(b) 7 covalent bonds
(c) 8 covalent bonds
(d) 9 covalent bonds

Correct Answer: (b) 7 covalent bonds (1 C–C single bond and 6 C–H single bonds).

Q2: Butanone is a four-carbon compound with the functional group:

(a) Carboxylic acid
(b) Aldehyde
(c) Ketone
(d) Alcohol

Correct Answer: (c) Ketone (–CO– group).

Q3: While cooking, if the bottom of the vessel is getting blackened on the outside, it means that:

(a) The food is not cooked completely.
(b) The fuel is not burning completely.
(c) The fuel is wet.
(d) The fuel is burning completely.

Correct Answer: (b) The fuel is not burning completely (Incomplete combustion releases unburnt carbon particles as soot).

Q4: Explain the mechanism of the cleaning action of soaps.
Answer: A soap molecule consists of two parts:

1. A hydrophobic ionic tail (hydrocarbon chain) that dissolves in oil/grease.

2. A hydrophilic ionic head (–COONa+) that remains in contact with water.

When soap is mixed with dirty water containing grease, soap molecules cluster together with their hydrophobic tails facing inwards towards the oil droplets and hydrophilic heads facing outwards into water, forming structures called micelles. These micelles trap the dirt and float in water, allowing dirt to be washed away easily during rinsing.

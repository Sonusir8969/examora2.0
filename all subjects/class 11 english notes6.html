NCERT Solutions for Class 11 English Ch-6: Childhood (Poem)

'Childhood' by Markus Natten is an introspective poem in which the poet reflects on the gradual loss of innocent childhood beliefs and explores when exactly one's innocent childhood truly comes to an end.

Overview of Topics & Subtopics

  • The Poet's Search for When Childhood 'Ended'
  • Loss of Innocent Beliefs (Santa Claus, Adults Always Being Right)
  • The Realization That Heaven Is Not a Physical Place
  • Themes of Growing Up and the Loss of Innocence

Important Questions and Answers

Q1: What central question does the poet repeatedly ask throughout the poem?
Answer: The poet repeatedly asks the question, 'When did my childhood go?', reflecting on various stages and realizations in his growing years to try and pinpoint the exact moment when his innocent childhood ended.
Q2: What are some of the childhood beliefs the poet mentions losing as he grew older?
Answer: The poet mentions losing his belief that adults always tell the truth and are always right, his belief in Santa Claus, and his childhood assumption that heaven exists as an actual physical place located up in the sky.
Q3: What realization about 'self' and 'truth' troubles the poet as he matures?
Answer: As he grows, the poet becomes troubled by the realization that his sense of 'self' seems to exist only in his own mind and cannot be verified or fully shared with others, and that truth and reality are far more complex and uncertain than he believed as a child.
Q4: What does the poet ultimately conclude about the exact moment his childhood ended?
Answer: The poet concludes that he cannot pinpoint one exact moment when his childhood ended; rather, it was a gradual, ongoing process, occurring through the slow accumulation of doubts and disillusionments rather than one single defining event.
Q5: What is the overarching theme of 'Childhood'?
Answer: The overarching theme of the poem is the gradual, often disorienting transition from childhood innocence to adult understanding, exploring how growing up inevitably involves losing certain naive certainties and beliefs held so firmly in childhood.

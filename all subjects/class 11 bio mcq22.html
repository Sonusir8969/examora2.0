Bio-22: Chemical Coordination and Integration 30 Questions

1. Glands that lack ducts and secrete their chemical messengers directly into the bloodstream are called:

  • A. Endocrine glands
  • B. Exocrine glands
  • C. Heterocrine glands
  • D. Merocrine glands

Answer: A. Endocrine glands

Explanation: Endocrine glands are ductless glands that release non-nutrient chemical messengers (hormones) directly into extracellular fluid and blood.

2. Which master structure connects the nervous system to the endocrine system and regulates the pituitary gland?

  • A. Hypothalamus
  • B. Epithalamus
  • C. Thalamus
  • D. Pineal gland

Answer: A. Hypothalamus

Explanation: The hypothalamus contains neurosecretory cells (nuclei) that synthesize neurohormones to control synthesis and secretion of anterior pituitary hormones.

3. The anterior lobe of the pituitary gland (Adenohypophysis) is connected to the hypothalamus via a:

  • A. Portal circulatory system
  • B. Direct neural connection
  • C. Lymphatic system
  • D. Glial bridge

Answer: A. Portal circulatory system

Explanation: The hypophyseal portal system transports releasing and inhibiting hormones from the hypothalamus directly to the anterior pituitary.

4. Posterior pituitary (Neurohypophysis) stores and releases which two hormones produced by the hypothalamus?

  • A. Oxytocin and Vasopressin (ADH)
  • B. Growth Hormone and Prolactin
  • C. ACTH and TSH
  • D. FSH and LH

Answer: A. Oxytocin and Vasopressin (ADH)

Explanation: Neurohypophysis does not synthesize hormones; it stores and releases Oxytocin and Vasopressin synthesized by hypothalamic neurosecretory cells.

5. Hypersecretion of Growth Hormone (GH) in adults leads to severe disfigurement, particularly of the face, called:

  • A. Acromegaly
  • B. Gigantism
  • C. Pituitary Dwarfism
  • D. Cretinism

Answer: A. Acromegaly

Explanation: Excess GH in adults causes acromegaly, marked by enlargement of facial bones and hands, whereas hypersecretion in children causes gigantism.

6. Which hormone stimulates milk synthesis and growth of mammary glands in females?

  • A. Prolactin (PRL)
  • B. Oxytocin
  • C. Luteinizing Hormone (LH)
  • D. Estrogen

Answer: A. Prolactin (PRL)

Explanation: Prolactin promotes development of mammary glands and milk production, whereas Oxytocin stimulates milk ejection (let-down reflex).

7. The hormone that regulates the 24-hour (diurnal) rhythm of the human body, such as sleep-wake cycles, is:

  • A. Melatonin
  • B. Melanocyte Stimulating Hormone (MSH)
  • C. Calcitonin
  • D. Thyroxine

Answer: A. Melatonin

Explanation: Melatonin is secreted by the pineal gland located on the dorsal side of the forebrain and maintains circadian rhythms.

8. Deficiency of iodine in diet leads to enlargement of the thyroid gland, commonly known as:

  • A. Goitre
  • B. Addison’s disease
  • C. Cushing’s syndrome
  • D. Acromegaly

Answer: A. Goitre

Explanation: Iodine is essential for thyroid hormone synthesis; deficiency causes hypothyroidism and compensatory enlargement of the gland (goitre).

9. Hypothyroidism during pregnancy causes stunted growth, mental retardation, and low IQ in infants, termed:

  • A. Cretinism
  • B. Myxedema
  • C. Grave’s disease
  • D. Dwarfism

Answer: A. Cretinism

Explanation: Congenital hypothyroidism leads to cretinism, characterized by dwarfism, mental disability, deaf-mutism, and abnormal skin.

10. Thyroid hormone that lowers blood calcium levels by promoting calcium deposition in bones is:

  • A. Thyrocalcitonin (TCT)
  • B. Parathyroid Hormone (PTH)
  • C. Tetraiodothyronine (T4)
  • D. Triiodothyronine (T3)

Answer: A. Thyrocalcitonin (TCT)

Explanation: Thyrocalcitonin is a protein hormone secreted by thyroid parafollicular C-cells that decreases blood Ca2+ levels.

11. Parathyroid hormone (PTH) increases blood Ca2+ levels, making it a:

  • A. Hypercalcemic hormone
  • B. Hypocalcemic hormone
  • C. Hypoglycemic hormone
  • D. Hyperglycemic hormone

Answer: A. Hypercalcemic hormone

Explanation: PTH stimulates bone resorption, renal calcium reabsorption, and intestinal Ca2+ absorption, elevating blood Ca2+ levels.

12. Which gland plays a major role in differentiation of T-lymphocytes and development of cell-mediated immunity?

  • A. Thymus
  • B. Thyroid
  • C. Adrenal
  • D. Pancreas

Answer: A. Thymus

Explanation: Thymus secretes thymosins, which stimulate maturation of T-lymphocytes essential for cell-mediated immunity.

13. Emergency hormones or fight-or-flight hormones (Adrenaline and Noradrenaline) are secreted by the:

  • A. Adrenal medulla
  • B. Adrenal cortex
  • C. Anterior pituitary
  • D. Hypothalamus

Answer: A. Adrenal medulla

Explanation: Catecholamines (Epinephrine/Adrenaline and Norepinephrine) are rapidly released by the adrenal medulla during emergency or stress situations.

14. Cortisol, the primary glucocorticoid secreted by the adrenal cortex, is involved in:

  • A. Stimulating gluconeogenesis, lipolysis, and anti-inflammatory responses
  • B. Regulating sodium and water balance only
  • C. Decreasing heart rate and blood pressure
  • D. Lowering blood glucose levels

Answer: A. Stimulating gluconeogenesis, lipolysis, and anti-inflammatory responses

Explanation: Glucocorticoids like cortisol increase blood glucose via gluconeogenesis, inhibit cellular glucose uptake, and suppress inflammatory responses.

15. The main mineralocorticoid in humans that regulates electrolyte and water balance in renal tubules is:

  • A. Aldosterone
  • B. Cortisol
  • C. Adrenaline
  • D. Melatonin

Answer: A. Aldosterone

Explanation: Aldosterone acts on distal convoluted tubules of nephrons to stimulate reabsorption of Na+ and water, and excretion of K+ and phosphate.

16. Underproduction of adrenal cortex hormones alters carbohydrate metabolism and causes severe weakness called:

  • A. Addison’s disease
  • B. Cushing’s disease
  • C. Diabetes insipidus
  • D. Grave’s disease

Answer: A. Addison’s disease

Explanation: Hypofunctioning of the adrenal cortex leads to deficiency of aldosterone and cortisol, manifesting as Addison's disease.

17. Insulin is a peptide hormone secreted by which cells of the Islets of Langerhans?

  • A. Beta cells (β-cells)
  • B. Alpha cells (α-cells)
  • C. Delta cells (δ-cells)
  • D. F cells

Answer: A. Beta cells (β-cells)

Explanation: Beta cells of pancreatic islets secrete insulin (hypoglycemic factor), while alpha cells secrete glucagon (hyperglycemic factor).

18. Prolonged hyperglycemia and presence of glucose (glycosuria) and ketone bodies in urine are symptoms of:

  • A. Diabetes mellitus
  • B. Diabetes insipidus
  • C. Addison’s disease
  • D. Diabetes dwarfism

Answer: A. Diabetes mellitus

Explanation: Deficiency or resistance to insulin causes Diabetes mellitus, leading to high blood glucose, glycosuria, and formation of ketone bodies.

19. Glucagon raises blood glucose levels by promoting:

  • A. Glycogenolysis and gluconeogenesis
  • B. Glycogenesis and lipogenesis
  • C. Cellular glucose uptake
  • D. Protein synthesis

Answer: A. Glycogenolysis and gluconeogenesis

Explanation: Glucagon acts on hepatocytes to trigger glycogen breakdown into glucose (glycogenolysis) and gluconeogenesis, elevating blood sugar.

20. Leydig cells (interstitial cells) present in testes produce male sex hormones called:

  • A. Androgens (e.g., Testosterone)
  • B. Estrogens
  • C. Progesterone
  • D. Corticoids

Answer: A. Androgens (e.g., Testosterone)

Explanation: Interstitial cells of Leydig located in intertubular spaces of testes synthesize and secrete androgens, mainly testosterone.

21. Ruptured Graafian follicle transforms after ovulation into a yellow endocrine structure called:

  • A. Corpus luteum
  • B. Corpus albicans
  • C. Corpus callosum
  • D. Macula lutea

Answer: A. Corpus luteum

Explanation: After ovulation, the collapsed ovarian follicle converts into the corpus luteum, which secretes large amounts of progesterone.

22. Which hormone maintains pregnancy and supports uterine lining development?

  • A. Progesterone
  • B. Luteinizing Hormone
  • C. Oxytocin
  • D. Prolactin

Answer: A. Progesterone

Explanation: Progesterone supports pregnancy by preparing and maintaining the endometrial lining for implantation.

23. Atrial wall of the heart secretes a peptide hormone that reduces blood pressure, called:

  • A. Atrial Natriuretic Factor (ANF)
  • B. Erythropoietin
  • C. Renin
  • D. Gastrin

Answer: A. Atrial Natriuretic Factor (ANF)

Explanation: ANF causes vasodilation and increased excretion of Na+ and water by kidneys, thereby lowering blood pressure.

24. Juxtaglomerular (JG) cells of kidneys produce which peptide hormone to stimulate RBC formation (erythropoiesis)?

  • A. Erythropoietin
  • B. Renin
  • C. Secretin
  • D. Angiotensinogen

Answer: A. Erythropoietin

Explanation: In response to hypoxia, renal JG cells secrete erythropoietin, which acts on bone marrow to promote red blood cell production.

25. Which gastrointestinal hormone stimulates the secretion of water and bicarbonate ions from the exocrine pancreas?

  • A. Secretin
  • B. Gastrin
  • C. Cholecystokinin (CCK)
  • D. Gastric Inhibitory Peptide (GIP)

Answer: A. Secretin

Explanation: Secretin acts on exocrine pancreatic ducts to promote release of water and bicarbonate ions to neutralize stomach acid.

26. Cholecystokinin (CCK) acts on which two organs to stimulate digestive secretions?

  • A. Gall bladder and Pancreas
  • B. Stomach and Liver
  • C. Small intestine and Large intestine
  • D. Kidneys and Heart

Answer: A. Gall bladder and Pancreas

Explanation: CCK stimulates contraction of the gall bladder to release bile juice and pancreatic acini to release digestive enzymes.

27. Peptide and protein hormones (like insulin, glucagon, pituitary hormones) act on target cells by binding to:

  • A. Membrane-bound receptors
  • B. Intracellular receptors
  • C. Nuclear receptors
  • D. Mitochondrial receptors

Answer: A. Membrane-bound receptors

Explanation: Water-soluble peptide hormones cannot cross cell membranes, so they bind to extracellular cell-surface receptors and generate secondary messengers.

28. Which of the following functions as a classic secondary messenger in peptide hormone action?

  • A. Cyclic AMP (cAMP)
  • B. DNA
  • C. RNA
  • D. Steroid nucleus

Answer: A. Cyclic AMP (cAMP)

Explanation: Common second messengers generated inside target cells include cyclic AMP (cAMP), IP3, and Ca2+ ions.

29. Steroid hormones (like estrogen, progesterone, cortisol, testosterone) exert action primarily by binding to:

  • A. Intracellular/Nuclear receptors
  • B. Cell surface G-protein receptors
  • C. Transmembrane ion channels
  • D. Extracellular matrix proteins

Answer: A. Intracellular/Nuclear receptors

Explanation: Lipid-soluble steroid hormones cross cell membranes easily and bind to intracellular/nuclear receptors to directly regulate gene expression.

30. Hyperthyroidism characterized by thyroid enlargement, loss of weight, and protrusion of eyeballs is called:

  • A. Exophthalmic goitre (Grave’s disease)
  • B. Simple goitre
  • C. Addison’s disease
  • D. Hashimoto’s thyroiditis

Answer: A. Exophthalmic goitre (Grave’s disease)

Explanation: Grave's disease is an autoimmune hyperthyroid condition featuring enlarged thyroid, elevated basal metabolic rate, weight loss, and protruded eyeballs (exophthalmos).

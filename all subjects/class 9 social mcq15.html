Chapter 15: Working of Institutions 30 Questions

1. Which three organs of government are examined in this chapter as working together to implement policies?

  • A. Legislature, Executive and Judiciary
  • B. Army, Police and Civil Service
  • C. Political parties, media and NGOs
  • D. Panchayat, Municipality and State Assembly

Answer: A. Legislature, Executive and Judiciary

Explanation: The chapter examines how the legislature (Parliament), the executive (government) and the judiciary work together to implement laws and policies.

2. What are the two houses that together make up the Indian Parliament?

  • A. Vidhan Sabha and Vidhan Parishad
  • B. Lok Sabha and Rajya Sabha
  • C. Cabinet and Council of Ministers
  • D. Supreme Court and High Court

Answer: B. Lok Sabha and Rajya Sabha

Explanation: The Indian Parliament is bicameral, consisting of the Lok Sabha (House of the People) and the Rajya Sabha (Council of States).

3. Which House of Parliament is directly elected by the people?

  • A. Rajya Sabha
  • B. Lok Sabha
  • C. Both equally
  • D. Neither is elected

Answer: B. Lok Sabha

Explanation: Members of the Lok Sabha are directly elected by the people, while members of the Rajya Sabha are elected indirectly by the state legislatures.

4. Who is the head of government in India, responsible for taking most major decisions?

  • A. The President
  • B. The Prime Minister
  • C. The Chief Justice
  • D. The Speaker

Answer: B. The Prime Minister

Explanation: The Prime Minister is the head of government and, along with the Council of Ministers, is responsible for taking major policy decisions.

5. What is the group of ministers headed by the Prime Minister called?

  • A. Cabinet
  • B. Politburo
  • C. Secretariat
  • D. Bench

Answer: A. Cabinet

Explanation: The Cabinet is the smaller and most powerful group of senior ministers within the Council of Ministers, headed by the Prime Minister.

6. Who is the constitutional/nominal head of state in India?

  • A. The Prime Minister
  • B. The President
  • C. The Speaker of Lok Sabha
  • D. The Chief Election Commissioner

Answer: B. The President

Explanation: The President of India is the head of state, functioning largely in accordance with the advice of the Council of Ministers headed by the Prime Minister.

7. How is the President of India elected?

  • A. Directly by all citizens
  • B. Indirectly by elected members of Parliament and state legislatures
  • C. Appointed by the Prime Minister
  • D. Selected by the Supreme Court

Answer: B. Indirectly by elected members of Parliament and state legislatures

Explanation: The President is elected indirectly through an electoral college consisting of elected members of Parliament and state legislative assemblies.

8. What is the primary function of Parliament in India?

  • A. To make laws and control the executive
  • B. To only conduct elections
  • C. To interpret laws
  • D. To act as the head of the army

Answer: A. To make laws and control the executive

Explanation: Parliament's key roles include making laws, controlling the finances and budget, and holding the executive government accountable.

9. Why is the Rajya Sabha called the 'Council of States'?

  • A. Because it represents the interests of the various states of India
  • B. Because it is directly elected by the people
  • C. Because only the Prime Minister sits in it
  • D. Because it has no legislative power

Answer: A. Because it represents the interests of the various states of India

Explanation: The Rajya Sabha represents the states of the Indian Union, with members elected by the elected members of the state legislative assemblies.

10. What is the role of the highest court in the country, the Supreme Court?

  • A. To make laws instead of Parliament
  • B. To interpret the Constitution and settle legal disputes, including those between citizens and the government
  • C. To conduct elections
  • D. To appoint the Prime Minister

Answer: B. To interpret the Constitution and settle legal disputes, including those between citizens and the government

Explanation: The Supreme Court is the apex judicial body responsible for interpreting the Constitution and resolving disputes, including those involving Fundamental Rights.

11. What is the power of courts to check whether a law made by the legislature follows the basic structure of the Constitution called?

  • A. Judicial review
  • B. Presidential veto
  • C. Legislative supremacy
  • D. Electoral audit

Answer: A. Judicial review

Explanation: Judicial review is the power of the judiciary to examine and strike down laws that violate the Constitution, including its basic structure.

12. Who presides over the sessions of the Lok Sabha and conducts its proceedings?

  • A. The Speaker
  • B. The President
  • C. The Chief Justice
  • D. The Prime Minister

Answer: A. The Speaker

Explanation: The Speaker is elected from among the members of the Lok Sabha and presides over its sessions, maintaining order and conducting proceedings.

13. In parliamentary democracy, why must the Prime Minister and Council of Ministers retain the support of the majority in the Lok Sabha?

  • A. Because they are accountable to Parliament and can be removed by a no-confidence vote
  • B. Because Parliament has no control over the government
  • C. Because the President can dismiss the government at will
  • D. Because the judiciary appoints ministers

Answer: A. Because they are accountable to Parliament and can be removed by a no-confidence vote

Explanation: In a parliamentary system, the government remains in office only as long as it enjoys the confidence of the majority in the Lok Sabha, which can remove it through a no-confidence motion.

14. What is meant by 'coalition government'?

  • A. A government formed by a single party with a clear majority
  • B. A government formed by an alliance of two or more political parties
  • C. A government without any ministers
  • D. A government run entirely by the judiciary

Answer: B. A government formed by an alliance of two or more political parties

Explanation: When no single party wins a majority, several parties may come together to form a coalition government to secure majority support.

15. Which institution is generally regarded as the most powerful in making policy decisions in the Indian system?

  • A. The President alone
  • B. The Prime Minister and the Council of Ministers
  • C. The Election Commission
  • D. The Speaker alone

Answer: B. The Prime Minister and the Council of Ministers

Explanation: While the President is the head of state, most policy decisions in India are actually taken by the Prime Minister and the Council of Ministers, who lead the executive.

16. What role does the President play regarding bills passed by Parliament?

  • A. The President has no role at all
  • B. The President's assent is required before a bill passed by Parliament becomes law
  • C. The President can create laws independently of Parliament
  • D. The President can dissolve Parliament without any reason

Answer: B. The President's assent is required before a bill passed by Parliament becomes law

Explanation: A bill passed by both Houses of Parliament must receive the President's assent to become an Act, though the President generally acts on the advice of the Council of Ministers.

17. Why does the chapter emphasise the need for institutions rather than relying only on individual leaders?

  • A. Institutions ensure decisions follow rules and continue even if individuals change
  • B. Individuals are always better decision makers than institutions
  • C. Institutions are unnecessary in a democracy
  • D. Only one institution should exist in a country

Answer: A. Institutions ensure decisions follow rules and continue even if individuals change

Explanation: Institutions provide stability, follow set procedures, and allow governance to continue smoothly regardless of which individuals hold office.

18. Which of the following best describes the function of the Council of Ministers?

  • A. To assist the Prime Minister in running various government departments and taking policy decisions
  • B. To replace Parliament entirely
  • C. To act as the judiciary
  • D. To conduct elections

Answer: A. To assist the Prime Minister in running various government departments and taking policy decisions

Explanation: The Council of Ministers, headed by the Prime Minister, is responsible for overseeing different government departments and taking key executive decisions.

19. What is a 'no-confidence motion'?

  • A. A motion to praise the government
  • B. A motion by which members of the legislature express that they no longer support the government
  • C. A motion to appoint judges
  • D. A motion related only to state budgets

Answer: B. A motion by which members of the legislature express that they no longer support the government

Explanation: If a no-confidence motion is passed against the government in the Lok Sabha, the government is required to resign.

20. What does the term 'judicial independence' mean?

  • A. The judiciary is controlled by the executive
  • B. The judiciary can function without interference from the legislature or executive
  • C. Judges are appointed by political parties
  • D. Courts have no power to review laws

Answer: B. The judiciary can function without interference from the legislature or executive

Explanation: Judicial independence ensures courts can make impartial decisions, including against the government, without undue influence from other branches.

21. Which of these is an example of the judiciary protecting the Fundamental Rights of citizens?

  • A. Passing the annual budget
  • B. Hearing cases and giving judgments when rights are violated
  • C. Conducting general elections
  • D. Appointing the Prime Minister

Answer: B. Hearing cases and giving judgments when rights are violated

Explanation: Citizens can approach the courts, including the Supreme Court, if their Fundamental Rights are violated, and the judiciary can order remedies.

22. What is the significance of the phrase 'separation of powers' in governance?

  • A. All power is concentrated in one office
  • B. Power is divided among the legislature, executive and judiciary to prevent misuse
  • C. Only the army holds power
  • D. There is no division of responsibilities

Answer: B. Power is divided among the legislature, executive and judiciary to prevent misuse

Explanation: Separation of powers distributes authority among different institutions so that no single branch of government can misuse power unchecked.

23. What happens to the Council of Ministers if the Lok Sabha is dissolved?

  • A. It continues indefinitely
  • B. A caretaker government generally functions until a new government is formed after fresh elections
  • C. The judiciary takes over governance
  • D. The President rules directly and permanently

Answer: B. A caretaker government generally functions until a new government is formed after fresh elections

Explanation: When the Lok Sabha is dissolved, a caretaker arrangement usually continues essential government functions until elections are held and a new government takes office.

24. Why is Parliament considered central to India's democratic system?

  • A. Because it has no real power
  • B. Because it represents the will of the people and controls the executive
  • C. Because it is appointed by the judiciary
  • D. Because it only meets once every ten years

Answer: B. Because it represents the will of the people and controls the executive

Explanation: As the elected body representing the citizens, Parliament plays the central role of law-making and holding the government accountable.

25. What is the primary reason institutions like Parliament, the executive and the judiciary need to work together?

  • A. To duplicate each other's work
  • B. To ensure checks and balances so that power is exercised fairly and lawfully
  • C. To eliminate the need for a constitution
  • D. To allow one institution to dominate all decisions

Answer: B. To ensure checks and balances so that power is exercised fairly and lawfully

Explanation: The interaction and mutual checks among institutions help ensure that governmental power is exercised responsibly and within constitutional limits.

26. Which of these best describes the position of the President in India's parliamentary system?

  • A. The President has absolute personal power over all decisions
  • B. The President is largely a constitutional head who acts on the advice of the Council of Ministers
  • C. The President is elected directly by the people every year
  • D. The President has no formal role in government

Answer: B. The President is largely a constitutional head who acts on the advice of the Council of Ministers

Explanation: While formally powerful, the President in practice acts according to the advice of the Prime Minister and the Council of Ministers in most matters.

27. What kind of questions can citizens or organisations bring before the courts through Public Interest Litigation (PIL)?

  • A. Only personal property disputes
  • B. Issues of public concern such as rights violations, even by someone acting on behalf of others
  • C. Only cases related to elections
  • D. Only cases involving government ministers

Answer: B. Issues of public concern such as rights violations, even by someone acting on behalf of others

Explanation: PIL allows any citizen or organisation to approach the courts on behalf of those whose rights may have been violated, widening access to justice.

28. What is one advantage of having institutions rather than depending on individuals to take every decision?

  • A. Institutions slow down all governance permanently
  • B. Institutions ensure consistency, accountability and rule-based functioning
  • C. Institutions remove the need for any laws
  • D. Institutions cannot be held accountable

Answer: B. Institutions ensure consistency, accountability and rule-based functioning

Explanation: Institutions provide continuity and predictability in governance, ensuring decisions are made through established, accountable procedures.

29. Who among the following is generally responsible for appointing the Prime Minister of India?

  • A. The Chief Justice
  • B. The President, based on the support commanded in the Lok Sabha
  • C. The Election Commission
  • D. The Speaker of the Lok Sabha

Answer: B. The President, based on the support commanded in the Lok Sabha

Explanation: The President formally appoints the Prime Minister, usually the leader of the party or coalition that commands a majority in the Lok Sabha.

30. Overall, what does the chapter conclude about why institutions matter in a democracy?

  • A. Institutions are unnecessary distractions
  • B. Institutions help implement the wishes of the people in a fair, accountable, and rule-based manner
  • C. Institutions exist only to serve one leader
  • D. Institutions have no relation to democracy

Answer: B. Institutions help implement the wishes of the people in a fair, accountable, and rule-based manner

Explanation: The chapter concludes that well-functioning institutions such as the legislature, executive and judiciary are essential for translating democratic will into effective, accountable governance.

NCERT Solutions for Class 11 English Ch-4: The Laburnum Top (Poem)

'The Laburnum Top' is a nature poem by Ted Hughes that vividly portrays a seemingly still laburnum tree that suddenly comes alive with the arrival of a goldfinch and her fledglings.

Overview of Topics & Subtopics

  • Imagery of Stillness and Silence in the Poem's Opening
  • The Arrival of the Goldfinch and the Tree Coming Alive
  • Sound Imagery: The 'Machine' Simile for Bird Calls
  • Themes of the Interconnection Between Nature and Life

Important Questions and Answers

Q1: How does the poem describe the laburnum tree at the beginning?
Answer: At the beginning, the poem describes the laburnum tree as still, silent, and seemingly lifeless, with yellowing September leaves and seed-pods, appearing dormant in the quiet autumn afternoon.
Q2: What event brings the tree 'alive' in the poem?
Answer: The tree comes alive when a goldfinch arrives with a 'twitch' and settles into it, immediately filling the tree with the lively, chattering sounds and movement of her fledgling chicks hidden within its foliage.
Q3: What literary device does Ted Hughes use to describe the sound of the birds, and what effect does it create?
Answer: Hughes uses a simile comparing the bird's chattering sound to a 'machine', specifically describing it as being 'like a machine starting up', which creates a striking, almost mechanical yet vivid auditory image of the sudden burst of activity and sound within the tree.
Q4: What happens at the end of the poem after the goldfinch departs?
Answer: After the goldfinch flies away, the tree returns to its former stillness and silence, as if nothing had happened, showing how briefly yet profoundly nature's small, fleeting moments of life and activity can transform an otherwise quiet scene.
Q5: What is the central theme of 'The Laburnum Top'?
Answer: The central theme of the poem is the quiet, often unnoticed vibrancy and interconnectedness of nature, showing how a seemingly still and lifeless tree is, in fact, a bustling home for other life forms, illustrating nature's cyclical rhythm of stillness and activity.

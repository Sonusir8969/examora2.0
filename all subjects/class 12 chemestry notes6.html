NCERT Solutions for Class 12 Chemistry Chapter 6 Haloalkanes and Haloarenes

NCERT Solutions for Class 12 Chemistry Chapter 6 Haloalkanes and Haloarenes covers detailed concepts of halogenated organic compounds, classification, IUPAC nomenclature, methods of preparation, physical properties, nucleophilic substitution mechanisms (SN1 and SN2), and polyhalogen compounds.

Overview of Topics & Subtopics (Click to Jump)

  • 1. Classification, Nomenclature & C-X Bond Nature
  • 2. Methods of Preparation & Physical Properties
  • 3. Chemical Reactions (SN1 vs SN2) & Polyhalogen Compounds

1. Classification, Nomenclature & C-X Bond Nature

Short Note:

• Classification: Based on the number of halogen atoms (mono, di, polyhalo) and hybridization of carbon attached to halogen:

  - sp3 C-X compounds: Alkyl halides (haloalkanes), Allylic halides (CH2=CH-CH2X), Benzylic halides (C6H5CH2X).

  - sp2 C-X compounds: Vinylic halides (CH2=CHX), Aryl halides (haloarenes).

• Nature of C-X Bond: Highly polar due to the greater electronegativity of halogen compared to carbon (Cδ+-Xδ-). Bond enthalpy decreases down the group: C-F > C-Cl > C-Br > C-I.

2. Methods of Preparation & Physical Properties

Short Note:

• Methods of Preparation:

  - From Alcohols: Reaction with HX, PX3, PX5, or SOCl2. Reaction with SOCl2 (Darzen's process) is preferred as by-products (SO2 and HCl) escape as gases.

  - Halogen Exchange: Finkelstein reaction (R-Cl/Br + NaI in dry acetone → R-I) and Swarts reaction (R-Cl/Br + AgF → R-F).

  - From Diazonium Salts: Sandmeyer's reaction using Cu2Cl2 or Cu2Br2 to yield chlorobenzene/bromobenzene.

• Physical Properties: Boiling points follow the order: R-I > R-Br > R-Cl > R-F (due to increasing van der Waals forces with molecular size). Parasubstituted isomers have higher melting points due to symmetrical crystal lattice packing.

3. Chemical Reactions (SN1 vs SN2) & Polyhalogen Compounds

Short Note:

• Nucleophilic Substitution Mechanisms:

  - SN2 Mechanism: Bimolecular, single-step reaction with transition state leading to inversion of configuration (Walden inversion). Reactivity order: 1° > 2° > 3° haloalkanes.

  - SN1 Mechanism: Unimolecular, two-step reaction proceeding via carbocation intermediate leading to racemisation. Reactivity order: 3° > 2° > 1° haloalkanes.

• Elimination & Metal Reactions: β-elimination yields alkenes (Saytzeff's rule: major product is highly alkylated alkene). Reaction with Mg forms Grignard reagents (RMgX).

• Polyhalogen Compounds: Includes Chloroform (CHCl3), Iodoform (CHI3), Carbon tetrachloride (CCl4), Freons (CFCs causing ozone depletion), and DDT (insecticide).

Intext & Conceptual Questions

Q1: Write the IUPAC names of the following organic halo-compounds:
(i) CH3CH(Cl)CH(Br)CH3
(ii) CHF2CBrClF
(iii) (CH3)3CCH2Br
Answer:
1. CH3CH(Cl)CH(Br)CH3: 2-Bromo-3-chlorobutane
2. CHF2CBrClF: 1-Bromo-1-chloro-1,2,2-trifluoroethane
3. (CH3)3CCH2Br: 1-Bromo-2,2-dimethylpropane
Q2: Compare SN1 and SN2 mechanisms of nucleophilic substitution reactions in haloalkanes.
Answer:
Feature SN1 Mechanism SN2 Mechanism
Kinetics / Order Unimolecular (First order reaction). Bimolecular (Second order reaction).
Steps Involved Two steps (Carbocation intermediate formed). Single concerted step (Transition state formed).
Stereochemistry Racemisation (50% retention + 50% inversion). Complete inversion of configuration.
Reactivity Order 3° > 2° > 1° alkyl halides. 1° > 2° > 3° alkyl halides.

Textbook Chapter End Exercise Questions

Q1: Why is thionyl chloride (SOCl2) preferred over other reagents for preparing alkyl chlorides from alcohols?
Answer:
Thionyl chloride (SOCl2) is preferred for preparing alkyl chlorides from alcohols because the by-products formed during the reaction, namely sulfur dioxide (SO2) and hydrogen chloride (HCl), are both gaseous in nature.

Chemical Equation:
R-OH + SOCl2 → R-Cl + SO2↑ + HCl↑

Since both by-products escape into the atmosphere easily, the reaction yields pure alkyl chloride (R-Cl) in the reaction mixture without requiring complex purification processes.
Q2: Arrange the following compounds in increasing order of reactivity towards SN2 displacement:
1-bromobutane, 2-bromobutane, 2-bromo-2-methylpropane.
Answer:
In SN2 reactions, steric hindrance around the carbon bearing the halogen atom plays the key role. Less steric hindrance leads to a faster SN2 attack by the incoming nucleophile.

• 1-bromobutane: 1° alkyl halide (least steric hindrance, most reactive).
• 2-bromobutane: 2° alkyl halide (intermediate steric hindrance).
• 2-bromo-2-methylpropane: 3° alkyl halide (maximum steric hindrance, least reactive).

Increasing Order of Reactivity:
2-bromo-2-methylpropane < 2-bromobutane < 1-bromobutane

Ch-4: Chemical Bonding and Molecular Structure 30 Questions

1. Which of the following molecules has a linear geometry?

  • A. BeCl₂
  • B. H₂O
  • C. NH₃
  • D. CH₄

Answer: A. BeCl₂

Explanation: BeCl₂ has sp hybridization with 2 bond pairs and 0 lone pairs, giving it a linear shape with a 180° bond angle.

2. What is the hybridization of the central carbon atom in Methane (CH₄)?

  • A. sp³
  • B. sp²
  • C. sp
  • D. dsp²

Answer: A. sp³

Explanation: Carbon forms 4 single sigma bonds with hydrogen atoms, resulting in sp³ hybridization and a tetrahedral geometry.

3. According to VSEPR theory, the geometry of Ammonia (NH₃) molecule is:

  • A. Trigonal pyramidal
  • B. Tetrahedral
  • C. Trigonal planar
  • D. Linear

Answer: A. Trigonal pyramidal

Explanation: NH₃ has 3 bond pairs and 1 lone pair on nitrogen, which distorts the regular tetrahedral arrangement into a trigonal pyramidal shape.

4. The bond order of Oxygen molecule (O₂) is:

  • A. 2
  • B. 1
  • C. 3
  • D. 1.5

Answer: A. 2

Explanation: Bond order = (Nb - Na)/2 = (10 - 6)/2 = 2, indicating a double bond between oxygen atoms.

5. Which of the following species is paramagnetic in nature?

  • A. O₂
  • B. N₂
  • C. C₂
  • D. F₂

Answer: A. O₂

Explanation: According to Molecular Orbital Theory, O₂ contains two unpaired electrons in its antibonding π*2p orbitals.

6. What is the shape of Water (H₂O) molecule?

  • A. Bent / V-shaped
  • B. Linear
  • C. Trigonal planar
  • D. Tetrahedral

Answer: A. Bent / V-shaped

Explanation: Oxygen has 2 bond pairs and 2 lone pairs, causing lone pair-lone pair repulsions that bend the molecule into a V-shape with a 104.5° angle.

7. The type of hybridization involved in Phosphorus Pentachloride (PCl₅) is:

  • A. sp³d
  • B. sp³d²
  • C. sp³
  • D. sp²

Answer: A. sp³d

Explanation: PCl₅ has 5 bonding pairs around phosphorus, resulting in sp³d hybridization and trigonal bipyramidal geometry.

8. Which molecule exhibits Hydrogen bonding?

  • A. HF
  • B. HCl
  • C. HBr
  • D. HI

Answer: A. HF

Explanation: Fluorine is highly electronegative, forming strong intermolecular hydrogen bonds in HF.

9. The number of sigma (σ) and pi (π) bonds in Ethyne (C₂H₂) are:

  • A. 3 σ and 2 π
  • B. 2 σ and 3 π
  • C. 4 σ and 1 π
  • D. 5 σ and 0 π

Answer: A. 3 σ and 2 π

Explanation: Ethyne (H-C≡C-H) has 2 C-H σ bonds, 1 C-C σ bond (total 3 σ bonds), and 2 C-C π bonds.

10. Which of the following has a zero dipole moment?

  • A. BF₃
  • B. NH₃
  • C. H₂O
  • D. SO₂

Answer: A. BF₃

Explanation: BF₃ has a symmetrical trigonal planar geometry where the individual bond dipole moments cancel each other out.

11. Hybridization of Sulfur in SF₆ is:

  • A. sp³d²
  • B. sp³d
  • C. sp³
  • D. d²sp³

Answer: A. sp³d²

Explanation: Sulfur forms 6 sigma bonds with fluorine atoms, leading to sp³d² hybridization with octahedral geometry.

12. The Octet Rule is not valid for which of the following molecules?

  • A. NO
  • B. CH₄
  • C. H₂O
  • D. NaCl

Answer: A. NO

Explanation: Nitric oxide (NO) is an odd-electron molecule and fails to satisfy the octet rule for nitrogen.

13. Which of the following bond types is the strongest?

  • A. Covalent bond
  • B. Hydrogen bond
  • C. Dipole-dipole interaction
  • D. London dispersion force

Answer: A. Covalent bond

Explanation: Covalent bonds are primary chemical bonds resulting from electron sharing, making them much stronger than secondary intermolecular forces.

14. What is the bond order of Nitrogen molecule (N₂)?

  • A. 3
  • B. 2
  • C. 1
  • D. 0

Answer: A. 3

Explanation: N₂ has 14 electrons with Bond Order = (10 - 4)/2 = 3, corresponding to a triple bond (N≡N).

15. According to Fajan's rules, covalent character in an ionic bond is favored by:

  • A. Small cation and large anion
  • B. Large cation and small anion
  • C. Large cation and large anion
  • D. Small cation and small anion

Answer: A. Small cation and large anion

Explanation: High polarising power of a small cation combined with high polarisability of a large anion enhances covalent character.

16. The bond angle in a tetrahedral molecule like CH₄ is:

  • A. 109.5°
  • B. 120°
  • C. 180°
  • D. 90°

Answer: A. 109.5°

Explanation: Regular sp³ hybridized tetrahedral geometry exhibits bond angles of 109°28' (109.5°).

17. Which molecular orbital has the lowest energy in homonuclear diatomic molecules like N₂?

  • A. σ1s
  • B. σ*1s
  • C. σ2s
  • D. π2px

Answer: A. σ1s

Explanation: The bonding molecular orbital σ1s formed by 1s-1s overlap has the minimum energy level.

18. The formal charge on central oxygen in Ozone (O₃) is:

  • A. +1
  • B. 0
  • C. -1
  • D. +2

Answer: A. +1

Explanation: Central oxygen has 6 valence electrons, 2 non-bonding electrons, and 3 bonds: FC = 6 - 2 - (1/2)*6 = +1.

19. Which molecule has a seesaw shape?

  • A. SF₄
  • B. XeF₄
  • C. CCl₄
  • D. NH₄⁺

Answer: A. SF₄

Explanation: SF₄ has 4 bond pairs and 1 lone pair with sp³d hybridization, resulting in a distorted seesaw geometry.

20. Intramolecular hydrogen bonding is present in:

  • A. o-Nitrophenol
  • B. p-Nitrophenol
  • C. Water
  • D. Ethanol

Answer: A. o-Nitrophenol

Explanation: In o-Nitrophenol, hydrogen bonding occurs within the same molecule between the -OH and -NO₂ groups.

21. In Benzene (C₆H₆), the hybridization of each carbon atom is:

  • A. sp²
  • B. sp³
  • C. sp
  • D. dsp²

Answer: A. sp²

Explanation: Each carbon atom in benzene forms 3 sigma bonds, giving sp² hybridization and planar hexagonal symmetry.

22. Bond length decreases with:

  • A. Increase in bond order
  • B. Decrease in bond order
  • C. Increase in atomic size
  • D. Decrease in electronegativity difference

Answer: A. Increase in bond order

Explanation: Higher bond order means more shared electron pairs attracting the nuclei closer together, shortening bond length.

23. Which of the following species does not exist according to Molecular Orbital Theory?

  • A. He₂
  • B. H₂
  • C. Li₂
  • D. O₂⁺

Answer: A. He₂

Explanation: For He₂, Nb = 2 and Na = 2, so Bond Order = (2 - 2)/2 = 0, meaning the molecule is unstable and cannot exist.

24. A pi (π) bond is formed by:

  • A. Lateral / sideways overlap of atomic orbitals
  • B. Axial / end-to-end overlap of atomic orbitals
  • C. Overlap of s-s orbitals only
  • D. Head-on collision of atomic nuclei

Answer: A. Lateral / sideways overlap of atomic orbitals

Explanation: Pi bonds result from sideways parallel overlap of unhybridized p-orbitals perpendicular to the internuclear axis.

25. What is the shape of Carbon Dioxide (CO₂) molecule?

  • A. Linear
  • B. Bent
  • C. Trigonal planar
  • D. V-shaped

Answer: A. Linear

Explanation: Carbon in CO₂ undergoes sp hybridization and forms two double bonds with zero lone pairs, making it linear.

26. Which of the following molecules has a T-shaped geometry?

  • A. ClF₃
  • B. BCl₃
  • C. NH₃
  • D. PCl₃

Answer: A. ClF₃

Explanation: ClF₃ has 3 bond pairs and 2 lone pairs on chlorine with sp³d hybridization, producing a T-shape.

27. The lattice enthalpy of an ionic crystal depends on:

  • A. Both charge and size of ions
  • B. Size of ions only
  • C. Charge on ions only
  • D. Temperature only

Answer: A. Both charge and size of ions

Explanation: Lattice enthalpy is directly proportional to ionic charges and inversely proportional to internuclear distance (size of ions).

28. Which of the following species is diamagnetic?

  • A. N₂
  • B. O₂
  • C. NO
  • D. B₂

Answer: A. N₂

Explanation: All 14 electrons in N₂ are completely paired up in its molecular orbitals.

29. What is the geometry of Xenon Tetrafluoride (XeF₄)?

  • A. Square planar
  • B. Tetrahedral
  • C. Octahedral
  • D. Trigonal bipyramidal

Answer: A. Square planar

Explanation: XeF₄ has sp³d² hybridization with 4 bond pairs and 2 lone pairs positioned axially, yielding a square planar shape.

30. Resonance structures of a molecule have:

  • A. Same position of nuclei, different distribution of electrons
  • B. Different position of nuclei, same distribution of electrons
  • C. Different total energy
  • D. Different number of paired electrons

Answer: A. Same position of nuclei, different distribution of electrons

Explanation: Canonical forms keep the atomic framework fixed and only differ in π-electron distribution.

Chapter 1: The Fun They Had 30 Questions

1. Who is the author of "The Fun They Had"?

  • A. Isaac Asimov
  • B. Anton Chekhov
  • C. Douglas James
  • D. Vikram Seth

Answer: A. Isaac Asimov

Explanation: "The Fun They Had" is a science fiction story written by Isaac Asimov, imagining a future of computerised schooling.

2. What is the name of the young girl who is the main character in the story?

  • A. Kezia
  • B. Margie
  • C. Sara
  • D. Ashiamma

Answer: B. Margie

Explanation: Margie is the eleven-year-old protagonist of the story, who writes about her old-fashioned book discovery in her diary.

3. What does Margie find in the story that surprises her and Tommy?

  • A. A robot
  • B. An old printed book
  • C. A television set
  • D. A telescope

Answer: B. An old printed book

Explanation: Margie and Tommy find an old, yellowed printed book, something unfamiliar to them since they usually read from screens.

4. In what year is the story set, according to Margie's diary entry?

  • A. 2055
  • B. 2157
  • C. 2200
  • D. 1990

Answer: B. 2157

Explanation: Margie's diary entry is dated May 17, 2157, setting the story firmly in a futuristic world.

5. Who teaches Margie and Tommy in the story?

  • A. A human teacher who visits their home
  • B. A mechanical teacher installed at home
  • C. Their grandparents
  • D. No one; they are self-taught

Answer: B. A mechanical teacher installed at home

Explanation: In Margie's world, children are taught individually at home by mechanical teachers rather than attending schools with human teachers.

6. Why is Margie's mechanical teacher sent away for adjustment?

  • A. It stopped working completely
  • B. It had been slowing down and gradually adjusting the geography sector too quickly for Margie
  • C. It caught fire
  • D. Margie broke it deliberately

Answer: B. It had been slowing down and gradually adjusting the geography sector too quickly for Margie

Explanation: Margie was doing worse and worse on her geography tests, and the County Inspector found her mechanical teacher's geography sector had been adjusted too fast for her level.

7. Who comes to fix Margie's mechanical teacher?

  • A. Tommy's father
  • B. The County Inspector
  • C. A librarian
  • D. Margie's grandfather

Answer: B. The County Inspector

Explanation: The County Inspector visits Margie's home to check and adjust her mechanical teacher after it develops a problem.

8. What subject does Margie find most difficult in the story?

  • A. Mathematics
  • B. Geography
  • C. History
  • D. Music

Answer: B. Geography

Explanation: Margie struggles particularly with geography, scoring poorly in her tests, which leads to the discovery of the fault in her mechanical teacher.

9. What does the old book describe that fascinates Margie and Tommy?

  • A. Robots taking over the world
  • B. Schools where children of the same age learned together in a schoolhouse with a human teacher
  • C. Space travel to Mars
  • D. A world without any technology

Answer: B. Schools where children of the same age learned together in a schoolhouse with a human teacher

Explanation: The old book describes schools of the past, where children gathered together and were taught by human teachers, a concept alien to Margie and Tommy.

10. How does Margie feel about school as described in the old book?

  • A. She thinks it sounds boring compared to her own education
  • B. She feels children in the old schools must have had fun learning together
  • C. She is scared of the idea
  • D. She has no reaction at all

Answer: B. She feels children in the old schools must have had fun learning together

Explanation: Margie is intrigued and thinks the children in the old school must have really had fun, since they learned together and even looked forward to going to school.

11. How old is Tommy compared to Margie?

  • A. Tommy is younger than Margie
  • B. Tommy is the same age as Margie
  • C. Tommy is older, at thirteen
  • D. Tommy's age is not mentioned

Answer: C. Tommy is older, at thirteen

Explanation: Tommy is thirteen years old, two years older than eleven-year-old Margie, and he is the one who finds the old book first.

12. What does Margie's mother do for a living, based on how her education is structured?

  • A. This is not specified in the story
  • B. She is Margie's mechanical teacher
  • C. She is a school inspector
  • D. She writes books

Answer: A. This is not specified in the story

Explanation: The story focuses on Margie's education and family life without detailing her mother's occupation.

13. Where is Margie's classroom located in her house?

  • A. In her bedroom
  • B. In a room next to her bedroom, set up especially for her lessons
  • C. In the garden
  • D. In the basement

Answer: B. In a room next to her bedroom, set up especially for her lessons

Explanation: Margie has a dedicated schoolroom at home where her mechanical teacher gives her lessons and tests.

14. How does Margie's education schedule compare to a typical modern school schedule?

  • A. Margie's schooling has fixed times set by her mother, similar to structured hours
  • B. Margie has no schedule at all and studies whenever she wants
  • C. Margie only studies once a week
  • D. Margie's schedule is decided by Tommy

Answer: A. Margie's schooling has fixed times set by her mother, similar to structured hours

Explanation: Margie's lessons follow a set schedule, with her mother ensuring she studies at fixed hours similar to how school hours are structured today.

15. What happens to old schools in the futuristic world described in the story?

  • A. They continue exactly as before
  • B. They no longer exist because education has become individualised through mechanical teachers
  • C. They have become amusement parks
  • D. They have moved underground

Answer: B. They no longer exist because education has become individualised through mechanical teachers

Explanation: In Margie's world, traditional schools with human teachers and classmates have been replaced by home-based mechanical instruction for each child.

16. What does the story suggest was one advantage of the old school system, as imagined by Margie?

  • A. Children could learn and play together, making learning more social and enjoyable
  • B. Children never had to take tests
  • C. Children could choose not to attend at all
  • D. There was no need for teachers

Answer: A. Children could learn and play together, making learning more social and enjoyable

Explanation: The story suggests that the human interaction and companionship of the old schools gave children a sense of fun that Margie's isolated, machine-based education lacks.

17. What is unusual about the pages of the old book Tommy and Margie find?

  • A. They are yellow and crinkly, unlike anything Margie has seen before
  • B. They glow in the dark
  • C. They are made of metal
  • D. They are blank

Answer: A. They are yellow and crinkly, unlike anything Margie has seen before

Explanation: The old book's yellowed, crinkly pages are unfamiliar to Margie since her generation reads from screens instead of paper.

18. What does Margie compare her mechanical teacher's test results to, in terms of speed?

  • A. The mechanical teacher gives results almost immediately after a test
  • B. Results take several weeks to arrive
  • C. Results are never given
  • D. Results depend on the weather

Answer: A. The mechanical teacher gives results almost immediately after a test

Explanation: The mechanical teacher in the story provides instant scoring after Margie completes a test, reflecting the efficiency of automated education.

19. What is one criticism implied in the story about individualised, mechanical education?

  • A. It can feel isolating and less enjoyable compared to learning with other children
  • B. It is always more efficient with no downsides
  • C. It eliminates the need for any learning at all
  • D. It has no relation to human emotion

Answer: A. It can feel isolating and less enjoyable compared to learning with other children

Explanation: Through Margie's longing for the old school system, the story critiques the isolation that can come from purely mechanical, individual-based education.

20. What genre does "The Fun They Had" belong to?

  • A. Historical fiction
  • B. Science fiction
  • C. Fantasy adventure
  • D. Detective fiction

Answer: B. Science fiction

Explanation: The story imagines a futuristic world with mechanical teachers and home-based schooling, making it a work of science fiction.

21. What does Tommy say happened to the old kind of school, based on his understanding from the book?

  • A. It was replaced because it became too expensive
  • B. It gradually disappeared as newer educational technology was developed
  • C. It never actually existed
  • D. It was banned by the government

Answer: B. It gradually disappeared as newer educational technology was developed

Explanation: Tommy explains that schools like the one in the book existed a long time ago, before mechanical, individualised teaching became the norm.

22. What does Margie decide to do with the old book by the end of the story?

  • A. She throws it away
  • B. She keeps thinking about the old schools and the fun children must have had
  • C. She gives it to the County Inspector
  • D. She burns it

Answer: B. She keeps thinking about the old schools and the fun children must have had

Explanation: By the end, Margie remains reflective, imagining the joy of the old schools and wondering what it would have been like to attend one.

23. Why might the story be seen as a commentary on the role of technology in education?

  • A. It shows how excessive automation might reduce the social and emotional aspects of learning
  • B. It shows technology has no role in education at all
  • C. It argues that machines are always better than humans in every way
  • D. It has no relevance to education

Answer: A. It shows how excessive automation might reduce the social and emotional aspects of learning

Explanation: Through Margie's nostalgia for a human-centred, social school experience, the story subtly critiques over-reliance on machines in education.

24. What does the term 'telebooks' likely refer to in the story's futuristic setting?

  • A. Digital books read on a screen instead of printed paper
  • B. Books delivered by post
  • C. Books written by robots
  • D. A type of television show

Answer: A. Digital books read on a screen instead of printed paper

Explanation: In Margie's world, telebooks are the digital, screen-based reading material that has replaced traditional printed books like the one she and Tommy find.

25. What is the significance of the title "The Fun They Had"?

  • A. It refers to the fun Margie and Tommy have while playing
  • B. It refers to the imagined fun that children of the old schools had, which Margie's generation misses out on
  • C. It refers to a game invented by Margie
  • D. It has no connection to the story's theme

Answer: B. It refers to the imagined fun that children of the old schools had, which Margie's generation misses out on

Explanation: The title reflects Margie's realisation that the children from the old school system enjoyed a kind of shared, social fun that her isolated, machine-taught generation lacks.

26. Which of these best describes Margie's overall attitude towards her mechanical teacher?

  • A. She loves it and prefers it to any other kind of teacher
  • B. She dislikes it, especially after it gives her difficulty with geography
  • C. She has never interacted with it
  • D. She wants to become a mechanical teacher herself

Answer: B. She dislikes it, especially after it gives her difficulty with geography

Explanation: Margie's frustration with her mechanical teacher's difficult geography tests shows her discontent with her impersonal, machine-based education.

27. What kind of relationship does Margie have with Tommy in the story?

  • A. They are strangers who never speak
  • B. They are neighbourhood friends who explore the old book together
  • C. They are siblings
  • D. They are enemies

Answer: B. They are neighbourhood friends who explore the old book together

Explanation: Margie and Tommy are friends who live near each other and share the curiosity of examining the old book Tommy discovers.

28. How does the story portray the pace of learning for Margie compared to a classroom setting?

  • A. Margie's mechanical teacher adjusts to her individual pace, though this can sometimes cause issues
  • B. Margie learns at the exact same pace as every other child in the world
  • C. Margie has no set pace of learning
  • D. Margie's pace is decided entirely by Tommy

Answer: A. Margie's mechanical teacher adjusts to her individual pace, though this can sometimes cause issues

Explanation: The individualised nature of Margie's education means the machine adjusts to her level, but a malfunction in this adjustment caused her recent struggles.

29. What broader theme about the future of education does the story explore?

  • A. The increasing role of technology and automation in learning, and what might be lost in the process
  • B. The complete irrelevance of technology to education
  • C. The idea that books will always remain the primary method of teaching
  • D. The importance of never changing traditional schooling methods

Answer: A. The increasing role of technology and automation in learning, and what might be lost in the process

Explanation: The story invites readers to reflect on how increasing automation in education might change, and possibly diminish, some of its social and communal aspects.

30. What overall feeling does the story leave the reader with regarding Margie's world?

  • A. A sense that the future described is entirely perfect with no drawbacks
  • B. A reflective sense that progress in technology may come with a loss of shared human experiences
  • C. A feeling of excitement with no deeper meaning
  • D. A sense that books will disappear entirely in the future

Answer: B. A reflective sense that progress in technology may come with a loss of shared human experiences

Explanation: The story closes on a thoughtful note, subtly suggesting that while technology has advanced Margie's world, it may have cost the children something valuable in shared, social learning.

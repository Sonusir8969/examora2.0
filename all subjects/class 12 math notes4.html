NCERT Solutions for Class 12 Mathematics Chapter 4 Determinants

NCERT Solutions for Class 12 Mathematics Chapter 4 Determinants covers the determinant of a square matrix and its properties, finding the area of a triangle using determinants, the adjoint and inverse of a matrix, and solving systems of linear equations.

1. Determinant and Its Properties

Short Note:

• The determinant of a square matrix is a scalar value computed from its elements; for a 2×2 matrix [[a, b], [c, d]], it equals ad - bc.

• Key properties include: the determinant changes sign when two rows/columns are interchanged, and it equals zero if any two rows/columns are identical or proportional.

2. Application: Area of a Triangle

Short Note:

• The area of a triangle with vertices (x1,y1), (x2,y2), (x3,y3) can be expressed as half the absolute value of a 3×3 determinant formed from the coordinates.

• If the computed determinant is zero, the three points are collinear and do not form a triangle.

3. Adjoint, Inverse and Cramer's Rule

Short Note:

• The adjoint of a matrix A is the transpose of its cofactor matrix, and satisfies A · (adj A) = |A| · I, leading to the inverse formula A⁻¹ = (1/|A|) adj A.

• Cramer's rule uses determinants to directly solve a system of linear equations when the coefficient determinant is non-zero, yielding a unique solution.

NCERT Textbook Exercise Questions

State any two properties of determinants.
Answer: If any two rows (or columns) of a determinant are interchanged, the sign of the determinant changes, though its magnitude remains unchanged. If any two rows (or columns) of a determinant are identical or proportional to each other, the value of the determinant becomes zero.
How is the area of a triangle calculated using determinants?
Answer: Given three vertices (x1, y1), (x2, y2), (x3, y3), the area is calculated as half the absolute value of the determinant formed by placing the coordinates, along with a column of 1's, as rows of a 3×3 matrix. This method is particularly useful for quickly checking whether three given points are collinear, since collinear points yield a determinant value of zero.
Explain how Cramer's rule is used to solve a system of linear equations.
Answer: Cramer's rule expresses each unknown variable as a ratio of two determinants: the numerator is the determinant formed by replacing the corresponding column of the coefficient matrix with the column of constants, and the denominator is the determinant of the original coefficient matrix. This rule provides a unique solution only when the coefficient determinant is non-zero; if it is zero, the system either has no solution or infinitely many solutions.
What is the relationship between a matrix, its adjoint, and its determinant?
Answer: For any square matrix A, the product of A and its adjoint (adj A) equals the determinant of A multiplied by the identity matrix, expressed as A · (adj A) = |A| · I. This fundamental relationship is used to derive the formula for the inverse of a matrix, A⁻¹ = (1/|A|) adj A, which is valid only when |A| is non-zero.

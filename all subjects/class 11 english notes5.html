NCERT Solutions for Class 11 English Ch-5: The Voice of the Rain (Poem)

'The Voice of the Rain' by Walt Whitman is a philosophical poem in which the rain personifies itself and explains its essential, cyclical role in nature, comparing itself to human speech and song.

Overview of Topics & Subtopics

  • Personification of Rain as the Poem's Speaker
  • The Water Cycle Described Poetically
  • Comparison Between Rain and Human Language/Song
  • Themes of Renewal, Purpose, and the Cycle of Life

Important Questions and Answers

Q1: How is the rain personified in the poem?
Answer: In the poem, the rain is personified as a living being who speaks directly to the poet, answering his question about its identity and explaining, in its own poetic voice, its origin, purpose, and eternal cyclical journey.
Q2: What does the rain explain about its origin and its journey?
Answer: The rain explains that it originates from the earth's oceans and land, rises invisibly as vapour into the sky, transforms into clouds, and then descends back to Earth as rain, nourishing the land before eventually returning again to its source, in a continuous, unending cycle.
Q3: What comparison does the poet draw between rain and human song or language?
Answer: The poet compares rain to a song or poem that originates from an abstract source (the sky/soul), descends to fulfil and beautify the world, and eventually returns to its origin, much like how a song or poem, born from the depths of a person's soul, journeys outward and eventually finds its way back with a transformed, deeper meaning.
Q4: What does the phrase 'eternal I rise' signify in the poem?
Answer: The phrase 'eternal I rise' signifies the endless, ever-repeating cyclical nature of the rain (and by extension, the water cycle), highlighting that despite its constant descent to the earth, the rain (or its essence) always rises again, symbolizing perpetuity and renewal.
Q5: What is the central theme conveyed in 'The Voice of the Rain'?
Answer: The central theme is the eternal cycle of nature and its life-giving, purifying purpose, drawing a parallel to the way human creativity, thought, and expression (like a poem or song) undergo a similar journey of purpose, transformation, and renewal.

Eng-04: The Laburnum Top (Poem) 25 Questions

1. Who is the poet of the poem 'The Laburnum Top'?

  • A. Ted Hughes
  • B. Shirley Toulson
  • C. Walt Whitman
  • D. Markus Natten

Answer: A. Ted Hughes

Explanation: The poem 'The Laburnum Top' is composed by the famous English poet Ted Hughes.

2. How is the Laburnum tree described in the beginning of the poem?

  • A. Silent and still
  • B. Noisy and lively
  • C. Full of fruits
  • D. Covered in snow

Answer: A. Silent and still

Explanation: In the opening lines, the top of the Laburnum tree is described as silent and completely motionless.

3. What season is depicted in the poem 'The Laburnum Top'?

  • A. Autumn season
  • B. Spring season
  • C. Summer season
  • D. Winter season

Answer: A. Autumn season

Explanation: The reference to "yellow September sunlight" and yellowing leaves signifies the autumn season.

4. What color light is mentioned during the afternoon in the poem?

  • A. Yellow September sunlight
  • B. Golden morning sun
  • C. Crimson red sunset
  • D. Bright blue sky

Answer: A. Yellow September sunlight

Explanation: The poem mentions "In the afternoon yellow September sunlight", reflecting the autumn light.

5. What has happened to the leaves of the Laburnum tree?

  • A. A few leaves are turning yellow
  • B. They have completely fallen off
  • C. They turned bright green
  • D. They turned dark brown

Answer: A. A few leaves are turning yellow

Explanation: The line states: "A few leaves yellowing, all its seeds fallen."

6. What bird arrives to break the silence of the Laburnum tree?

  • A. Goldfinch
  • B. Sparrow
  • C. Cuckoo
  • D. Nightingale

Answer: A. Goldfinch

Explanation: The arrival of the mother Goldfinch brings sudden sound and movement to the quiet tree.

7. How does the Goldfinch arrive at the tree?

  • A. With a twitching chirrup
  • B. With a loud scream
  • C. Silently without any noise
  • D. Flapping heavy wings

Answer: A. With a twitching chirrup

Explanation: "Till the goldfinch comes, with a twitching chirrup..." marks her sudden entry.

8. What animal is the movement of the Goldfinch compared to?

  • A. Lizard
  • B. Snake
  • C. Squirrel
  • D. Cat

Answer: A. Lizard

Explanation: The poet uses a simile comparing the bird's smooth and alert movement: "Sleek as a lizard".

9. What poetic device is used in the phrase "Sleek as a lizard"?

  • A. Simile
  • B. Metaphor
  • C. Personification
  • D. Alliteration

Answer: A. Simile

Explanation: A comparison using 'as' ("Sleek as a lizard") is a simile.

10. What words are used to describe the Goldfinch's movement into the thickness?

  • A. Sleek, alert, and abrupt
  • B. Slow, careless, and quiet
  • C. Fast, clumsy, and heavy
  • D. Lazy, gentle, and smooth

Answer: A. Sleek, alert, and abrupt

Explanation: The poet writes: "Sleek as a lizard, and alert, and abrupt, She enters the thickness."

11. What starts up when the bird enters the thick branch of the tree?

  • A. A machine of chitterings, tremor of wings, and trillings
  • B. A strong wind gust
  • C. Raindrops falling
  • D. Thunder sound

Answer: A. A machine of chitterings, tremor of wings, and trillings

Explanation: The entry of the bird triggers her young chicks into activity, creating a chatter like a starting machine.

12. What metaphor is used for the family/chicks in the tree?

  • A. Machine
  • B. Engine
  • C. Factory
  • D. Orchestra

Answer: A. Machine

Explanation: The sudden noise created by the chicks is metaphorically referred to as a "machine".

13. Who is referred to as "the engine of her family"?

  • A. The Goldfinch
  • B. The Laburnum tree
  • C. The poet
  • D. The September wind

Answer: A. The Goldfinch

Explanation: The Goldfinch is called the "engine of her family" as she provides nourishment and life to her nestlings.

14. What does the bird do to "stokes it full"?

  • A. Feeds her young ones
  • B. Builds a bigger nest
  • C. Sings a loud song
  • D. Shakes all the branches

Answer: A. Feeds her young ones

Explanation: "She stokes it full" means she feeds her family thoroughly with food.

15. What shows on the branch end after the bird moves out?

  • A. Her barred face identity mask
  • B. Yellow feathers
  • C. A flower bloom
  • D. A broken leaf

Answer: A. Her barred face identity mask

Explanation: The poet writes: "Flirts out to a branch-end Showing her barred face identity mask."

16. What is the meaning of "barred face identity mask"?

  • A. Striped pattern on the bird's face serving as identification
  • B. A wooden mask covering her head
  • C. Mud on the bird's beak
  • D. Shadow of tree leaves on her face

Answer: A. Striped pattern on the bird's face serving as identification

Explanation: The natural stripe markings on the Goldfinch's face act like an identity tag for her species.

17. Where does the bird fly away after feeding her chicks?

  • A. Towards the infinite (sky)
  • B. To another tree nearby
  • C. To the ground below
  • D. To a river bank

Answer: A. Towards the infinite (sky)

Explanation: "Then with eerie delicate whistle-chirrup whisperings She launches away, towards the infinite..."

18. What happens to the Laburnum tree after the bird flies away?

  • A. It subsides to empty and silent state again
  • B. It falls down completely
  • C. Its flowers start blooming again
  • D. It catches fire

Answer: A. It subsides to empty and silent state again

Explanation: The poem ends with: "and the laburnum subsides to empty."

19. What is the Laburnum tree commonly called in Hindi?

  • A. Amaltas
  • B. Gulmohar
  • C. Neem
  • D. Peepal

Answer: A. Amaltas

Explanation: Laburnum is known as 'Amaltas', a short tree with hanging yellow flowers and poisonous seeds.

20. Which feature of the Laburnum tree is poisonous?

  • A. Its seeds
  • B. Its roots
  • C. Its trunk bark
  • D. Its yellow petals

Answer: A. Its seeds

Explanation: The seeds of the Laburnum (Amaltas) tree are naturally poisonous.

21. What image is created by "tremor of wings and trillings"?

  • A. Auditory and visual image of excitement and life
  • B. Image of destruction and fear
  • C. Image of darkness and solitude
  • D. Image of extreme cold weather

Answer: A. Auditory and visual image of excitement and life

Explanation: It evokes the lively, energetic fluttering and chirping of baby birds receiving food.

22. What does the word 'chitterings' mean in the poem?

  • A. High-pitched chirping sounds of small birds
  • B. Sound of rustling dry leaves
  • C. Sound of blowing wind
  • D. Sound of footsteps

Answer: A. High-pitched chirping sounds of small birds

Explanation: 'Chitterings' refers to the rapid, continuous, high-pitched chirping made by young nestlings.

23. How does the entire tree respond to the bird and her chicks' chatter?

  • A. It trembles and thrills
  • B. It sways violently and breaks
  • C. It sheds all its leaves at once
  • D. It remains completely dead and untouched

Answer: A. It trembles and thrills

Explanation: "The whole tree trembles and thrills" shows how life fills the once-dead tree.

24. What primary theme is expressed in 'The Laburnum Top'?

  • A. The symbiotic relationship between nature and living beings
  • B. The horrors of deforestation
  • C. The loneliness of human life
  • D. The cruelty of predatory birds

Answer: A. The symbiotic relationship between nature and living beings

Explanation: The poem highlights how the bird relies on the tree for shelter, and the tree gains life and vibrancy from the bird.

25. What color dominant imagery is present throughout the poem?

  • A. Yellow
  • B. Red
  • C. Green
  • D. White

Answer: A. Yellow

Explanation: Yellow flowers, yellowing leaves, yellow September sunlight, and yellow feathers of the goldfinch make yellow the dominant color theme.

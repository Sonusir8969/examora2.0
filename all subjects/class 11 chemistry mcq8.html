Ch-8: Organic Chemistry – Some Basic Principles and Techniques 30 Questions

1. Homolytic cleavage of a covalent bond produces:

  • A. Free radicals
  • B. Carbocations
  • C. Carbanions
  • D. Electrophiles

Answer: A. Free radicals

Explanation: Homolytic fission involves equal sharing of bonding electrons, yielding neutral species with single unpaired electrons (free radicals).

2. Heterolytic cleavage of a covalent bond results in the formation of:

  • A. Cations and Anions
  • B. Free radicals
  • C. Neutral molecules
  • D. Uncharged species

Answer: A. Cations and Anions

Explanation: Heterolytic cleavage leaves both bonding electrons with the more electronegative atom, producing positive and negative ions.

3. What is the hybridization state of carbon in methane (CH₄)?

  • A. sp³
  • B. sp²
  • C. sp
  • D. dsp²

Answer: A. sp³

Explanation: The carbon atom in methane forms 4 single sigma bonds with tetrahedral geometry, requiring sp³ hybridization.

4. The shape of a carbocation (carbonium ion) is:

  • A. Trigonal planar
  • B. Pyramidal
  • C. Tetrahedral
  • D. Linear

Answer: A. Trigonal planar

Explanation: Carbon in a carbocation is sp² hybridized with six valence electrons, creating a flat trigonal planar geometry.

5. Which of the following carbocations is the most stable?

  • A. (CH₃)₃C⁺ (Tertiary)
  • B. (CH₃)₂CH⁺ (Secondary)
  • C. CH₃CH₂⁺ (Primary)
  • D. CH₃⁺ (Methyl)

Answer: A. (CH₃)₃C⁺ (Tertiary)

Explanation: Tertiary carbocations are stabilized by maximum hyperconjugation structures and +I inductive effects of three alkyl groups.

6. An electrophile is a reagent that:

  • A. Accepts an electron pair
  • B. Donates an electron pair
  • C. Donates a proton
  • D. Contains a negative charge only

Answer: A. Accepts an electron pair

Explanation: Electrophiles are electron-deficient species (Lewis acids) that seek out electron-dense sites.

7. Which of the following species acts as a nucleophile?

  • A. NH₃
  • B. BF₃
  • C. AlCl₃
  • D. NO₂⁺

Answer: A. NH₃

Explanation: Ammonia (NH₃) possesses an unshared lone pair of electrons on nitrogen, enabling it to act as a nucleophile.

8. What is the IUPAC name of CH₃-CH(OH)-CH₃?

  • A. Propan-2-ol
  • B. Propan-1-ol
  • C. Isopropyl alcohol
  • D. Propanone

Answer: A. Propan-2-ol

Explanation: The main chain contains 3 carbons with a hydroxyl group at position 2, giving Propan-2-ol.

9. Isomers having the same molecular formula but different functional groups are called:

  • A. Functional group isomers
  • B. Chain isomers
  • C. Position isomers
  • D. Metamers

Answer: A. Functional group isomers

Explanation: Functional isomerism occurs when compounds share the same formula but differ in functional group classes (e.g., ethanol and dimethyl ether).

10. The IUPAC name of CH₃-CH₂-CHO is:

  • A. Propanal
  • B. Propanone
  • C. Propanoic acid
  • D. Ethyl methyl ketone

Answer: A. Propanal

Explanation: A three-carbon chain containing an aldehyde suffix (-al) is named propanal.

11. Hyperconjugation involves the delocalization of electrons of:

  • A. C-H σ bond with adjacent empty or partially filled p-orbital
  • B. π bonds with adjacent π bonds
  • C. Lone pairs with σ bonds
  • D. Lone pairs with lone pairs

Answer: A. C-H σ bond with adjacent empty or partially filled p-orbital

Explanation: Also known as Baker-Nathan effect or no-bond resonance, hyperconjugation involves σ(C-H) electron interaction with adjacent p-orbitals.

12. Inductive effect involves complete or partial displacement of:

  • A. σ (sigma) electrons
  • B. π (pi) electrons
  • C. Lone pair electrons
  • D. Unpaired electrons

Answer: A. σ (sigma) electrons

Explanation: Inductive effect is a permanent displacement of σ-electrons along a carbon chain due to electronegativity differences.

13. Which separation technique is based on difference in boiling points of liquids?

  • A. Distillation
  • B. Crystallization
  • C. Chromatography
  • D. Sublimation

Answer: A. Distillation

Explanation: Simple distillation separates volatile liquids with sufficient boiling point differences through selective evaporation and condensation.

14. Steam distillation is used to purify substances that are:

  • A. Steam volatile and insoluble in water
  • B. Non-volatile and soluble in water
  • C. Decompose at boiling point and soluble in water
  • D. Highly soluble in ethanol

Answer: A. Steam volatile and insoluble in water

Explanation: Steam distillation purifies organic compounds like aniline that are immiscible with water and steam-volatile.

15. In Lassaigne's test, nitrogen present in an organic compound is converted to:

  • A. Sodium Cyanide (NaCN)
  • B. Sodium Nitrate (NaNO₃)
  • C. Ammonia (NH₃)
  • D. Sodium Nitrite (NaNO₂)

Answer: A. Sodium Cyanide (NaCN)

Explanation: Fusion of nitrogenous organic matter with sodium metal yields ionic NaCN (Na + C + N → NaCN).

16. Nitrogen estimation by Kjeldahl's method is NOT applicable to compounds containing:

  • A. Nitro group (-NO₂) and Azo groups (-N=N-)
  • B. Primary amines
  • C. Amides
  • D. Secondary amines

Answer: A. Nitro group (-NO₂) and Azo groups (-N=N-)

Explanation: Nitrogen in nitro, azo groups, and ring structures (like pyridine) cannot be converted quantitatively to ammonium sulfate in standard digestion.

17. Dumas method is used for the quantitative estimation of:

  • A. Nitrogen
  • B. Halogens
  • C. Carbon and Hydrogen
  • D. Sulfur

Answer: A. Nitrogen

Explanation: Dumas method estimates total nitrogen by converting organic nitrogen to gaseous N₂ over CuO.

18. Halogens in an organic compound are quantitatively estimated using:

  • A. Carius method
  • B. Kjeldahl's method
  • C. Dumas method
  • D. Liebig's method

Answer: A. Carius method

Explanation: Carius method heats organic halogens with fuming HNO₃ and AgNO₃ inside a sealed tube to precipitate silver halides (AgX).

19. What is the IUPAC name of HC≡C-CH=CH-CH₃?

  • A. Pent-3-en-1-yne
  • B. Pent-2-en-4-yne
  • C. Pent-1-yn-3-ene
  • D. Pent-4-yn-2-ene

Answer: A. Pent-3-en-1-yne

Explanation: Numbering starts from the left to give lower locants to unsaturation (triple bond gets 1, double bond gets 3); alkynes rank lower in prefix order, written Pent-3-en-1-yne.

20. Which of the following is a temporary electronic effect operating only in the presence of an attacking reagent?

  • A. Electromeric effect
  • B. Inductive effect
  • C. Resonance effect
  • D. Hyperconjugation

Answer: A. Electromeric effect

Explanation: Electromeric effect involves temporary complete transfer of shared π-electrons driven by an approaching electrophile or nucleophile.

21. Prussian blue color in Lassaigne's test for nitrogen is due to the formation of:

  • A. Fe₄[Fe(CN)₆]₃
  • B. Na₄[Fe(CN)₆]
  • C. Fe(SCN)₃
  • D. K₃[Fe(CN)₆]

Answer: A. Fe₄[Fe(CN)₆]₃

Explanation: Iron(III) hexacyanoferrate(II), ferric ferrocyanide Fe₄[Fe(CN)₆]₃, gives the characteristic Prussian blue complex.

22. What is the shape of a carbanion like CH₃⁻?

  • A. Pyramidal
  • B. Trigonal planar
  • C. Linear
  • D. Octahedral

Answer: A. Pyramidal

Explanation: The carbon atom in a carbanion is sp³ hybridized with three bond pairs and one lone pair, assuming trigonal pyramidal geometry.

23. Which of the following compounds exhibits metamerism?

  • A. CH₃-O-C₃H₇ and C₂H₅-O-C₂H₅
  • B. CH₃-CH₂-CH₂-OH and CH₃-CH(OH)-CH₃
  • C. CH₃-CH₂-COOH and CH₃-CO-OCH₃
  • D. C₄H₁₀ and C₃H₈

Answer: A. CH₃-O-C₃H₇ and C₂H₅-O-C₂H₅

Explanation: Metamerism arises due to unequal distribution of alkyl chains attached to the same polyvalent functional group (-O-).

24. What is the formula of the functional group present in ketones?

  • A. >C=O
  • B. -CHO
  • C. -COOH
  • D. -OH

Answer: A. >C=O

Explanation: Ketones contain a carbonyl group (>C=O) bonded to two carbon alkyl or aryl substituents.

25. Chromatography is a physical method used mainly for:

  • A. Separation, isolation, and purification of components of a mixture
  • B. Estimating molecular mass
  • C. Measuring density
  • D. Determining melting point

Answer: A. Separation, isolation, and purification of components of a mixture

Explanation: Chromatography relies on differential partitioning between stationary and mobile phases to isolate pure compounds.

26. Which group shows -I (negative inductive) effect?

  • A. -NO₂
  • B. -CH₃
  • C. -C₂H₅
  • D. -(CH₃)₃C

Answer: A. -NO₂

Explanation: Strongly electronegative groups like -NO₂ withdraw σ-electron density, exhibiting a powerful -I effect.

27. Organic compounds having the same general formula and chemical properties but differing by -CH₂- group are called:

  • A. Homologues
  • B. Isomers
  • C. Isotopes
  • D. Allotropes

Answer: A. Homologues

Explanation: Members of a homologous series share identical functional groups and differ sequentially by a methylene (-CH₂-) unit (14 u).

28. What is the hybridization state of carbon in ethyne (C₂H₂)?

  • A. sp
  • B. sp²
  • C. sp³
  • D. dsp²

Answer: A. sp

Explanation: Triple-bonded carbons form two σ bonds and two π bonds, requiring sp hybridization with a linear 180° geometry.

29. Purification of solid organic compounds based on solubility differences in a solvent is known as:

  • A. Crystallization
  • B. Distillation
  • C. Sublimation
  • D. Extraction

Answer: A. Crystallization

Explanation: Crystallization purifies solids by dissolving them in hot solvent and precipitating target crystals upon cooling.

30. What is the IUPAC name of CH₃-CO-CH₃?

  • A. Propan-2-one
  • B. Propanal
  • C. Ethanoic acid
  • D. Dimethyl ether

Answer: A. Propan-2-one

Explanation: Propan-2-one (acetone) contains 3 carbons with a carbonyl oxygen at position 2.

Ch-7: Agriculture 30 Questions

1. What proportion of India's population is engaged in agricultural activities?

  • A. Two-thirds
  • B. One-fourth
  • C. One-third
  • D. Three-fourths

Answer: A. Two-thirds

Explanation: Agriculture is a primary activity in India, producing most of the food consumed and providing employment to nearly two-thirds of the population.

2. Primitive subsistence farming is also known as 'slash and burn' agriculture. What is it locally called in the North-Eastern states of India like Assam and Meghalaya?

  • A. Jhumming
  • B. Dahiya
  • C. Podu
  • D. Milpa

Answer: A. Jhumming

Explanation: 'Slash and burn' agriculture is commonly known as 'Jhumming' in the north-eastern states such as Assam, Meghalaya, Mizoram, and Nagaland.

3. What type of farming is characterized by the use of higher doses of modern inputs like High Yielding Variety (HYV) seeds, chemical fertilizers, and insecticides?

  • A. Commercial Farming
  • B. Primitive Subsistence Farming
  • C. Organic Farming
  • D. Pastoral Farming

Answer: A. Commercial Farming

Explanation: The main characteristic of commercial farming is the use of higher doses of modern inputs to achieve higher productivity for market sale.

4. Plantation agriculture is a type of commercial farming where a single crop is grown over a large area. Which of the following is an example of a plantation crop?

  • A. Tea
  • B. Wheat
  • C. Bajra
  • D. Pulses

Answer: A. Tea

Explanation: Tea, coffee, rubber, sugarcane, and banana are important plantation crops grown across large estates using capital-intensive inputs.

5. Which cropping season in India begins with the onset of monsoons and is harvested in September–October?

  • A. Kharif
  • B. Rabi
  • C. Zaid
  • D. Autumn

Answer: A. Kharif

Explanation: Kharif crops are grown with the onset of monsoon in different parts of the country and harvested in September-October.

6. Rabi crops are sown in winter from October to December and harvested in which season?

  • A. Summer (April to June)
  • B. Rainy (July to August)
  • C. Late Autumn (November)
  • D. Mid-Winter (January)

Answer: A. Summer (April to June)

Explanation: Rabi crops are sown in winter (October–December) and harvested in summer from April to June.

7. Which short season falls between the Rabi and Kharif seasons during the summer months?

  • A. Zaid
  • B. Boro
  • C. Aus
  • D. Aman

Answer: A. Zaid

Explanation: In between the Rabi and Kharif seasons, there is a short season during the summer months known as the Zaid season (producing crops like watermelon, cucumber, etc.).

8. Which crop is the staple food choice for a majority of the population in India, making India the second-largest producer in the world after China?

  • A. Rice
  • B. Wheat
  • C. Maize
  • D. Millets

Answer: A. Rice

Explanation: Rice is the staple food crop for a majority of people in India, which is the second largest producer of rice in the world after China.

9. What temperature and rainfall conditions are required for optimal growth of rice?

  • A. Above 25°C temperature and high humidity with annual rainfall above 100 cm
  • B. Below 15°C temperature and rainfall below 50 cm
  • C. 10°C to 15°C temperature and bright sunshine with no rainfall
  • D. 40°C temperature and rainfall around 20 cm

Answer: A. Above 25°C temperature and high humidity with annual rainfall above 100 cm

Explanation: Rice requires high temperature (above 25°C), high humidity, and annual rainfall above 100 cm to grow properly.

10. What is the second most important cereal crop in India, acting as the main food crop in north and north-western parts of the country?

  • A. Wheat
  • B. Rice
  • C. Jowar
  • D. Bajra

Answer: A. Wheat

Explanation: Wheat is the second most important cereal crop and the main food crop in north and north-western parts of India.

11. Which major coarse grains (millets) are rich in nutritional value and widely grown in India?

  • A. Jowar, Bajra, and Ragi
  • B. Wheat, Rice, and Maize
  • C. Pulses, Gram, and Tur
  • D. Sesame, Linseed, and Castor

Answer: A. Jowar, Bajra, and Ragi

Explanation: Jowar, Bajra, and Ragi are the important millets grown in India. Though known as coarse grains, they have very high nutritional value.

12. Which millet is the third most important food crop with respect to area and production in India?

  • A. Jowar
  • B. Bajra
  • C. Ragi
  • D. Maize

Answer: A. Jowar

Explanation: Jowar is the third most important food crop with respect to area and production. It is a rain-fed crop mostly grown in moist areas.

13. Which crop is used both as food and fodder, requiring temperatures between 21°C to 27°C and growing well in old alluvial soil?

  • A. Maize
  • B. Wheat
  • C. Sugarcane
  • D. Barley

Answer: A. Maize

Explanation: Maize is a crop used both as food and fodder. It is a Kharif crop that grows well in old alluvial soil at 21°C to 27°C.

14. India is the largest producer as well as the consumer of which nutritional crop category in the world?

  • A. Pulses
  • B. Rice
  • C. Sugarcane
  • D. Tea

Answer: A. Pulses

Explanation: India is the largest producer as well as consumer of pulses in the world, which are the major source of protein in a vegetarian diet.

15. Pulses help restore soil fertility by fixing nitrogen from the air. Which pulse crop is an exception and does NOT fix nitrogen?

  • A. Arhar (Tur)
  • B. Moong
  • C. Gram
  • D. Urad

Answer: A. Arhar (Tur)

Explanation: Being leguminous crops, all pulses except Arhar (Tur) help in restoring soil fertility by fixing nitrogen from the air.

16. Sugarcane grows best in which type of climate?

  • A. Hot and humid climate (21°C to 27°C and 75 to 100 cm rainfall)
  • B. Cold and dry climate
  • C. Temperate climate with heavy snowfall
  • D. Extremely arid desert climate

Answer: A. Hot and humid climate (21°C to 27°C and 75 to 100 cm rainfall)

Explanation: Sugarcane is a tropical as well as a subtropical crop that grows well in hot and humid climates with temperatures from 21°C to 27°C and rainfall between 75 cm and 100 cm.

17. Which beverage crop was initially introduced in India by the British on the Baba Budan Hills?

  • A. Coffee
  • B. Tea
  • C. Cocoa
  • D. Rubber

Answer: A. Coffee

Explanation: Arabica variety of coffee was initially brought from Yemen and introduced on the Baba Budan Hills in Karnataka.

18. Rearing of silkworms for the production of silk fibre is known as:

  • A. Sericulture
  • B. Pisciculture
  • C. Viticulture
  • D. Horticulture

Answer: A. Sericulture

Explanation: Rearing of silkworms for the production of natural silk fiber fed on mulberry leaves is known as sericulture.

19. Which crop is known as the "Golden Fibre" of India?

  • A. Jute
  • B. Cotton
  • C. Hemp
  • D. Silk

Answer: A. Jute

Explanation: Jute is known as the golden fibre. It grows well on well-drained fertile soils in the flood plains, especially in West Bengal.

20. Cotton requires how many frost-free days and bright sunshine for its growth?

  • A. 210 frost-free days
  • B. 100 frost-free days
  • C. 365 frost-free days
  • D. 50 frost-free days

Answer: A. 210 frost-free days

Explanation: Cotton requires high temperature, light rainfall, 210 frost-free days, and bright sunshine for its growth on black cotton soil.

21. Rubber is an equatorial crop, but under special conditions, it is also grown in tropical and sub-tropical areas requiring rainfall of more than:

  • A. 200 cm
  • B. 50 cm
  • C. 100 cm
  • D. 75 cm

Answer: A. 200 cm

Explanation: Rubber requires a moist and humid climate with rainfall of more than 200 cm and temperature above 25°C.

22. What was the main focus of the First Five Year Plan regarding agrarian reforms in India?

  • A. Land Reforms (Consolidation of holdings)
  • B. Green Revolution
  • C. White Revolution
  • D. Digital Agriculture

Answer: A. Land Reforms (Consolidation of holdings)

Explanation: Institutional reforms like land reforms, abolition of zamindari, and consolidation of holdings were the main focus of the First Five Year Plan.

23. The 'Green Revolution' introduced in the 1960s and 1970s was based on the use of:

  • A. Package technology involving HYV seeds, fertilizers, and irrigation
  • B. Organic bio-pesticides and rain-fed techniques
  • C. Complete mechanization without chemical inputs
  • D. Plantation forestry

Answer: A. Package technology involving HYV seeds, fertilizers, and irrigation

Explanation: The Green Revolution based on the use of package technology (HYV seeds, chemical fertilizers, modern irrigation) was initiated to improve Indian agricultural production.

24. What is the 'Operation Flood' in India associated with?

  • A. White Revolution (Milk Production)
  • B. Green Revolution (Wheat Production)
  • C. Blue Revolution (Fish Production)
  • D. Yellow Revolution (Oilseed Production)

Answer: A. White Revolution (Milk Production)

Explanation: Operation Flood is associated with the White Revolution, which aimed to increase milk production and support dairy farmers.

25. Who initiated the Bhoodan-Gramdan movement, also referred to as the 'Bloodless Revolution'?

  • A. Vinoba Bhave
  • B. Mahatma Gandhi
  • C. Jawaharlal Nehru
  • D. Jayaprakash Narayan

Answer: A. Vinoba Bhave

Explanation: Vinoba Bhave initiated the Bhoodan-Gramdan movement, encouraging voluntary land donation for landless villagers, called the Bloodless Revolution.

26. Shri Ram Chandra Reddy offered 80 acres of land to landless villagers during the Bhoodan movement in which village of Telangana?

  • A. Pochampalli
  • B. Champaran
  • C. Bardoli
  • D. Sabarmati

Answer: A. Pochampalli

Explanation: At Pochampalli in Andhra Pradesh (now Telangana), Shri Ram Chandra Reddy offered 80 acres of land to 80 landless villagers, marking the birth of Bhoodan.

27. Kissan Credit Card (KCC) and Personal Accident Insurance Scheme (PAIS) were introduced by the government for what purpose?

  • A. To provide cheap and easy credit facilities to farmers
  • B. To promote import of foreign food grains
  • C. To restrict farmers from taking agricultural loans
  • D. To tax rich farmers

Answer: A. To provide cheap and easy credit facilities to farmers

Explanation: KCC and PAIS were introduced for the benefit of farmers to ensure smooth credit and financial safety against crop failures and accidents.

28. What price policy does the government announce before the sowing season to check exploitation of farmers by speculators?

  • A. Minimum Support Price (MSP)
  • B. Maximum Retail Price (MRP)
  • C. Fixed Export Price
  • D. Wholesale Index Rate

Answer: A. Minimum Support Price (MSP)

Explanation: The government announces Minimum Support Price (MSP), remunerative, and procurement prices for important crops to check exploitation by middlemen.

29. Which state is the leading producer of Tea in India?

  • A. Assam
  • B. Kerala
  • C. Tamil Nadu
  • D. West Bengal

Answer: A. Assam

Explanation: Major tea-producing states are Assam, hills of Darjeeling and Jalpaiguri districts in West Bengal, Tamil Nadu, and Kerala. Assam leads in overall production.

30. Organic farming is gaining popularity today because it is practiced without using which inputs?

  • A. Factory-made chemical fertilizers and pesticides
  • B. Natural organic manure
  • C. Bio-pesticides
  • D. Traditional irrigation tools

Answer: A. Factory-made chemical fertilizers and pesticides

Explanation: Organic farming is practiced without factory-made chemicals such as fertilizers and pesticides, so it does not affect the environment adversely.

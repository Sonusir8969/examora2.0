NCERT Solutions for Class 11 Biology Chapter 15 Body Fluids and Circulation

NCERT Solutions for Class 11 Biology Chapter 15 Body Fluids and Circulation covers the composition of blood and lymph, the human heart and cardiac cycle, and double circulation.

Overview of Topics & Subtopics (Click to Jump)

  • 1. Blood
  • 2. Lymph
  • 3. The Human Heart and Cardiac Cycle

1. Blood

Short Note:

• Blood is a fluid connective tissue consisting of a fluid matrix, plasma (containing water, proteins like fibrinogen/globulins/albumins), and formed elements: RBCs (oxygen transport via haemoglobin), WBCs (immunity/defence), and platelets (blood clotting).

• Blood groups (ABO system): based on presence/absence of antigens A and B on RBCs; Rh factor: presence (Rh+) or absence (Rh−) of the Rh antigen, important in blood transfusion and Rh incompatibility during pregnancy (erythroblastosis foetalis).

2. Lymph

Short Note:

• Lymph is a colourless fluid, similar to plasma but with lower protein content, that drains from tissue spaces into lymphatic vessels; important for fat transport and immune function.

3. The Human Heart and Cardiac Cycle

Short Note:

• The human heart is four-chambered (two atria, two ventricles), pumping blood through double circulation — pulmonary circulation (heart to lungs and back) and systemic circulation (heart to body and back).

• The cardiac cycle consists of systole (contraction) and diastole (relaxation) of the atria and ventricles, generating heart sounds (lub-dub) and regulated by the sinoatrial (SA) node, the natural pacemaker.

NCERT Textbook Exercise Questions

What is the difference between blood and lymph?
Answer: Blood is a red-coloured fluid connective tissue containing plasma and formed elements (RBC, WBC, platelets), flows within a closed system of blood vessels, and transports oxygen, nutrients, and wastes. Lymph is a colourless fluid derived from blood plasma (with lower protein content and no RBCs), flows through lymphatic vessels, and is primarily involved in fat absorption/transport and immune defence.
Why do we call our heart myogenic?
Answer: The heart is called myogenic because the initiation and maintenance of its rhythmic contractions originates from specialised cardiac muscle tissue itself (the sinoatrial node), rather than from an external nerve impulse, distinguishing it from neurogenic hearts (like in some invertebrates) where contraction is initiated by nerve signals.
Describe the evolutionary changes in the pattern of heart chambers in vertebrates.
Answer: Fish have a two-chambered heart (one atrium, one ventricle) with single circulation. Amphibians and most reptiles have a three-chambered heart (two atria, one ventricle), allowing some mixing of oxygenated and deoxygenated blood. Crocodiles, birds, and mammals have a four-chambered heart (two atria, two ventricles), completely separating oxygenated and deoxygenated blood, enabling efficient double circulation.
What is a cardiac cycle and what is its cardiac output?
Answer: A cardiac cycle refers to the sequence of events in one complete heartbeat, consisting of the contraction (systole) and relaxation (diastole) of the atria and ventricles, occurring in a coordinated manner. Cardiac output is the volume of blood pumped by each ventricle per minute, calculated as stroke volume (blood pumped per beat) multiplied by heart rate (beats per minute).

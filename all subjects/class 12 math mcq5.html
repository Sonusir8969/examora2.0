Ch-5: Continuity and Differentiability 12 Questions

1. A function f(x) is said to be continuous at x = a if:

  • A. lim(x→a) f(x) = f(a)
  • B. f(a) is defined only, with no other condition
  • C. f is differentiable at a
  • D. f is bounded near a

Answer: A. lim(x→a) f(x) = f(a)

Explanation: Continuity at a point requires that the limit of the function as x approaches a equals the function's value at a.

2. Every differentiable function is necessarily:

  • A. Continuous
  • B. Discontinuous
  • C. Never continuous
  • D. Unrelated to continuity

Answer: A. Continuous

Explanation: Differentiability at a point implies continuity at that point, though the converse is not always true.

3. The function f(x) = |x| is:

  • A. Continuous everywhere but not differentiable at x = 0
  • B. Differentiable everywhere
  • C. Discontinuous at x = 0
  • D. Neither continuous nor differentiable anywhere

Answer: A. Continuous everywhere but not differentiable at x = 0

Explanation: |x| has a sharp corner at x = 0, so while it is continuous there, its left-hand and right-hand derivatives differ.

4. The derivative of sin x with respect to x is:

  • A. cos x
  • B. -cos x
  • C. -sin x
  • D. sec²x

Answer: A. cos x

Explanation: This is a standard derivative result: d/dx (sin x) = cos x.

5. Rolle's theorem requires the function to be:

  • A. Continuous on [a, b], differentiable on (a, b), and f(a) = f(b)
  • B. Only continuous on [a, b]
  • C. Only differentiable on (a, b)
  • D. Strictly monotonic on [a, b]

Answer: A. Continuous on [a, b], differentiable on (a, b), and f(a) = f(b)

Explanation: These are the exact hypotheses of Rolle's theorem, guaranteeing at least one point where f'(c) = 0.

6. The Mean Value Theorem states that there exists a point c in (a, b) such that:

  • A. f'(c) = [f(b) - f(a)] / (b - a)
  • B. f'(c) = 0 always
  • C. f(c) = 0
  • D. f''(c) = 0

Answer: A. f'(c) = [f(b) - f(a)] / (b - a)

Explanation: The Mean Value Theorem guarantees a point where the instantaneous rate of change equals the average rate of change over [a, b].

7. If y = e^x, then dy/dx equals:

  • A. e^x
  • B. x · e^(x-1)
  • C. e^x · ln x
  • D. 1

Answer: A. e^x

Explanation: The exponential function e^x is unique in that it is its own derivative.

8. Logarithmic differentiation is especially useful for functions of the form:

  • A. [f(x)]^g(x)
  • B. Simple polynomials
  • C. Linear functions
  • D. Constant functions

Answer: A. [f(x)]^g(x)

Explanation: Taking the logarithm of both sides converts a variable exponent into a product, making differentiation of such functions much simpler.

9. The derivative of the natural logarithm function log x with respect to x is:

  • A. 1/x
  • B. x
  • C. log x
  • D. 1

Answer: A. 1/x

Explanation: This is a standard derivative result: d/dx (ln x) = 1/x, valid for x > 0.

10. The chain rule is primarily used to differentiate:

  • A. Composite functions
  • B. Only the sum of two functions
  • C. Only constant functions
  • D. Only polynomial functions

Answer: A. Composite functions

Explanation: The chain rule allows differentiation of a function of a function, i.e., f(g(x)), by multiplying the outer and inner derivatives.

11. The second order derivative of a function represents the:

  • A. Rate of change of the first derivative
  • B. The original function itself
  • C. Only the slope of the tangent line
  • D. The area under the curve

Answer: A. Rate of change of the first derivative

Explanation: The second derivative measures how the rate of change (first derivative) itself is changing, often related to concavity.

12. A function can be differentiable at a point only if it is first:

  • A. Continuous at that point
  • B. Bounded at that point
  • C. Monotonic at that point
  • D. Periodic

Answer: A. Continuous at that point

Explanation: Continuity is a necessary (but not sufficient) condition for differentiability at a point.

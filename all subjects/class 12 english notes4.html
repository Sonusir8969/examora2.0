NCERT Solutions for Class 12 English Chapter 4 The Rattrap

NCERT Solutions for Class 12 English Flamingo Chapter 4 The Rattrap covers Selma Lagerlof's story of a peddler's moral transformation, the theme of human connection and redemption, and important questions and answers.

1. Story Summary

Short Note:

• The story follows a poor peddler who sells small rattraps for a living and has come to view the world itself as one big rattrap, in which people are lured by pleasures and comforts only to become trapped by hardship and misfortune.

• After being shown kindness by a crofter who shares his home and money with him, the peddler steals the crofter's savings and flees, later disguising himself as a former acquaintance, Captain von Stahle, to gain hospitality from an ironmaster and his daughter, Edla.

• Despite his deception being discovered, Edla's genuine kindness and trust deeply move the peddler, leading him to leave behind the stolen money along with a rattrap as a gift and a note, in a symbolic act of gratitude and moral transformation, addressing Edla as a friend who helped him escape the 'rattrap' of the world.

2. Character Sketch

Short Note:

• The Peddler: An initially cynical, dishonest man shaped by years of poverty and mistrust, who undergoes a genuine moral transformation after experiencing unconditional kindness and respect from Edla.

• Edla Willmansson: The ironmaster's compassionate and perceptive daughter, who treats the peddler with dignity and kindness despite knowing his true identity, ultimately inspiring his transformation.

3. Theme & Message

Short Note:

• The story's central theme is the transformative power of kindness, trust, and human connection in redeeming even those who have been hardened by a difficult and dishonest life.

• The metaphor of the 'rattrap' represents how material temptations can trap people in cycles of dishonesty and isolation, while genuine compassion offers a way to escape this trap.

Reading With Insight (Textbook Questions)

Q1: Why does the peddler compare the world to a rattrap?
Answer: The peddler compares the world to a rattrap because he believes that, just as a trap lures rats with tempting bait like cheese, the world tempts people with the promise of riches, comforts, shelter and other pleasures, only for these temptations to trap them once they take the bait, leading to hardship and downfall.
Q2: How did Edla Willmansson treat the peddler differently from others, and what impact did this have on him?
Answer: Edla treated the peddler with genuine kindness, dignity and trust, insisting he stay for Christmas celebrations even after discovering his deception and criminal past, refusing to judge or humiliate him unlike others; this unexpected compassion deeply moved the peddler, ultimately inspiring him to change his dishonest ways.
Q3: Why did the peddler leave behind the stolen money and a rattrap as a gift for Edla?
Answer: The peddler left the stolen money along with a rattrap and a note as an act of gratitude and moral redemption, addressing Edla as the one who had helped him escape the metaphorical 'rattrap' of a dishonest and cynical existence, showing his transformation into a more honest and grateful person because of her kindness.

Extract-Based / Short Answer Questions

Q4: What does the ending of the story suggest about the power of trust and respect in transforming a person?
Answer: The ending suggests that trust and respect, rather than judgment or punishment, have the power to inspire genuine change in a person, as demonstrated by the peddler's decision to return the stolen money and express heartfelt gratitude, showing how compassion can succeed where suspicion and condemnation might fail.
Q5: How does the crofter's kindness and the peddler's betrayal of it set up the moral conflict of the story?
Answer: The crofter's genuine, unguarded kindness towards the peddler, followed by the peddler's theft and betrayal of this trust, establishes the central moral conflict of the story, highlighting the peddler's initial cynicism and dishonesty, which is later resolved through his transformation inspired by Edla's contrasting act of continued kindness despite knowing the truth.

NCERT Solutions for Class 9 Economics Chapter 4 Food Security in India

NCERT Solutions for Class 9 Economics Chapter 4 covers Dimensions of Food Security, Need for Food Security, Food Insecurity, Buffer Stock, Public Distribution System (PDS), and Role of Cooperatives.

1. Concept & Dimensions of Food Security

Short Note:

• Food Security: Means availability, accessibility, and affordability of food to all people at all times.

• Three Dimensions:
1. Availability: Food production in country plus food imports and previous years' stocks.
2. Accessibility: Food is within reach of every person.
3. Affordability: An individual has enough money to buy sufficient, safe, and nutritious food.

• Hunger Types:
• Chronic Hunger: Consequences of diets persistently inadequate in terms of quantity and/or quality.
• Seasonal Hunger: Related to cycles of food growing and harvesting (common in rural areas during agricultural off-season).

2. Food Security System in India: Buffer Stock & PDS

Short Note:

India maintains food security through two main components:

• Buffer Stock: Stock of food grains (wheat and rice) procured by the government through the Food Corporation of India (FCI) at Minimum Support Price (MSP) from surplus states.

• Public Distribution System (PDS): Distribution of food grains procured by FCI through government-regulated fair price shops (ration shops) at subsidized rates (Issue Price) to poor families.

• Ration Cards: Antyodaya cards (poorest of poor), BPL cards (below poverty line), and APL cards (others).

3. Role of Cooperatives & NGO Interventions

Short Note:

• Cooperatives: Play a vital role in food security in India, especially in the southern and western parts (e.g., Amul in Gujarat, Mother Dairy in Delhi, Academy of Development Science in Maharashtra).

• They set up fair price shops to sell goods at controlled prices and set up grain banks in rural areas to prevent food shortages.

Intext & Conceptual Questions

Q1: What are the three main dimensions of Food Security?
1. Availability of Food: Total food production, net imports, and stock stored in government granaries.
2. Accessibility of Food: Food should be within reach of every individual.
3. Affordability of Food: Individuals should have sufficient economic capacity to purchase nutritious food.
Q2: Differentiate between Chronic Hunger and Seasonal Hunger.
Chronic Hunger Seasonal Hunger
Caused by persistently inadequate diets in terms of quantity or quality. Caused by seasonal cycles of crop cultivation and harvest.
Prevalent among extremely poor people who lack purchasing power continuously. Prevalent among rural agricultural labor during off-seasons or urban casual labor during rainy seasons.

Textbook Chapter End Exercise Questions

Q1: How is food security ensured in India?
Answer: India ensures food security through a multi-tiered mechanism:
1. Buffer Stock Creation: The Food Corporation of India (FCI) purchases wheat and rice from farmers at Minimum Support Price (MSP) to maintain reserves.
2. Public Distribution System (PDS): Distributes food grains through a nationwide network of fair price shops at subsidized issue prices.
3. Targeted Welfare Schemes: Programs like Integrated Child Development Services (ICDS), Mid-Day Meal scheme, and Antyodaya Anna Yojana (AAY).
Q2: What is Minimum Support Price (MSP) and Issue Price?
Answer:
• Minimum Support Price (MSP): Pre-announced price paid by the government/FCI to farmers for purchasing their crops prior to the sowing season to ensure fair returns and encourage production.
• Issue Price: The lower, subsidized price at which the government distributes buffer stock food grains to the poorer sections of society through ration shops.
Q3: Write a short note on the role of Cooperatives in providing food security.
Answer:
1. Fair Price Outlets: In states like Tamil Nadu, nearly 94% of fair price shops are run by cooperatives.
2. Milk & Dairy Supply: Amul in Gujarat transformed dairy cooperative networks, ensuring nationwide milk availability; Mother Dairy in Delhi provides milk and vegetables at controlled rates.
3. Grain Banks: Organizations like Academy of Development Science (ADS) in Maharashtra facilitate NGOs to set up Grain Banks in different regions to prevent starvation during lean months.

Ch-7: How do Organisms Reproduce? 30 Questions

1. Which molecule in the cell nucleus carries the genetic information for inheritance of features from parents to next generation?

  • A. RNA
  • B. DNA
  • C. Protein
  • D. ATP

Answer: B. DNA

Explanation: DNA (Deoxyribonucleic Acid) contains the master blueprint for cellular structure, function, and hereditary traits.

2. Asexual reproduction in Amoeba takes place by:

  • A. Multiple fission
  • B. Binary fission
  • C. Budding
  • D. Fragmentation

Answer: B. Binary fission

Explanation: Amoeba splits into two equal daughter cells in any plane through binary fission.

3. Plasmodium (malarial parasite) reproduces inside host red blood cells by:

  • A. Binary fission
  • B. Multiple fission
  • C. Budding
  • D. Regeneration

Answer: B. Multiple fission

Explanation: Plasmodium divides into many daughter cells simultaneously within a protective cyst during multiple fission.

4. Yeast reproduces asexually through the process of:

  • A. Budding
  • B. Fragmentation
  • C. Spore formation
  • D. Regeneration

Answer: A. Budding

Explanation: Small outgrowths (buds) form on the parent yeast cell, detach upon maturity, and form new independent cells.

5. Which multicellular organism reproduces by fragmentation when mature?

  • A. Amoeba
  • B. Spirogyra
  • C. Hydra
  • D. Planaria

Answer: B. Spirogyra

Explanation: Filamentous green alga Spirogyra simply breaks up into smaller pieces (fragments) upon maturation, each growing into a new individual.

6. The ability of an organism to regrow its entire body from cut body parts is known as:

  • A. Fragmentation
  • B. Regeneration
  • C. Vegetative propagation
  • D. Budding

Answer: B. Regeneration

Explanation: Simple animals like Planaria and Hydra possess specialized cells capable of proliferating to regenerate complete organisms from body fragments.

7. Rhizopus (bread mould) reproduces asexually by forming structures called:

  • A. Buds
  • B. Spores in Sporangia
  • C. Seeds
  • D. Cysts

Answer: B. Spores in Sporangia

Explanation: Thread-like hyphae develop knob-like sporangia containing thick-walled spores that disperse to grow into new fungus plants.

8. Bryophyllum plants propagate vegetatively using buds produced on their:

  • A. Roots
  • B. Stem
  • C. Leaf margins (notches)
  • D. Flowers

Answer: C. Leaf margins (notches)

Explanation: Adventitious buds develop in the notches along Bryophyllum leaf margins and drop into moist soil to form new plants.

9. Which of the following is an artificial method of vegetative propagation?

  • A. Cutting
  • B. Layering
  • C. Grafting
  • D. All of the above

Answer: D. All of the above

Explanation: Cutting, layering, and grafting are horticultural techniques used to multiply plants like rose, sugarcane, and mango.

10. The male reproductive organ in a flower is called:

  • A. Sepal
  • B. Petal
  • C. Stamen
  • D. Carpel (Pistil)

Answer: C. Stamen

Explanation: The stamen consists of anther and filament, producing male gametes inside pollen grains.

11. Female reproductive unit located in the center of a flower is the:

  • A. Stamen
  • B. Carpel (Pistil)
  • C. Anther
  • D. Filament

Answer: B. Carpel (Pistil)

Explanation: The carpel/pistil consists of three parts: sticky stigma, slender style, and swollen basal ovary.

12. Transfer of pollen grains from the anther to the stigma of a flower is known as:

  • A. Fertilization
  • B. Pollination
  • C. Germination
  • D. Transpiration

Answer: B. Pollination

Explanation: Pollination can be self-pollination (same flower/plant) or cross-pollination (between different plants of same species).

13. After successful fertilization in flowering plants, the ovary develops into a:

  • A. Seed
  • B. Fruit
  • C. Embryo
  • D. Endosperm

Answer: B. Fruit

Explanation: Following fertilization, ovules turn into seeds while the surrounding ovary ripens to form a fruit.

14. Inside a seed, the part that gives rise to the future shoot is called:

  • A. Radicle
  • B. Plumule
  • C. Cotyledon
  • D. Seed coat

Answer: B. Plumule

Explanation: The embryo consists of plumule (future shoot), radicle (future root), and cotyledons (food store).

15. Human male germ cells (sperms) are produced in the:

  • A. Prostate gland
  • B. Testes
  • C. Vas deferens
  • D. Seminal vesicles

Answer: B. Testes

Explanation: Testes located outside abdominal cavity in scrotum produce sperms and male hormone testosterone.

16. Why are human testes located outside the abdominal cavity within the scrotum?

  • A. For better protection
  • B. Sperm formation requires a lower temperature than body core temperature
  • C. To save space in abdomen
  • D. To prevent hormone degradation

Answer: B. Sperm formation requires a lower temperature than body core temperature

Explanation: Spermatogenesis requires a temperature about 2-2.5°C lower than normal body temperature (37°C).

17. Which duct carries sperms from the testes toward the urethra?

  • A. Ureter
  • B. Vas deferens
  • C. Fallopian tube
  • D. Oviduct

Answer: B. Vas deferens

Explanation: Vas deferens connects testes to urethra, receiving secretions from seminal vesicles and prostate gland along its path.

18. Female germ cells (eggs or ova) are produced in human females by the:

  • A. Uterus
  • B. Ovaries
  • C. Oviducts
  • D. Cervix

Answer: B. Ovaries

Explanation: A pair of ovaries located in female lower abdomen produces mature ova and female hormones (estrogen & progesterone).

19. In the human female reproductive system, fertilization of egg by sperm takes place in the:

  • A. Uterus
  • B. Ovary
  • C. Fallopian tube (Oviduct)
  • D. Vagina

Answer: C. Fallopian tube (Oviduct)

Explanation: Fertilization occurs in the fallopian tube, after which the fertilized zygote travels down to implant into uterus.

20. Special disc-like tissue embedded in uterine wall through which embryo receives nutrition and oxygen from mother's blood is:

  • A. Amnion
  • B. Placenta
  • C. Umbilical cord
  • D. Corpus luteum

Answer: B. Placenta

Explanation: Placenta contains villi providing huge surface area for exchange of glucose, oxygen, and waste products between mother and fetus.

21. Gestation period (time from fertilization to childbirth) in human females is approximately:

  • A. 200 days
  • B. 280 days (approx. 9 months)
  • C. 365 days
  • D. 150 days

Answer: B. 280 days (approx. 9 months)

Explanation: Human embryonic development in uterus lasts approximately 9 months (38-40 weeks / 280 days).

22. What happens when an unfertilized egg is released by female ovary during monthly cycle?

  • A. It implants in uterus wall
  • B. Thick uterine lining breaks down leading to menstruation
  • C. It forms a cyst
  • D. It instantly turns into twin embryos

Answer: B. Thick uterine lining breaks down leading to menstruation

Explanation: Without fertilization, spongy uterine lining sheds along with blood and mucus through vagina as menstruation (lasts 2-8 days).

23. Which of the following is a bacterial Sexually Transmitted Disease (STD)?

  • A. Gonorrhoea and Syphilis
  • B. AIDS and Warts
  • C. Malaria and Typhoid
  • D. Hepatitis B and Flu

Answer: A. Gonorrhoea and Syphilis

Explanation: Gonorrhoea and Syphilis are caused by bacterial infections, whereas AIDS and genital warts are viral STDs.

24. AIDS (Acquired Immuno Deficiency Syndrome) is caused by which viral agent?

  • A. HPV
  • B. HIV
  • C. HBV
  • D. Influenza virus

Answer: B. HIV

Explanation: HIV (Human Immunodeficiency Virus) attacks white blood cells, severely compromising human immune response.

25. Mechanical barrier method that prevents pregnancy and protects against STDs during intercourse is:

  • A. Copper-T
  • B. Condom
  • C. Oral contraceptive pills
  • D. Tubectomy

Answer: B. Condom

Explanation: Condoms act as physical barriers blocking sperm entry into female tract, simultaneously preventing transmission of STDs.

26. Intrauterine contraceptive device (IUCD) placed inside female uterus to prevent pregnancy is:

  • A. Condom
  • B. Copper-T
  • C. Diaphragm
  • D. Oral pill

Answer: B. Copper-T

Explanation: Copper-T release copper ions which suppress sperm motility and fertilization capacity inside uterus.

27. Surgical contraception method in human males involving cutting and ligating vas deferens is called:

  • A. Tubectomy
  • B. Vasectomy
  • C. Hysterectomy
  • D. MTP

Answer: B. Vasectomy

Explanation: In vasectomy, a small section of vas deferens is blocked so that sperms cannot travel into semen.

28. Surgical contraception method in human females involving blocking fallopian tubes is called:

  • A. Vasectomy
  • B. Tubectomy
  • C. Dialysis
  • D. Ovariotomy

Answer: B. Tubectomy

Explanation: Tubectomy blocks fallopian tubes so egg and sperm cannot meet for fertilization.

29. Oral contraceptive pills prevent pregnancy primarily by:

  • A. Killing sperms
  • B. Altering hormonal balance to prevent ovulation
  • C. Blocking fallopian tubes physically
  • D. Destroying fertilized zygote

Answer: B. Altering hormonal balance to prevent ovulation

Explanation: Oral pills contain synthetic estrogen/progesterone hormones that inhibit egg release (ovulation) from ovaries.

30. Flowers containing either stamen or carpel (e.g. papaya, watermelon) are termed as:

  • A. Bisexual flowers
  • B. Unisexual flowers
  • C. Asexual flowers
  • D. Complete flowers

Answer: B. Unisexual flowers

Explanation: Unisexual flowers possess only male (stamen) or female (carpel) reproductive structures, requiring cross-pollination.

Chapter 10: Nationalism in India 30 Questions

1. When did the Indian National Congress (INC) establish its first session?

  • A. 1857
  • B. 1885
  • C. 1905
  • D. 1919

Answer: B. 1885

Explanation: The Indian National Congress was founded in 1885 by Allan Octavian Hume to serve as a platform for Indian political leaders.

2. Which movement was started by Mahatma Gandhi in 1920 to oppose the Rowlatt Act?

  • A. Non-Cooperation Movement
  • B. Quit India Movement
  • C. Civil Disobedience Movement
  • D. Dandi March

Answer: A. Non-Cooperation Movement

Explanation: Gandhi launched the Non-Cooperation Movement in 1920 as a peaceful protest against the unjust Rowlatt Act and British rule.

3. What was the main cause of the Jallianwala Bagh massacre in 1919?

  • A. A peaceful protest against the Rowlatt Act
  • B. A religious gathering
  • C. A football match
  • D. A military exercise

Answer: A. A peaceful protest against the Rowlatt Act

Explanation: A peaceful gathering in Amritsar protesting the Rowlatt Acts was fired upon by British troops, killing hundreds of Indians.

4. Who was the British general responsible for the Jallianwala Bagh massacre?

  • A. Lord Curzon
  • B. General Dyer
  • C. Lord Irwin
  • D. Winston Churchill

Answer: B. General Dyer

Explanation: General Reginald Dyer ordered his troops to open fire on the unarmed crowd at Jallianwala Bagh on 13 April 1919.

5. Which event marked the beginning of the Civil Disobedience Movement?

  • A. The Salt March
  • B. The Quit India Resolution
  • C. The Chauri Chaura incident
  • D. The Poona Pact

Answer: A. The Salt March

Explanation: Gandhi's famous Dandi March (Salt March) in 1930 marked the inauguration of the Civil Disobedience Movement.

6. Why did Gandhi call off the Non-Cooperation Movement in 1922?

  • A. He was arrested
  • B. The Chauri Chaura incident
  • C. He got a deal from the British
  • D. The movement failed

Answer: B. The Chauri Chaura incident

Explanation: Gandhi withdrew the movement after a violent clash at Chauri Chaura, where a police station was burnt, killing policemen.

7. The 'Poona Pact' of 1932 was signed between whom?

  • A. Gandhi and Ambedkar
  • B. Nehru and Jinnah
  • C. Gandhi and Irwin
  • D. Patel and Wavell

Answer: A. Gandhi and Ambedkar

Explanation: The Poona Pact was signed between Mahatma Gandhi and B.R. Ambedkar to reserve seats for depressed classes in the legislatures.

8. The 'Round Table Conferences' were held in which country?

  • A. India
  • B. USA
  • C. London
  • D. France

Answer: C. London

Explanation: The Round Table Conferences were held in London between 1930 and 1932 to discuss constitutional reforms in India.

9. What was the main objective of the Quit India Movement of 1942?

  • A. To negotiate with the British
  • B. To demand complete independence
  • C. To ask for provincial autonomy
  • D. To stop the World War II

Answer: B. To demand complete independence

Explanation: The Quit India Movement, launched by Gandhi in August 1942, was a mass protest demanding an immediate end to British rule.

10. The Rowlatt Act of 1919 allowed the British government to do what?

  • A. Arrest anyone without trial
  • B. Increase taxes
  • C. Ban newspapers
  • D. Stop imports

Answer: A. Arrest anyone without trial

Explanation: The Rowlatt Act allowed the British to imprison individuals for up to two years without trial, sparking mass Indian outrage.

11. Which of the following was a technique used by Gandhi to fight the British?

  • A. Violent uprisings
  • B. Non-violent non-cooperation
  • C. Armed rebellion
  • D. Burning British ships

Answer: B. Non-violent non-cooperation

Explanation: Gandhi consistently preached and practiced 'Satyagraha', a policy of non-violent, passive resistance against the colonial rulers.

12. The Dandi March was undertaken to break which British law?

  • A. The Salt Tax
  • B. The Press Act
  • C. The Arms Act
  • D. The Forest Act

Answer: A. The Salt Tax

Explanation: The Dandi March in 1930 was a direct challenge to the British monopoly on salt production and the harsh salt tax.

13. What was the immediate result of the Swadeshi Movement?

  • A. Ban on foreign goods
  • B. Growth of Indian industries
  • C. Partition of Bengal
  • D. Merger of Princely states

Answer: B. Growth of Indian industries

Explanation: The Swadeshi Movement promoted the use of Indian-made goods, significantly boosting the growth of indigenous industries and textiles.

14. The 'Partition of Bengal' in 1905 was annulled in which year?

  • A. 1906
  • B. 1911
  • C. 1920
  • D. 1925

Answer: B. 1911

Explanation: Lord Curzon partitioned Bengal in 1905. Due to massive nationalist protests, the British annulled the partition in 1911.

15. Who was the Viceroy of India during the Quit India Movement?

  • A. Lord Linlithgow
  • B. Lord Wavell
  • C. Lord Mountbatten
  • D. Lord Reading

Answer: A. Lord Linlithgow

Explanation: Lord Linlithgow was the Viceroy of India during 1936-1943 and led the British response to the Quit India Movement.

16. The Gandhi-Irwin Pact (1931) resulted in the release of whom?

  • A. Indian soldiers
  • B. Political prisoners
  • C. British prisoners
  • D. Farmers

Answer: B. Political prisoners

Explanation: The Gandhi-Irwin Pact in 1931 promised the release of all political prisoners arrested during the Civil Disobedience Movement.

17. Which event prompted the British to appoint the Simon Commission?

  • A. The 1919 Montague-Chelmsford Reforms
  • B. The non-cooperation movement
  • C. The Jallianwala Bagh massacre
  • D. The Dandi March

Answer: A. The 1919 Montague-Chelmsford Reforms

Explanation: The Simon Commission was appointed in 1928 to review the Montague-Chelmsford Reforms, but it had no Indian members, sparking outrage.

18. Why did the Indian nationalists boycott the Simon Commission?

  • A. It was headed by an Indian
  • B. It had no Indian representatives
  • C. It was too costly
  • D. It was banned

Answer: B. It had no Indian representatives

Explanation: The Simon Commission was an all-white British delegation with no Indian members, leading to the famous slogan "Simon Go Back".

19. What was the 'Chauri Chaura Incident' about?

  • A. A peaceful protest
  • B. Burning of a police station
  • C. A successful rally
  • D. A meeting with Gandhi

Answer: B. Burning of a police station

Explanation: In 1922, a violent mob at Chauri Chaura burned down a police station, killing 22 policemen, leading Gandhi to withdraw the Non-Cooperation movement.

20. Which of the following was a direct impact of the Second World War on India?

  • A. Food shortages and increased prices
  • B. Industrial boom
  • C. Peace and prosperity
  • D. Decrease in British control

Answer: A. Food shortages and increased prices

Explanation: WWII caused severe economic distress in India due to the diversion of resources, leading to shortages and the Quit India Movement.

21. Who coined the slogan 'Swaraj is my birthright'?

  • A. Mahatma Gandhi
  • B. Bal Gangadhar Tilak
  • C. Jawaharlal Nehru
  • D. Subhas Chandra Bose

Answer: B. Bal Gangadhar Tilak

Explanation: Bal Gangadhar Tilak, a radical leader, famously declared, "Swaraj is my birthright and I shall have it".

22. Who led the 'Indian National Army' (INA) during World War II?

  • A. Bhagat Singh
  • B. Subhas Chandra Bose
  • C. Jawaharlal Nehru
  • D. Sardar Patel

Answer: B. Subhas Chandra Bose

Explanation: Subhas Chandra Bose escaped house arrest and raised the INA in Southeast Asia to fight the British with Japanese support.

23. The 'Cabinet Mission Plan' was proposed in which year?

  • A. 1945
  • B. 1946
  • C. 1947
  • D. 1948

Answer: B. 1946

Explanation: The 1946 Cabinet Mission was sent by the British to India to discuss the transfer of power and the future constitution.

24. What was the 'Delhi Chalo' slogan associated with?

  • A. The Red Fort trial
  • B. The INA's march to Delhi
  • C. The Dandi March
  • D. The Jallianwala Bagh protest

Answer: B. The INA's march to Delhi

Explanation: 'Delhi Chalo' was the rallying cry of the Indian National Army (INA) under Subhas Chandra Bose to liberate Delhi from British control.

25. The 1942 Quit India resolution was passed at which INC session?

  • A. The Bombay session
  • B. The Calcutta session
  • C. The Lahore session
  • D. The Madras session

Answer: A. The Bombay session

Explanation: The Quit India resolution was passed at the Bombay session of the Indian National Congress in August 1942.

26. Gandhi's 'Satyagraha' is best translated as what?

  • A. War of aggression
  • B. Truth and non-violence
  • C. Rebellion
  • D. Peace treaty

Answer: B. Truth and non-violence

Explanation: Satyagraha literally means "holding firmly to truth" and was Gandhi's philosophy of non-violent civil resistance against injustice.

27. What was the main significance of the 'Nagpur Session' of the INC in 1920?

  • A. The split of the Congress
  • B. The approval of the Non-Cooperation Movement
  • C. The announcement of Quit India
  • D. The formation of the Muslim League

Answer: B. The approval of the Non-Cooperation Movement

Explanation: The 1920 Nagpur session officially approved the launch of the Non-Cooperation Movement as proposed by Mahatma Gandhi.

28. The Second World War ended in which year, paving the way for Indian independence?

  • A. 1943
  • B. 1944
  • C. 1945
  • D. 1946

Answer: C. 1945

Explanation: The end of WWII in 1945 left Britain economically exhausted, making it impossible for them to hold onto their Indian colony.

29. Who was the first Governor-General of independent India?

  • A. Lord Mountbatten
  • B. C. Rajagopalachari
  • C. Jawaharlal Nehru
  • D. Lord Wavell

Answer: A. Lord Mountbatten

Explanation: Lord Mountbatten was the last Viceroy of British India and served as the first Governor-General of independent India.

30. The prominent slogan 'Inquilab Zindabad' was raised by which revolutionary group?

  • A. The Ghadar Party
  • B. The Hindustan Socialist Republican Association
  • C. The Indian National Congress
  • D. The Muslim League

Answer: B. The Hindustan Socialist Republican Association

Explanation: The HSRA, led by revolutionaries like Bhagat Singh and Chandrasekhar Azad, popularized the slogan "Inquilab Zindabad" (Long Live the Revolution).

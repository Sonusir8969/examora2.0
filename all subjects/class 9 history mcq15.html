Chapter 15: The Making of the Modern World (Globalisation) 30 Questions

1. The modern global economy is often linked to the end of which event?

  • A. The French Revolution
  • B. The Cold War
  • C. World War I
  • D. The Great Depression

Answer: B. The Cold War

Explanation: The end of the Cold War in 1991 opened the door for a more integrated, US-led global capitalist system and rapid globalisation.

2. Where was the Bretton Woods Conference held in 1944?

  • A. New York, USA
  • B. London, UK
  • C. Bretton Woods, New Hampshire, USA
  • D. Paris, France

Answer: C. Bretton Woods, New Hampshire, USA

Explanation: The historic Bretton Woods Conference took place in New Hampshire, creating the framework for the post-war international monetary system.

3. Which two major financial institutions were established at the Bretton Woods Conference?

  • A. NATO and the UN
  • B. The World Bank and the IMF
  • C. The WTO and the ILO
  • D. The G8 and the G20

Answer: B. The World Bank and the IMF

Explanation: The International Monetary Fund (IMF) and the World Bank were created to stabilize global currencies and rebuild war-torn Europe.

4. What was the purpose of the IMF (International Monetary Fund)?

  • A. To provide long-term loans for infrastructure
  • B. To maintain international monetary stability and exchange rates
  • C. To give free money to all countries
  • D. To regulate global trade

Answer: B. To maintain international monetary stability and exchange rates

Explanation: The IMF was created to oversee the global financial system, preventing currency crises and promoting cooperation.

5. What was the 'Gold Standard' that was replaced by the Bretton Woods system?

  • A. A system where currencies were backed by gold
  • B. A system where everyone used gold coins
  • C. A gold mining agreement
  • D. A trade embargo

Answer: A. A system where currencies were backed by gold

Explanation: Under the Bretton Woods system, the US dollar was pegged to gold, and other currencies were pegged to the dollar.

6. The World Bank was primarily set up to do what?

  • A. Promote military alliances
  • B. Provide loans for reconstruction and development
  • C. Fund space exploration
  • D. Settle political disputes

Answer: B. Provide loans for reconstruction and development

Explanation: The World Bank (originally IBRD) was designed to finance the reconstruction of Europe after WWII and later support developing nations.

7. In which year was the World Trade Organization (WTO) established?

  • A. 1947
  • B. 1991
  • C. 1995
  • D. 2000

Answer: C. 1995

Explanation: The WTO was established in 1995, replacing GATT (General Agreement on Tariffs and Trade), to oversee international trade rules.

8. What is the primary role of the WTO today?

  • A. To wage wars
  • B. To promote free trade and settle trade disputes
  • C. To protect local markets from imports
  • D. To impose heavy taxes on trade

Answer: B. To promote free trade and settle trade disputes

Explanation: The WTO provides a framework for global trade agreements and acts as an arbiter for international trade conflicts.

9. Globalisation can be best defined as what?

  • A. Total isolation of countries
  • B. Increasing economic, cultural, and political integration across nations
  • C. Building more walls between countries
  • D. Discouraging technological sharing

Answer: B. Increasing economic, cultural, and political integration across nations

Explanation: Globalisation is the complex process of interconnectedness, driven by the flow of goods, capital, ideas, and people across borders.

10. The rapid advancement in which technology was the primary driver of modern globalisation?

  • A. Steam engines
  • B. Information Technology and the Internet
  • C. Nuclear power
  • D. Automobiles

Answer: B. Information Technology and the Internet

Explanation: The IT revolution and the internet have drastically shrunk the world, enabling instantaneous communication, finance, and data transfer globally.

11. What is a 'Multinational Corporation' (MNC)?

  • A. A company based in one country
  • B. A company that operates in multiple countries
  • C. A government agency
  • D. A local small business

Answer: B. A company that operates in multiple countries

Explanation: MNCs are large corporations that have headquarters in one country but factories, offices, and markets across the globe.

12. The term 'Outsourcing' refers to what economic practice?

  • A. Hiring internal workers
  • B. Contracting work to foreign companies
  • C. Buying local products
  • D. Reducing production costs in a single country

Answer: B. Contracting work to foreign companies

Explanation: Outsourcing involves MNCs hiring companies in lower-wage countries (like India or China) to perform tasks like manufacturing and call centers.

13. How has globalisation impacted cultural identities?

  • A. It has completely destroyed all local cultures
  • B. It has led to cultural homogenisation and the spread of global pop culture
  • C. It has had no impact
  • D. It has caused countries to isolate again

Answer: B. It has led to cultural homogenisation and the spread of global pop culture

Explanation: Globalisation promotes the exchange of cultural values, but it often leads to the dominance of Western culture and the decline of local traditions.

14. The 'Green Revolution' was a movement to increase agricultural production using what?

  • A. Advanced biotech seeds and fertilizers
  • B. Organic farming only
  • C. Ancient farming techniques
  • D. Importing food from abroad

Answer: A. Advanced biotech seeds and fertilizers

Explanation: The Green Revolution in the 1960s introduced high-yield, disease-resistant seeds and chemical fertilizers to feed the exploding global population.

15. How has globalisation impacted the environment?

  • A. It has improved environmental standards everywhere
  • B. It has led to increased industrial pollution and global warming
  • C. It has stopped all pollution
  • D. It has reversed climate change

Answer: B. It has led to increased industrial pollution and global warming

Explanation: The massive increase in global manufacturing, shipping, and consumption has resulted in severe carbon emissions and environmental degradation.

16. The North American Free Trade Agreement (NAFTA) was a trade pact between which countries?

  • A. USA, Mexico, Canada
  • B. USA, UK, Canada
  • C. USA, France, Mexico
  • D. Mexico, Brazil, Argentina

Answer: A. USA, Mexico, Canada

Explanation: NAFTA eliminated tariff barriers between the three North American nations, creating one of the world's largest free trade zones.

17. The European Union (EU) was formed to promote economic and political integration across which continent?

  • A. Asia
  • B. Africa
  • C. Europe
  • D. North America

Answer: C. Europe

Explanation: The EU evolved from the European Economic Community (EEC) and now embodies economic and political cooperation between European states.

18. A major criticism of globalisation is that it increases what?

  • A. National sovereignty
  • B. Economic inequality between rich and poor nations
  • C. Local employment in the West
  • D. Environmental safety

Answer: B. Economic inequality between rich and poor nations

Explanation: Critics argue that while globalisation enriches multinational corporations, it widens the gap between wealthy nations and developing countries.

19. The internet and global communication networks have facilitated the growth of what global job sector?

  • A. Agriculture
  • B. Remote Work and the Digital Economy
  • C. Fishery
  • D. Animal husbandry

Answer: B. Remote Work and the Digital Economy

Explanation: The internet allows people to work for companies based in other continents without migrating, fueling the digital and gig economy.

20. When did the Bretton Woods system finally collapse?

  • A. 1960
  • B. 1971
  • C. 1980
  • D. 1991

Answer: B. 1971

Explanation: The Bretton Woods system collapsed in 1971 when US President Nixon took the US dollar off the gold standard, leading to floating exchange rates.

21. What is the 'GATT' that preceded the WTO?

  • A. General Agreement on Tariffs and Trade
  • B. Global Agreement on Technology
  • C. Great Allies of Trade
  • D. Government Authority for Tax

Answer: A. General Agreement on Tariffs and Trade

Explanation: GATT was established in 1947 to reduce tariffs and trade barriers among members, paving the way for the WTO.

22. Globalisation has led to a drastic reduction in what phenomenon in modern society?

  • A. Internet usage
  • B. International travel restrictions and costs
  • C. Trade agreements
  • D. Migrant workers

Answer: B. International travel restrictions and costs

Explanation: The advent of budget airlines, fast railways, and global transport networks has made crossing borders cheaper and faster than ever before.

23. What role do 'Free Trade Agreements' play in the global economy?

  • A. They ban all imports
  • B. They reduce tariffs and promote cross-border commerce
  • C. They restrict foreign investments
  • D. They increase state control

Answer: B. They reduce tariffs and promote cross-border commerce

Explanation: Free Trade Agreements (FTAs) are signed between countries to lower taxes on imported goods, boosting international trade volumes.

24. Which country became the "World's Factory" in the era of modern globalisation?

  • A. USA
  • B. India
  • C. China
  • D. Germany

Answer: C. China

Explanation: China leveraged its massive labor force and low costs to become the global manufacturing hub, exporting goods to every continent.

25. Globalisation often involves the spread of what international financial practice?

  • A. Barter systems
  • B. Foreign Direct Investment (FDI)
  • C. Gold trading only
  • D. Local coinage

Answer: B. Foreign Direct Investment (FDI)

Explanation: FDI is the practice where MNCs and foreign governments invest heavily in the infrastructure, factories, and businesses of other nations.

26. What is one major socio-economic challenge created by globalisation?

  • A. Complete equal wealth
  • B. The 'Brain Drain' from developing countries
  • C. A universal world language
  • D. Easy access to healthcare

Answer: B. The 'Brain Drain' from developing countries

Explanation: Brain drain occurs when highly skilled workers from poorer nations migrate to richer nations, depriving their home countries of talent.

27. The General Agreement on Tariffs and Trade (GATT) was signed by how many initial nations in 1947?

  • A. 23
  • B. 50
  • C. 100
  • D. 10

Answer: A. 23

Explanation: GATT started with just 23 signatory nations, but it laid the groundwork for the massive global trade network that exists today.

28. What has been a major consequence of globalisation for consumer goods?

  • A. Goods are exclusively expensive
  • B. A massive abundance of cheap and diverse products
  • C. Complete ban on imports
  • D. Shortage of products in markets

Answer: B. A massive abundance of cheap and diverse products

Explanation: Global supply chains allow consumers worldwide to access a huge variety of affordable goods manufactured in different countries.

29. The collapse of the Bretton Woods system led to what new economic era?

  • A. Era of fixed gold exchange
  • B. Era of Floating Exchange Rates
  • C. Era of Silver trade
  • D. Era of barter trade

Answer: B. Era of Floating Exchange Rates

Explanation: After 1971, global currencies began to float freely against one another, with their values determined by open market trading.

30. The term 'Viral' in the modern world is an example of what kind of global cultural exchange?

  • A. The fast spread of internet memes and media globally
  • B. A virus disease
  • C. A new currency
  • D. A type of trade agreement

Answer: A. The fast spread of internet memes and media globally

Explanation: The internet allows culturally shared content (like videos, trends, and memes) to go "viral" and reach billions of people within hours.

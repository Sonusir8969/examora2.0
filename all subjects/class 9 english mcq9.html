Chapter 9: If I Were You 30 Questions

1. "If I Were You" is written in which literary form?

  • A. A one-act play
  • B. A poem
  • C. A biography
  • D. A travelogue

Answer: A. A one-act play

Explanation: "If I Were You" is a one-act play written by Douglas James, involving suspense and a dramatic confrontation.

2. Who is Gerrard, the main character in the play?

  • A. A film writer relaxing at home
  • B. A police inspector
  • C. A gangster on the run
  • D. A newspaper reporter

Answer: A. A film writer relaxing at home

Explanation: Gerrard is a film story writer who is quietly relaxing at home when the events of the play unfold.

3. Who intrudes into Gerrard's house at the beginning of the play?

  • A. A dangerous escaped criminal
  • B. A police officer
  • C. Gerrard's old friend
  • D. A delivery person

Answer: A. A dangerous escaped criminal

Explanation: A dangerous escaped criminal breaks into Gerrard's house, threatening him at gunpoint and setting up the play's central conflict.

4. Why does the criminal intrude into Gerrard's house?

  • A. He is a fugitive hiding from the police after committing a crime
  • B. He wants to rob the house of valuables
  • C. He is lost and seeking directions
  • D. He is Gerrard's relative visiting unexpectedly

Answer: A. He is a fugitive hiding from the police after committing a crime

Explanation: The intruder is an escaped convict on the run from the police, seeking refuge and a way to escape capture.

5. What does the criminal demand from Gerrard once he enters the house?

  • A. Gerrard's identity, clothes, and belongings so he can assume his identity and escape
  • B. Only some food and water
  • C. Directions to the nearest town
  • D. Nothing; he just wanted shelter for the night

Answer: A. Gerrard's identity, clothes, and belongings so he can assume his identity and escape

Explanation: The criminal forces Gerrard to exchange identities with him, taking his clothes and belongings in order to escape disguised as Gerrard.

6. How does Gerrard manage to outwit the criminal in the play?

  • A. Through his intelligence and quick thinking, he devises a plan to reverse the situation
  • B. He physically overpowers the criminal in a fight
  • C. He calls for help immediately, and the police arrive to save him
  • D. He does not manage to outwit the criminal at all

Answer: A. Through his intelligence and quick thinking, he devises a plan to reverse the situation

Explanation: Gerrard uses his sharp wit and presence of mind to cleverly turn the tables on the criminal, ultimately outsmarting him.

7. What clever trick does Gerrard use involving the exchange of identities?

  • A. He convinces the criminal to take on his identity fully, which later leads to the criminal's capture instead of him
  • B. He switches his gun for a fake one
  • C. He disguises himself as a police officer
  • D. He drugs the criminal's drink

Answer: A. He convinces the criminal to take on his identity fully, which later leads to the criminal's capture instead of him

Explanation: Gerrard manipulates the situation so that the criminal, having taken on his identity, ends up walking straight into police custody instead of Gerrard.

8. What is the setting of the play "If I Were You"?

  • A. Gerrard's house
  • B. A police station
  • C. A forest
  • D. A film studio

Answer: A. Gerrard's house

Explanation: The entire play takes place within the confines of Gerrard's own house, where the tense confrontation unfolds.

9. What weapon does the criminal use to threaten Gerrard throughout much of the play?

  • A. A gun
  • B. A knife
  • C. A rope
  • D. A poisoned drink

Answer: A. A gun

Explanation: The escaped criminal holds Gerrard at gunpoint for much of the play, using the weapon to force compliance.

10. Why does the title "If I Were You" hold particular significance to the play's plot?

  • A. It reflects the central theme of identity exchange between Gerrard and the criminal
  • B. It refers to a game the characters play
  • C. It has no connection to the plot
  • D. It refers to a film script Gerrard is writing

Answer: A. It reflects the central theme of identity exchange between Gerrard and the criminal

Explanation: The title directly relates to the theme of the criminal literally trying to become Gerrard, taking on his identity as part of his escape plan.

11. What profession does Gerrard have, which becomes relevant to how he handles the crisis?

  • A. A writer of film scripts, which gives him a creative, quick-thinking mindset
  • B. A lawyer skilled in negotiation
  • C. A retired police officer
  • D. A locksmith with technical skills

Answer: A. A writer of film scripts, which gives him a creative, quick-thinking mindset

Explanation: As a film script writer, Gerrard possesses a creative and strategic mind, skills that prove crucial in devising his clever counter-plan.

12. What emotion does Gerrard primarily display when the criminal first threatens him?

  • A. Initial fear, followed by calm and calculated composure
  • B. Complete indifference throughout
  • C. Immediate anger and aggression
  • D. Total panic with no ability to think clearly

Answer: A. Initial fear, followed by calm and calculated composure

Explanation: Though understandably frightened at first, Gerrard quickly composes himself and begins thinking strategically to handle the dangerous situation.

13. How does Gerrard eventually persuade the criminal to leave in his identity, believing it a safe escape plan?

  • A. He convinces the criminal that assuming his identity, including going to the police station under a false pretext, would help him escape safely
  • B. He simply orders the criminal out of the house
  • C. He offers the criminal a large sum of money
  • D. He calls the police directly in front of the criminal

Answer: A. He convinces the criminal that assuming his identity, including going to the police station under a false pretext, would help him escape safely

Explanation: Gerrard cleverly persuades the criminal that fully adopting his identity, including a visit to the police under a plausible pretext, is his safest route to escape, unaware it will lead to his capture.

14. What happens to the criminal at the end of the play?

  • A. He is caught by the police, believing he was Gerrard the entire time due to the identity switch
  • B. He successfully escapes without being caught
  • C. He is killed in a shootout
  • D. He turns himself in voluntarily out of guilt

Answer: A. He is caught by the police, believing he was Gerrard the entire time due to the identity switch

Explanation: The play's twist ending sees the criminal captured by police, ironically caught while impersonating Gerrard due to the clever identity swap orchestrated by Gerrard.

15. What does Gerrard's successful escape from danger illustrate about human resourcefulness?

  • A. Intelligence and quick thinking can help overcome life-threatening situations
  • B. Physical strength is always necessary to survive danger
  • C. Escaping danger requires external help alone
  • D. It is impossible to outsmart a dangerous criminal

Answer: A. Intelligence and quick thinking can help overcome life-threatening situations

Explanation: The play demonstrates how mental sharpness and clever strategy, rather than physical confrontation, can be the key to overcoming danger.

16. What overall genre does the play "If I Were You" belong to?

  • A. Suspense/thriller drama
  • B. Romantic comedy
  • C. Historical drama
  • D. Musical

Answer: A. Suspense/thriller drama

Explanation: With its tense confrontation, danger, and a clever twist ending, the play falls under the suspense or thriller drama genre.

17. Why does the criminal initially believe that exchanging identities with Gerrard is a good plan for him?

  • A. He believes it will help him avoid police detection since Gerrard is not wanted by the law
  • B. He believes Gerrard is also a criminal like himself
  • C. He simply wants to steal Gerrard's money
  • D. He has no actual reason and acts randomly

Answer: A. He believes it will help him avoid police detection since Gerrard is not wanted by the law

Explanation: The criminal's plan hinges on the idea that taking Gerrard's identity, an innocent and unsuspected person, will allow him to move freely without arousing police suspicion.

18. What does Gerrard's calm demeanour under extreme threat suggest about his character?

  • A. He possesses courage, mental resilience, and the ability to think clearly under pressure
  • B. He is reckless and unaware of the danger he is in
  • C. He is emotionally detached and uncaring about his own safety
  • D. He panics uncontrollably throughout the play

Answer: A. He possesses courage, mental resilience, and the ability to think clearly under pressure

Explanation: Despite being in a genuinely life-threatening situation, Gerrard's composure demonstrates strong mental resilience and courage.

19. How does the play build tension for the audience throughout its progression?

  • A. Through the constant threat of violence and uncertainty about how Gerrard will escape the situation
  • B. Through slow, uneventful dialogue with no conflict
  • C. Through comedic relief that removes all tension
  • D. Through multiple unrelated subplots

Answer: A. Through the constant threat of violence and uncertainty about how Gerrard will escape the situation

Explanation: The play maintains suspense by keeping the audience uncertain about Gerrard's fate, given the constant threat posed by the armed criminal.

20. What broader message about intelligence versus brute force does the play convey?

  • A. Wit and strategic thinking can be more powerful than physical force or violence
  • B. Physical strength always triumphs over intelligence
  • C. Violence is the only effective solution to danger
  • D. Intelligence has no role in overcoming dangerous situations

Answer: A. Wit and strategic thinking can be more powerful than physical force or violence

Explanation: The play's resolution emphasises that clever, strategic thinking can triumph over brute force, as Gerrard outsmarts an armed and dangerous criminal.

21. What role does dramatic irony play in the ending of "If I Were You"?

  • A. The audience realises the criminal's plan will backfire before he does, heightening the suspense of the twist
  • B. There is no dramatic irony present in the play
  • C. The audience is as surprised as the criminal by the ending
  • D. Dramatic irony is used only in the play's opening scene

Answer: A. The audience realises the criminal's plan will backfire before he does, heightening the suspense of the twist

Explanation: As Gerrard's clever plan unfolds, the audience gradually understands the trap being set for the criminal, creating dramatic irony that builds toward the twist ending.

22. What does Gerrard's ability to remain calm and negotiate suggest about effective crisis management?

  • A. Staying composed and thinking strategically is crucial when facing a dangerous crisis
  • B. Panic is always the best response to danger
  • C. Crisis situations cannot be managed through calm thinking
  • D. Aggressive confrontation is always the best approach

Answer: A. Staying composed and thinking strategically is crucial when facing a dangerous crisis

Explanation: Gerrard's success in overcoming a life-threatening situation demonstrates the value of remaining calm and thinking strategically during a crisis.

23. Why might the play be considered effective in a limited setting, such as one house and few characters?

  • A. The confined setting intensifies the suspense and focuses attention on the psychological battle between the two characters
  • B. It makes the play boring due to lack of variety
  • C. It requires an elaborate set with many locations
  • D. The limited setting weakens the plot significantly

Answer: A. The confined setting intensifies the suspense and focuses attention on the psychological battle between the two characters

Explanation: The single-location setting heightens tension by focusing entirely on the tense psychological duel between Gerrard and the criminal, without distraction.

24. What can be inferred about the criminal's level of trust in Gerrard by the end of the play?

  • A. He ultimately trusted Gerrard's plan completely, which led to his downfall
  • B. He never trusted Gerrard at any point
  • C. He suspected Gerrard's plan and refused to follow it
  • D. He forced Gerrard to accompany him instead

Answer: A. He ultimately trusted Gerrard's plan completely, which led to his downfall

Explanation: The criminal's full trust in Gerrard's suggested plan of action ultimately proves to be his undoing, as it leads him straight into police custody.

25. How does the play's structure as a one-act drama affect its pacing and impact?

  • A. It creates a tightly paced, intense narrative without unnecessary digressions
  • B. It results in a slow-moving, drawn-out story
  • C. It requires multiple intermissions to be effective
  • D. It has no impact on pacing at all

Answer: A. It creates a tightly paced, intense narrative without unnecessary digressions

Explanation: As a one-act play, "If I Were You" maintains a compact, intense pace, keeping the audience engaged throughout the short but suspenseful narrative.

26. What does the play suggest about the risks a criminal takes when trying to assume someone else's identity?

  • A. Such attempts can backfire unexpectedly, especially if the original person is clever enough to manipulate the situation
  • B. Identity theft always succeeds without any risk
  • C. There are no risks involved in taking someone else's identity
  • D. The play does not address this theme at all

Answer: A. Such attempts can backfire unexpectedly, especially if the original person is clever enough to manipulate the situation

Explanation: The play cleverly shows how an attempt at identity theft can go disastrously wrong for the perpetrator when outsmarted by a sharp-witted victim.

27. What does Gerrard's successful trick reveal about the importance of staying observant during a crisis?

  • A. Being observant and understanding the criminal's mindset helped Gerrard devise an effective counter-strategy
  • B. Observation played no role in his eventual success
  • C. He succeeded purely by chance with no observation involved
  • D. He ignored the criminal's behaviour completely

Answer: A. Being observant and understanding the criminal's mindset helped Gerrard devise an effective counter-strategy

Explanation: Gerrard's ability to understand and predict the criminal's thinking allowed him to craft a plan that ultimately outwitted his dangerous captor.

28. What contrast is highlighted between Gerrard and the criminal throughout the play?

  • A. Gerrard's intelligence and composure versus the criminal's aggression and desperation
  • B. Both characters are shown as equally violent
  • C. Both characters share identical personalities
  • D. There is no notable contrast between the two characters

Answer: A. Gerrard's intelligence and composure versus the criminal's aggression and desperation

Explanation: The play sharply contrasts Gerrard's calm, clever approach with the criminal's aggressive and increasingly desperate behaviour throughout their encounter.

29. What does the play's resolution suggest about justice ultimately being served?

  • A. Justice prevails as the criminal is caught, despite his attempts to escape through deception
  • B. The criminal escapes justice entirely
  • C. Justice is left unresolved at the end of the play
  • D. Gerrard is wrongly arrested instead of the criminal

Answer: A. Justice prevails as the criminal is caught, despite his attempts to escape through deception

Explanation: The play concludes with a satisfying resolution where justice is served, as the criminal is ultimately captured by the police due to his own deception.

30. What is the central theme conveyed by the play "If I Were You"?

  • A. Presence of mind and clever thinking can overcome even the most dangerous and threatening situations
  • B. Violence is the only way to resolve conflict
  • C. Criminals always succeed in evading capture
  • D. Identity has no significance in real life

Answer: A. Presence of mind and clever thinking can overcome even the most dangerous and threatening situations

Explanation: The play's central message emphasises that intelligence, calmness, and quick thinking can triumph over danger, even when facing a life-threatening confrontation.

Ch-12: Federalism 30 Questions

1. What system of government divides power between a central authority and various constituent units of the country?

  • A. Federalism
  • B. Unitary System
  • C. Monarchy
  • D. Dictatorship

Answer: A. Federalism

Explanation: Federalism is a system of government in which power is divided between a central authority and constituent political units.

2. Which of the following countries is an example of a 'Coming Together' federation?

  • A. USA
  • B. India
  • C. Spain
  • D. Belgium

Answer: A. USA

Explanation: USA, Switzerland, and Australia are classic examples of 'Coming Together' federations formed by independent states pooling sovereignty.

3. India, Spain, and Belgium are prime examples of which type of federation?

  • A. Holding Together Federation
  • B. Coming Together Federation
  • C. Confederation
  • D. Unitary Federation

Answer: A. Holding Together Federation

Explanation: In 'Holding Together' federations, a large country decides to divide its power between constituent states and the national government.

4. Subjects of national importance such as Defence, Foreign Affairs, Banking, and Currency fall under which list?

  • A. Union List
  • B. State List
  • C. Concurrent List
  • D. Residuary List

Answer: A. Union List

Explanation: The Union List includes subjects of national importance. The Union Government alone can make laws on these subjects.

5. Police, Trade, Commerce, Agriculture, and Irrigation are subjects included in which list?

  • A. State List
  • B. Union List
  • C. Concurrent List
  • D. Central List

Answer: A. State List

Explanation: State List contains subjects of state and local importance. State Governments alone can pass laws on them.

6. Education, Forests, Trade Unions, Marriage, Adoption, and Succession are included in which list?

  • A. Concurrent List
  • B. Union List
  • C. State List
  • D. Residuary List

Answer: A. Concurrent List

Explanation: Concurrent List includes subjects of common interest to both Central and State Governments. Both can legislate on them.

7. If there is a conflict between central laws and state laws on a subject in the Concurrent List, whose law prevails?

  • A. Union (Central) Law
  • B. State Law
  • C. The law made first
  • D. Both laws get cancelled

Answer: A. Union (Central) Law

Explanation: In case of a conflict over a Concurrent List topic, the law made by the Union Government prevails.

8. Subjects that came up after the Constitution was written, like Computer Software, fall under which category?

  • A. Residuary Subjects
  • B. Concurrent List
  • C. State List
  • D. Union List

Answer: A. Residuary Subjects

Explanation: Subjects not mentioned in any of the three lists are called residuary subjects, and the Union Government has the power to legislate on them.

9. Chandigarh, Lakshadweep, and Delhi are small territories that do not have the powers of a full State. What are they called?

  • A. Union Territories
  • B. Autonomous Regions
  • C. Federal Enclaves
  • D. Dependent States

Answer: A. Union Territories

Explanation: Areas too small to become an independent State but unable to merge with existing States are administered as Union Territories by the Central Government.

10. Who plays an important role in overseeing the implementation of constitutional provisions and resolves disputes regarding division of powers in India?

  • A. Judiciary (High Courts and Supreme Court)
  • B. Election Commission
  • C. Union Cabinet
  • D. President of India

Answer: A. Judiciary (High Courts and Supreme Court)

Explanation: The Judiciary acts as an umpire in case of any dispute between different levels of government about the exercise of powers.

11. What was the first and major test for democratic politics in independent India?

  • A. Creation of Linguistic States
  • B. Language policy
  • C. Centre-State relations
  • D. Local government reform

Answer: A. Creation of Linguistic States

Explanation: Reorganizing state boundaries based on languages spoken by people was the first major test for Indian federalism.

12. According to the Constitution of India, how many languages are recognized as Scheduled Languages including Hindi?

  • A. 22 languages
  • B. 18 languages
  • C. 25 languages
  • D. 14 languages

Answer: A. 22 languages

Explanation: Besides Hindi, there are 21 other languages recognized as Scheduled Languages in the Eighth Schedule of the Indian Constitution (total 22).

13. What proportion of the population in India has Hindi as its mother tongue?

  • A. About 40%
  • B. About 60%
  • C. About 25%
  • D. About 70%

Answer: A. About 40%

Explanation: Hindi is the mother tongue of only about 40 per cent of Indians, which is why it was not forcibly imposed as the sole national language.

14. According to the original Constitution, the use of English for official purposes was to stop in which year?

  • A. 1965
  • B. 1950
  • C. 1971
  • D. 1980

Answer: A. 1965

Explanation: According to the constitution, English for official purposes was scheduled to stop in 1965, but non-Hindi speaking states demanded its continuation.

15. Taking power away from Central and State government and giving it to local government is called:

  • A. Decentralisation
  • B. Centralisation
  • C. Privatisation
  • D. Nationalisation

Answer: A. Decentralisation

Explanation: Decentralisation refers to the transfer of powers from central/state tiers down to local self-governing bodies.

16. A major step towards decentralisation in India was taken in which year through constitutional amendments?

  • A. 1992
  • B. 1986
  • C. 2000
  • D. 1975

Answer: A. 1992

Explanation: The 73rd and 74th Constitutional Amendment Acts were passed in 1992 to strengthen local democratic institutions.

17. What percentage of positions in all local bodies are reserved for women after the 1992 amendment?

  • A. At least one-third (33%)
  • B. Half (50%)
  • C. One-fourth (25%)
  • D. One-fifth (20%)

Answer: A. At least one-third (33%)

Explanation: At least one-third of all elected positions and leadership posts in local bodies are constitutionally reserved for women.

18. An independent institution created in each State to conduct Panchayat and Municipal elections is called:

  • A. State Election Commission
  • B. Election Commission of India
  • C. State Public Service Commission
  • D. NITI Aayog

Answer: A. State Election Commission

Explanation: An independent State Election Commission was created in every state to ensure regular and fair local body elections.

19. Rural local government is popularly known by which name in India?

  • A. Panchayati Raj
  • B. Municipality
  • C. Zila Parishad
  • D. Cantonment Board

Answer: A. Panchayati Raj

Explanation: Rural local self-government in India is universally known as Panchayati Raj.

20. Every village or group of villages in rural areas has a council consisting of several ward members and a head. What is this council called?

  • A. Gram Panchayat
  • B. Gram Sabha
  • C. Panchayat Samiti
  • D. Nyaya Panchayat

Answer: A. Gram Panchayat

Explanation: The decision-making body at the grassroot village level is the Gram Panchayat headed by the Sarpanch.

21. Who acts as the supervisory body over the Gram Panchayat, consisting of all adult voters of the village?

  • A. Gram Sabha
  • B. Block Samiti
  • C. Zila Parishad
  • D. State Assembly

Answer: A. Gram Sabha

Explanation: All voters in the village are members of Gram Sabha, which meets at least twice or thrice a year to approve budgets and review work.

22. A few Gram Panchayats grouped together form what intermediate level of local government?

  • A. Panchayat Samiti / Block / Mandal
  • B. Zila Parishad
  • C. Gram Sabha
  • D. Municipal Council

Answer: A. Panchayat Samiti / Block / Mandal

Explanation: A cluster of Gram Panchayats forms a Block or Panchayat Samiti or Mandal.

23. All the Panchayat Samitis or Mandals in a district together constitute the:

  • A. Zila Parishad
  • B. Municipal Corporation
  • C. State Assembly
  • D. Gram Sabha

Answer: A. Zila Parishad

Explanation: The Zila (District) Parishad represents the top tier of Panchayati Raj system, covering the entire rural area of a district.

24. Who is the political head of a Municipal Corporation in big cities?

  • A. Mayor
  • B. Municipal Commissioner
  • C. Sarpanch
  • D. Collector

Answer: A. Mayor

Explanation: The Mayor is the elected political head of a Municipal Corporation in urban metropolitan areas.

25. Which system of government has only one level of government or sub-units subordinate to the central government?

  • A. Unitary System
  • B. Federal System
  • C. Confederal System
  • D. Decentralized System

Answer: A. Unitary System

Explanation: Under a unitary system, either there is only one level of government or sub-units are completely subordinate to the central government.

26. What makes the basic structure of the Indian Constitution difficult to change or amend unilaterally by Parliament?

  • A. Amendments require a two-thirds majority in Parliament AND approval by at least half of the total State legislatures
  • B. The Prime Minister alone decides amendments
  • C. State governors can veto any amendment easily
  • D. A public referendum is mandatory for all laws

Answer: A. Amendments require a two-thirds majority in Parliament AND approval by at least half of the total State legislatures

Explanation: Power sharing features are fundamental; changing them requires a two-thirds special majority in both Houses of Parliament plus ratification by half of the States.

27. States such as Nagaland, Mizoram, and Arunachal Pradesh enjoy special status under which Article of the Indian Constitution to protect indigenous culture?

  • A. Article 371
  • B. Article 370
  • C. Article 356
  • D. Article 32

Answer: A. Article 371

Explanation: Special provisions under Article 371 grant specific rights to several northeastern states regarding land rights and customary laws.

28. Which article of the Constitution was misused frequently in the past to dismiss state governments controlled by rival parties?

  • A. Article 356 (President's Rule)
  • B. Article 370
  • C. Article 21
  • D. Article 14

Answer: A. Article 356 (President's Rule)

Explanation: Before the 1990s, the Central government frequently misused Article 356 to dismiss state governments ruled by opposition parties.

29. What key political change occurred in India after 1990 that significantly strengthened federalism and state autonomy?

  • A. Rise of regional political parties and Coalition Governments at the Centre
  • B. Abolition of state assemblies
  • C. Single party dominance
  • D. Ban on regional parties

Answer: A. Rise of regional political parties and Coalition Governments at the Centre

Explanation: The coalition era post-1990 meant national parties had to partner with regional parties, leading to greater respect for state autonomy.

30. How many levels (tiers) of government are present in the Indian federal system after 1992?

  • A. Three tiers (Union, State, Local)
  • B. Two tiers
  • C. Four tiers
  • D. Single tier

Answer: A. Three tiers (Union, State, Local)

Explanation: Originally two-tiered, Indian federalism added a third tier of local self-governments (Panchayats and Municipalities) in 1992.

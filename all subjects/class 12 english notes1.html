NCERT Solutions for Class 12 English Chapter 1 The Last Lesson

NCERT Solutions for Class 12 English Flamingo Chapter 1 The Last Lesson covers Alphonse Daudet's story of the final French lesson under Prussian occupation, the theme of language and identity, and important questions and answers.

1. Story Summary

Short Note:

• Set in an Alsatian village during the Franco-Prussian war, the story follows a young boy, Franz, who arrives late to school expecting the usual routine, only to discover it is the last French lesson before German is made compulsory in the region.

• M. Hamel, the schoolteacher, dressed in his best clothes for the occasion, delivers an emotional final lesson, expressing deep regret over the students' and even his own past neglect of the French language.

• The lesson ends with the church clock striking twelve and the sound of Prussian soldiers' bugles, after which M. Hamel, too overcome with emotion to speak, writes 'Vive La France!' on the blackboard.

2. Character Sketch

Short Note:

• Franz: The young narrator who initially dreads school and Grammar lessons, but undergoes a change of heart, feeling deep regret and newfound respect for his language and teacher on this final day.

• M. Hamel: The devoted French teacher who, despite his own past shortcomings, delivers a moving final lesson, symbolising the deep emotional connection between language, culture and national identity.

3. Theme & Message

Short Note:

• The story powerfully conveys the theme of the loss of cultural and linguistic identity under political oppression, showing how language is deeply tied to a community's sense of self and freedom.

• It also emphasises the value of education and mother tongue, suggesting that people often realise the true worth of something only when they are about to lose it.

Reading With Insight (Textbook Questions)

Q1: Why was Franz surprised at the scene he saw in the school when he entered it that day?
Answer: Franz was surprised to see an unusual stillness and formality; the classroom was quiet instead of the usual noisy commotion, M. Hamel was dressed in his special ceremonial clothes rather than his everyday attire, and elderly villagers were sitting at the back of the classroom, all signalling that something significant and different was happening that day.
Q2: What changes did the order from Berlin bring for the students of Alsace?
Answer: The order from Berlin mandated that only German would be taught in the schools of Alsace and Lorraine from then on, meaning this was to be the very last lesson in French, a decision that filled both the teacher and the students with a deep sense of loss and regret over the impending disappearance of their native language from their education.
Q3: How did M. Hamel say goodbye to his students and the school?
Answer: M. Hamel gave a moving speech praising the French language as the most beautiful in the world, urging the students to always guard it carefully, and at the end, too choked with emotion to speak further, he wrote 'Vive La France!' (Long Live France) on the blackboard before dismissing the class with a simple hand gesture.

Extract-Based / Short Answer Questions

Q4: What does the last line 'that's all... you may go' suggest about M. Hamel's emotional state?
Answer: This abrupt, simple dismissal, in contrast to his earlier passionate speech, suggests that M. Hamel was overwhelmed with grief and could not bring himself to say a proper, elaborate farewell, showing the depth of his emotional pain at losing his position and his beloved subject.
Q5: How does the story use the classroom setting to reflect on larger themes of freedom and identity?
Answer: The classroom becomes a microcosm for the loss of national identity and freedom under occupation, with the forced replacement of French by German symbolising a broader erasure of cultural heritage, showing how education and language are central to a people's sense of belonging and autonomy.

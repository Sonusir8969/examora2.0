Ch-7: Glimpses of India 30 Questions

1. Who is the author of 'A Baker from Goa'?

  • A. Lucio Rodrigues
  • B. Lokesh Abrol
  • C. Arup Kumar Datta
  • D. Gavin Maxwell

Answer: A. Lucio Rodrigues

Explanation: 'A Baker from Goa' was written by Lucio Rodrigues.

2. What are the traditional Goan bakers called in Portuguese heritage?

  • A. Pader
  • B. Kabai
  • C. Bolinhas
  • D. Bol

Answer: A. Pader

Explanation: In Goa, the traditional baker is still known as 'pader'.

3. What traditional dress did the bakers in the old days wear?

  • A. A single-piece long frock called Kabai
  • B. Shirt and trousers
  • C. Kurta Pyjama
  • D. Dhoti and Kurta

Answer: A. A single-piece long frock called Kabai

Explanation: The baker originally wore a peculiar dress called the kabai, a single-piece long frock reaching down to the knees.

4. What sound heralded the arrival of the baker in the morning?

  • A. The 'jhang, jhang' sound of his bamboo staff
  • B. A loud whistle
  • C. A ringing bell
  • D. Calling out names

Answer: A. The 'jhang, jhang' sound of his bamboo staff

Explanation: The baker made his musical entry on the scene with the 'jhang, jhang' sound of his specially made bamboo staff.

5. Which bread was a must for sweet bread delicacies and marriage gifts in Goa?

  • A. Bol
  • B. Bolinhas
  • C. Cake
  • D. Loaf

Answer: A. Bol

Explanation: Marriage gifts were meaningless without the sweet bread known as the bol.

6. What was a baker's physical appearance traditionally compared to?

  • A. A jackfruit-like appearance
  • B. A mango-like appearance
  • C. A palm tree
  • D. A barrel

Answer: A. A jackfruit-like appearance

Explanation: Anyone with a jackfruit-like physical appearance was easily compared to a baker due to their plump physique.

7. When did the baker usually collect his monthly bills?

  • A. At the end of the month
  • B. Every evening
  • C. At the beginning of the month
  • D. Every week

Answer: A. At the end of the month

Explanation: The baker usually collected his bills at the end of the month, recorded on walls in pencil.

8. Who is the author of 'Coorg'?

  • A. Lokesh Abrol
  • B. Lucio Rodrigues
  • C. Arup Kumar Datta
  • D. Liam O'Flaherty

Answer: A. Lokesh Abrol

Explanation: 'Coorg' was written by Dr. Lokesh Abrol.

9. Where is Coorg situated?

  • A. Midway between Mysore and the coastal town of Mangalore
  • B. Between Goa and Mumbai
  • C. Near Assam and Bengal
  • D. In northern Karnataka near Bangalore

Answer: A. Midway between Mysore and the coastal town of Mangalore

Explanation: Coorg, or Kodagu, lies midway between Mysore and Mangalore.

10. What is Coorg famous for?

  • A. Coffee plantations, evergreen rainforests, and spices
  • B. Tea gardens and silk
  • C. Wheat fields and rivers
  • D. Apple orchards and pine trees

Answer: A. Coffee plantations, evergreen rainforests, and spices

Explanation: Coorg is famous for its evergreen rainforests, spices, and coffee plantations.

11. What descent are the fiercely independent people of Coorg believed to be of?

  • A. Greek or Arabic descent
  • B. British descent
  • C. Portuguese descent
  • D. French descent

Answer: A. Greek or Arabic descent

Explanation: According to story, the people of Coorg are possibly of Greek or Arabic descent.

12. What is the traditional long black coat worn by Kodavus called?

  • A. Kuppia
  • B. Kuffia
  • C. Kabai
  • D. Kurta

Answer: A. Kuppia

Explanation: Kodavus wear a long black coat with an embroidered waist-belt known as kuppia.

13. The Kodavus are the only people in India permitted to do what without a license?

  • A. Carry firearms
  • B. Own land
  • C. Hunt wild animals
  • D. Drive heavy vehicles

Answer: A. Carry firearms

Explanation: Kodavus are the only people in India permitted to carry firearms without a license.

14. Who was the first Chief of the Indian Army, hailing from Coorg?

  • A. General Cariappa
  • B. General Sam Manekshaw
  • C. General Bipin Rawat
  • D. General Sundarji

Answer: A. General Cariappa

Explanation: The first Chief of the Indian Army, General Cariappa, was a Coorgi.

15. What river obtains its water from the hills and forests of Coorg?

  • A. Kaveri
  • B. Krishna
  • C. Godavari
  • D. Narmada

Answer: A. Kaveri

Explanation: The river Kaveri obtains its water from the hills and forests of Coorg.

16. What is Nisargadhama in Coorg?

  • A. A sixty-four-acre island reached via a rope bridge
  • B. A mountain peak
  • C. A famous temple
  • D. A coffee plantation

Answer: A. A sixty-four-acre island reached via a rope bridge

Explanation: Walking across the rope bridge leads to the sixty-four-acre island of Nisargadhama.

17. Where is India's largest Tibetan settlement located near Coorg?

  • A. Bylakuppe
  • B. Brahmagiri
  • C. Madikeri
  • D. Mangalore

Answer: A. Bylakuppe

Explanation: India's largest Tibetan settlement is located at nearby Bylakuppe.

18. Who is the author of 'Tea from Assam'?

  • A. Arup Kumar Datta
  • B. Lokesh Abrol
  • C. Lucio Rodrigues
  • D. Roald Dahl

Answer: A. Arup Kumar Datta

Explanation: 'Tea from Assam' was written by Arup Kumar Datta.

19. Who were the two classmates traveling together to Assam by train?

  • A. Rajvir and Pranjol
  • B. Pranjol and Lencho
  • C. Rajvir and Peggy
  • D. Anil and Hari

Answer: A. Rajvir and Pranjol

Explanation: Pranjol, a youngster from Assam, invited his Delhi classmate Rajvir to visit his home.

20. Approximately how many cups of tea are drunk every day throughout the world according to Rajvir?

  • A. Over 80 crore cups
  • B. 50 crore cups
  • C. 100 crore cups
  • D. 10 crore cups

Answer: A. Over 80 crore cups

Explanation: Rajvir told Pranjol that over eighty crore cups of tea are drunk every day throughout the world.

21. According to Chinese legend, how was tea discovered?

  • A. Leaves fell into boiling water used by an Emperor for drinking
  • B. A monk planted tea seeds deliberately
  • C. A farmer boiled tea leaves for soup
  • D. It was discovered by birds

Answer: A. Leaves fell into boiling water used by an Emperor for drinking

Explanation: A Chinese emperor always boiled water before drinking, and once tea twigs fell into it, giving a delicious flavor.

22. Who was the Buddhist ascetic in the Indian legend of tea discovery?

  • A. Bodhidharma
  • B. Gautama Buddha
  • C. Mahavira
  • D. Ashoka

Answer: A. Bodhidharma

Explanation: Bodhidharma, an ancient Buddhist ascetic, cut off his eyelids because he felt sleepy during meditations.

23. How many tea plants grew out of Bodhidharma's eyelids?

  • A. Ten tea plants
  • B. Five tea plants
  • C. Twenty tea plants
  • D. Twelve tea plants

Answer: A. Ten tea plants

Explanation: Ten tea plants grew out of the eyelids of Bodhidharma.

24. Where was tea first drunk according to historical records?

  • A. China around 2700 B.C.
  • B. India in 1500 A.D.
  • C. Europe in 1600 A.D.
  • D. England in 1800 A.D.

Answer: A. China around 2700 B.C.

Explanation: Tea was first drunk in China as far back as 2700 B.C.

25. Which words such as 'chai' and 'chini' originate from?

  • A. Chinese origin
  • B. Hindi origin
  • C. Arabic origin
  • D. Portuguese origin

Answer: A. Chinese origin

Explanation: Words like 'chai' and 'chini' are derived from Chinese.

26. When did tea come to Europe and how was it consumed initially?

  • A. In the sixteenth century, as medicine rather than beverage
  • B. In the tenth century, as juice
  • C. In the eighteenth century, as soup
  • D. In the twentieth century, as a cold drink

Answer: A. In the sixteenth century, as medicine rather than beverage

Explanation: Tea came to Europe in the 16th century and was drunk more as medicine than as a beverage.

27. What was the name of the tea estate managed by Pranjol's father?

  • A. Dhekiabari Tea Estate
  • B. Mariani Tea Estate
  • C. Assam Green Estate
  • D. Himalayan Tea Estate

Answer: A. Dhekiabari Tea Estate

Explanation: Pranjol's father was the manager of Dhekiabari, a tea garden in Assam.

28. Which flushing period yields the best quality tea in Assam?

  • A. Second-flush or sprouting period (May to July)
  • B. First-flush (January to March)
  • C. Winter flush (October to December)
  • D. Autumn flush (August to September)

Answer: A. Second-flush or sprouting period (May to July)

Explanation: The second-flush or sprouting period lasts from May to July and yields the finest tea.

29. What were tea-pluckers wearing on their backs while plucking newly sprouted leaves?

  • A. Bamboo baskets and plastic aprons
  • B. Leather bags and woolen coats
  • C. Cloth sacks and caps
  • D. Wooden boxes

Answer: A. Bamboo baskets and plastic aprons

Explanation: Groups of tea-pluckers carried bamboo baskets on their backs and wore plastic aprons.

30. Why was Pranjol's father impressed by Rajvir?

  • A. Rajvir had done extensive homework and knowledge-gathering before visiting Assam
  • B. Rajvir was a fast tea-plucker
  • C. Rajvir bought tea for everyone
  • D. Rajvir drove the tractor safely

Answer: A. Rajvir had done extensive homework and knowledge-gathering before visiting Assam

Explanation: Pranjol's father acknowledged that Rajvir had done his homework before coming to Assam.

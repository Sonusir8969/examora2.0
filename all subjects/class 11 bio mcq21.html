Bio-21: Neural Control and Coordination 30 Questions

1. The basic structural and functional unit of the human nervous system is:

  • A. Neuron
  • B. Nephron
  • C. Axon
  • D. Neuroglia

Answer: A. Neuron

Explanation: Neurons are microscopic, highly specialized cells that conduct electrical impulses throughout the nervous system.

2. Which type of neurons are predominantly found in the embryonic stage of humans?

  • A. Unipolar neurons
  • B. Bipolar neurons
  • C. Multipolar neurons
  • D. Pseudounipolar neurons

Answer: A. Unipolar neurons

Explanation: Unipolar neurons (cell body with a single axon) are usually found in the embryonic stage.

3. Bipolar neurons are characteristic components of which specialized organ in humans?

  • A. Retina of the eye
  • B. Cerebral cortex
  • C. Spinal cord
  • D. Dorsal root ganglion

Answer: A. Retina of the eye

Explanation: Bipolar neurons, possessing one axon and one dendrite, are found in the retina of the eye and olfactory epithelium.

4. Nissl’s granules are granular structures present in neurons composed mainly of:

  • A. Ribosomes and Rough Endoplasmic Reticulum
  • B. Golgi apparatus and Lysosomes
  • C. Smooth Endoplasmic Reticulum and Lipids
  • D. Mitochondria and Peroxisomes

Answer: A. Ribosomes and Rough Endoplasmic Reticulum

Explanation: Nissl's granules consist of aggregated ribosomes and RER, serving as the site of protein synthesis in the neuronal soma and dendrites.

5. In the resting state, the axonal membrane is comparatively more permeable to:

  • A. Potassium ions (K+)
  • B. Sodium ions (Na+)
  • C. Calcium ions (Ca2+)
  • D. Negatively charged proteins

Answer: A. Potassium ions (K+)

Explanation: When a neuron is at rest (not conducting an impulse), its membrane is significantly more permeable to K+ than to Na+.

6. The sodium-potassium pump actively transports across the axonal membrane:

  • A. 3 Na+ outwards for 2 K+ into the cell
  • B. 2 Na+ outwards for 3 K+ into the cell
  • C. 3 K+ outwards for 2 Na+ into the cell
  • D. 3 Na+ inwards for 3 K+ out of the cell

Answer: A. 3 Na+ outwards for 2 K+ into the cell

Explanation: The active Na+/K+ ATPase pump extrudes 3 Na+ ions out of the cell while pumping 2 K+ ions into the intracellular fluid.

7. Rapid influx of which ion causes depolarization during an action potential?

  • A. Sodium ions (Na+)
  • B. Potassium ions (K+)
  • C. Chloride ions (Cl-)
  • D. Bicarbonate ions (HCO3-)

Answer: A. Sodium ions (Na+)

Explanation: Application of a stimulus increases membrane permeability to Na+, causing a rapid influx of Na+ that reverses polarity (depolarization).

8. Gaps between two adjacent myelin sheaths on an axon are called:

  • A. Nodes of Ranvier
  • B. Synaptic clefts
  • C. Schwann nodes
  • D. Axon hillocks

Answer: A. Nodes of Ranvier

Explanation: Myelin sheaths surrounding axons are discontinuous; the gaps between consecutive segments are called Nodes of Ranvier.

9. Chemical transmission of nerve impulses across a synaptic cleft relies on chemicals called:

  • A. Neurotransmitters
  • B. Hormones
  • C. Enzymes
  • D. Pheromones

Answer: A. Neurotransmitters

Explanation: Synaptic vesicles in presynaptic knobs release chemical substances called neurotransmitters (e.g., Acetylcholine) across the synaptic cleft.

10. The three layers of cranial meninges from outer to inner are:

  • A. Dura mater, Arachnoid, Pia mater
  • B. Pia mater, Arachnoid, Dura mater
  • C. Arachnoid, Dura mater, Pia mater
  • D. Dura mater, Pia mater, Arachnoid

Answer: A. Dura mater, Arachnoid, Pia mater

Explanation: The brain is covered by cranial meninges consisting of an outer tough Dura mater, a middle thin Arachnoid, and an inner vascular Pia mater.

11. A thick band of nerve fibers connecting the right and left cerebral hemispheres is the:

  • A. Corpus callosum
  • B. Corpus albicans
  • C. Corpus striatum
  • D. Hypothalamus

Answer: A. Corpus callosum

Explanation: The cerebral hemispheres are connected internally by a broad transverse tract of myelinated nerve fibers called the corpus callosum.

12. Which region of the forebrain acts as a master controller for body temperature, hunger, and thirst?

  • A. Hypothalamus
  • B. Thalamus
  • C. Epithalamus
  • D. Cerebellum

Answer: A. Hypothalamus

Explanation: The hypothalamus contains centers that regulate body temperature, urge for eating (hunger), and drinking (thirst).

13. The inner part of the cerebral hemispheres and a group of deep structures like the amygdala and hippocampus form the:

  • A. Limbic system
  • B. Reticular activating system
  • C. Autonomic system
  • D. Somatic system

Answer: A. Limbic system

Explanation: The limbic system (or emotional brain) regulates emotional reactions like pleasure, rage, fear, and motivation.

14. Four round swellings present on the dorsal portion of the midbrain are together named:

  • A. Corpora quadrigemina
  • B. Corpora allata
  • C. Corpus striatum
  • D. Cerebral peduncles

Answer: A. Corpora quadrigemina

Explanation: The dorsal side of the midbrain exhibits four rounded lobes or optic colliculi collectively known as corpora quadrigemina.

15. Which part of the hindbrain coordinates voluntary muscular movements and maintains posture and balance?

  • A. Cerebellum
  • B. Medulla oblongata
  • C. Pons
  • D. Thalamus

Answer: A. Cerebellum

Explanation: The cerebellum has a convoluted surface to accommodate many neurons and regulates motor coordination and balance.

16. The brain stem is composed of which three main divisions of the brain?

  • A. Midbrain, Pons, and Medulla oblongata
  • B. Forebrain, Midbrain, and Hindbrain
  • C. Cerebrum, Cerebellum, and Medulla
  • D. Thalamus, Hypothalamus, and Pons

Answer: A. Midbrain, Pons, and Medulla oblongata

Explanation: The midbrain, pons, and medulla oblongata form the brain stem, connecting the cerebrum to the spinal cord.

17. Centers controlling cardiovascular reflexes, respiration, and gastric secretions are located in the:

  • A. Medulla oblongata
  • B. Cerebellum
  • C. Hypothalamus
  • D. Cerebrum

Answer: A. Medulla oblongata

Explanation: The medulla oblongata contains vital autonomous centers for breathing, cardiac function, and digestion.

18. Reflex action is an involuntary response mediated primarily through the:

  • A. Spinal cord
  • B. Cerebral cortex
  • C. Corpus callosum
  • D. Cerebellum

Answer: A. Spinal cord

Explanation: A reflex arc involves peripheral nerves and the spinal cord, executing rapid automatic responses without conscious brain intervention.

19. The aperture in the center of the iris through which light enters the human eye is called the:

  • A. Pupil
  • B. Cornea
  • C. Fovea
  • D. Lens

Answer: A. Pupil

Explanation: The pupil is the central opening enclosed by the iris; smooth muscle fibers in the iris regulate its diameter.

20. The point on the retina with the highest visual acuity (resolution), containing densely packed cones, is the:

  • A. Fovea centralis
  • B. Blind spot
  • C. Optic disc
  • D. Choroid

Answer: A. Fovea centralis

Explanation: The fovea is a thinned-out central pit of the macula lutea containing tightly packed cones, providing maximum visual resolution.

21. Photoreceptor cells responsible for twilight (scotopic) vision are:

  • A. Rods
  • B. Cones
  • C. Ganglion cells
  • D. Amacrine cells

Answer: A. Rods

Explanation: Rods contain rhodopsin and mediate dim light/twilight (scotopic) vision, whereas cones mediate daylight (photopic) and color vision.

22. Rhodopsin (visual purple) is a derivative pigment formed by opsin protein and:

  • A. Retinal (Vitamin A aldehyde)
  • B. Retinol (Vitamin A alcohol)
  • C. Calciferol (Vitamin D)
  • D. Ascorbic acid (Vitamin C)

Answer: A. Retinal (Vitamin A aldehyde)

Explanation: Rhodopsin consists of opsin (a protein) bound to retinal, an aldehyde derivative of Vitamin A.

23. The middle ear contains three tiny ear ossicles arranged from outer to inner in the order of:

  • A. Malleus, Incus, Stapes
  • B. Stapes, Incus, Malleus
  • C. Incus, Malleus, Stapes
  • D. Malleus, Stapes, Incus

Answer: A. Malleus, Incus, Stapes

Explanation: The three auditory ossicles in sequence from the tympanic membrane to the oval window are Hammer (Malleus), Anvil (Incus), and Stirrup (Stapes).

24. Which structure connects the middle ear cavity with the pharynx to equalize air pressure?

  • A. Eustachian tube
  • B. Cochlear duct
  • C. Semicircular canal
  • D. External auditory meatus

Answer: A. Eustachian tube

Explanation: The Eustachian tube links the tympanic cavity of the middle ear to the nasopharynx to balance pressure on both sides of the eardrum.

25. The actual organ of hearing located on the basilar membrane inside the cochlea is the:

  • A. Organ of Corti
  • B. Macula
  • C. Crista ampullaris
  • D. Otolith organ

Answer: A. Organ of Corti

Explanation: The Organ of Corti rests on the basilar membrane and contains auditory hair cells that act as auditory receptors.

26. Sensory structure responsible for dynamic equilibrium during body rotation is the:

  • A. Crista ampullaris
  • B. Macula
  • C. Organ of Corti
  • D. Tectorial membrane

Answer: A. Crista ampullaris

Explanation: The ampulla of each semicircular canal contains a projecting ridge named crista ampullaris, which senses rotational/dynamic equilibrium.

27. Static equilibrium and linear acceleration of the body are detected by the:

  • A. Macula of saccule and utricle
  • B. Crista ampullaris
  • C. Organ of Corti
  • D. Scala vestibuli

Answer: A. Macula of saccule and utricle

Explanation: The otolith organs (saccule and utricle) contain sensory maculae that maintain static balance and respond to linear movement/gravity.

28. The fluid filled inside the membranous labyrinth of the internal ear is:

  • A. Endolymph
  • B. Perilymph
  • C. Hemolymph
  • D. Cerebrospinal fluid

Answer: A. Endolymph

Explanation: The space inside the membranous labyrinth is filled with endolymph, whereas perilymph surrounds the membranous labyrinth.

29. Somatic nervous system relays impulses from the CNS to which tissue?

  • A. Skeletal muscles
  • B. Smooth muscles
  • C. Cardiac muscle
  • D. Involuntary glands

Answer: A. Skeletal muscles

Explanation: The somatic neural system relays impulses from CNS to voluntary skeletal muscles, while the autonomic nervous system transmits impulses to involuntary organs/smooth muscles.

30. Myelin sheath around peripheral nerve fibers is produced by which specialized cells?

  • A. Schwann cells
  • B. Oligodendrocytes
  • C. Astrocytes
  • D. Microglia

Answer: A. Schwann cells

Explanation: Schwann cells form the myelin sheath around axons in the peripheral nervous system (PNS), whereas oligodendrocytes do so in the central nervous system (CNS).

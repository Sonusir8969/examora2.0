NCERT Solutions for Class 12 English Chapter 19 Memories of Childhood

NCERT Solutions for Class 12 English Vistas Chapter 19 Memories of Childhood covers two personal accounts - Zitkala-Sa's 'The Cutting of My Long Hair' and Bama's 'We Too Are Human Beings' - along with important questions and answers.

1. Part I: The Cutting of My Long Hair (Zitkala-Sa)

Short Note:

• Zitkala-Sa recounts her painful childhood experience at a boarding school for Native American children, where she and other students faced immense cultural insensitivity, including forced conformity to unfamiliar Western customs and clothing.

• The particularly traumatic experience of having her long hair forcibly cut, despite it holding deep cultural and personal significance representing courage and mourning within Native American tradition, symbolises the broader loss of identity and autonomy imposed upon her by colonial educational practices.

• Her narrative highlights themes of cultural oppression, forced assimilation, and personal resistance against the systemic erasure of indigenous identity and heritage.

2. Part II: We Too Are Human Beings (Bama)

Short Note:

• Bama, a Tamil Dalit writer, recounts her childhood realisation of the deeply ingrained caste discrimination present in Indian society, triggered by witnessing an elder from her community carrying food respectfully to avoid physical contact with an upper-caste individual.

• This early, formative experience awakens Bama's consciousness regarding the pervasive injustice and humiliation faced by Dalits, prompting her to reflect deeply on issues of social equality, dignity, and justice from a young age.

• Her narrative emphasises the importance of education as a powerful tool for empowerment and social change, encouraging resistance against caste-based discrimination through knowledge, self-respect, and hard work.

3. Theme & Message

Short Note:

• Both accounts powerfully illustrate childhood experiences of discrimination and oppression, whether based on race, culture or caste, highlighting the resilience, awareness and personal growth resulting from confronting such systemic injustices at an early age.

• The chapter emphasises the broader themes of identity, dignity, and empowerment through education and self-awareness, encouraging readers to critically reflect upon issues of cultural respect, equality, and social justice.

Reading With Insight (Textbook Questions)

Q1: Why was the cutting of Zitkala-Sa's hair such a traumatic experience for her?
Answer: The cutting of her hair was traumatic because, within her Native American cultural tradition, long hair symbolised courage, dignity and was typically only cut short as a sign of mourning or cowardice; being forced to have her hair cut against her will represented not just a personal violation but a deeply symbolic act of cultural erasure and forced assimilation imposed by the boarding school authorities.
Q2: What incident triggers Bama's realisation about caste discrimination, and how does she initially react?
Answer: Bama's realisation is triggered by witnessing an elder from her community carrying a package of food by its string to avoid direct physical contact with an upper-caste landlord, a practice rooted in caste-based untouchability; initially, young Bama finds this incident confusing and even amusing, but as she reflects further, she becomes deeply disturbed and angered by the profound injustice and humiliation it represents.
Q3: How does education play a role in Bama's response to caste discrimination?
Answer: Bama's brother encourages her to view education as an empowering tool that could help her transcend the humiliating restrictions imposed by caste discrimination, motivating her to study hard, achieve success, and command respect, using knowledge as a powerful means of resistance against social injustice and inequality.

Extract-Based / Short Answer Questions

Q4: What common themes connect Zitkala-Sa's and Bama's childhood experiences, despite their different cultural contexts?
Answer: Despite their different specific cultural and social contexts, both narratives share common themes of childhood confrontation with systemic discrimination and oppression, whether based on race and culture or caste, highlighting how early experiences of injustice can profoundly shape personal identity, awareness and resilience against oppressive social structures.
Q5: What broader message about resilience and empowerment do these two personal narratives convey?
Answer: Both narratives convey a powerful message about resilience, personal dignity, and empowerment in the face of systemic discrimination, emphasising the importance of self-awareness, education, and active resistance in overcoming oppressive social or cultural structures, encouraging readers to challenge injustice and strive for equality and respect.

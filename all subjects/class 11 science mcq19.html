Chem-11: The p-Block Elements 30 Questions

1. The p-block elements of the periodic table comprise Groups:

  • A. 13 to 18
  • B. 1 and 2
  • C. 3 to 12
  • D. Only Group 18

Answer: A. 13 to 18

Explanation: The p-block spans Groups 13 through 18, where the valence electrons of these elements occupy the p-subshell.

2. Group 13 elements are commonly known as the:

  • A. Boron family
  • B. Carbon family
  • C. Nitrogen family
  • D. Oxygen family

Answer: A. Boron family

Explanation: Group 13, headed by boron, is commonly referred to as the boron family.

3. Group 14 elements are commonly known as the:

  • A. Carbon family
  • B. Boron family
  • C. Nitrogen family
  • D. Halogen family

Answer: A. Carbon family

Explanation: Group 14, headed by carbon, is commonly referred to as the carbon family.

4. Group 15 elements are commonly known as the:

  • A. Nitrogen family (pnictogens)
  • B. Carbon family
  • C. Oxygen family
  • D. Halogen family

Answer: A. Nitrogen family (pnictogens)

Explanation: Group 15, headed by nitrogen, is commonly known as the nitrogen family or pnictogens.

5. Group 16 elements are commonly known as the:

  • A. Oxygen family (chalcogens)
  • B. Nitrogen family
  • C. Halogen family
  • D. Boron family

Answer: A. Oxygen family (chalcogens)

Explanation: Group 16, headed by oxygen, is commonly referred to as the oxygen family or chalcogens.

6. Group 17 elements are commonly known as the:

  • A. Halogens
  • B. Chalcogens
  • C. Pnictogens
  • D. Noble gases

Answer: A. Halogens

Explanation: Group 17 elements are known as halogens, meaning 'salt-formers', due to their tendency to form salts with metals.

7. Group 18 elements are commonly known as the:

  • A. Noble gases
  • B. Halogens
  • C. Chalcogens
  • D. Pnictogens

Answer: A. Noble gases

Explanation: Group 18 elements, characterised by complete valence shells and low reactivity, are known as noble gases.

8. The general valence electron configuration of p-block elements is:

  • A. ns²np¹⁻⁶
  • B. ns¹
  • C. ns²
  • D. ns²(n-1)d¹⁻¹⁰

Answer: A. ns²np¹⁻⁶

Explanation: p-block elements are characterised by the general valence configuration ns²np¹ through ns²np⁶, depending on the specific group.

9. Boron shows anomalous properties compared to other Group 13 elements primarily due to its:

  • A. Very small atomic size and absence of d-orbitals
  • B. Very large atomic size
  • C. High metallic character
  • D. Complete absence of valence electrons

Answer: A. Very small atomic size and absence of d-orbitals

Explanation: Boron's unusually small size and lack of available d-orbitals result in distinct chemical behaviour compared to the rest of Group 13.

10. Carbon exhibits catenation, the ability to form long chains, to a much greater extent than other Group 14 elements due to its:

  • A. Small size and strong C-C bond strength
  • B. Large atomic radius
  • C. High metallic character
  • D. Lack of valence electrons

Answer: A. Small size and strong C-C bond strength

Explanation: Carbon's small atomic size allows for exceptionally strong carbon-carbon bonds, enabling extensive catenation not seen as prominently in heavier Group 14 elements.

11. The three main allotropes of carbon include diamond, graphite and:

  • A. Fullerenes
  • B. Silica
  • C. Quartz
  • D. Graphite oxide only

Answer: A. Fullerenes

Explanation: Alongside diamond and graphite, fullerenes represent a third major allotropic form of carbon, consisting of cage-like molecular structures.

12. In diamond, each carbon atom is bonded to four other carbon atoms in a:

  • A. Tetrahedral arrangement
  • B. Planar hexagonal arrangement
  • C. Linear arrangement
  • D. Octahedral arrangement

Answer: A. Tetrahedral arrangement

Explanation: Diamond's exceptional hardness arises from each carbon atom being covalently bonded to four neighbouring carbons in a rigid tetrahedral structure.

13. In graphite, carbon atoms are arranged in flat, hexagonal layers held together by weak:

  • A. Van der Waals forces between layers
  • B. Strong covalent bonds between layers
  • C. Ionic bonds between layers
  • D. Metallic bonds between layers

Answer: A. Van der Waals forces between layers

Explanation: Graphite's layered structure consists of strongly bonded hexagonal sheets, with only weak van der Waals forces holding the layers together, enabling its slippery, lubricating properties.

14. Nitrogen exists in nature predominantly as a diatomic molecule due to the presence of a strong:

  • A. Triple bond between the two nitrogen atoms
  • B. Single bond only
  • C. Double bond only
  • D. No bond at all

Answer: A. Triple bond between the two nitrogen atoms

Explanation: Nitrogen's stable diatomic form (N₂) arises from a strong triple bond between the two nitrogen atoms, making it relatively inert.

15. Ammonia (NH₃) is an important compound of nitrogen, widely used in the manufacture of:

  • A. Fertilisers
  • B. Only explosives
  • C. Only plastics
  • D. Only dyes

Answer: A. Fertilisers

Explanation: Ammonia serves as a crucial raw material in the large-scale manufacture of nitrogenous fertilisers, essential for agriculture.

16. The industrial process used for the large-scale synthesis of ammonia from nitrogen and hydrogen gas is called the:

  • A. Haber process
  • B. Contact process
  • C. Ostwald process
  • D. Solvay process

Answer: A. Haber process

Explanation: The Haber process is the industrial method used to synthesise ammonia by directly combining nitrogen and hydrogen gases under high pressure and temperature with a catalyst.

17. Oxygen exists in nature in two main allotropic forms: dioxygen and:

  • A. Ozone
  • B. Peroxide
  • C. Superoxide
  • D. Oxide

Answer: A. Ozone

Explanation: Besides the common dioxygen (O₂) form, oxygen also exists as ozone (O₃), a triatomic allotrope with distinct properties.

18. The industrial process used for the large-scale manufacture of sulphuric acid is called the:

  • A. Contact process
  • B. Haber process
  • C. Ostwald process
  • D. Solvay process

Answer: A. Contact process

Explanation: The contact process is the standard industrial method used for producing sulphuric acid on a large commercial scale.

19. Halogens are highly reactive non-metals due to their strong tendency to:

  • A. Gain one electron to achieve a stable octet
  • B. Lose seven electrons
  • C. Remain entirely inert
  • D. Share all their electrons equally

Answer: A. Gain one electron to achieve a stable octet

Explanation: Halogens, having seven valence electrons, readily gain one additional electron to achieve a stable, complete octet configuration.

20. Fluorine is the most reactive of all halogens primarily due to its:

  • A. Small atomic size and high electronegativity
  • B. Large atomic size
  • C. Low electronegativity
  • D. Presence of d-orbitals

Answer: A. Small atomic size and high electronegativity

Explanation: Fluorine's small atomic size and extremely high electronegativity make it the most reactive element among the halogens.

21. Noble gases were historically considered chemically inert primarily due to their:

  • A. Completely filled valence electron shells
  • B. Very reactive nature
  • C. Large atomic size only
  • D. High density only

Answer: A. Completely filled valence electron shells

Explanation: The complete filling of their outermost electron shell gives noble gases exceptional stability, historically leading to the belief they were entirely unreactive.

22. Which noble gas was the first to be shown capable of forming actual chemical compounds?

  • A. Xenon
  • B. Helium
  • C. Neon
  • D. Argon

Answer: A. Xenon

Explanation: Xenon was the first noble gas experimentally shown to form genuine chemical compounds, such as xenon hexafluoroplatinate, challenging the earlier belief in complete inertness.

23. The general trend of metallic character across a p-block period, from left to right, tends to:

  • A. Decrease
  • B. Increase
  • C. Remain constant
  • D. Fluctuate randomly

Answer: A. Decrease

Explanation: As one moves left to right across a period, increasing nuclear charge and electronegativity generally cause metallic character to decrease.

24. The general trend of metallic character down a p-block group tends to:

  • A. Increase
  • B. Decrease
  • C. Remain constant
  • D. Become zero

Answer: A. Increase

Explanation: Down a group, increasing atomic size and decreasing ionisation energy generally lead to an increase in metallic character.

25. Silicon, a key element in Group 14, is widely used in the semiconductor and:

  • A. Electronics industry
  • B. Textile industry only
  • C. Food industry only
  • D. Pharmaceutical industry only

Answer: A. Electronics industry

Explanation: Silicon's unique semiconducting properties make it fundamentally important to the modern electronics and computer chip industry.

26. The oxidation state commonly exhibited by halogens when combined with more electronegative elements like oxygen or fluorine is:

  • A. Positive
  • B. Always -1
  • C. Always 0
  • D. Always +7 only

Answer: A. Positive

Explanation: While halogens typically exhibit a -1 oxidation state, they can show positive oxidation states when bonded to more electronegative elements like oxygen or fluorine.

27. Diborane (B₂H₆) is a notable compound of boron, characterised by unique:

  • A. Three-centre two-electron bonds
  • B. Simple ionic bonds only
  • C. Metallic bonding
  • D. Hydrogen bonding exclusively

Answer: A. Three-centre two-electron bonds

Explanation: Diborane exhibits unusual bridging bonds known as three-centre two-electron bonds, a distinctive feature of certain boron compounds.

28. The inert pair effect, observed in heavier p-block elements, refers to the reluctance of the outermost s-electrons to:

  • A. Participate in bonding
  • B. Leave the atom entirely
  • C. Form ionic bonds only
  • D. Pair with p-electrons

Answer: A. Participate in bonding

Explanation: The inert pair effect describes the tendency of the ns² electron pair in heavier p-block elements to remain unreactive and not participate readily in bond formation.

29. Ozone (O₃) plays a crucial protective role in the atmosphere by absorbing harmful:

  • A. Ultraviolet radiation from the sun
  • B. Visible light only
  • C. Infrared radiation only
  • D. Radio waves

Answer: A. Ultraviolet radiation from the sun

Explanation: The ozone layer in the stratosphere absorbs a significant portion of the sun's harmful ultraviolet radiation, protecting life on Earth.

30. Which p-block element is essential for life and found abundantly in proteins, DNA and RNA as a key structural component?

  • A. Nitrogen (and phosphorus)
  • B. Neon
  • C. Argon
  • D. Helium

Answer: A. Nitrogen (and phosphorus)

Explanation: Nitrogen and phosphorus, both p-block elements, are essential structural components of biologically vital molecules such as proteins, DNA and RNA.

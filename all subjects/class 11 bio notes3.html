NCERT Solutions for Class 11 Biology Chapter 3 Plant Kingdom

NCERT Solutions for Class 11 Biology Chapter 3 Plant Kingdom covers the classification of plants into Algae, Bryophytes, Pteridophytes, Gymnosperms and Angiosperms, along with the concept of alternation of generations.

1. Algae and Bryophytes

Short Note:

• Algae are chlorophyll-bearing, simple, thalloid, autotrophic, largely aquatic organisms, classified into Chlorophyceae (green algae), Phaeophyceae (brown algae), and Rhodophyceae (red algae) based on pigments and stored food.

• Bryophytes (amphibians of the plant kingdom) include mosses and liverworts; they need water for fertilisation and show a dominant gametophyte stage.

2. Pteridophytes

Short Note:

• Pteridophytes (e.g. ferns) have well-differentiated plant body into root, stem and leaves, with vascular tissue; the sporophyte is the dominant, independent phase, unlike in bryophytes.

3. Gymnosperms and Angiosperms

Short Note:

• Gymnosperms ('naked seed' plants) have seeds that are not enclosed within a fruit, e.g. Pinus, Cycas.

• Angiosperms (flowering plants) have seeds enclosed within a fruit; classified into monocotyledons and dicotyledons based on the number of cotyledons in the seed.

• Double fertilisation, unique to angiosperms, involves fusion of one male gamete with the egg (forming the zygote) and another with the two polar nuclei (forming the triploid endosperm).

4. Alternation of Generations

Short Note:

• Plants show alternation of generations — a haploid gametophytic (gamete-producing) phase alternates with a diploid sporophytic (spore-producing) phase in the life cycle.

NCERT Textbook Exercise Questions

Explain briefly the alternation of generation in plants.
Answer: In plants, the life cycle alternates between a haploid gametophyte (which produces gametes by mitosis) and a diploid sporophyte (which produces haploid spores by meiosis). The spore germinates to form the gametophyte again, and fusion of gametes forms the zygote, which develops into the sporophyte, completing the cycle.
What is heterospory? Briefly comment on its significance.
Answer: Heterospory is the phenomenon in which a plant produces two kinds of spores — microspores (male) and megaspores (female). Its significance lies in being a precursor to seed habit, since the megaspore is often retained within the sporangium and fertilised there, eventually leading to seed formation as seen in seed plants.
Draw a labelled diagram of a moss plant, and explain why bryophytes are called 'amphibians of the plant kingdom'.
Answer: Bryophytes are called amphibians of the plant kingdom because, although they can live in soil, they require water (a film of water) for the male gametes (antherozoids) to swim to and fertilise the egg, thus depending on both land and water for completing their life cycle, similar to amphibian animals.
Differentiate between monocot and dicot plants based on seed structure.
Answer: Monocot seeds have a single cotyledon, parallel leaf venation, and fibrous roots, while dicot seeds have two cotyledons, reticulate leaf venation, and a taproot system.

Ch-6: Haloalkanes and Haloarenes 12 Questions

1. Haloalkanes readily undergo nucleophilic substitution reactions mainly because:

  • A. The carbon-halogen bond is polar, with the carbon bearing a partial positive charge
  • B. They are completely non-polar
  • C. The halogen has very low electronegativity
  • D. They lack any lone pairs

Answer: A. The carbon-halogen bond is polar, with the carbon bearing a partial positive charge

Explanation: The higher electronegativity of the halogen polarizes the C-X bond, making carbon electrophilic and susceptible to nucleophilic attack.

2. SN1 reactions proceed through the formation of a(n):

  • A. Carbocation intermediate
  • B. Concerted transition state without intermediate
  • C. Free radical intermediate
  • D. Direct one-step substitution

Answer: A. Carbocation intermediate

Explanation: In SN1 mechanism, the leaving group departs first to form a carbocation intermediate, followed by nucleophilic attack.

3. Tertiary alkyl halides predominantly react via the:

  • A. SN1 mechanism
  • B. SN2 mechanism
  • C. Elimination only, never substitution
  • D. Addition mechanism

Answer: A. SN1 mechanism

Explanation: Steric hindrance disfavors SN2 attack on tertiary carbons, while the stability of the resulting tertiary carbocation favors SN1.

4. Which of the following alkyl halides shows the highest reactivity towards the SN2 mechanism?

  • A. CH3Br
  • B. (CH3)3CBr
  • C. (CH3)2CHBr
  • D. CH3CH2CH(CH3)Br

Answer: A. CH3Br

Explanation: Methyl halides have the least steric hindrance around the carbon, allowing the fastest backside nucleophilic attack in SN2 reactions.

5. A Grignard reagent is prepared by reacting a haloalkane with:

  • A. Magnesium metal in dry ether
  • B. Sodium metal in wet ether
  • C. Zinc metal in water
  • D. Copper metal in ethanol

Answer: A. Magnesium metal in dry ether

Explanation: Alkyl or aryl halides react with magnesium turnings in dry ether under anhydrous conditions to form Grignard reagents (RMgX).

6. Chlorobenzene is less reactive than an alkyl halide towards nucleophilic substitution mainly because:

  • A. Resonance gives the C-Cl bond partial double bond character, strengthening it
  • B. Chlorine has an unusually high electronegativity
  • C. It has no lone pairs on chlorine
  • D. Of steric hindrance alone

Answer: A. Resonance gives the C-Cl bond partial double bond character, strengthening it

Explanation: Delocalization of a chlorine lone pair into the aromatic ring increases the C-Cl bond order and shortens/strengthens it, reducing reactivity.

7. Freons (chlorofluorocarbons) are mainly used as:

  • A. Refrigerants and aerosol propellants
  • B. Insecticides
  • C. Solvents for polymerization
  • D. Anesthetics

Answer: A. Refrigerants and aerosol propellants

Explanation: Freons such as CCl2F2 have historically been widely used as refrigerants and in aerosol sprays.

8. DDT is chemically known as:

  • A. Dichlorodiphenyltrichloroethane
  • B. Dichlorodifluoromethane
  • C. Trichloroethylene
  • D. Tetrachloroethane

Answer: A. Dichlorodiphenyltrichloroethane

Explanation: DDT (dichlorodiphenyltrichloroethane) is a well-known organochlorine insecticide.

9. Treatment of haloalkanes with alcoholic KOH predominantly gives:

  • A. Alkenes, through an elimination reaction
  • B. Alcohols
  • C. Ethers
  • D. Alkanes

Answer: A. Alkenes, through an elimination reaction

Explanation: Alcoholic KOH acts as a base and favors β-elimination (dehydrohalogenation) to form alkenes.

10. Racemization is most commonly observed in reactions proceeding through the:

  • A. SN1 mechanism
  • B. SN2 mechanism
  • C. Simple addition reactions
  • D. Free radical halogenation only

Answer: A. SN1 mechanism

Explanation: The planar carbocation intermediate in SN1 reactions can be attacked from either face, giving a racemic mixture of products.

11. Which type of halide is generally most reactive towards the SN1 mechanism?

  • A. Tertiary halide
  • B. Primary halide
  • C. Methyl halide
  • D. Vinyl halide

Answer: A. Tertiary halide

Explanation: Tertiary halides form the most stable carbocation intermediates, favoring the SN1 pathway.

12. The iodoform test is used to detect the presence of:

  • A. A CH3CO- group, or a CH3CH(OH)- group that can be oxidized to it
  • B. A -COOH group
  • C. A -OH group in any alcohol
  • D. A -CHO group only

Answer: A. A CH3CO- group, or a CH3CH(OH)- group that can be oxidized to it

Explanation: Compounds containing a methyl ketone group (or oxidizable to one) give a positive iodoform test, forming a yellow precipitate of CHI3.

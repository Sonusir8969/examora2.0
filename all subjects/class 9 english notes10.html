NCERT Solutions for Class 9 English Poem 1 The Road Not Taken

NCERT Solutions for Class 9 English Beehive Poem 1 The Road Not Taken by Robert Frost covers the theme of choices in life, poetic devices, and important questions and answers.

1. About the Poet and Poem

Short Note:

• Robert Frost was a celebrated American poet known for using simple, rural imagery to explore deep philosophical ideas about life.

• In this poem, the speaker stands at a fork in a forest path and must choose one of two roads that look almost equally worn and inviting.

• The poem is written in first person and uses the image of diverging roads as an extended metaphor for the choices we make in life.

2. Theme & Central Idea

Short Note:

• The poem reflects on how every choice we make closes off other possibilities, and we can never know for certain how the untaken path might have turned out.

• It captures the universal human experience of decision-making, hesitation, and the tendency to look back with curiosity or slight regret at roads not taken.

• The final lines suggest that the speaker will later claim his choice made 'all the difference', hinting at how we often build meaning and identity around the decisions we have made, whether or not they were truly different.

3. Poetic Devices

Short Note:

• Metaphor: The two roads represent the different choices and paths available in a person's life.

• Symbolism: The yellow wood and the diverging paths symbolise a moment of important decision-making.

• Rhyme Scheme: The poem follows an ABAAB rhyme scheme in each of its four stanzas, giving it a musical, reflective quality.

Reading With Insight (Textbook Questions)

Q1: Where does the speaker find himself, and what is his dilemma?
Answer: The speaker finds himself in a yellow wood where the path splits into two roads, and his dilemma is that he must choose only one direction to travel, even though he wishes he could explore both.
Q2: How does the speaker describe the two roads, and are they really very different?
Answer: The speaker initially describes one road as perhaps having a slightly better claim because it was grassier and less worn, but he then admits that both roads were actually worn about the same and equally covered with fresh, untrodden leaves, showing that the difference between them was minimal.
Q3: What does the speaker predict he will say in the future about his choice?
Answer: The speaker predicts that in the future, he will recall this moment with a sigh and say that choosing the road less travelled by made all the difference in his life, even though at the time the two roads were nearly identical.

Extract-Based / Short Answer Questions

Q4: What does the poem suggest about the nature of choices in life?
Answer: The poem suggests that choices in life often appear more distinct in hindsight than they actually were at the time, and that we tend to attach great significance to our decisions as we look back on our journey.
Q5: Why is the poem's title significant?
Answer: The title, 'The Road Not Taken', is significant because the poem is ultimately more focused on the path the speaker did not choose and the lingering curiosity about what might have been, rather than the road he actually walked.

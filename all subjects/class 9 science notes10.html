NCERT Solutions for Class 9 Science Chapter 10 Work and Energy

NCERT Solutions for Class 9 Science Chapter 10 Work and Energy covers scientific concepts of work, positive and negative work, kinetic energy, potential energy, mechanical energy, the law of conservation of energy, rate of doing work (power), and the commercial unit of electrical energy.

1. Work & Conditions for Work Done

Short Note:

• Work: Work is said to be done when a force applied on an object produces displacement in the direction of the force.

  - Formula: Work Done (W) = Force (F) × Displacement (s)

  - SI Unit: Joule (J) or Newton-metre (N·m). 1 Joule is the work done when a force of 1 N displaces an object by 1 m in the direction of the force.

• Conditions for Work to be Done:

  1. A force must act on the object.

  2. The object must undergo displacement.

• Types of Work Done:

  - Positive Work: Force and displacement are in the same direction.

  - Negative Work: Force and displacement are in opposite directions (e.g., friction).

  - Zero Work: Force and displacement are perpendicular to each other, or displacement is zero.

2. Forms of Energy: Kinetic & Potential Energy

Short Note:

• Energy: The capacity of a body to do work. SI Unit: Joule (J). Scalar quantity.

• Kinetic Energy (Eₖ): Energy possessed by an object due to its motion.

  - Formula: Eₖ = ½ × m × v² (where m = mass, v = velocity).

• Potential Energy (Eₚ): Energy possessed by an object due to its position, shape, or configuration.

  - Gravitational Potential Energy: Energy stored in an object raised to a height (h) above the ground.

  - Formula: Eₚ = m × g × h (where m = mass, g = acceleration due to gravity, h = height).

• Mechanical Energy: Sum of Kinetic Energy and Potential Energy of a system.

3. Law of Conservation of Energy

Short Note:

• Law of Conservation of Energy: Energy can neither be created nor destroyed; it can only be transformed from one form to another. The total energy of an isolated system always remains constant.

  - Formula: Potential Energy + Kinetic Energy = Constant (Mechanical Energy)

  - Free Fall Example: As an object falls, its potential energy gradually decreases while its kinetic energy increases by an equal amount, keeping total mechanical energy constant at every point.

4. Rate of Doing Work (Power) & Commercial Unit

Short Note:

• Power (P): The rate of doing work or rate of energy transfer.

  - Formula: Power (P) = Work Done (W) / Time (t) or P = Energy / Time

  - SI Unit: Watt (W) or Joules per second (J/s). 1 Watt = 1 J/s.

  - 1 Kilowatt (kW) = 1000 W

• Commercial Unit of Electrical Energy: Kilowatt-hour (kWh), commonly called 1 'unit'.

  - 1 kWh = 1 kW × 1 hour = 1000 W × 3600 s = 3.6 × 10⁶ Joules (J)

Intext Questions - Page Number: 115

Q1: A force of 7 N acts on an object. The displacement is, say 8 m, in the direction of the force. Let us take it that the force acts on the object through the displacement. What is the work done in this case?
Answer:
Given:

• Force (F) = 7 N

• Displacement (s) = 8 m

• Work Done (W) = Force × Displacement = F × s

• W = 7 N × 8 m = 56 J

Intext Questions - Page Number: 119

Q1: What is the kinetic energy of an object? Write an expression for the kinetic energy of an object.
Answer: The kinetic energy of an object is the energy possessed by it due to its motion. Moving objects like a running athlete, a blowing wind, or a moving car possess kinetic energy.

Expression: Eₖ = ½ × m × v²
Where m is the mass of the object and v is its uniform velocity.

Intext Questions - Page Number: 125

Q1: What is power? Define 1 watt of power.
Answer:
• Power: Power is defined as the rate of doing work or the rate at which energy is consumed or transferred.
• 1 Watt: Power is said to be 1 Watt when work is done at the rate of 1 Joule per second (i.e., 1 W = 1 J/s).

Textbook Chapter End Exercise Questions

Q1: Look at the activities listed below. Reason whether or not work is done in the light of your understanding of the term 'work':

(a) Suma is swimming in a pond: Work is done. She applies force against water to move forward.

(b) A donkey is carrying a load on its back: No work is done in moving forward against gravity because the lifting force is vertical while displacement is horizontal (force and displacement are perpendicular to each other).

(c) A wind-mill is lifting water from a well: Work is done. Force acts against gravity in the direction of water movement.

(d) A green plant is carrying out photosynthesis: No mechanical work is done since there is no displacement of an object.

(e) An engine is pulling a train: Work is done. Force applied by the engine causes displacement of the train in the direction of force.

(f) Food grains are getting dried in the sun: No work is done because there is no displacement.

(g) A sailboat is moving due to wind energy: Work is done. Wind force causes the sailboat to move in the direction of wind.

Q2: An electric heater is rated 1500 W. How much energy does it use in 10 hours?
Answer:
Given:

• Power (P) = 1500 W = 1.5 kW

• Time (t) = 10 h

• Energy consumed = Power × Time

• Energy = 1.5 kW × 10 h = 15 kWh (or 15 units)

In Joules: 15 × 3.6 × 10⁶ J = 5.4 × 10⁷ Joules

Ch-1: Solutions 12 Questions

1. Molarity is defined as the number of moles of solute per:

  • A. Litre of solution
  • B. Kg of solvent
  • C. Litre of solvent
  • D. Kg of solution

Answer: A. Litre of solution

Explanation: Molarity (M) is defined as moles of solute dissolved per litre of solution.

2. Which colligative property is most suitable for determining the molar mass of macromolecules such as proteins and polymers?

  • A. Osmotic pressure
  • B. Relative lowering of vapour pressure
  • C. Elevation in boiling point
  • D. Depression in freezing point

Answer: A. Osmotic pressure

Explanation: Osmotic pressure produces a measurable value even for very dilute solutions of macromolecules, making it the most accurate method.

3. Raoult's law states that the partial vapour pressure of each component in a solution is proportional to:

  • A. Its mole fraction
  • B. Its molarity
  • C. Its molality
  • D. Its mass fraction

Answer: A. Its mole fraction

Explanation: According to Raoult's law, p_i = x_i * p_i°, where x_i is the mole fraction of component i.

4. Which of the following pairs shows positive deviation from Raoult's law?

  • A. Ethanol and acetone
  • B. HCl and water
  • C. Chloroform and acetone
  • D. Acetic acid and pyridine

Answer: A. Ethanol and acetone

Explanation: Ethanol-acetone mixtures have weaker A-B interactions than A-A/B-B interactions, causing positive deviation.

5. The van't Hoff factor (i) is greater than 1 for solutes that:

  • A. Dissociate into ions in solution
  • B. Associate in solution
  • C. Are non-electrolytes
  • D. Do not ionize at all

Answer: A. Dissociate into ions in solution

Explanation: Dissociation increases the number of particles in solution, making i > 1.

6. Molality of a solution is independent of:

  • A. Temperature
  • B. Amount of solute
  • C. Mass of solvent
  • D. Nature of solute

Answer: A. Temperature

Explanation: Since molality is defined using mass (not volume) of solvent, it does not change with temperature.

7. The SI unit of the ebullioscopic constant (Kb) is:

  • A. K kg mol⁻¹
  • B. mol kg⁻¹
  • C. K mol⁻¹
  • D. kg mol⁻¹ K⁻¹

Answer: A. K kg mol⁻¹

Explanation: Kb relates the elevation in boiling point (K) to molality (mol kg⁻¹), giving units K kg mol⁻¹.

8. Azeotropes are mixtures of two liquids that:

  • A. Have the same composition in liquid and vapour phase and boil at a constant temperature
  • B. Are pure compounds
  • C. Always behave as ideal solutions
  • D. Always obey Raoult's law

Answer: A. Have the same composition in liquid and vapour phase and boil at a constant temperature

Explanation: Azeotropic mixtures cannot be separated by simple distillation because the vapour has the same composition as the liquid.

9. Henry's law is primarily used to determine the:

  • A. Solubility of a gas in a liquid
  • B. Solubility of a solid in a liquid
  • C. Vapour pressure of a pure liquid
  • D. Boiling point elevation of a solution

Answer: A. Solubility of a gas in a liquid

Explanation: Henry's law states that the partial pressure of a gas is proportional to its mole fraction dissolved in a liquid.

10. For an ideal solution, the enthalpy of mixing (ΔH_mix) is:

  • A. Zero
  • B. Positive
  • C. Negative
  • D. Undefined

Answer: A. Zero

Explanation: Ideal solutions form with no net heat change because solute-solvent interactions equal solute-solute and solvent-solvent interactions.

11. Which of the following 0.1 m aqueous solutions has the highest boiling point?

  • A. Al2(SO4)3
  • B. NaCl
  • C. Glucose
  • D. Urea

Answer: A. Al2(SO4)3

Explanation: Al2(SO4)3 dissociates into the largest number of ions, giving the highest van't Hoff factor and hence the greatest boiling point elevation.

12. Reverse osmosis is commonly used for:

  • A. Desalination of sea water
  • B. Determining the molar mass of solutes
  • C. Preparing azeotropic mixtures
  • D. Increasing the vapour pressure of a solution

Answer: A. Desalination of sea water

Explanation: In reverse osmosis, pressure greater than osmotic pressure is applied to force pure water through a semi-permeable membrane, leaving salts behind.

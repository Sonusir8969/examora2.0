Ch-8: Aldehydes, Ketones and Carboxylic Acids 12 Questions

1. The characteristic functional group of aldehydes is:

  • A. -CHO
  • B. -COOH
  • C. >C=O only, without an attached H
  • D. -OH

Answer: A. -CHO

Explanation: Aldehydes are characterized by the -CHO group, where a carbonyl carbon is bonded to at least one hydrogen atom.

2. Tollens' reagent test is commonly used to distinguish:

  • A. Aldehydes from ketones
  • B. Alcohols from phenols
  • C. Acids from esters
  • D. Amines from amides

Answer: A. Aldehydes from ketones

Explanation: Aldehydes reduce Tollens' reagent (ammoniacal AgNO3) to form a silver mirror, while ketones generally do not react.

3. Aldol condensation occurs in carbonyl compounds that possess:

  • A. At least one α-hydrogen atom
  • B. No α-hydrogen atom
  • C. Only an aromatic ring
  • D. A carbon-carbon triple bond

Answer: A. At least one α-hydrogen atom

Explanation: α-hydrogens are essential for enolate formation, which then attacks another carbonyl carbon in aldol condensation.

4. The Cannizzaro reaction is characteristic of aldehydes that:

  • A. Lack an α-hydrogen atom
  • B. Possess an α-hydrogen atom
  • C. Are only aliphatic with α-H
  • D. Are actually ketones

Answer: A. Lack an α-hydrogen atom

Explanation: In the absence of α-hydrogens, aldehydes cannot undergo aldol reactions and instead undergo disproportionation (Cannizzaro reaction) in strong base.

5. Carboxylic acids are more acidic than phenols mainly because:

  • A. The carboxylate ion is more effectively stabilized by resonance
  • B. Carboxylic acids have a higher molecular weight
  • C. Phenol shows no resonance stabilization
  • D. Carboxylic acids are purely ionic

Answer: A. The carboxylate ion is more effectively stabilized by resonance

Explanation: In the carboxylate ion, the negative charge is delocalized equally over two oxygen atoms, giving greater stabilization than in the phenoxide ion.

6. The Hell-Volhard-Zelinsky (HVZ) reaction is used to introduce a halogen atom at the:

  • A. α-carbon of a carboxylic acid
  • B. Carbonyl carbon
  • C. β-carbon of the chain
  • D. Terminal carbon of the chain, regardless of position

Answer: A. α-carbon of a carboxylic acid

Explanation: The HVZ reaction uses red phosphorus and a halogen to selectively halogenate the α-carbon of a carboxylic acid.

7. Which test can distinguish acetaldehyde from acetone?

  • A. Tollens' test, since only acetaldehyde gives a silver mirror
  • B. Iodoform test, given by acetaldehyde only
  • C. Fehling's test, given only by acetone
  • D. Both give an identical positive result

Answer: A. Tollens' test, since only acetaldehyde gives a silver mirror

Explanation: Acetaldehyde, having a carbonyl carbon bonded to hydrogen, is oxidized by Tollens' reagent; the ketone acetone is not.

8. Nucleophilic addition reactions of carbonyl compounds are favoured due to the:

  • A. Electrophilic nature of the carbonyl carbon
  • B. Nucleophilic nature of the carbonyl carbon
  • C. Complete absence of polarity in C=O
  • D. High steric hindrance around the carbonyl group

Answer: A. Electrophilic nature of the carbonyl carbon

Explanation: The carbonyl carbon carries a partial positive charge due to oxygen's electronegativity, making it susceptible to nucleophilic attack.

9. Esterification of a carboxylic acid with an alcohol is typically carried out using:

  • A. Concentrated H2SO4 as a catalyst
  • B. NaOH as a catalyst
  • C. Zn dust
  • D. LiAlH4

Answer: A. Concentrated H2SO4 as a catalyst

Explanation: Conc. H2SO4 acts as an acid catalyst and dehydrating agent, driving the equilibrium of Fischer esterification towards ester formation.

10. Formic acid gives a positive Tollens' test because it contains:

  • A. A -CHO-like reducing group in addition to -COOH
  • B. Only a -COOH group with no reducing ability
  • C. No functional group capable of oxidation
  • D. A ketonic carbonyl group

Answer: A. A -CHO-like reducing group in addition to -COOH

Explanation: Formic acid (HCOOH) has an aldehydic hydrogen directly attached to the carbonyl carbon, giving it reducing properties similar to aldehydes.

11. Among compounds with a similar number of carbon atoms, which generally has the highest boiling point?

  • A. Carboxylic acid
  • B. Aldehyde
  • C. Ketone
  • D. Alkane

Answer: A. Carboxylic acid

Explanation: Carboxylic acids form strong intermolecular hydrogen-bonded dimers, giving them notably higher boiling points than aldehydes, ketones, or alkanes of similar size.

12. Decarboxylation of the sodium salt of a carboxylic acid with soda lime gives:

  • A. An alkane
  • B. An alkene
  • C. An alcohol
  • D. An aldehyde

Answer: A. An alkane

Explanation: Heating sodium salts of carboxylic acids with soda lime (NaOH + CaO) removes CO2, yielding the corresponding alkane.

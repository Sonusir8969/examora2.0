Chapter 17: The Story of Village Palampur 30 Questions

1. Village Palampur, discussed in the chapter, is well connected to which nearby town?

  • A. Raiganj
  • B. Shahpur
  • C. Palampur town
  • D. Chandigarh

Answer: B. Shahpur

Explanation: In the chapter's example, Palampur is well connected to the neighbouring town of Shahpur by roads, enabling trade and transport of goods.

2. What is the main economic activity carried out in Palampur?

  • A. Fishing
  • B. Farming
  • C. Mining
  • D. Software development

Answer: B. Farming

Explanation: Farming is the main economic activity in the village of Palampur, with most families involved in agriculture in some form.

3. Which of the following is NOT considered one of the four factors of production discussed in the chapter?

  • A. Land
  • B. Labour
  • C. Physical capital
  • D. Advertisement

Answer: D. Advertisement

Explanation: The four factors of production are land, labour, physical capital, and human capital; advertisement is not one of them.

4. What does 'human capital' refer to in the context of production?

  • A. The money invested in machinery
  • B. The knowledge and enterprise of people that goes into production
  • C. The area of farmland owned
  • D. The number of tools used in farming

Answer: B. The knowledge and enterprise of people that goes into production

Explanation: Human capital refers to the skills, knowledge and enterprise that people bring to the process of production, distinguishing it from raw physical labour.

5. What does HYV stand for, as used in the context of Palampur's farming?

  • A. High Yielding Variety
  • B. Highly Vulnerable Variety
  • C. High Value Vegetables
  • D. Hybrid Yielding Vegetables

Answer: A. High Yielding Variety

Explanation: HYV seeds, or High Yielding Variety seeds, were introduced during the Green Revolution and allowed farmers in Palampur to produce more crops from the same land.

6. What is 'multiple cropping'?

  • A. Growing only one crop throughout the year
  • B. Growing more than one crop on the same piece of land during the year
  • C. Growing crops only in summer
  • D. Growing crops without any irrigation

Answer: B. Growing more than one crop on the same piece of land during the year

Explanation: Multiple cropping allows farmers to grow several crops in a single year on the same land, increasing overall production.

7. Why is irrigation important for farming in Palampur, according to the chapter?

  • A. It has no impact on crop production
  • B. It provides a reliable source of water, essential for growing HYV crops and multiple cropping
  • C. It reduces the need for seeds
  • D. It eliminates the need for labour

Answer: B. It provides a reliable source of water, essential for growing HYV crops and multiple cropping

Explanation: Assured irrigation allows farmers to grow HYV crops reliably and practise multiple cropping, both of which increase agricultural production.

8. Which of the following is an example of 'fixed capital' used in farming?

  • A. Seeds and fertilizers
  • B. Tools and machines like tractors and ploughs
  • C. Wages paid to labourers
  • D. Raw cotton

Answer: B. Tools and machines like tractors and ploughs

Explanation: Fixed capital includes tools, machines and buildings that are used over many years of production, unlike working capital which is used up in a single production cycle.

9. What is 'working capital' in the context of farming?

  • A. Land owned by farmers
  • B. Raw materials and money required during the production process, such as seeds and fertilizers
  • C. Buildings used for storage only
  • D. Skills of the farmer

Answer: B. Raw materials and money required during the production process, such as seeds and fertilizers

Explanation: Working capital includes raw materials and money on hand needed during production, which are used up or transformed in the process, unlike fixed capital.

10. Why is land considered a fixed factor of production in the short run?

  • A. Because its total quantity cannot easily be increased in a short period
  • B. Because land has no value
  • C. Because land can be created instantly
  • D. Because land is not used in farming

Answer: A. Because its total quantity cannot easily be increased in a short period

Explanation: Unlike labour or capital, the total area of cultivable land is largely fixed and cannot be significantly expanded in the short run.

11. What is meant by 'landless labourers' in the context of Palampur?

  • A. Farmers who own vast amounts of land
  • B. Workers who do not own any land and work on the land of others for wages
  • C. Landowners who lease out their land
  • D. Government officers managing land records

Answer: B. Workers who do not own any land and work on the land of others for wages

Explanation: Landless labourers do not own land themselves and depend on wage labour, working on the fields of other farmers for income.

12. What non-farm activity is described in Palampur involving the manufacturing of goods from sugarcane?

  • A. Small-scale manufacturing (jaggery/gur production)
  • B. Fishing
  • C. Transport services
  • D. Shopkeeping

Answer: A. Small-scale manufacturing (jaggery/gur production)

Explanation: Small-scale manufacturing units in Palampur process raw sugarcane into gur, adding value through simple production methods using both labour and machines.

13. Which non-farm activity involves rearing animals for milk and its products in Palampur?

  • A. Dairy farming
  • B. Shopkeeping
  • C. Transport
  • D. Small-scale manufacturing

Answer: A. Dairy farming

Explanation: Dairy is a common non-farm activity where families rear buffaloes and cows to produce milk that is sold locally and to nearby towns.

14. Why is transport regarded as a rapidly growing activity in villages like Palampur?

  • A. Because it has no connection to trade or farming
  • B. Because it helps carry goods and people between villages, towns and cities
  • C. Because farming has completely stopped
  • D. Because it reduces the need for markets

Answer: B. Because it helps carry goods and people between villages, towns and cities

Explanation: A variety of transport modes, including rickshaws, tempos, trucks and bullock carts, are used to connect villages to towns for trade in agricultural and other goods.

15. What is 'production' as defined in the chapter?

  • A. The process of buying goods from a shop
  • B. The process of combining various resources to create goods or services
  • C. The act of only consuming goods
  • D. The act of storing money in a bank

Answer: B. The process of combining various resources to create goods or services

Explanation: Production involves combining factors like land, labour and capital in an organised way to produce goods and services for use or sale.

16. Why do some farmers in Palampur borrow money from large farmers or moneylenders?

  • A. To buy inputs like seeds, fertilizers and pay for irrigation and labour
  • B. Because they have no crops to grow
  • C. Because banks always refuse to give loans
  • D. To donate to the village temple

Answer: A. To buy inputs like seeds, fertilizers and pay for irrigation and labour

Explanation: Since farming requires upfront working capital, many small farmers borrow money to purchase inputs, often turning to local moneylenders due to limited access to formal credit.

17. What problem is highlighted regarding land distribution among farming families in Palampur?

  • A. Land is distributed equally among all families
  • B. A large number of families are landless or have very small landholdings, while a few have large holdings
  • C. All farmers own the same amount of land
  • D. There is no shortage of land at all

Answer: B. A large number of families are landless or have very small landholdings, while a few have large holdings

Explanation: The chapter highlights unequal distribution of land, with many families being landless or holding small plots while a minority of large farmers own most of the land.

18. What are the three main crops mentioned as grown in Palampur across the seasons?

  • A. Wheat, jowar and bajra
  • B. Jute, rice and cotton
  • C. Coffee, tea and rubber
  • D. Mango, banana and apple

Answer: A. Wheat, jowar and bajra

Explanation: In Palampur, farmers typically grow jowar and bajra in the rainy season and wheat in the winter season, reflecting multiple cropping practices.

19. What role does electricity play in Palampur's development, according to the chapter?

  • A. It has helped irrigate land through tube wells and supports small-scale industries
  • B. Electricity has no use in agriculture
  • C. It is used only for street lighting
  • D. It is unavailable in Palampur

Answer: A. It has helped irrigate land through tube wells and supports small-scale industries

Explanation: The spread of electricity in Palampur enabled farmers to run tube wells for irrigation and supported various non-farm production activities in the village.

20. What is meant by 'surplus' production in farming?

  • A. The amount of crop that is left after the farmer's family consumption, which is sold in the market
  • B. The total loss suffered by a farmer
  • C. The tax paid by farmers
  • D. The amount borrowed by farmers

Answer: A. The amount of crop that is left after the farmer's family consumption, which is sold in the market

Explanation: Farmers who produce more than what their family needs sell this surplus in the market, generating income for other purchases.

21. What type of workers are hired by medium and large farmers to work on their fields?

  • A. Hired farm labourers
  • B. Only family members
  • C. Government employees
  • D. Foreign workers

Answer: A. Hired farm labourers

Explanation: Larger farmers, who cannot manage all farm work with family labour alone, hire farm labourers, usually paid in cash or crop, to work on their land.

22. What is one major reason why farm labourers in Palampur are often paid low wages?

  • A. There is intense competition for jobs among a large number of labourers seeking limited work
  • B. Because there are very few labourers available
  • C. Because the government fixes very high wages
  • D. Because farming requires no labour

Answer: A. There is intense competition for jobs among a large number of labourers seeking limited work

Explanation: With many more labourers than available jobs, wages tend to be pushed down, often below the minimum wages fixed by the government.

23. What is the general relationship shown between capital investment and farm output in Palampur?

  • A. Higher capital investment, such as HYV seeds and irrigation, tends to raise farm output
  • B. Capital investment has no effect on farm output
  • C. Farm output decreases with more capital investment
  • D. Farm output depends only on rainfall

Answer: A. Higher capital investment, such as HYV seeds and irrigation, tends to raise farm output

Explanation: Farmers who could invest in modern inputs like HYV seeds, fertilizers and irrigation generally achieved significantly higher output on their land.

24. Why do many small farmers in Palampur struggle to raise capital for farming?

  • A. They have limited savings and often lack easy access to affordable credit
  • B. Because they do not want to farm
  • C. Because they have too much land
  • D. Because capital is not required in agriculture

Answer: A. They have limited savings and often lack easy access to affordable credit

Explanation: Small farmers typically have little savings of their own and may face difficulty accessing formal loans, forcing them to depend on costlier informal credit.

25. Which of these best explains why non-farm activities are growing in importance in villages like Palampur?

  • A. Farming alone cannot provide employment to the entire growing rural workforce
  • B. Non-farm activities have completely replaced farming
  • C. Non-farm work is banned in rural India
  • D. Villages no longer need any income

Answer: A. Farming alone cannot provide employment to the entire growing rural workforce

Explanation: Since land is limited and the rural population continues to grow, farming alone cannot generate enough employment, making diversification into non-farm activities important.

26. What role do small shopkeepers play in the local economy of Palampur?

  • A. They provide goods and services needed by villagers close to their homes
  • B. They have no role in village economy
  • C. They only sell agricultural machinery
  • D. They replace farming entirely

Answer: A. They provide goods and services needed by villagers close to their homes

Explanation: Small shops in the village sell items of daily use, providing an important service and a source of non-farm income for shopkeepers.

27. What is one of the modern farming methods mentioned that helped increase Palampur's wheat production?

  • A. Use of chemical fertilizers along with HYV seeds and irrigation
  • B. Complete avoidance of any fertilizers
  • C. Farming only during monsoons
  • D. Using no modern tools at all

Answer: A. Use of chemical fertilizers along with HYV seeds and irrigation

Explanation: A combination of chemical fertilizers, HYV seeds and reliable irrigation contributed to a significant increase in wheat production in Palampur.

28. According to the chapter, what determines whether farming in an area can support multiple cropping?

  • A. Availability of assured and adequate irrigation facilities
  • B. The number of shops in the village
  • C. The size of the local market alone
  • D. The distance to the nearest city

Answer: A. Availability of assured and adequate irrigation facilities

Explanation: Reliable irrigation is a key requirement that allows farmers to grow more than one crop on the same land within a year.

29. What is the overall message of the chapter about the organisation of production in Palampur?

  • A. Production requires combining land, labour, physical capital and human capital in an organised way
  • B. Production requires only land and nothing else
  • C. Production has no relation to capital or labour
  • D. Production occurs automatically without any resources

Answer: A. Production requires combining land, labour, physical capital and human capital in an organised way

Explanation: The chapter uses Palampur's example to show how the four factors of production must work together for any output, whether farm or non-farm, to be produced.

30. What does the story of Palampur ultimately illustrate about rural economies in India?

  • A. Rural economies rely solely on one activity with no diversity
  • B. Rural economies combine farm and non-farm activities, though inequality in land and capital ownership remains a challenge
  • C. Villages have no connection to towns or markets
  • D. Rural economies have completely stopped farming

Answer: B. Rural economies combine farm and non-farm activities, though inequality in land and capital ownership remains a challenge

Explanation: Palampur's story illustrates how villages depend on a mix of farming and non-farm work for livelihood, while unequal access to land and capital continues to create disparities among families.

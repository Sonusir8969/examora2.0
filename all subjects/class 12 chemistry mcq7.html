Ch-7: Alcohols, Phenols and Ethers 12 Questions

1. Alcohols generally have higher boiling points than alkanes of comparable molecular mass mainly due to:

  • A. Intermolecular hydrogen bonding
  • B. Their higher molecular mass
  • C. Ionic character of the O-H bond
  • D. Van der Waals forces alone

Answer: A. Intermolecular hydrogen bonding

Explanation: The -OH group in alcohols allows hydrogen bonding between molecules, requiring more energy to separate them and raising the boiling point.

2. Phenol is more acidic than an aliphatic alcohol mainly because:

  • A. The phenoxide ion formed is stabilized by resonance with the benzene ring
  • B. Phenol has a higher molecular weight
  • C. Alcohols are basic in nature
  • D. Phenol lacks an -OH group

Answer: A. The phenoxide ion formed is stabilized by resonance with the benzene ring

Explanation: Delocalization of the negative charge on oxygen into the aromatic ring stabilizes the phenoxide ion, increasing phenol's acidity.

3. The Lucas test is used to distinguish between:

  • A. Primary, secondary and tertiary alcohols
  • B. Phenols and alcohols
  • C. Ethers and alcohols
  • D. Aldehydes and ketones

Answer: A. Primary, secondary and tertiary alcohols

Explanation: Lucas reagent (conc. HCl + anhydrous ZnCl2) reacts with alcohols at different rates depending on class, producing turbidity at different speeds.

4. The Williamson synthesis is a standard method for preparing:

  • A. Ethers
  • B. Alcohols
  • C. Esters
  • D. Aldehydes

Answer: A. Ethers

Explanation: In Williamson ether synthesis, an alkyl halide reacts with a sodium alkoxide/phenoxide via SN2 to give an ether.

5. Reaction of phenol with chloroform and NaOH (Reimer-Tiemann reaction) followed by hydrolysis gives:

  • A. Salicylaldehyde
  • B. Salicylic acid directly
  • C. Picric acid
  • D. Benzoic acid

Answer: A. Salicylaldehyde

Explanation: The Reimer-Tiemann reaction introduces a -CHO group ortho to the -OH group of phenol, forming salicylaldehyde.

6. Ethers are relatively less reactive compared to alcohols mainly because they:

  • A. Lack a reactive hydrogen atom attached to oxygen
  • B. Contain reactive double bonds
  • C. Are highly polar
  • D. Have an ionic C-O bond

Answer: A. Lack a reactive hydrogen atom attached to oxygen

Explanation: Since the oxygen in ethers is bonded to two carbon groups rather than hydrogen, ethers cannot undergo reactions typical of the O-H bond.

7. Kolbe's reaction converts sodium phenoxide into:

  • A. Salicylic acid
  • B. Benzoic acid
  • C. Picric acid
  • D. Salicylaldehyde

Answer: A. Salicylic acid

Explanation: Sodium phenoxide reacts with CO2 under pressure, followed by acidification, to give salicylic acid via Kolbe's reaction.

8. Acid-catalyzed dehydration of alcohols to alkenes generally follows:

  • A. Zaitsev's rule via an E1 mechanism
  • B. Markovnikov's rule for addition
  • C. Anti-Markovnikov's rule
  • D. An SN2 mechanism

Answer: A. Zaitsev's rule via an E1 mechanism

Explanation: Dehydration typically proceeds through a carbocation intermediate (E1), favoring the more substituted (more stable) alkene as per Zaitsev's rule.

9. Which of the following is the most acidic compound?

  • A. p-Nitrophenol
  • B. Phenol
  • C. p-Cresol
  • D. Ethanol

Answer: A. p-Nitrophenol

Explanation: The electron-withdrawing nitro group at the para position further stabilizes the phenoxide ion by resonance, making p-nitrophenol more acidic than phenol.

10. Heating ethanol with concentrated H2SO4 at about 413 K primarily gives:

  • A. Ethene
  • B. Diethyl ether
  • C. Ethanal
  • D. Ethanoic acid

Answer: A. Ethene

Explanation: At higher temperature (413 K), concentrated H2SO4 favors dehydration of ethanol to ethene; lower temperatures instead favor ether formation.

11. Anisole is an example of an:

  • A. Aromatic ether
  • B. Aliphatic alcohol
  • C. Aromatic alcohol
  • D. Aliphatic ether

Answer: A. Aromatic ether

Explanation: Anisole (methoxybenzene) has a methyl group attached via oxygen to a benzene ring, making it an aromatic ether.

12. Victor Meyer's test is used to identify whether an alcohol is:

  • A. Primary, secondary or tertiary
  • B. Aromatic or aliphatic
  • C. Saturated or unsaturated
  • D. Acidic or basic

Answer: A. Primary, secondary or tertiary

Explanation: The Victor Meyer test uses a series of colour changes to distinguish primary, secondary and tertiary alcohols.

Ch-13: Our Environment 30 Questions

1. Which of the following is a biodegradable substance?

  • A. Plastic bottles
  • B. Glass pieces
  • C. Cotton cloth
  • D. DDT

Answer: C. Cotton cloth

Explanation: Cotton cloth is derived from plant sources and can be decomposed naturally by micro-organisms like bacteria and fungi.

2. Organisms that synthesize their own food using inorganic substances are called:

  • A. Herbivores
  • B. Autotrophs (Producers)
  • C. Decomposers
  • D. Carnivores

Answer: B. Autotrophs (Producers)

Explanation: Producers, such as green plants and photosynthetic bacteria, convert inorganic solar energy and CO2 into organic nutrients.

3. According to Lindeman's 10% law, how much energy is transferred from one trophic level to the next higher level?

  • A. 1%
  • B. 10%
  • C. 50%
  • D. 90%

Answer: B. 10%

Explanation: Only 10% of the energy present at a particular trophic level is available for transfer to the next higher trophic level; 90% is lost in metabolic processes and heat.

4. Green plants in a terrestrial ecosystem capture about what percentage of the energy of sunlight that falls on their leaves?

  • A. 1%
  • B. 10%
  • C. 25%
  • D. 50%

Answer: A. 1%

Explanation: Green plants capture only about 1% of total solar energy hitting their leaves and convert it into chemical energy through photosynthesis.

5. Chemical formula of Ozone molecule is:

  • A. O
  • B. O₂
  • C. O₃
  • D. O₄

Answer: C. O₃

Explanation: Ozone is a molecule formed by three atoms of oxygen bound together (O₃).

6. The ozone layer protects the Earth's surface from which harmful radiation?

  • A. Infrared radiation
  • B. Ultraviolet (UV) radiation
  • C. X-rays
  • D. Gamma rays

Answer: B. Ultraviolet (UV) radiation

Explanation: Ozone shields the earth by absorbing harmful UV radiation from the sun, which can cause skin cancer and cataracts.

7. Which synthetic chemical compound is primarily responsible for the depletion of the ozone layer?

  • A. Chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs)
  • B. Carbon dioxide
  • C. Methane
  • D. Nitrous oxide

Answer: A. Chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs)

Explanation: CFCs used in refrigerants and fire extinguishers release chlorine atoms in the stratosphere, destroying ozone molecules.

8. In 1987, which organization forged an agreement to freeze CFC production at 1986 levels?

  • A. WHO
  • B. UNEP
  • C. UNESCO
  • D. WWF

Answer: B. UNEP

Explanation: UNEP (United Nations Environment Programme) succeeded in forging the Montreal Protocol agreement to limit CFC production globally.

9. The phenomenon of progressive accumulation of non-biodegradable toxic chemicals at each successive trophic level is known as:

  • A. Eutrophication
  • B. Biological Magnification
  • C. Bio-degradation
  • D. Bio-accumulation

Answer: B. Biological Magnification

Explanation: Non-degradable chemicals like pesticides cannot be metabolized and thus accumulate in increasing concentrations up the food chain.

10. In a food chain consisting of Grass → Deer → Tiger, which organism will have the maximum concentration of harmful non-biodegradable chemicals?

  • A. Grass
  • B. Deer
  • C. Tiger
  • D. Equal in all three

Answer: C. Tiger

Explanation: Due to biological magnification, the organism at the topmost trophic level (Tiger) accumulates the highest concentration of toxic chemicals.

11. Decomposers in an ecosystem are responsible for:

  • A. Converting organic matter into inorganic substances
  • B. Converting inorganic substance into organic matter
  • C. Converting solar energy into chemical energy
  • D. Fixing atmospheric nitrogen into plants

Answer: A. Converting organic matter into inorganic substances

Explanation: Bacteria and fungi break down dead plant/animal remains, returning inorganic nutrients back to soil and atmosphere.

12. Which of the following forms an artificial (man-made) ecosystem?

  • A. Forest
  • B. Lake
  • C. Crop field
  • D. Pond

Answer: C. Crop field

Explanation: Crop fields, aquariums, and gardens are human-maintained ecosystems, whereas forests and lakes are natural ecosystems.

13. Movement of energy in an ecosystem is always:

  • A. Unidirectional
  • B. Bidirectional
  • C. Multidirectional
  • D. Cyclic

Answer: A. Unidirectional

Explanation: Energy flows one way—from sun to producers, to herbivores, and then to carnivores. It never flows backwards.

14. What are the biotic components of an ecosystem?

  • A. Temperature, water, and soil
  • B. Air, sunlight, and minerals
  • C. Plants, animals, and microorganisms
  • D. Rainfall and humidity

Answer: C. Plants, animals, and microorganisms

Explanation: Biotic components consist of all living organisms, whereas abiotic components consist of non-living physical factors.

15. The network of interconnected food chains operating in an ecosystem is called a:

  • A. Food web
  • B. Trophic system
  • C. Ecological pyramid
  • D. Ecosystem cycle

Answer: A. Food web

Explanation: A food web represents multiple interconnected feeding pathways, offering alternative food choices in an ecosystem.

16. Ozone is formed at higher levels of the atmosphere by the action of UV rays on:

  • A. Oxygen molecules (O₂)
  • B. Carbon dioxide (CO₂)
  • C. Nitrogen gas (N₂)
  • D. Water vapor (H₂O)

Answer: A. Oxygen molecules (O₂)

Explanation: UV radiation splits O₂ molecules into free oxygen atoms (O + O), which then combine with molecular oxygen (O₂ + O → O₃).

17. Which of the following is a non-biodegradable pollutant?

  • A. Vegetable peels
  • B. Paper
  • C. Plastic bag
  • D. Animal dung

Answer: C. Plastic bag

Explanation: Plastic bags cannot be broken down by biological processes and persist in the environment for hundreds of years.

18. The first trophic level in any food chain is always represented by:

  • A. Primary consumers
  • B. Producers
  • C. Decomposers
  • D. Secondary consumers

Answer: B. Producers

Explanation: Producers form the base (1st trophic level) of every ecological food chain by trapping solar energy.

19. If 20,000 J of energy is available at the producer level, how much energy will be available to secondary consumers?

  • A. 2,000 J
  • B. 200 J
  • C. 20 J
  • D. 2 J

Answer: B. 200 J

Explanation: Producer (20,000 J) → Primary consumer (10% = 2,000 J) → Secondary consumer (10% = 200 J).

20. Disposable kulhads (clay cups) were replaced by paper cups in trains primarily because:

  • A. Paper cups are cheaper to produce
  • B. Making clay cups on a large scale causes loss of fertile topsoil
  • C. Clay cups are harmful to health
  • D. Paper cups hold hot tea longer

Answer: B. Making clay cups on a large scale causes loss of fertile topsoil

Explanation: Mass production of clay cups requires removing large quantities of topsoil, leading to soil degradation.

21. Carnivores occupying the third trophic level are classified as:

  • A. Producers
  • B. Primary consumers
  • C. Secondary consumers
  • D. Decomposers

Answer: C. Secondary consumers

Explanation: Level 1 = Producers, Level 2 = Herbivores (Primary Consumers), Level 3 = Carnivores (Secondary Consumers).

22. Which of the following statements about food chains is FALSE?

  • A. Food chains usually consist of 3 to 4 steps
  • B. Energy flow is circular and reversible
  • C. Loss of energy occurs at each step
  • D. Producers are at the first trophic level

Answer: B. Energy flow is circular and reversible

Explanation: Energy flow is non-cyclic and strictly unidirectional. Once energy is dissipated as heat, it cannot be reused by previous levels.

23. Microorganisms like bacteria and fungi are called decomposers because they:

  • A. Produce complex organic matter
  • B. Break down complex organic matter into simple inorganic substances
  • C. Synthesize food in the presence of sunlight
  • D. Consume live animals

Answer: B. Break down complex organic matter into simple inorganic substances

Explanation: Decomposers secrete enzymes to break down organic waste into simpler inorganic nutrients absorbed into soil.

24. What happens if all decomposers are completely removed from an ecosystem?

  • A. Herbivores will increase rapidly
  • B. Dead remains will accumulate and nutrient cycling will stop
  • C. Producers will grow much faster
  • D. Energy flow will increase

Answer: B. Dead remains will accumulate and nutrient cycling will stop

Explanation: Without decomposers, organic matter remains un-decomposed, blocking soil nutrient replenishment necessary for producers.

25. Accumulation of non-biodegradable waste in environment causes:

  • A. Enrichment of soil fertility
  • B. Environmental pollution and ecological imbalance
  • C. Rapid increase in biodiversity
  • D. Increase in atmospheric oxygen

Answer: B. Environmental pollution and ecological imbalance

Explanation: Non-biodegradable items persist for long periods, harming wildlife, polluting soil/water, and disrupting ecological equilibrium.

26. Which chemical reaction represents the formation of ozone in the upper atmosphere?

  • A. O₂ + UV → O + O, and O + O₂ → O₃
  • B. O₂ + O₂ → O₄
  • C. H₂O + O₂ → O₃ + H₂
  • D. CO₂ + O → O₃ + C

Answer: A. O₂ + UV → O + O, and O + O₂ → O₃

Explanation: High energy UV splits molecular oxygen into atomic oxygen, which combines with O₂ to synthesize ozone (O₃).

27. Human beings occupy which position in any food chain?

  • A. Only Producers
  • B. Only Primary Consumers
  • C. Omnivores at Top Trophic Levels
  • D. Decomposers

Answer: C. Omnivores at Top Trophic Levels

Explanation: Humans consume both plant products and meat, placing them as omnivores at higher trophic levels in various food chains.

28. Which of the following is an abiotic component of an ecosystem?

  • A. Bacteria
  • B. Fungi
  • C. Temperature
  • D. Earthworms

Answer: C. Temperature

Explanation: Temperature, pressure, soil, water, and sunlight are abiotic (non-living) physical factors.

29. Why are food chains generally limited to 3 or 4 trophic levels?

  • A. Organisms run out of space
  • B. Very little usable energy remains available after 3-4 levels
  • C. Top carnivores stop reproducing
  • D. Decomposers eat all energy at 4th level

Answer: B. Very little usable energy remains available after 3-4 levels

Explanation: Because 90% energy is lost at each step, after 3-4 levels the remaining energy is insufficient to support another organism level.

30. An aquarium needs regular cleaning, whereas a natural pond does not. Why?

  • A. Aquariums have more decomposers than ponds
  • B. Aquarium is an incomplete artificial ecosystem with lack of natural decomposers
  • C. Fish in aquariums produce more waste
  • D. Ponds do not contain fish

Answer: B. Aquarium is an incomplete artificial ecosystem with lack of natural decomposers

Explanation: Ponds have self-sustaining decomposer communities to clean waste, whereas aquariums lack natural nutrient cycling and require artificial cleaning.

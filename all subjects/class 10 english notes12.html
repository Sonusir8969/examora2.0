Class 10 English Grammar - Tenses and Modals

This chapter covers the essential grammar concepts of Tenses and Modals for Class 10 English, including their forms, uses, and common rules, along with practice questions and answers.

  • 3. Common Errors and Rules to Remember

1. Tenses - Overview and Uses

Short Note:

• Present Tense: Used to describe habitual actions (Simple Present), actions happening now (Present Continuous), and actions completed in the recent past with present relevance (Present Perfect).

• Past Tense: Used to describe completed actions in the past (Simple Past), ongoing actions in the past (Past Continuous), and actions completed before another past action (Past Perfect).

• Future Tense: Used to describe actions that will happen (Simple Future), actions that will be ongoing at a future point (Future Continuous), and actions that will be completed before a specific future time (Future Perfect).

2. Modals - Overview and Uses

Short Note:

• Can/Could: Used to express ability, possibility, or to make polite requests, with 'could' often used for past ability or more polite/formal requests.

• May/Might: Used to express possibility or to seek/give permission, with 'might' indicating a weaker or more uncertain possibility than 'may'.

• Must/Should/Ought to: Used to express necessity, strong obligation or advice, with 'must' indicating strong obligation and 'should'/'ought to' indicating recommendation or moral duty.

• Will/Would/Shall: Used to express future intention, willingness, promises, or in the case of 'would', polite requests and hypothetical situations.

3. Common Errors and Rules to Remember

Short Note:

• Avoid mixing tenses incorrectly within the same sentence unless the context genuinely requires a shift, such as when describing a past habit versus a present fact.

• Modal verbs are always followed by the base form of the main verb without 'to' (e.g., 'she can swim', not 'she can to swim').

• Pay attention to subject-verb agreement in present tense sentences, ensuring third-person singular subjects take the correct verb form (e.g., 'he goes', not 'he go').

Practice Questions on Tenses

Q1: Fill in the blank with the correct tense: 'She ___ (finish) her homework before dinner.'
Answer: 'She had finished her homework before dinner.' This uses the Past Perfect tense because the action of finishing homework was completed before another past action (dinner).
Q2: Identify the tense: 'They will be travelling to Delhi next week.'
Answer: This sentence is in the Future Continuous tense, used to describe an action that will be ongoing at a specific point in the future.
Q3: Correct the error: 'He is knowing the answer.'
Answer: The correct sentence is 'He knows the answer.' Verbs expressing states of mind or knowledge, like 'know', are generally not used in the continuous form.

Practice Questions on Modals

Q4: Choose the correct modal: 'You ___ finish your project by tomorrow.' (must/might)
Answer: 'You must finish your project by tomorrow.' 'Must' is used here to express strong necessity or obligation regarding the deadline.
Q5: Rewrite using an appropriate modal: 'It is possible that it will rain today.'
Answer: 'It might rain today.' The modal 'might' is used to express a possibility that is uncertain, matching the meaning of the original sentence.

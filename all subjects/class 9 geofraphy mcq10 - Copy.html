Chapter 4: Climate 30 Questions

1. What does the term 'Climate' refer to?

  • A. The daily temperature
  • B. The average weather conditions over a long period
  • C. The rainfall of a single day
  • D. The wind speed on a specific day

Answer: B. The average weather conditions over a long period

Explanation: Climate is the average state of weather conditions at a place over a long span of time, typically 30 years.

2. India's climate is broadly classified as what type?

  • A. Equatorial Climate
  • B. Mediterranean Climate
  • C. Tropical Monsoon Climate
  • D. Desert Climate

Answer: C. Tropical Monsoon Climate

Explanation: Due to its location near the Tropic of Cancer and the influence of the Himalayas, India has a tropical monsoon climate.

3. Which is the wettest place on Earth located in India?

  • A. Mawsynram
  • B. Cherrapunji
  • C. Mumbai
  • D. Shillong

Answer: A. Mawsynram

Explanation: Mawsynram, located in Meghalaya, is the wettest place on Earth, receiving one of the highest average annual rainfalls.

4. The southwest monsoon arrives in India around which month?

  • A. January
  • B. June
  • C. October
  • D. March

Answer: B. June

Explanation: The southwest monsoon, which brings the majority of India's rainfall, normally reaches Kerala by the first week of June.

5. Which is the driest part of India?

  • A. Rajasthan
  • B. Gujarat
  • C. Madhya Pradesh
  • D. Haryana

Answer: A. Rajasthan

Explanation: Western Rajasthan is the driest region in India, receiving extremely low and erratic rainfall due to the presence of the Thar Desert.

6. The monsoon in India is usually associated with which type of wind?

  • A. Easterly winds
  • B. Westerly winds
  • C. Seasonal reversal of winds
  • D. Constant trade winds

Answer: C. Seasonal reversal of winds

Explanation: The term 'monsoon' refers to the seasonal reversal of winds, which blow from the southwest during summer and from the northeast during winter.

7. The Himalayas play a vital role in India's climate by acting as what?

  • A. A barrier against cold winds from Central Asia
  • B. A barrier against hot winds from the south
  • C. A source of dust storms
  • D. An ocean current barrier

Answer: A. A barrier against cold winds from Central Asia

Explanation: The Himalayas act as a formidable climatic barrier, protecting northern India from the freezing, dry winds that blow south from Central Asia.

8. Which place in India has recorded the highest temperature ever?

  • A. Jaisalmer
  • B. Ganganagar
  • C. Alwar
  • D. Ahmedabad

Answer: B. Ganganagar

Explanation: Ganganagar in Rajasthan holds the record for the highest temperature (50°C) ever recorded in India.

9. Which of the following factors DOES NOT influence India's climate?

  • A. Latitude
  • B. Altitude
  • C. Longitude
  • D. Pressure and Winds

Answer: C. Longitude

Explanation: While latitude, altitude, and pressure systems heavily influence climate, longitude has only a minimal effect (affecting time zones).

10. The retreating monsoon season in India occurs in which months?

  • A. April to June
  • B. October to November
  • C. December to February
  • D. July to September

Answer: B. October to November

Explanation: The period from October to November is known as the retreating monsoon season when the south-west monsoon winds withdraw from India.

11. In which season does India experience frequent cyclones in the Bay of Bengal?

  • A. Winter season
  • B. Retreating monsoon
  • C. Summer season
  • D. Advancing monsoon

Answer: B. Retreating monsoon

Explanation: The retreating monsoon season is notorious for causing violent tropical cyclones in the Bay of Bengal, heavily affecting the eastern coast.

12. 'Norwesters' are violent thunderstorms that occur in which state of India?

  • A. Gujarat
  • B. West Bengal and Assam
  • C. Kerala
  • D. Tamil Nadu

Answer: B. West Bengal and Assam

Explanation: Norwesters are intense local thunderstorms that frequently hit West Bengal and Assam during the pre-monsoon period.

13. The winter rainfall in Tamil Nadu is brought by which winds?

  • A. Southwest Monsoon
  • B. Northeast Monsoon (Retreating)
  • C. Western Disturbances
  • D. Jet Streams

Answer: B. Northeast Monsoon (Retreating)

Explanation: Tamil Nadu receives heavy rainfall in winter (Oct-Dec) from the retreating northeast monsoon winds that blow over the Bay of Bengal.

14. What is the ITCZ (Inter-Tropical Convergence Zone) associated with?

  • A. A zone of high pressure
  • B. A zone of low pressure near the equator
  • C. A desert region
  • D. A cold front

Answer: B. A zone of low pressure near the equator

Explanation: The ITCZ is a low-pressure belt at the equator where the trade winds converge, often leading to heavy rainfall.

15. The term 'Mango Shower' refers to which natural phenomenon in India?

  • A. Rain that helps mangoes ripen in Kerala
  • B. Summer rainfall in Tamil Nadu
  • C. Winter snowfall in the Himalayas
  • D. Dust storms in Rajasthan

Answer: A. Rain that helps mangoes ripen in Kerala

Explanation: 'Mango showers' are pre-monsoon rainfalls in Kerala and coastal areas that promote the ripening of mangoes.

16. In India, the word 'Monsoon' is derived from which Arabic word?

  • A. Mausam
  • B. Mousim
  • C. Monsoon
  • D. Monza

Answer: A. Mausam

Explanation: The word monsoon is derived from the Arabic word 'Mausam', which means season, reflecting the seasonal reversal of winds.

17. The southwest monsoon is divided into two main branches. These are:

  • A. The Bay of Bengal branch and the Arabian Sea branch
  • B. The Himalayan branch and the Ganga branch
  • C. The eastern branch and the western branch
  • D. The north and south branches

Answer: A. The Bay of Bengal branch and the Arabian Sea branch

Explanation: The SW monsoon splits into two branches: one moves over the Bay of Bengal towards NE India, while the other moves over the Arabian Sea towards the west coast.

18. Which geographical feature causes the 'Mawsynram' and 'Cherrapunji' to receive such enormous rainfall?

  • A. The Western Ghats
  • B. The Khasi Hills
  • C. The Himalayas
  • D. The Thar Desert

Answer: B. The Khasi Hills

Explanation: The funnel-shaped Khasi Hills in Meghalaya trap moisture-laden clouds, causing immense orographic rainfall in Mawsynram and Cherrapunji.

19. The 'Rain Shadow' region in India receives very little rainfall. Which area is a prime example?

  • A. The eastern slopes of the Western Ghats
  • B. The western slopes of the Western Ghats
  • C. The Northern Plains
  • D. The Gangetic Delta

Answer: A. The eastern slopes of the Western Ghats

Explanation: The eastern side (rain shadow) of the Western Ghats receives much less rainfall than the western coast because the clouds are forced to release rain on the windward side.

20. The 'Cool Season' or Winter season in India spans which months?

  • A. October to March
  • B. June to September
  • C. December to February
  • D. March to May

Answer: C. December to February

Explanation: The winter or cool season in India generally lasts from the month of December to February.

21. Which of the following is a feature of the 'Hot Weather Season' in India?

  • A. Snowfall in Rajasthan
  • B. Dust storms and local thunderstorms
  • C. Heavy rainfall in the north
  • D. Cyclones in the Bay of Bengal

Answer: B. Dust storms and local thunderstorms

Explanation: During the hot summer season (March to May), northern India experiences severe local storms, dust storms, and thunderstorms.

22. The distribution of rainfall in India is highly uneven. Which factor is primarily responsible for this?

  • A. The location of the Himalayas
  • B. The direction of the monsoon winds
  • C. The presence of deserts
  • D. The latitude of India

Answer: B. The direction of the monsoon winds

Explanation: The monsoon winds, which are erratic and erratic in their direction, are the primary reason for the highly uneven rainfall distribution.

23. What is the impact of the 'Jet Streams' on India's climate?

  • A. They bring the southwest monsoon
  • B. They influence the western disturbances
  • C. They cause winter snowfall in the Himalayas
  • D. They bring cyclones to the coast

Answer: B. They influence the western disturbances

Explanation: The subtropical westerly jet streams control the movement of western disturbances, which bring winter rainfall to north-west India.

24. The average annual rainfall in India is approximately how much?

  • A. 50 cm
  • B. 100 cm
  • C. 125 cm
  • D. 200 cm

Answer: C. 125 cm

Explanation: The average annual rainfall in India is about 125 cm (1,250 mm), though it varies heavily from region to region.

25. Which part of India experiences 'Cyclonic Depression' during the monsoon season?

  • A. Western Ghats
  • B. The Bay of Bengal and the Arabian Sea
  • C. The Himalayas
  • D. The deserts of Rajasthan

Answer: B. The Bay of Bengal and the Arabian Sea

Explanation: Low-pressure cyclonic depressions frequently originate in the Bay of Bengal and the Arabian Sea and travel inland during the monsoon.

26. Which soil type receives the least rainfall, resulting in a desert?

  • A. Alluvial soil
  • B. Black soil
  • C. Arid soil
  • D. Laterite soil

Answer: C. Arid soil

Explanation: Arid soil is found in desert regions like Rajasthan, where rainfall is extremely low and the soil is sandy and saline.

27. The 'El Nino' phenomenon has a significant impact on which aspect of India's climate?

  • A. Increased snowfall
  • B. Reduction in monsoon rainfall
  • C. A drop in temperature
  • D. Increase in western disturbances

Answer: B. Reduction in monsoon rainfall

Explanation: El Nino (the warming of the Pacific Ocean) is often linked to weaker and erratic monsoons, leading to droughts in India.

28. The 'Arabian Sea' branch of the monsoon brings heavy rainfall to which region?

  • A. Kerala, Karnataka, and Maharashtra
  • B. West Bengal and Assam
  • C. Tamil Nadu
  • D. Rajasthan

Answer: A. Kerala, Karnataka, and Maharashtra

Explanation: The Arabian Sea branch strikes the western coast of India, causing heavy orographic rainfall along the Western Ghats.

29. What is the effect of the 'Jet Stream' on the advance of the SW monsoon?

  • A. It delays the monsoon
  • B. It accelerates the monsoon's arrival
  • C. It changes the direction of the monsoon to the north
  • D. It stops the monsoon

Answer: B. It accelerates the monsoon's arrival

Explanation: The shift of the easterly jet stream south of the Himalayas triggers the sudden onset of the southwest monsoon over the Indian subcontinent.

30. The term 'La Nina' refers to what climate event?

  • A. The cooling of Pacific Ocean waters
  • B. The warming of Pacific Ocean waters
  • C. A sudden rise in sea levels
  • D. A massive glacier melt

Answer: A. The cooling of Pacific Ocean waters

Explanation: La Nina is the opposite of El Nino; it involves the cooling of Pacific waters and often leads to stronger monsoons in India.

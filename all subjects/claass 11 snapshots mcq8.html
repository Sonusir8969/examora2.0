Suppl-8: The Tale of Melon City 25 Questions

1. Who wrote the poem 'The Tale of Melon City'?

  • A. Vikram Seth
  • B. A.J. Cronin
  • C. Amitav Ghosh
  • D. William Saroyan

Answer: A. Vikram Seth

Explanation: 'The Tale of Melon City' is a humorous narrative poem composed by famous Indian poet and novelist Vikram Seth.

2. How is the King described at the beginning of the poem?

  • A. Placid and just
  • B. Cruel and strict
  • C. Wise and powerful
  • D. Greedy and foolish

Answer: A. Placid and just

Explanation: The king is humorously described as "placid" (calm) and "just" by the poet.

3. What did the King order to be constructed across the major thoroughfare?

  • A. An arch
  • B. A bridge
  • C. A high wall
  • D. A grand palace

Answer: A. An arch

Explanation: The King proclaimed that an arch should be built spanning the main road to enlighten spectators.

4. What happened when the King rode under the newly built arch?

  • A. His crown was knocked off because the arch was built too low
  • B. The arch collapsed completely
  • C. His horse tripped and fell
  • D. People showered flowers on him

Answer: A. His crown was knocked off because the arch was built too low

Explanation: The arch was constructed too low, causing the King's crown to strike against it and fall off into the dust.

5. How did the King feel when his crown fell off?

  • A. He lost his placid composure and felt disgraced
  • B. He laughed it off
  • C. He ignored it and moved on
  • D. He cried loudly

Answer: A. He lost his placid composure and felt disgraced

Explanation: The King felt dishonored and immediately lost his calm demeanor, demanding that someone be hanged for it.

6. Whom did the King first order to be hanged?

  • A. The chief of builders
  • B. The workmen
  • C. The masons
  • D. The architect

Answer: A. The chief of builders

Explanation: The King initially ordered the chief of builders to be hanged for constructing a faulty arch.

7. How did the chief of builders defend himself?

  • A. He blamed the workmen
  • B. He blamed the architect
  • C. He blamed the bad quality cement
  • D. He blamed the King

Answer: A. He blamed the workmen

Explanation: The chief of builders stated that it was the fault of the workmen who constructed the arch.

8. Whom did the workmen blame when called by the King?

  • A. The masons who made the wrong sized bricks
  • B. The chief of builders
  • C. The architect
  • D. The rain

Answer: A. The masons who made the wrong sized bricks

Explanation: The workmen shifted the blame onto the masons for laying bricks of incorrect dimensions.

9. Whom did the masons blame for the wrong brick size?

  • A. The architect
  • B. The chief of builders
  • C. The King himself
  • D. The royal guards

Answer: A. The architect

Explanation: The masons claimed that they only followed the design plan drawn up by the architect.

10. How did the architect turn the blame back onto the King?

  • A. He reminded the King that he had made amendments to the original plans
  • B. He accused the King of being drunk
  • C. He said the King ordered smaller bricks
  • D. He denied drawing the map

Answer: A. He reminded the King that he had made amendments to the original plans

Explanation: The architect cleverly pointed out that the King himself had altered the architectural blueprints when shown to him.

11. What did the King do when he was blamed by the architect?

  • A. He nearly lost his mind and demanded counsel from the wisest man in the realm
  • B. He immediately hanged the architect
  • C. He pardoned everyone
  • D. He resigned from his throne

Answer: A. He nearly lost his mind and demanded counsel from the wisest man in the realm

Explanation: Confused and trapped by logic, the King called for the wisest man in the country to guide him.

12. Who was chosen as the 'wisest man' in the country?

  • A. An old man who could neither walk nor see
  • B. The court priest
  • C. The oldest school teacher
  • D. A travelling scholar

Answer: A. An old man who could neither walk nor see

Explanation: The people selected the oldest man assuming age equals wisdom; he was so old he couldn't walk or see.

13. What verdict did the 'wisest man' give to resolve the matter?

  • A. The arch itself was the real culprit and must be hanged
  • B. The King should be hanged
  • C. All builders must be executed
  • D. The case should be dismissed

Answer: A. The arch itself was the real culprit and must be hanged

Explanation: In his nonsensical wisdom, the old man declared that since the arch knocked off the crown, the arch itself must be hanged.

14. Why was the arch NOT hanged in the end?

  • A. A councillor pointed out that hanging something that touched the royal head would be a disgrace
  • B. The gallows broke into pieces
  • C. The rope was too short
  • D. People protested against hanging stone

Answer: A. A councillor pointed out that hanging something that touched the royal head would be a disgrace

Explanation: A councillor noted that it would be sacrilege to hang an object that had touched the King's sacred head.

15. Why did the crowd begin to grow restless?

  • A. They demanded a hanging immediately
  • B. They wanted the King to lower taxes
  • C. They wanted free food
  • D. They were bored of court speeches

Answer: A. They demanded a hanging immediately

Explanation: The public wanted spectacle and execution, so the King realized someone must be hanged to satisfy them.

16. How was the person to be hanged finally selected?

  • A. Everyone was measured against the gallows, and whoever fitted the height would be hanged
  • B. By drawing secret lots
  • C. By public voting
  • D. The tallest person in town was chosen

Answer: A. Everyone was measured against the gallows, and whoever fitted the height would be hanged

Explanation: The gallows was set up, and each man was measured; only one person was tall enough to fit the noose perfectly.

17. Who was the only person who fitted the gallows perfectly?

  • A. The King himself
  • B. The Architect
  • C. The Chief of Builders
  • D. The Minister

Answer: A. The King himself

Explanation: Ironically, only the King was tall enough to reach the high noose, so he was hanged by his own decree.

18. What custom did the ministers follow to select the new ruler after the King's death?

  • A. The first person to pass through the city arch would choose the new king
  • B. The minister's son would automatically become king
  • C. Holding a general election
  • D. A sword fighting tournament

Answer: A. The first person to pass through the city arch would choose the new king

Explanation: According to ancient custom, the next traveler passing through the arch was empowered to name the ruler.

19. Who was the first person to pass under the arch after the execution?

  • A. An idiot who loved melons
  • B. A wise merchant
  • C. A beggar
  • D. A foreign prince

Answer: A. An idiot who loved melons

Explanation: An idiot passed by whose standard answer to every single question was "A melon."

20. What answer did the idiot give when asked who should be the King?

  • A. A Melon
  • B. Himself
  • C. The Prime Minister
  • D. Nobody

Answer: A. A Melon

Explanation: True to his habit, the idiot replied "A melon," which the ministers accepted without question.

21. What was crowned as the new King of the state with full ceremony?

  • A. A Melon
  • B. A statue
  • C. The idiot's dog
  • D. A wooden chair

Answer: A. A Melon

Explanation: A melon was respectfully carried to the throne and officially crowned as the new ruler.

22. How did the citizens feel about having a Melon as their King?

  • A. Perfectly satisfied, as long as they enjoyed peace and non-interference (Laissez-faire)
  • B. Ashamed and angry
  • C. Terrified
  • D. Sad and depressed

Answer: A. Perfectly satisfied, as long as they enjoyed peace and non-interference (Laissez-faire)

Explanation: The people didn't care who ruled them as long as the government left them alone to live in peace and liberty.

23. What political concept is satirized in 'The Tale of Melon City'?

  • A. Blind reverence for authority, mindless bureaucracy, and Laissez-faire governance
  • B. Military dictatorship
  • C. Communism
  • D. Democracy and voting rights

Answer: A. Blind reverence for authority, mindless bureaucracy, and Laissez-faire governance

Explanation: The poem uses absurdity to critique foolish governance, shifting responsibilities, and public apathy.

24. What does the principle of 'Laissez-faire' mentioned in the poem mean?

  • A. Policy of minimal government interference in people's lives
  • B. Strict law enforcement
  • C. High tax collection
  • D. Complete military rule

Answer: A. Policy of minimal government interference in people's lives

Explanation: 'Laissez-faire' refers to a state of non-interference where citizens are left alone to do as they wish.

25. What genre of literature does 'The Tale of Melon City' belong to?

  • A. Narrative Verse / Political Satire
  • B. Historical Biography
  • C. Tragic Monologue
  • D. Gothic Horror

Answer: A. Narrative Verse / Political Satire

Explanation: It is a narrative satirical poem that uses humor and ironical allegory to mock foolish rulers and systems.

Ch-5: Forest and Wildlife Resources 30 Questions

1. According to the International Union for Conservation of Nature (IUCN), species whose population levels are normal for their survival are categorized as:

  • A. Normal species
  • B. Endangered species
  • C. Vulnerable species
  • D. Rare species

Answer: A. Normal species

Explanation: Normal species are those whose population levels are considered normal for their survival, such as cattle, sal, pine, and rodents.

2. Species that are in danger of extinction if the adverse factors continuing their decline are not corrected are known as:

  • A. Endangered species
  • B. Rare species
  • C. Extinct species
  • D. Endemic species

Answer: A. Endangered species

Explanation: Endangered species are species which are in danger of extinction, such as the Black buck, Crocodile, Indian wild ass, and Indian rhino.

3. Which of the following is an example of an Endemic species found exclusively in isolated natural geographic areas?

  • A. Andaman teal
  • B. Asiatic cheetah
  • C. Pink-headed duck
  • D. Blue sheep

Answer: A. Andaman teal

Explanation: Endemic species are species which are only found in some particular areas usually isolated by natural or geographical barriers, such as Andaman teal, Nicobar pigeon, and Andaman wild pig.

4. Species that are not found after searches of known or likely areas where they may occur are classified as:

  • A. Extinct species
  • B. Vulnerable species
  • C. Rare species
  • D. Endemic species

Answer: A. Extinct species

Explanation: Extinct species are species which are not found after searches of known or likely areas where they may occur, such as the Asiatic cheetah and Pink-headed duck.

5. In which year was the Asiatic cheetah declared extinct in India?

  • A. 1952
  • B. 1972
  • C. 1980
  • D. 1991

Answer: A. 1952

Explanation: The Asiatic cheetah was declared extinct in India long ago in the year 1952 due to habitat loss and hunting.

6. What was the single largest factor for the clearance and loss of forest cover in India between 1951 and 1980?

  • A. Expansion of agricultural land
  • B. River valley development projects
  • C. Mining activities
  • D. Urbanization and housing

Answer: A. Expansion of agricultural land

Explanation: According to Forest Survey of India, between 1951 and 1980, over 26,200 sq. km of forest area was converted into agricultural land across India.

7. The Buxa Tiger Reserve in West Bengal is seriously threatened by which of the following activities?

  • A. Dolomite mining
  • B. Coal extraction
  • C. Tea plantation development
  • D. Railway track construction

Answer: A. Dolomite mining

Explanation: The Buxa Tiger Reserve in West Bengal is seriously threatened by ongoing dolomite mining in the surrounding region.

8. Which medicinal plant found in Himachal Pradesh and Arunachal Pradesh yields a chemical compound called 'taxol' used to treat cancer?

  • A. Himalayan Yew
  • B. Neem
  • C. Tulsi
  • D. Sarpagandha

Answer: A. Himalayan Yew

Explanation: The Himalayan Yew (Taxus wallichiana) is a medicinal plant. A chemical compound called 'taxol' is extracted from its bark, needles, and roots to manufacture anti-cancer drugs.

9. In which year was the Indian Wildlife (Protection) Act implemented to protect habitats and endangered species?

  • A. 1972
  • B. 1952
  • C. 1986
  • D. 1992

Answer: A. 1972

Explanation: The Indian Wildlife (Protection) Act was implemented in 1972, with various provisions for protecting habitats and listing protected species all over India.

10. 'Project Tiger', one of the well-publicized wildlife campaigns in the world, was launched by the Government of India in which year?

  • A. 1973
  • B. 1981
  • C. 1965
  • D. 1995

Answer: A. 1973

Explanation: 'Project Tiger' was launched in 1973 as a major wildlife conservation initiative to save the declining population of tigers in India.

11. In which state of India is the famous Corbett National Park located?

  • A. Uttarakhand
  • B. Madhya Pradesh
  • C. Assam
  • D. Rajasthan

Answer: A. Uttarakhand

Explanation: Corbett National Park is a prominent tiger reserve located in the state of Uttarakhand.

12. Which national park in Assam is famous for protecting the One-horned Rhinoceros?

  • A. Kaziranga National Park
  • B. Sundarbans National Park
  • C. Bandhavgarh National Park
  • D. Periyar Tiger Reserve

Answer: A. Kaziranga National Park

Explanation: Kaziranga National Park in Assam is world-famous for its conservation of the Great Indian One-horned Rhinoceros.

13. The Sariska Tiger Reserve is located in which state of India?

  • A. Rajasthan
  • B. Gujarat
  • C. Maharashtra
  • D. Punjab

Answer: A. Rajasthan

Explanation: Sariska Tiger Reserve is located in the Alwar district of Rajasthan.

14. Forests that are regarded as the most valuable as far as the conservation of forest and wildlife resources are concerned are classified as:

  • A. Reserved Forests
  • B. Protected Forests
  • C. Unclassed Forests
  • D. Private Forests

Answer: A. Reserved Forests

Explanation: Reserved Forests are regarded as the most valuable as far as the conservation of forest and wildlife resources are concerned (more than half of total forest land).

15. What percentage of the total forest area in India is declared as Protected Forest?

  • A. Almost one-third
  • B. Almost half
  • C. Almost two-thirds
  • D. Less than one-tenth

Answer: A. Almost one-third

Explanation: Almost one-third of the total forest area is designated as Protected Forest, as declared by the Forest Department to prevent further depletion.

16. Which category includes forests and wastelands belonging to both government and private individuals or communities?

  • A. Unclassed Forests
  • B. Reserved Forests
  • C. Protected Forests
  • D. Permanent Forests

Answer: A. Unclassed Forests

Explanation: Unclassed Forests are other forests and wastelands belonging to both government and private individuals and communities (especially prevalent in North-Eastern states).

17. Which Indian state has the largest area under permanent forests (accounting for 75% of its total forest area)?

  • A. Madhya Pradesh
  • B. Jammu & Kashmir
  • C. Uttarakhand
  • D. Odisha

Answer: A. Madhya Pradesh

Explanation: Madhya Pradesh has the largest area under permanent forests, constituting 75 percent of its total forest area.

18. In which state are almost all forests managed as Unclassed Forests by local tribal communities?

  • A. North-Eastern states (like Meghalaya)
  • B. Punjab and Haryana
  • C. Andhra Pradesh
  • D. Gujarat

Answer: A. North-Eastern states (like Meghalaya)

Explanation: All North-Eastern states and parts of Gujarat have a very high percentage of their forests managed as Unclassed Forests by local communities.

19. Villagers in five villages of Alwar district in Rajasthan declared 1,200 hectares of forest as what to protect wildlife against hunting?

  • A. Bhairodev Dakav 'Sonchuri'
  • B. Bishnoi Sacred Grove
  • C. Chipko Sanctuary
  • D. Sariska Reserve Zone

Answer: A. Bhairodev Dakav 'Sonchuri'

Explanation: In Alwar, Rajasthan, villagers of five villages declared 1,200 hectares of forest as Bhairodev Dakav 'Sonchuri' with their own rules and regulations prohibiting hunting and encroachment.

20. The famous 'Chipko Movement' in the Himalayas was primarily aimed at resisting what activity?

  • A. Deforestation and tree felling
  • B. Illegal animal poaching
  • C. Construction of mega dams
  • D. Mining in sacred hills

Answer: A. Deforestation and tree felling

Explanation: The famous Chipko Movement in the Himalayas successfully resisted deforestation in several areas by hugging trees to prevent them from being cut.

21. 'Beej Bachao Andolan' (Save the Seeds) and 'Navdanya' movements were launched in which region to promote ecological farming without synthetic chemicals?

  • A. Tehri, Uttarakhand
  • B. Chota Nagpur plateau
  • C. Silent Valley, Kerala
  • D. Bastar, Chhattisgarh

Answer: A. Tehri, Uttarakhand

Explanation: Farmers and citizen groups such as Beej Bachao Andolan in Tehri and Navdanya demonstrated that chemical-free ecological farming produces viable crop yields.

22. Which Indian state passed the first formal resolution for Joint Forest Management (JFM) in 1988?

  • A. Odisha
  • B. West Bengal
  • C. Bihar
  • D. Madhya Pradesh

Answer: A. Odisha

Explanation: The state of Odisha passed the first resolution for Joint Forest Management (JFM) in 1988, involving local communities in managing degraded forests.

23. Which community in Rajasthan is famous for protecting Blackbucks, Peacocks, and Nilgai as an integral part of their culture?

  • A. Bishnoi community
  • B. Gujjar community
  • C. Gaddi community
  • D. Toda community

Answer: A. Bishnoi community

Explanation: In and around Bishnoi villages in Rajasthan, herds of blackbuck, nilgai, and peacocks can be seen as an integral part of the community and nobody harms them.

24. The Munda and Santhal tribes of the Chota Nagpur region worship which of the following trees?

  • A. Mahua and Kadamba
  • B. Peepal and Banyan
  • C. Tamarind and Mango
  • D. Sal and Teak

Answer: A. Mahua and Kadamba

Explanation: The Mundas and Santhals of Chota Nagpur region worship Mahua (Bassia latifolia) and Kadamba (Anthocephalus cadamba) trees during festivals.

25. During wedding ceremonies, tribes of Odisha and Bihar worship which combination of trees?

  • A. Tamarind and Mango
  • B. Banyan and Peepal
  • C. Neem and Tulsi
  • D. Teak and Sal

Answer: A. Tamarind and Mango

Explanation: Tribes of Odisha and Bihar worship tamarind (Tamarindus indica) and mango (Mangifera indica) trees during weddings.

26. Which of the following wildlife projects was NOT explicitly covered under the Wildlife Protection Act amendments of 1980 and 1986?

  • A. Inclusion of several hundred butterflies, moths, and beetles to the protected list
  • B. Inclusion of plants for the first time in 1991
  • C. Project Tiger implementation in 1973
  • D. Legal protection to dragonflies

Answer: C. Project Tiger implementation in 1973

Explanation: Project Tiger was launched in 1973 prior to the 1980 and 1986 amendments which specifically brought insects like butterflies, moths, beetles, and dragonflies under full protection.

27. What is the main objective of Joint Forest Management (JFM) in India?

  • A. Involving local communities in management and restoration of degraded forests
  • B. Handing over commercial logging rights to private corporations
  • C. Converting natural forests into monoculture commercial plantations
  • D. Fencing off all forest lands completely from human touch

Answer: A. Involving local communities in management and restoration of degraded forests

Explanation: JFM depends on formation of local (village) institutions that undertake protection activities mostly on degraded forest land managed by the forest department.

28. Which of the following is considered a primary driver of biodiversity enrichment rather than destruction?

  • A. Community-led sacred groves and traditional protection practices
  • B. Habitat destruction and agricultural expansion
  • C. Environmental pollution and forest fires
  • D. Over-exploitation of forest resources

Answer: A. Community-led sacred groves and traditional protection practices

Explanation: Sacred groves and community protection preserve pristine virgin forests and rare plant/animal species from human interference.

29. Which group of states has the highest proportion of Protected Forests out of their total forest area?

  • A. Jammu & Kashmir, Punjab, Haryana, Himachal Pradesh, and Bihar
  • B. Assam, Tripura, Mizoram, and Meghalaya
  • C. Madhya Pradesh, Maharashtra, and Chhattisgarh
  • D. Kerala, Tamil Nadu, and Karnataka

Answer: A. Jammu & Kashmir, Punjab, Haryana, Himachal Pradesh, and Bihar

Explanation: Jammu & Kashmir, Andhra Pradesh, Uttarakhand, Kerala, Tamil Nadu, West Bengal, and Maharashtra have large percentages of reserved forests, while Punjab, Haryana, Bihar, HP, and J&K have large proportions of protected forests.

30. Periyar Tiger Reserve, known for its conservation of wild elephants and tigers, is located in which state?

  • A. Kerala
  • B. Tamil Nadu
  • C. Karnataka
  • D. Andhra Pradesh

Answer: A. Kerala

Explanation: Periyar Tiger Reserve is situated in Kerala and is one of the important tiger and elephant reserves in southern India.

NCERT Solutions for Class 10 Science Chapter 5 Life Processes

NCERT Solutions for Class 10 Science Chapter 5 Life Processes covers all vital biological mechanisms in living organisms including autotrophic and heterotrophic nutrition, human digestive system, respiration, double circulation, plant transport, and excretion mechanisms.

Intext Questions - Page Number: 81

Q1: Why is diffusion insufficient to meet the oxygen requirements of multicellular organisms like humans?
Answer: In multicellular organisms like humans, all body cells are not in direct contact with the external environment. Diffusion is a slow physical process that cannot move oxygen quickly over large distances to reach deep-seated tissues. Therefore, complex circulatory systems with respiratory pigments (hemoglobin) are required.
Q2: What criteria do we use to decide whether something is alive?
Answer: The most important criterion is molecular movement inside body cells. Additionally, visible external movements (such as breathing, growth, locomotion, and response to stimuli) help determine if an organism is living.

Intext Questions - Page Number: 87

Q1: What are the differences between autotrophic nutrition and heterotrophic nutrition?

Autotrophic Nutrition: Organisms synthesize their own organic food from simple inorganic raw materials (CO2 and H2O) using sunlight and chlorophyll. Example: Green plants and autotrophic bacteria.

Heterotrophic Nutrition: Organisms cannot synthesize their own food and depend directly or indirectly on producers for nutrition. Example: Animals, fungi, and human beings.

Q2: What is the role of acid in our stomach?
Answer: Hydrochloric acid (HCl) in the stomach:

1. Creates an acidic medium (pH ~ 1.5 - 2.5) necessary for the activation and proper functioning of the protein-digesting enzyme pepsin.

2. Kills harmful bacteria and microbes present in swallowed food.

Q3: How is the small intestine designed to absorb digested food?
Answer: The inner lining of the small intestine features millions of tiny finger-like projections called villi. These villi greatly increase the surface area available for absorption and are richly supplied with blood capillaries to transport absorbed nutrients to all body cells.

Intext Questions - Page Number: 91

Q1: What advantage over an aquatic organism does a terrestrial organism have with regard to obtaining oxygen for respiration?
Answer: Terrestrial organisms obtain oxygen from the atmosphere, where the concentration of oxygen is significantly higher (~21%). Aquatic organisms extract dissolved oxygen from water, which is present in much lower concentrations. Thus, terrestrial organisms do not need to breathe as rapidly as aquatic animals to get sufficient oxygen.
Q2: How are the lungs designed in human beings to maximize the area for exchange of gases?
Answer: Inside the lungs, the passage divides into smaller tubes called bronchioles, terminating in balloon-like structures called alveoli. Alveoli provide an extremely thin, highly vascularized network of blood capillaries with a huge surface area (approx. 80 m2) for rapid exchange of gases.

Intext Questions - Page Number: 96

Q1: What are the components of the transport system in human beings? What are the functions of these components?

1. Heart: A muscular pumping organ that pumps oxygenated blood to tissues and deoxygenated blood to lungs.

2. Blood: Fluid connective tissue containing RBCs (carries O2), WBCs (fights infections), Platelets (blood clotting), and Plasma (transports nutrients, CO2, and nitrogenous wastes).

3. Blood Vessels: Arteries (carry blood away from heart), Veins (carry blood towards heart), and Capillaries (allow exchange between blood and tissues).

Textbook Chapter End Exercise Questions

Q1: The kidneys in human beings are a part of the system for:

(a) nutrition
(b) respiration
(c) excretion
(d) transportation

Correct Answer: (c) excretion.

Q2: The xylem in plants is responsible for:

(a) transport of water
(b) transport of food
(c) transport of amino acids
(d) transport of oxygen

Correct Answer: (a) transport of water.

Q3: What are the differences between aerobic and anaerobic respiration?

Aerobic Respiration: Takes place in the presence of oxygen in mitochondria, produces CO2, H2O, and releases a high amount of energy (38 ATP).

Anaerobic Respiration: Takes place in the absence of oxygen in cytoplasm, produces Ethanol/Lactic acid and CO2, and releases very little energy (2 ATP).

Q4: Describe the structure and functioning of a nephron.
Answer:

• Structure: A nephron is the structural unit of a kidney consisting of a cup-shaped Bowman's capsule containing a cluster of capillaries called Glomerulus, connected to a long coiled renal tubule surrounded by capillaries.

• Functioning: Blood enters the glomerulus under high pressure where glucose, amino acids, salts, and urea are filtered into Bowman's capsule (Ultrafiltration). As filtrate moves down the tubular part, essential substances (glucose, water, salts) are selectively reabsorbed back into capillaries, while concentrated urine containing urea passes into the collecting duct.

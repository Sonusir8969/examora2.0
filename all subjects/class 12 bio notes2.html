NCERT Solutions for Class 12 Biology Chapter 2 Human Reproduction

NCERT Solutions for Class 12 Biology Chapter 2 Human Reproduction covers the male and female reproductive systems, gametogenesis, the menstrual cycle, fertilisation, and embryonic development up to parturition.

1. Male Reproductive System

Short Note:

• The male reproductive system includes a pair of testes enclosed in the scrotum (which maintains a lower temperature needed for sperm formation), along with accessory ducts and glands.

• Each testis has seminiferous tubules where spermatogenesis occurs, producing sperm with the help of Sertoli cells, while Leydig cells present outside the tubules secrete testosterone.

• A mature sperm has a head with an acrosome (containing enzymes to help penetrate the egg), a middle piece with mitochondria for energy, and a tail for motility.

2. Female Reproductive System

Short Note:

• The female reproductive system consists of a pair of ovaries, oviducts (fallopian tubes), uterus, cervix and vagina, along with external genitalia.

• Oogenesis begins during foetal development itself, with oogonia forming primary oocytes that remain arrested until puberty, when one egg is usually released per menstrual cycle.

• The menstrual cycle, roughly 28 days long, is regulated by hormones (LH, FSH, oestrogen, progesterone) and includes menstrual, follicular, ovulatory and luteal phases.

3. Fertilisation and Embryonic Development

Short Note:

• Fertilisation typically occurs in the ampullary region of the fallopian tube, where the sperm fuses with the egg to form a diploid zygote, which undergoes cleavage divisions to form a blastocyst.

• The blastocyst gets implanted in the uterine wall (implantation), and the placenta forms as a vital connection between the mother and the foetus, facilitating nutrient, oxygen and waste exchange.

• Human pregnancy lasts about nine months (approximately 40 weeks), ending in parturition (childbirth), which is induced by hormonal signals, mainly oxytocin, causing strong uterine contractions.

NCERT Textbook Exercise Questions

Q1: Draw a labelled diagram of the sperm and mention the function of the acrosome.
Answer: A human sperm has a head containing the haploid nucleus covered by the acrosome, a middle piece rich in mitochondria, and a long tail for movement. The acrosome contains enzymes that help the sperm penetrate the corona radiata and zona pellucida layers surrounding the egg during fertilisation, allowing the sperm to reach and fuse with the egg.
Q2: Name the hormones involved in the regulation of spermatogenesis.
Answer: Spermatogenesis is regulated by Gonadotropin Releasing Hormone (GnRH) from the hypothalamus, which stimulates the pituitary to release LH and FSH. LH acts on Leydig cells to produce testosterone, while FSH acts on Sertoli cells, and testosterone together with FSH stimulates the process of spermatogenesis within the seminiferous tubules.
Q3: What is parturition, and which hormone plays the major role in it?
Answer: Parturition is the process of childbirth, marking the end of pregnancy through delivery of the foetus. It is induced by signals from the fully developed foetus and placenta that trigger mild uterine contractions called the 'foetal ejection reflex', and the hormone oxytocin, released from the mother's pituitary gland, plays the major role by causing strong, coordinated uterine contractions that lead to expulsion of the baby.
Q4: Why does the menstrual cycle stop during pregnancy?
Answer: The menstrual cycle stops during pregnancy because high levels of progesterone (secreted first by the corpus luteum and later by the placenta) maintain the uterine lining and suppress the hormonal changes (LH surge) that would normally trigger ovulation and menstruation, thus preventing the shedding of the endometrium that is needed to support the developing embryo.

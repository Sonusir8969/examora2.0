NCERT Solutions for Class 9 Civics Chapter 1 What is Democracy? Why Democracy?

NCERT Solutions for Class 9 Political Science Chapter 1 covers Simple Definition of Democracy, Features of Democracy, Arguments For and Against Democracy, and Broader Meaning of Democracy.

Overview of Topics & Subtopics (Click to Jump)

  • 1. What is Democracy & Its Key Features
  • 2. Why Democracy? (Arguments For & Against)
  • 3. Broader Meaning of Democracy

1. What is Democracy & Its Key Features

Short Note:

• Definition: Democracy is a form of government in which the rulers are elected by the people.

• Major Decisions by Elected Leaders: Final decision-making power must rest with those elected by the people (Case study: General Musharraf in Pakistan).

• Free and Fair Electoral Competition: A democracy must be based on free and fair elections where those currently in power have a fair chance of losing (Case study: PRI in Mexico, China's National People's Congress).

• One Person, One Vote, One Value: Each adult citizen must have one vote and each vote must have one value (Denial of voting rights in Saudi Arabia, Estonia, Fiji).

• Rule of Law and Respect for Rights: A democratic government rules within limits set by constitutional law and citizens' rights (Case study: Mugabe's ZANU-PF in Zimbabwe).

2. Why Democracy? (Arguments For & Against)

Short Note:

• Arguments Against Democracy: Instability due to changing leaders; delay in decision-making due to consultations; corruption; political competition and power play.

• Arguments For Democracy: More accountable government (e.g., China's famine vs. India's economic response in 1958-1961); improves decision-making quality; provides a peaceful method to deal with differences; allows citizens to correct their own mistakes; enhances dignity of citizens.

3. Broader Meaning of Democracy

Short Note:

• Representative Democracy: Modern democracies are representative because it is impossible for all citizens to sit together and make every decision directly.

• Democracy beyond Government: Applying democratic principles to families, classrooms, organizations, and decision-making bodies where every stakeholder has an equal say.

Intext & Conceptual Questions

Q1: Distinguish between Democratic and Non-Democratic Governments.
Democratic Government Non-Democratic Government
Rulers are elected by the citizens through free and fair elections. Rulers are self-appointed, hereditary monarchs, or military dictators.
Citizens enjoy fundamental rights, freedom of speech, and equal vote value. Rights of citizens are restricted, and opposition is often suppressed.
Government decisions are accountable to the public and constitution. Rulers are not accountable to the public or any legal framework.
Q2: Why is General Pervez Musharraf’s regime in Pakistan not considered a democracy?
Answer:
1. Military Coup: In 1999, General Musharraf led a military coup to overthrow a democratically elected government.
2. Legal Framework Order: He issued an order giving the President power to dismiss national and provincial assemblies.
3. Power Concentration: Final decision-making power rested with military officers and General Musharraf, not with elected representatives.

Textbook Chapter End Exercise Questions

Q1: Here is some information about four countries. Based on this information, how would you classify each of these countries? Write ‘democratic’, ‘undemocratic’ or ‘not sure’ against each of these.
(a) Country A: People who do not accept the country’s official religion do not have a right to vote.
Answer: Undemocratic

(b) Country B: The same party has been winning elections for the last twenty years.
Answer: Not Sure (Depends on whether elections are free and fair)

(c) Country C: Ruling party has lost in the last three elections.
Answer: Democratic

(d) Country D: There is no independent election commission.
Answer: Undemocratic
Q2: Which of these is NOT a good argument in favour of democracy?
(a) People feel free and equal in a democracy.
(b) Democracies resolve conflict in a better way than others.
(c) Democratic government is more accountable to the people.
(d) Democracies are more prosperous than others.
Answer: (d) Democracies are more prosperous than others (Economic prosperity depends on multiple factors like resources and policy, not solely on democracy).
Q3: Why is democracy considered the best form of government despite its drawbacks?
Answer:
1. Accountability: Democratic governments respond to the needs of citizens better than non-democratic regimes.
2. Conflict Resolution: Provides a peaceful mechanism to manage social diversity and differences without violence.
3. Dignity of Citizens: Based on the principle of political equality, granting every citizen equal rights and respect.
4. Correcting Mistakes: Offers space for public discussion and opportunities for citizens and governments to rectify errors.

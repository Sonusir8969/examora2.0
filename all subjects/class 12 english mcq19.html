Ch-19: Memories of Childhood 6 Questions

1. 'Memories of Childhood' comprises accounts by which two authors?

  • A. Zitkala-Sa and Bama
  • B. Pearl S. Buck and Susan Hill
  • C. Kalki and Anees Jung
  • D. Kamala Das and Adrienne Rich

Answer: A. Zitkala-Sa and Bama

Explanation: The chapter presents two autobiographical accounts of childhood discrimination: 'The Cutting of My Long Hair' by Zitkala-Sa, and 'We Too Are Human Beings' by Bama.

2. In 'The Cutting of My Long Hair', what forced cultural assimilation does Zitkala-Sa experience at the American boarding school?

  • A. Being forced to learn a new language only
  • B. Having her long hair, a symbol of her Native American identity and dignity, forcibly cut against her will
  • C. Being denied food
  • D. Being sent back home immediately

Answer: B. Having her long hair, a symbol of her Native American identity and dignity, forcibly cut against her will

Explanation: Zitkala-Sa, a Native American girl, recounts the traumatic and humiliating experience of having her culturally significant long hair forcibly shorn at a government boarding school as part of enforced assimilation.

3. What does the cutting of hair symbolize in Zitkala-Sa's narrative?

  • A. A a fashion trend
  • B. The loss of individual identity, cultural heritage, and personal dignity imposed by an oppressive institution
  • C. A punishment for bad behavior
  • D. A religious ceremony

Answer: B. The loss of individual identity, cultural heritage, and personal dignity imposed by an oppressive institution

Explanation: For Zitkala-Sa, the forced haircut represents a deeply painful erasure of her Native American identity and cultural dignity, a practice historically imposed to force conformity to white American norms.

4. In 'We Too Are Human Beings', what social issue does the young Bama become aware of in her village?

  • A. Gender inequality only
  • B. The practice of caste-based untouchability and discrimination against Dalits
  • C. Religious conflict
  • D. Economic disparity due to farming

Answer: B. The practice of caste-based untouchability and discrimination against Dalits

Explanation: Bama's narrative describes her childhood realization of the deeply entrenched caste discrimination and untouchability practiced against Dalits like her own community in her village in Tamil Nadu.

5. What incident triggers young Bama's awareness of caste discrimination?

  • A. She witnesses an elder from her community carrying food for an upper-caste man without touching it directly, out of forced deference
  • B. She is denied entry to school
  • C. She is scolded by her parents
  • D. She wins an award at school

Answer: A. She witnesses an elder from her community carrying food for an upper-caste man without touching it directly, out of forced deference

Explanation: Bama observes an elderly man from her community carrying a package of snacks by its string, without touching it directly, so as not to 'pollute' it before handing it to an upper-caste landlord, revealing the humiliating reality of caste discrimination.

6. What common theme unites both accounts in 'Memories of Childhood'?

  • A. The celebration of festivals
  • B. Childhood experiences of discrimination based on race or caste, and the resilience shown in resisting it
  • C. The joys of rural life
  • D. The importance of formal schooling

Answer: B. Childhood experiences of discrimination based on race or caste, and the resilience shown in resisting it

Explanation: Both accounts, though set in different cultures and contexts, explore childhood experiences of systemic discrimination based on race and caste respectively, alongside the protagonists' emerging awareness and resistance.

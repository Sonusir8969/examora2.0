NCERT Solutions for Class 10 English Chapter 10 The Sermon at Benares

NCERT Solutions for Class 10 English First Flight Chapter 10 The Sermon at Benares covers the story of Kisa Gotami and the Buddha's teachings on death and mortality, and important questions and answers.

  • 1. Story Summary
  • 2. Key Teachings
  • 3. Theme & Message

1. Story Summary

Short Note:

• Kisa Gotami, grief-stricken after the death of her only child, desperately searches for a medicine to bring him back to life and eventually approaches the Buddha for help.

• The Buddha instructs her to bring back a handful of mustard seeds, but only from a household that has never experienced the death of a family member, a condition that seems simple at first.

• As Kisa Gotami goes from house to house, she finds that every family has experienced loss in some form, and through this journey, she comes to understand and accept the universal truth of mortality, ultimately finding peace and becoming a follower of the Buddha.

2. Key Teachings

Short Note:

• The Buddha's sermon teaches that death is an unavoidable and universal part of life that spares no one, regardless of wealth, status or personal grief.

• He explains that true peace comes not from denying or fighting against the reality of mortality, but from accepting it and recognising that all beings share this same fate.

• The chapter also emphasises compassion and gentle wisdom, as the Buddha helps Kisa Gotami come to this understanding through her own experience rather than direct argument.

3. Theme & Message

Short Note:

• The central theme is the acceptance of the universality of death and suffering, urging readers to find peace through understanding rather than resistance to life's harsh realities.

• The chapter also reflects on the value of compassionate wisdom, showing how understanding can come through personal, lived experience guided by a wise teacher.

Reading With Insight (Textbook Questions)

Q1: Why did Kisa Gotami approach the Buddha, and what did she ask for?
Answer: Kisa Gotami approached the Buddha in deep grief after her only child died, and she asked him for medicine that could restore her child to life, refusing to accept that he was truly dead.
Q2: What condition did the Buddha place on the mustard seeds, and why?
Answer: The Buddha asked Kisa Gotami to bring back mustard seeds only from a household that had never lost a family member to death, a condition designed to gently show her, through her own search, that death is a universal experience shared by every family.
Q3: What realisation did Kisa Gotami come to after searching for the mustard seeds?
Answer: After visiting many houses and finding that every single family had experienced the death of a loved one, Kisa Gotami realised that death is an unavoidable and universal part of life, not something limited to her own personal misfortune, which helped her come to terms with her loss.

Extract-Based / Short Answer Questions

Q4: What does the story teach about coping with grief and loss?
Answer: The story teaches that grief can be eased through the understanding and acceptance that suffering and death are universal experiences shared by all humanity, and that this shared understanding can bring comfort and peace rather than a sense of isolated misfortune.
Q5: How does the Buddha's method of teaching Kisa Gotami reflect his wisdom?
Answer: Instead of directly telling Kisa Gotami that her child could not be revived, the Buddha wisely allowed her to discover this painful truth herself through personal experience, making the lesson far more powerful and effective in helping her find genuine acceptance and peace.

Ch-1: Development 30 Questions

1. What is the main criterion used by the World Bank in classifying different countries in its World Development Reports?

  • A. Per Capita Income
  • B. Human Development Index
  • C. Literacy Rate
  • D. Gross Enrolment Ratio

Answer: A. Per Capita Income

Explanation: The World Bank uses Per Capita Income (Average Income) as the primary indicator to classify nations as high-income or low-income countries.

2. How is Per Capita Income calculated?

  • A. Total Income of the country divided by its total population
  • B. Total Income divided by working population
  • C. Gross National Product divided by total land area
  • D. Total Exports divided by total Imports

Answer: A. Total Income of the country divided by its total population

Explanation: Per Capita Income, also known as average income, is obtained by dividing the total national income of a country by its total population.

3. What is a major limitation of using Per Capita Income as the sole measure of economic development?

  • A. It hides disparities in income distribution among citizens
  • B. It is difficult to calculate mathematically
  • C. It excludes agricultural output
  • D. It only considers foreign transactions

Answer: A. It hides disparities in income distribution among citizens

Explanation: Per Capita Income shows average wealth but fails to reveal how income is actually distributed across different sections of society.

4. Which organization publishes the Human Development Report (HDR)?

  • A. UNDP (United Nations Development Programme)
  • B. World Bank
  • C. IMF (International Monetary Fund)
  • D. UNESCO

Answer: A. UNDP (United Nations Development Programme)

Explanation: UNDP publishes the Human Development Report, comparing countries based on health levels, education levels, and per capita income.

5. Which three indicators are evaluated to calculate the Human Development Index (HDI)?

  • A. Life Expectancy, Education (Literacy), and Per Capita Income
  • B. Population, Poverty Line, and Inflation Rate
  • C. Employment Rate, Land Ownership, and Exports
  • D. Industrial Output, Infrastructure, and Defense Spending

Answer: A. Life Expectancy, Education (Literacy), and Per Capita Income

Explanation: HDI assesses development holistically through longevity/health (Life Expectancy), knowledge (Educational Attainment), and standard of living (Per Capita Income).

6. What does Infant Mortality Rate (IMR) indicate?

  • A. The number of children that die before the age of one year per 1,000 live births in that year
  • B. The number of children dying before five years per 100 births
  • C. The total death rate of infants in a country every month
  • D. The proportion of ill infants admitted to hospitals

Answer: A. The number of children that die before the age of one year per 1,000 live births in that year

Explanation: IMR measures health and healthcare accessibility by tracking infant deaths before age one per 1,000 live births.

7. Literacy rate measures the proportion of literate population in which age group?

  • A. 7 years and above
  • B. 10 years and above
  • C. 15 years and above
  • D. 5 years and above

Answer: A. 7 years and above

Explanation: In official censuses and economic statistics, literacy rate represents the percentage of people aged 7 and above who can read and write with understanding.

8. Net Attendance Ratio is defined as the total number of children attending school as a percentage of total children in which age group?

  • A. 14 and 15 years
  • B. 6 and 10 years
  • C. 18 and 21 years
  • D. 3 and 5 years

Answer: A. 14 and 15 years

Explanation: Net Attendance Ratio measures secondary school attendance for children in the age group of 14 and 15 years.

9. Development that meets the needs of the present without compromising the ability of future generations to meet their own needs is called:

  • A. Sustainable Development
  • B. Economic Growth
  • C. Human Development
  • D. Industrialization

Answer: A. Sustainable Development

Explanation: Sustainable development balances present resource utilization while preserving natural resources and ecosystems for future generations.

10. Why are groundwater resources severely overused in regions like Punjab and Western UP?

  • A. Excessive pumping for intensive agriculture and irrigation
  • B. High rates of natural evaporation
  • C. Lack of rainfall throughout the year
  • D. Contamination from mining activities

Answer: A. Excessive pumping for intensive agriculture and irrigation

Explanation: Agriculture in agricultural surplus regions relies heavily on deep tube-well irrigation, depleting groundwater faster than natural recharge rates.

11. What is Body Mass Index (BMI) used to measure in adults?

  • A. Nutritional status (underdeveloped, normal, or overweight)
  • B. Total blood volume
  • C. Daily caloric expenditure
  • D. Lung capacity and heart fitness

Answer: A. Nutritional status (underdeveloped, normal, or overweight)

Explanation: BMI evaluates nutritional health by dividing weight in kilograms by the square of height in meters.

12. If a person's Body Mass Index (BMI) is less than 18.5, the individual is categorized as:

  • A. Undernourished
  • B. Normal weight
  • C. Overweight
  • D. Obese

Answer: A. Undernourished

Explanation: A BMI below 18.5 indicates undernourishment, whereas a BMI above 25 signifies that a person is overweight.

13. Different individuals have different development goals because:

  • A. People have different life situations and aspirations
  • B. Everyone wants higher income only
  • C. Development goals are determined by the government
  • D. People belong to different countries

Answer: A. People have different life situations and aspirations

Explanation: Because life contexts vary, what constitutes progress or development differs from person to person (e.g., a landless worker vs. a prosperous farmer).

14. Which of the following is an example of conflicting developmental goals?

  • A. Industrialists wanting large dams for electricity while tribal people get displaced
  • B. Farmers wanting better seeds and irrigation
  • C. Students desiring better educational institutions
  • D. Workers seeking higher wages and safe environments

Answer: A. Industrialists wanting large dams for electricity while tribal people get displaced

Explanation: Development for one section (industrialists) can lead to destruction and displacement for another (indigenous/tribal populations).

15. Apart from income, what other attributes do people look for as quality of life goals?

  • A. Freedom, equality, security, and respect
  • B. Higher prices of consumer items
  • C. Longer working hours
  • D. Increased taxes

Answer: A. Freedom, equality, security, and respect

Explanation: Income is a material goal, but non-material goals like dignity, safety, equality, and freedom are equally important for quality of life.

16. Why is Kerala considered to have a better Human Development Index despite lower per capita income than some other states?

  • A. It has excellent basic health and educational facilities
  • B. It relies heavily on heavy industrial plants
  • C. It has the highest agricultural yield in India
  • D. It has a low population density

Answer: A. It has excellent basic health and educational facilities

Explanation: Kerala prioritizes public provision of health and primary education, resulting in lower infant mortality and higher literacy rates.

17. What system ensures the distribution of food grains at subsidized prices to poor families in India?

  • A. Public Distribution System (PDS)
  • B. Mid-Day Meal Scheme
  • C. Integrated Child Development Services
  • D. National Rural Employment Guarantee Act

Answer: A. Public Distribution System (PDS)

Explanation: The Public Distribution System (PDS) uses ration shops to supply essential food grains to ensure nutritional security among vulnerable populations.

18. What non-renewable resource is crucial for global energy but facing rapid depletion?

  • A. Crude oil
  • B. Solar energy
  • C. Wind energy
  • D. Hydroelectric power

Answer: A. Crude oil

Explanation: Crude oil is a non-renewable fossil fuel with finite global reserves that cannot be replenished once exhausted.

19. Average income is also known as:

  • A. Per Capita Income
  • B. National Income
  • C. Disposable Income
  • D. Gross Domestic Product

Answer: A. Per Capita Income

Explanation: Average income and Per Capita Income are synonymous economic terms used to represent per-person earning capacity.

20. What expected lifespan of an individual at birth is referred to as?

  • A. Life Expectancy at Birth
  • B. Infant Survival Rate
  • C. Mortality Index
  • D. Human Survival Standard

Answer: A. Life Expectancy at Birth

Explanation: Life Expectancy at birth denotes the average number of years a newborn infant is expected to live under prevailing health conditions.

21. Which neighbor of India performs better than India in several Human Development Index parameters despite having a smaller economy?

  • A. Sri Lanka
  • B. Pakistan
  • C. Nepal
  • D. Bangladesh

Answer: A. Sri Lanka

Explanation: Sri Lanka consistently outperforms India in HDI rankings due to historical investments in public education and healthcare systems.

22. Public facilities are those services which:

  • A. Are provided collectively by the government because private costs are too high for individuals
  • B. Are owned exclusively by private corporations
  • C. Are provided only to urban high-income earners
  • D. Can only be accessed using foreign currencies

Answer: A. Are provided collectively by the government because private costs are too high for individuals

Explanation: Public facilities (schools, hospitals, public transit, sanitation) are efficiently supplied by governments to benefit the community as a whole.

23. Money cannot buy all goods and services that a person needs to live well. Which of the following CANNOT be directly bought by money?

  • A. Pollution-free environment and protection from infectious diseases
  • B. Branded clothing and cars
  • C. Processed food items
  • D. Personal electronic gadgets

Answer: A. Pollution-free environment and protection from infectious diseases

Explanation: Environmental quality, community health security, and social peace require collective community action and public policy rather than personal wealth.

24. What is the key focus of national development?

  • A. Improving the living standards and welfare of all citizens
  • B. Increasing total military power
  • C. Expanding physical territory
  • D. Maximizing foreign reserves

Answer: A. Improving the living standards and welfare of all citizens

Explanation: National development involves improving social, political, and economic conditions to raise overall quality of life across the entire population.

25. In World Development Reports, currency values are converted using US dollars because:

  • A. It makes international comparisons uniform and standard across countries
  • B. The US Dollar is the only currency in circulation
  • C. All other currencies are legally invalid
  • D. US banks conduct all global trade

Answer: A. It makes international comparisons uniform and standard across countries

Explanation: Income data is converted into US Dollars (adjusted for purchasing power parity) to enable standard worldwide statistical comparisons.

26. Renewable resources are those resources which:

  • A. Are replenished by nature over time (e.g., groundwater, solar energy)
  • B. Cannot be replaced once used up
  • C. Are created through industrial chemical reactions
  • D. Belong exclusively to single individuals

Answer: A. Are replenished by nature over time (e.g., groundwater, solar energy)

Explanation: Renewable resources get naturally recharged, provided their rate of consumption does not exceed their rate of natural replenishment.

27. What is a key developmental goal for an urban unemployed youth?

  • A. Decent job opportunities with stable income
  • B. Lower prices for agricultural inputs
  • C. Higher support prices for crops
  • D. Subsidies on tractor machinery

Answer: A. Decent job opportunities with stable income

Explanation: For educated unemployed urban youth, gainful employment and economic independence represent the primary developmental aspiration.

28. Which Indian state has a very low Infant Mortality Rate due to adequate provision of basic health and educational facilities?

  • A. Kerala
  • B. Bihar
  • C. Uttar Pradesh
  • D. Odisha

Answer: A. Kerala

Explanation: High literacy, female empowerment, and robust public healthcare infrastructure in Kerala have reduced its Infant Mortality Rate significantly.

29. Why is per capita income calculated in US Dollars adjusted for purchasing power?

  • A. To compare the real buying power of money across different countries
  • B. Because US dollars are easy to print
  • C. To force countries to trade with the United States
  • D. Because local currencies do not have fixed rates

Answer: A. To compare the real buying power of money across different countries

Explanation: Adjusting for Purchasing Power Parity (PPP) ensures that the calculation reflects what a dollar can actually buy in terms of goods in each respective country.

30. Sustainability of development emphasizes that resources must be used:

  • A. Judiciously so that future generations also benefit from them
  • B. Rapidly to maximize short-term profits
  • C. Exclusively by industrial sectors
  • D. Only after converting them into export items

Answer: A. Judiciously so that future generations also benefit from them

Explanation: Sustainable development mandates responsible consumption to prevent environmental degradation and resource depletion for future humanity.

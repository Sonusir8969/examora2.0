Ch-5: Molecular Basis of Inheritance 12 Questions

1. The genetic material in the vast majority of organisms is:

  • A. DNA
  • B. Protein
  • C. Carbohydrate
  • D. Lipid

Answer: A. DNA

Explanation: Experiments by Griffith, Avery-MacLeod-McCarty, and Hershey-Chase established DNA as the primary hereditary material in most organisms.

2. The Watson-Crick model describes the structure of DNA as a:

  • A. Double helix
  • B. Single stranded molecule
  • C. Triple helix
  • D. Circular protein

Answer: A. Double helix

Explanation: Watson and Crick proposed that DNA consists of two antiparallel strands coiled around each other to form a double helix.

3. In the DNA double helix, adenine specifically pairs with:

  • A. Thymine
  • B. Cytosine
  • C. Guanine
  • D. Uracil

Answer: A. Thymine

Explanation: Adenine forms two hydrogen bonds with thymine, one of the defining complementary base pairing rules of DNA.

4. The process by which a DNA molecule produces an identical copy of itself is called:

  • A. Replication
  • B. Transcription
  • C. Translation
  • D. Transformation

Answer: A. Replication

Explanation: DNA replication ensures that genetic information is accurately copied and passed on during cell division.

5. The enzyme mainly responsible for synthesizing RNA using a DNA template is:

  • A. RNA polymerase
  • B. DNA polymerase
  • C. DNA ligase
  • D. Helicase

Answer: A. RNA polymerase

Explanation: RNA polymerase catalyzes the transcription process, synthesizing an RNA strand complementary to the DNA template.

6. The central dogma of molecular biology describes the flow of genetic information as:

  • A. DNA → RNA → Protein
  • B. Protein → RNA → DNA
  • C. RNA → DNA → Protein
  • D. DNA → Protein → RNA

Answer: A. DNA → RNA → Protein

Explanation: The central dogma outlines the general direction of information flow within a cell, from DNA to RNA to functional protein.

7. A codon, the basic unit of the genetic code specifying one amino acid, consists of:

  • A. Three nucleotides
  • B. One nucleotide
  • C. Two nucleotides
  • D. Four nucleotides

Answer: A. Three nucleotides

Explanation: Each set of three consecutive nucleotides on mRNA (a codon) specifies a particular amino acid or a stop signal during translation.

8. The process by which the mRNA sequence is decoded to synthesize a polypeptide chain is called:

  • A. Translation
  • B. Transcription
  • C. Replication
  • D. Splicing

Answer: A. Translation

Explanation: Translation occurs at the ribosome, where the mRNA sequence is read in codons to assemble a corresponding chain of amino acids.

9. The type of RNA responsible for carrying specific amino acids to the ribosome during protein synthesis is:

  • A. tRNA
  • B. mRNA
  • C. rRNA
  • D. hnRNA

Answer: A. tRNA

Explanation: Transfer RNA (tRNA) molecules recognize specific codons on mRNA and deliver the corresponding amino acids to the growing polypeptide chain.

10. The lac operon in bacteria is a classic and well-studied example of:

  • A. Gene regulation in prokaryotes
  • B. Gene regulation occurring only in eukaryotes
  • C. A type of spontaneous mutation
  • D. The process of DNA replication

Answer: A. Gene regulation in prokaryotes

Explanation: The lac operon demonstrates how bacterial gene expression is switched on or off in response to the availability of lactose in the environment.

11. The Human Genome Project was primarily aimed at:

  • A. Sequencing and mapping all the genes present in human DNA
  • B. Cloning human beings
  • C. Treating genetic diseases directly
  • D. Identifying human blood groups only

Answer: A. Sequencing and mapping all the genes present in human DNA

Explanation: This international scientific effort successfully determined the complete sequence of nucleotide base pairs that make up human DNA.

12. DNA fingerprinting is a technique that relies mainly on analyzing:

  • A. Repetitive DNA sequences (VNTRs) that are unique to individuals
  • B. Overall protein sequences
  • C. The total chromosome number
  • D. An individual's blood type

Answer: A. Repetitive DNA sequences (VNTRs) that are unique to individuals

Explanation: Since the number of repeats in these variable regions differs greatly between individuals, they provide a unique genetic identifier.

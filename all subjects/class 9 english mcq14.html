Poem 5: A Legend of the Northland 30 Questions

1. Who is the poet of "A Legend of the Northland"?

  • A. Phoebe Cary
  • B. William Butler Yeats
  • C. Robert Frost
  • D. Coates Kinney

Answer: A. Phoebe Cary

Explanation: The poem "A Legend of the Northland" was written by the American poet Phoebe Cary.

2. Who is the main character in the legend narrated in the poem?

  • A. An old, selfish woman
  • B. A young child
  • C. A wise king
  • D. A brave soldier

Answer: A. An old, selfish woman

Explanation: The poem centres around an old, stingy woman living in the cold Northland, whose selfishness forms the crux of the legend.

3. Who visits the old woman's cottage, asking for food, at the start of the legend?

  • A. Saint Peter, disguised as a traveller
  • B. A hungry child
  • C. A lost traveller with no religious significance
  • D. A group of soldiers

Answer: A. Saint Peter, disguised as a traveller

Explanation: According to the legend, Saint Peter, appearing as a humble traveller, visits the old woman's cottage seeking food, testing her generosity.

4. What was the old woman doing when the visitor arrived at her cottage?

  • A. Baking a cake on her hearth
  • B. Sleeping
  • C. Sewing clothes
  • D. Washing dishes

Answer: A. Baking a cake on her hearth

Explanation: When the visitor arrived, the old woman was in the process of baking a cake, which becomes central to the unfolding legend.

5. How did the old woman initially respond to the traveller's request for a small piece of cake?

  • A. She grudgingly agreed to give him a piece, but kept reducing its size out of greed
  • B. She happily gave him the largest piece immediately
  • C. She refused to give him anything at all from the start
  • D. She gave him the entire cake generously

Answer: A. She grudgingly agreed to give him a piece, but kept reducing its size out of greed

Explanation: The old woman's greed is illustrated as she repeatedly makes the piece of cake she intends to give smaller and smaller, unable to part with even a modest portion.

6. What eventually happened to the small piece of cake as the old woman kept shrinking its size out of greed?

  • A. It ended up being far too small to be considered a proper gift, showing her extreme selfishness
  • B. It grew larger the more she tried to reduce it
  • C. It disappeared completely without her noticing
  • D. It transformed into a completely different food item

Answer: A. It ended up being far too small to be considered a proper gift, showing her extreme selfishness

Explanation: The old woman's greed caused her to shrink the cake piece to an absurdly tiny size, symbolising the depth of her selfishness and unwillingness to share.

7. How did the traveller, who was actually Saint Peter, react to the old woman's stinginess?

  • A. He became angry and pronounced a curse upon her for her selfish behaviour
  • B. He thanked her warmly and left peacefully
  • C. He had no reaction at all
  • D. He rewarded her generously despite her selfishness

Answer: A. He became angry and pronounced a curse upon her for her selfish behaviour

Explanation: Recognising the old woman's extreme selfishness, the traveller, revealed as Saint Peter, cursed her, resulting in a dramatic punishment for her greed.

8. What punishment or transformation did the old woman undergo as a result of the curse?

  • A. She was transformed into a woodpecker
  • B. She was turned to stone
  • C. She lost her ability to speak
  • D. She was banished from her home forever

Answer: A. She was transformed into a woodpecker

Explanation: As punishment for her selfishness, the old woman was magically transformed into a woodpecker, forced to seek her food by pecking through tree bark.

9. What is significant about the woodpecker's method of finding food, in relation to the woman's punishment?

  • A. It symbolises how she must now toil hard for her food, unlike the ease she once had but refused to share
  • B. It shows the woodpecker leads an easier life than before
  • C. It has no symbolic connection to her earlier selfishness
  • D. It represents freedom and joy rather than punishment

Answer: A. It symbolises how she must now toil hard for her food, unlike the ease she once had but refused to share

Explanation: The difficult, laborious way the woodpecker must search for food symbolically reflects the punishment for the woman's earlier refusal to share generously.

10. What central moral lesson does "A Legend of the Northland" convey?

  • A. The importance of generosity and kindness, and the consequences of selfishness
  • B. The value of hard work above all else
  • C. The importance of physical strength
  • D. The dangers of trusting strangers

Answer: A. The importance of generosity and kindness, and the consequences of selfishness

Explanation: The poem's central moral highlights the value of generosity and warns against the negative consequences that selfishness and greed can bring.

11. Which region does the poem's title suggest as the story's setting?

  • A. A cold, northern land
  • B. A tropical island
  • C. A desert region
  • D. A bustling city

Answer: A. A cold, northern land

Explanation: The title itself indicates that the legend takes place in a cold, northern region, fitting the folkloric tone of the story.

12. What literary form does "A Legend of the Northland" take?

  • A. A narrative poem telling a folk legend
  • B. A sonnet
  • C. A haiku
  • D. A limerick

Answer: A. A narrative poem telling a folk legend

Explanation: The poem is structured as a narrative, telling a traditional folk legend with a clear storyline, characters, and moral lesson.

13. What does the poem suggest about the true nature of generosity, through the contrast between the woman's initial and later actions?

  • A. True generosity involves genuine willingness to share, not grudging or minimal giving
  • B. Generosity is only meaningful when given reluctantly
  • C. Generosity has no real value according to the poem
  • D. The size of a gift matters more than the intention behind it

Answer: A. True generosity involves genuine willingness to share, not grudging or minimal giving

Explanation: The poem illustrates that true generosity requires a genuine, willing spirit of sharing, contrasting sharply with the old woman's reluctant, minimal offering.

14. What supernatural or religious element is present in the poem's narrative?

  • A. The disguised appearance of Saint Peter and his power to curse the woman
  • B. A dragon threatening the village
  • C. A fairy granting wishes
  • D. No supernatural elements are present at all

Answer: A. The disguised appearance of Saint Peter and his power to curse the woman

Explanation: The poem incorporates a religious, supernatural element through the disguised Saint Peter, whose divine power results in the woman's transformation as punishment.

15. What does the poem imply about how selfishness can affect one's fate or destiny?

  • A. Selfish actions can lead to severe and lasting consequences
  • B. Selfishness has no bearing on a person's fate
  • C. Selfishness always leads to great rewards
  • D. The poem suggests selfishness is a positive trait

Answer: A. Selfish actions can lead to severe and lasting consequences

Explanation: The old woman's transformation into a woodpecker as punishment for her greed clearly conveys that selfish behaviour can result in serious, lasting consequences.

16. What can be inferred about the folk tradition and cultural origin of this type of legend?

  • A. Such legends often originate from oral folk traditions meant to teach moral lessons
  • B. This type of story is purely modern with no folk tradition
  • C. Folk legends never involve moral teachings
  • D. The poem has no connection to any folk tradition

Answer: A. Such legends often originate from oral folk traditions meant to teach moral lessons

Explanation: The poem reflects a classic folk legend structure, typical of oral storytelling traditions that use fantastical elements to impart moral lessons.

17. What does the constant shrinking of the cake piece by the old woman reveal about the psychological nature of greed?

  • A. Greed can escalate progressively, making it increasingly difficult to give even small amounts
  • B. Greed remains constant and never changes in intensity
  • C. Greed has no psychological basis according to the poem
  • D. The poem suggests greed leads to increased generosity over time

Answer: A. Greed can escalate progressively, making it increasingly difficult to give even small amounts

Explanation: The gradual shrinking of the cake piece humorously but effectively illustrates how greed can intensify, making even minimal generosity increasingly difficult.

18. What tone does the poet primarily use in narrating this cautionary legend?

  • A. A storytelling, moralistic tone with elements of gentle humour
  • B. A purely tragic and sorrowful tone throughout
  • C. An angry and harshly critical tone
  • D. A completely neutral, factual tone with no moral undertone

Answer: A. A storytelling, moralistic tone with elements of gentle humour

Explanation: The poem combines an engaging storytelling tone with subtle humour, particularly evident in the exaggerated shrinking of the cake, while conveying a clear moral message.

19. Why might a bird like the woodpecker be considered a fitting transformation for the punished woman?

  • A. Because its constant pecking for food symbolically mirrors continuous labour, contrasting with her earlier refusal to give easily
  • B. Because woodpeckers are traditionally symbols of wealth and abundance
  • C. Because there is no symbolic connection between the woodpecker and her punishment
  • D. Because woodpeckers are known for their generosity in folklore

Answer: A. Because its constant pecking for food symbolically mirrors continuous labour, contrasting with her earlier refusal to give easily

Explanation: The relentless, laborious pecking of the woodpecker fittingly symbolises the ongoing toil the woman must now endure, a direct consequence of her earlier stinginess.

20. What does the poem suggest about how acts of kindness or unkindness towards strangers can carry significant consequences?

  • A. Treating strangers, who may be more than they appear, with kindness or unkindness can have serious repercussions
  • B. Strangers should always be treated with suspicion and denied any assistance
  • C. The poem suggests that actions towards strangers carry no consequences
  • D. Kindness towards strangers is portrayed as unnecessary or unimportant

Answer: A. Treating strangers, who may be more than they appear, with kindness or unkindness can have serious repercussions

Explanation: The revelation that the traveller was actually Saint Peter reinforces the poem's message about the potential significance of how we treat seemingly ordinary strangers.

21. What does the poem imply about the relationship between material wealth and true happiness or virtue?

  • A. Possessing material things, like cake, means little if one lacks the virtue of generosity
  • B. Material wealth is portrayed as the ultimate source of happiness
  • C. The poem suggests wealth and virtue are unrelated concepts
  • D. Material wealth is shown to guarantee moral goodness

Answer: A. Possessing material things, like cake, means little if one lacks the virtue of generosity

Explanation: The poem suggests that simply having material possessions, like the cake, holds little true value without accompanying generosity and virtue.

22. Why might this legend have endured as a memorable and often-repeated tale across generations?

  • A. Its simple yet powerful moral message about generosity makes it easily relatable and memorable
  • B. It contains complex, difficult-to-understand themes unsuitable for wide audiences
  • C. It has no clear moral message, making it forgettable
  • D. It focuses on themes irrelevant to most audiences

Answer: A. Its simple yet powerful moral message about generosity makes it easily relatable and memorable

Explanation: The legend's clear, easily understood moral lesson about the importance of generosity has likely contributed to its endurance and popularity across generations.

23. What might the transformation into a bird, rather than a more severe punishment, suggest about the nature of the curse?

  • A. It serves as a fitting, symbolic reminder of her selfishness rather than an outright destructive punishment
  • B. It suggests the punishment was extremely mild and inconsequential
  • C. It implies the curse had no real effect on her life
  • D. It shows the punishment was unrelated to her original offence

Answer: A. It serves as a fitting, symbolic reminder of her selfishness rather than an outright destructive punishment

Explanation: Transforming her into a bird that must constantly work for food serves as a symbolically appropriate consequence, directly tied to her earlier unwillingness to share.

24. What does the poem suggest about the visibility of a person's true character during moments of testing?

  • A. True character, whether generous or selfish, often reveals itself when a person is tested unexpectedly
  • B. A person's true character can never be revealed through such tests
  • C. The poem claims that testing reveals nothing meaningful about character
  • D. Only wealthy individuals are subject to such moral tests

Answer: A. True character, whether generous or selfish, often reveals itself when a person is tested unexpectedly

Explanation: The old woman's unexpected encounter with the disguised traveller serves as a test that reveals her true selfish character, a common theme in moral folk tales.

25. What does the poem's narrative structure, moving from ordinary event to supernatural consequence, achieve for readers?

  • A. It creates a memorable and dramatic way of teaching a valuable moral lesson
  • B. It confuses readers with an unclear storyline
  • C. It removes any moral significance from the tale
  • D. It has no particular impact on how the lesson is conveyed

Answer: A. It creates a memorable and dramatic way of teaching a valuable moral lesson

Explanation: The dramatic shift from an everyday scenario to a supernatural punishment effectively reinforces the poem's moral lesson in a memorable, engaging way.

26. What role does the setting of a humble Northland cottage play in establishing the story's atmosphere?

  • A. It creates a modest, folk-tale atmosphere fitting for a simple yet powerful moral story
  • B. It creates a grand, luxurious atmosphere unsuitable for a moral tale
  • C. The setting has no bearing on the story's atmosphere
  • D. It suggests the story takes place in a technologically advanced society

Answer: A. It creates a modest, folk-tale atmosphere fitting for a simple yet powerful moral story

Explanation: The humble cottage setting reinforces the simple, folk-tale nature of the story, appropriately framing its powerful moral message about generosity.

27. What broader cultural value does the poem's condemnation of stinginess reflect?

  • A. The widely shared cultural value that hospitality and generosity toward others, especially strangers, is important
  • B. A cultural value that prioritises personal wealth over kindness
  • C. A rejection of hospitality as a valued social practice
  • D. A value system with no connection to hospitality or kindness

Answer: A. The widely shared cultural value that hospitality and generosity toward others, especially strangers, is important

Explanation: The poem's central message reflects a broadly shared cultural value emphasising hospitality and generosity, particularly towards strangers or those in need.

28. What does the poem suggest happens when kindness is offered grudgingly rather than wholeheartedly?

  • A. Grudging kindness may still fail to satisfy the true spirit of generosity, leading to negative consequences
  • B. Grudging kindness is always considered equally valuable as wholehearted generosity
  • C. The poem suggests grudging kindness has no negative consequences at all
  • D. Grudging kindness is shown to be the ideal form of generosity

Answer: A. Grudging kindness may still fail to satisfy the true spirit of generosity, leading to negative consequences

Explanation: The poem illustrates that even a grudging, minimal act of giving falls short of true generosity, ultimately leading to the woman's severe punishment.

29. Why might children's literature and educational curricula often include legends like this one?

  • A. Because such stories effectively teach important moral values through engaging, memorable storytelling
  • B. Because legends have no educational value for children
  • C. Because such stories are considered too complex for young audiences
  • D. Because legends discourage moral development in children

Answer: A. Because such stories effectively teach important moral values through engaging, memorable storytelling

Explanation: Legends like this one are valuable in education because they convey important moral lessons through engaging and memorable narrative techniques, appealing to young readers.

30. What is the central theme of "A Legend of the Northland"?

  • A. The importance of generosity and the serious consequences that can result from selfishness and greed
  • B. The beauty of the northern landscape
  • C. The value of hard physical labour
  • D. The dangers of trusting religious figures

Answer: A. The importance of generosity and the serious consequences that can result from selfishness and greed

Explanation: The poem's central theme powerfully conveys the value of true generosity, warning against the negative consequences that selfishness and greed can ultimately bring.

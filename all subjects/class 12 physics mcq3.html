Ch-3: Current Electricity 25 Questions

1. What is the SI unit of electric current?

  • A. Volt
  • B. Ampere
  • C. Ohm
  • D. Coulomb

Answer: B. Ampere

Explanation: Electric current is defined as rate of flow of charge (I = Q/t), and its SI unit is Ampere (A), equivalent to Coulomb per second.

2. Electric current is a scalar quantity because:

  • A. It has magnitude only
  • B. It does not obey laws of vector addition
  • C. It flows in all directions
  • D. Its unit is Ampere

Answer: B. It does not obey laws of vector addition

Explanation: Although electric current has direction, it adds up algebraically like scalars rather than obeying vector addition rules, so it is a scalar quantity.

3. What is current density (J)?

  • A. Scalar quantity (J = I × A)
  • B. Vector quantity (J = I / A)
  • C. Scalar quantity (J = I / A)
  • D. Dimensionless quantity

Answer: B. Vector quantity (J = I / A)

Explanation: Current density is defined as electric current per unit cross-sectional area (J = I/A) and is a vector quantity directed along current flow.

4. According to Ohm's Law, the relation between potential difference (V) and current (I) is:

  • A. V ∝ I
  • B. V ∝ 1/I
  • C. V ∝ I²
  • D. V is independent of I

Answer: A. V ∝ I

Explanation: Ohm's Law states that current flowing through a conductor is directly proportional to potential difference across its ends (V = IR), provided physical conditions remain constant.

5. The microscopic form of Ohm’s Law is given by:

  • A. J = σ E
  • B. V = IR
  • C. I = n e A v_d
  • D. E = ρ J²

Answer: A. J = σ E

Explanation: The vector form/microscopic form of Ohm's Law relates current density (J), conductivity (σ), and electric field (E) as J = σ E.

6. What is the relation between electric current (I) and drift velocity (v_d)?

  • A. I = n e A v_d
  • B. I = n e / (A v_d)
  • C. I = v_d / (n e A)
  • D. I = n A v_d

Answer: A. I = n e A v_d

Explanation: Electric current flowing in a conductor is related to drift velocity by I = n e A v_d, where n is electron density and A is area of cross section.

7. When temperature of a metallic conductor increases, its resistance:

  • A. Decreases
  • B. Increases
  • C. Remains constant
  • D. Becomes zero

Answer: B. Increases

Explanation: Increasing temperature increases thermal vibrations of metal ions, decreasing relaxation time (τ) between electron collisions, thereby increasing resistance.

8. When temperature of a semiconductor increases, its resistance:

  • A. Increases
  • B. Decreases
  • C. Remains same
  • D. First increases then decreases

Answer: B. Decreases

Explanation: In semiconductors, raising temperature breaks covalent bonds, significantly increasing free charge carrier density (n), which lowers resistance.

9. What is the SI unit of Specific Resistance (Resistivity, ρ)?

  • A. Ohm (Ω)
  • B. Ohm-meter (Ω m)
  • C. Ohm / meter
  • D. Mho

Answer: B. Ohm-meter (Ω m)

Explanation: Resistance R = ρ L / A, which gives ρ = R A / L. The unit is (Ω × m²) / m = Ohm-meter (Ω m).

10. Electrical conductance is defined as the reciprocal of resistance. Its SI unit is:

  • A. Siemens (S) or Ohm^-1
  • B. Ohm-meter
  • C. Henry
  • D. Tesla

Answer: A. Siemens (S) or Ohm^-1

Explanation: Electrical conductance G = 1/R, so its SI unit is Siemens (S) or mho (Ω^-1).

11. If a wire of resistance R is stretched to double its original length keeping mass constant, its new resistance will be:

  • A. 2 R
  • B. 4 R
  • C. R / 2
  • D. R / 4

Answer: B. 4 R

Explanation: When length doubles (L' = 2L), area becomes half (A' = A/2) to keep volume constant. Thus R' = ρ (2L) / (A/2) = 4 (ρ L / A) = 4R.

12. What is the order of drift velocity of free electrons in a conductor?

  • A. 10^8 m/s
  • B. 10^-4 m/s
  • C. 10^3 m/s
  • D. 10^-10 m/s

Answer: B. 10^-4 m/s

Explanation: The average drift velocity of electrons under an applied electric field in standard conductors is very small, typically around 10^-4 m/s (or a few mm/s).

13. Kirchhoff’s First Law (Junction Rule), ∑ I = 0, is based on the conservation of:

  • A. Energy
  • B. Charge
  • C. Momentum
  • D. Mass

Answer: B. Charge

Explanation: Kirchhoff's Junction Rule states total current entering a junction equals total current leaving, which directly reflects conservation of electric charge.

14. Kirchhoff’s Second Law (Loop Rule), ∑ ΔV = 0, is based on the conservation of:

  • A. Charge
  • B. Energy
  • C. Linear momentum
  • D. Angular momentum

Answer: B. Energy

Explanation: The loop rule states sum of potential changes around any closed loop is zero, which is a statement of conservation of energy for electric circuits.

15. Wheatstone bridge is balanced when:

  • A. P / Q = R / S
  • B. P × Q = R × S
  • C. P + Q = R + S
  • D. P - Q = R - S

Answer: A. P / Q = R / S

Explanation: When no current flows through the galvanometer, the bridge is balanced and ratio of adjacent resistances satisfies P/Q = R/S.

16. Wheatstone bridge is most sensitive when all four resistances are:

  • A. Of equal magnitude order
  • B. Very high
  • C. Very low
  • D. Zero

Answer: A. Of equal magnitude order

Explanation: A Wheatstone bridge offers maximum sensitivity when all four arm resistances are nearly equal in order of magnitude.

17. Meter Bridge works on the principle of:

  • A. Potentiometer
  • B. Wheatstone Bridge
  • C. Faraday's Law
  • D. Joule's Heating Effect

Answer: B. Wheatstone Bridge

Explanation: A meter bridge is a practical setup operating on the balanced Wheatstone bridge principle to measure an unknown resistance.

18. The terminal potential difference (V) of a cell of EMF 'E' and internal resistance 'r' during discharging is:

  • A. V = E + Ir
  • B. V = E - Ir
  • C. V = E / Ir
  • D. V = E

Answer: B. V = E - Ir

Explanation: When a cell delivers current (discharging), terminal potential difference drops below EMF due to internal resistance loss: V = E - Ir.

19. When a cell is being charged by an external source, its terminal potential difference (V) is:

  • A. V = E - Ir
  • B. V = E + Ir
  • C. V = 0
  • D. V = E

Answer: B. V = E + Ir

Explanation: During charging, current enters positive terminal of the cell, making terminal voltage higher than its EMF: V = E + Ir.

20. In parallel combination of two cells of EMFs E₁, E₂ and internal resistances r₁, r₂, equivalent EMF is:

  • A. E_eq = (E₁r₂ + E₂r₁) / (r₁ + r₂)
  • B. E_eq = E₁ + E₂
  • C. E_eq = (E₁r₁ + E₂r₂) / (r₁ + r₂)
  • D. E_eq = E₁ - E₂

Answer: A. E_eq = (E₁r₂ + E₂r₁) / (r₁ + r₂)

Explanation: For two cells connected in parallel aiding each other, equivalent EMF is given by E_eq = (E₁/r₁ + E₂/r₂) / (1/r₁ + 1/r₂) = (E₁r₂ + E₂r₁) / (r₁ + r₂).

21. Potentiometer is preferred over a voltmeter for measuring EMF because:

  • A. It draws no current from the cell at null point
  • B. It is very small and portable
  • C. It measures internal resistance directly
  • D. It works on AC only

Answer: A. It draws no current from the cell at null point

Explanation: Potentiometer acts as an ideal voltmeter with infinite resistance at null balance point, measuring true open-circuit EMF without drawing current.

22. Sensitivity of a potentiometer can be increased by:

  • A. Decreasing length of potentiometer wire
  • B. Increasing length of potentiometer wire
  • C. Increasing current in primary circuit
  • D. Using thicker wire

Answer: B. Increasing length of potentiometer wire

Explanation: Increasing wire length decreases potential gradient (K = V/L), making the potentiometer more sensitive to small voltage differences.

23. According to Joule's Law of heating, heat produced in a resistor of resistance R carrying current I for time t is:

  • A. H = I R t
  • B. H = I² R t
  • C. H = I R² t
  • D. H = I² R / t

Answer: B. H = I² R t

Explanation: Joule's heating effect states heat generated H = I² R t (in Joules), directly proportional to square of current, resistance, and time.

24. What is the electric power consumed by a device of resistance R connected across potential difference V?

  • A. P = V I = I² R = V² / R
  • B. P = V / I
  • C. P = V² R
  • D. P = I R²

Answer: A. P = V I = I² R = V² / R

Explanation: Electric power is work done per unit time: P = V × I. Substituting V = IR gives P = I²R = V²/R.

25. Two electric bulbs rated (60 W, 220 V) and (100 W, 220 V) are connected in series across 220 V supply. Which bulb will glow brighter?

  • A. 100 W bulb
  • B. 60 W bulb
  • C. Both glow equally
  • D. Neither will glow

Answer: B. 60 W bulb

Explanation: R = V²/P, so 60 W bulb has higher resistance. In series, current I is same, power consumed P_consumed = I²R, so higher resistance bulb (60 W) glows brighter.

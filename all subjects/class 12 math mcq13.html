Ch-13: Probability 12 Questions

1. The conditional probability P(A|B) is defined as:

  • A. P(A∩B) / P(B), provided P(B) ≠ 0
  • B. P(A) + P(B)
  • C. P(A) · P(B)
  • D. P(A) - P(B)

Answer: A. P(A∩B) / P(B), provided P(B) ≠ 0

Explanation: Conditional probability measures the likelihood of event A occurring given that event B has already occurred.

2. Two events A and B are said to be independent if:

  • A. P(A∩B) = P(A) · P(B)
  • B. P(A∩B) = P(A) + P(B)
  • C. P(A|B) = 0
  • D. A and B are mutually exclusive

Answer: A. P(A∩B) = P(A) · P(B)

Explanation: Independence means the occurrence of one event does not affect the probability of the other, expressed through this multiplication rule.

3. Bayes' theorem is primarily used to find:

  • A. The probability of a cause given an observed effect
  • B. The probability of independent events only
  • C. The mean of a probability distribution
  • D. The variance of a probability distribution

Answer: A. The probability of a cause given an observed effect

Explanation: Bayes' theorem allows the reversal of conditional probabilities, useful for finding posterior probabilities of causes given observed outcomes.

4. A random variable that can take only specific, countable values is called a:

  • A. Discrete random variable
  • B. Continuous random variable
  • C. Independent variable
  • D. Dependent variable, without further qualification

Answer: A. Discrete random variable

Explanation: Discrete random variables take on distinct, separate values, such as the outcomes of rolling a die.

5. The probability distribution of a discrete random variable must satisfy the condition that:

  • A. The sum of all the probabilities equals 1
  • B. The sum of all the probabilities equals 0
  • C. Each individual probability equals 1
  • D. Probabilities can take negative values

Answer: A. The sum of all the probabilities equals 1

Explanation: Since the random variable must take one of its possible values with certainty, all probabilities in its distribution must sum to 1.

6. The mean (expectation) of a discrete random variable X is given by:

  • A. E(X) = Σ x · P(x)
  • B. E(X) = Σ P(x)
  • C. E(X) = Σ x
  • D. E(X) = x · n

Answer: A. E(X) = Σ x · P(x)

Explanation: The expectation is calculated as the weighted average of all possible values, weighted by their respective probabilities.

7. Two events are called mutually exclusive if:

  • A. They cannot occur simultaneously, i.e., A∩B = ∅
  • B. They always occur together
  • C. They are statistically independent
  • D. Their individual probabilities are equal

Answer: A. They cannot occur simultaneously, i.e., A∩B = ∅

Explanation: Mutually exclusive events share no common outcomes, so both cannot happen at the same time.

8. The Binomial distribution is applicable in situations involving:

  • A. A fixed number of independent trials, each with exactly two possible outcomes
  • B. Continuous outcomes over an interval
  • C. An infinite, unspecified number of trials
  • D. Dependent trials only

Answer: A. A fixed number of independent trials, each with exactly two possible outcomes

Explanation: The Binomial distribution models the number of successes in a fixed number of independent Bernoulli trials, each with the same success probability.

9. For two mutually exclusive events A and B, P(A∪B) is equal to:

  • A. P(A) + P(B)
  • B. P(A) · P(B)
  • C. P(A) - P(B)
  • D. P(A|B)

Answer: A. P(A) + P(B)

Explanation: Since mutually exclusive events have no overlap, the probability of their union is simply the sum of their individual probabilities.

10. The theorem of total probability is applied when the sample space is partitioned into:

  • A. Mutually exclusive and exhaustive events
  • B. Independent events only, without any other condition
  • C. A single event covering the entire sample space
  • D. Continuous intervals only

Answer: A. Mutually exclusive and exhaustive events

Explanation: The theorem requires the sample space to be divided into non-overlapping events that together cover all possible outcomes.

11. In Bayes' theorem, the probabilities P(Ei), assigned before observing any evidence, are called:

  • A. Prior probabilities
  • B. Posterior probabilities
  • C. Conditional probabilities, without further distinction
  • D. Joint probabilities, without further distinction

Answer: A. Prior probabilities

Explanation: Prior probabilities represent the initial estimated likelihoods of each event before any additional evidence is taken into account.

12. If P(A) = 1 for some event A, then A is said to be a(n):

  • A. Certain (sure) event
  • B. Impossible event
  • C. Mutually exclusive event, without further qualification
  • D. Independent event, without further qualification

Answer: A. Certain (sure) event

Explanation: A probability of exactly 1 indicates that the event is certain to occur.

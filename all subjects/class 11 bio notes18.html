NCERT Solutions for Class 11 Biology Chapter 18 Neural Control and Coordination

NCERT Solutions for Class 11 Biology Chapter 18 Neural Control and Coordination covers the structure of a neuron, generation and conduction of nerve impulses, the human nervous system, and the structure and function of the eye and ear.

  • 1. Neuron and Nerve Impulse
  • 2. Human Nervous System
  • 3. Sensory Reception: Eye and Ear

1. Neuron and Nerve Impulse

Short Note:

• A neuron consists of a cell body (soma), dendrites (receive signals), and an axon (transmits signals); a synapse is the junction between two neurons where signal transmission occurs, usually via neurotransmitters (chemical synapse).

• Nerve impulse conduction involves generation of an action potential, based on changes in membrane permeability to Na+ and K+ ions, causing depolarisation and repolarisation of the neuron membrane.

2. Human Nervous System

Short Note:

• Divided into Central Nervous System (CNS) — brain and spinal cord (control and coordination) — and Peripheral Nervous System (PNS) — cranial and spinal nerves.

• The brain is divided into forebrain (cerebrum — higher functions, thalamus, hypothalamus), midbrain, and hindbrain (cerebellum — balance/coordination, pons, medulla — controls involuntary functions like breathing/heart rate).

3. Sensory Reception: Eye and Ear

Short Note:

• The eye is the organ of vision; light is focused by the lens onto the retina, where photoreceptor cells (rods for dim light, cones for colour vision) convert light into nerve impulses sent to the brain via the optic nerve.

• The ear is the organ for both hearing (via the cochlea, converting sound waves into nerve impulses) and balance/equilibrium (via the semicircular canals, utricle, and saccule of the inner ear).

NCERT Textbook Exercise Questions

Draw the structure of a neuron and explain its function.
Answer: A neuron has a cell body containing the nucleus, dendrites extending from the cell body that receive incoming signals, and a long axon (often myelinated) that conducts the nerve impulse away from the cell body to the axon terminal, which transmits the signal to the next neuron or effector organ via a synapse.
Explain the mechanism of generation of an action potential.
Answer: At rest, the neuron membrane is polarised (inside negative relative to outside). Upon stimulation reaching threshold, voltage-gated Na+ channels open, causing rapid influx of Na+ ions, making the inside positive (depolarisation). This is followed by opening of K+ channels and efflux of K+ ions, restoring the negative internal charge (repolarisation), and this local change propagates along the axon as a nerve impulse (action potential).
Explain the structure of the human eye with the help of a labelled diagram.
Answer: The human eye consists of an outer sclera (protective) and cornea (transparent, allows light entry), a middle choroid layer (pigmented, includes the iris controlling pupil size and the ciliary body controlling lens shape), and an inner retina containing photoreceptors (rods and cones) that convert light signals into nerve impulses transmitted via the optic nerve to the brain.
Distinguish between rods and cones present in the retina of the human eye.
Answer: Rods are highly sensitive to dim light and are responsible for vision in low light (scotopic vision) but do not detect colour, containing the pigment rhodopsin. Cones function best in bright light (photopic vision) and are responsible for colour vision and high visual acuity, being of three types sensitive to red, green, or blue light respectively.

Ch-4: Carbon and its Compounds 30 Questions

1. How many valence electrons does a carbon atom have in its outermost shell?

  • A. 2
  • B. 4
  • C. 6
  • D. 8

Answer: B. 4

Explanation: Carbon has atomic number 6 with electronic configuration 2, 4. It has 4 valence electrons.

2. Carbon forms bonds with other elements by:

  • A. Gaining 4 electrons
  • B. Losing 4 electrons
  • C. Sharing electrons (covalent bonding)
  • D. Transferring electrons (ionic bonding)

Answer: C. Sharing electrons (covalent bonding)

Explanation: Carbon achieves a stable octet by sharing valence electrons with other atoms, forming covalent bonds.

3. The unique property of carbon to form bonds with other atoms of carbon giving rise to large molecules is called:

  • A. Tetravalency
  • B. Catenation
  • C. Isomerism
  • D. Polymerization

Answer: B. Catenation

Explanation: Catenation is the self-linking property of carbon atoms to form long chains, branched chains, or rings.

4. What is the general formula for saturated hydrocarbons (Alkanes)?

  • A. CₙH₂ₙ₊₂
  • B. CₙH₂ₙ
  • C. CₙH₂ₙ₋₂
  • D. CₙH₂ₙ₊₁

Answer: A. CₙH₂ₙ₊₂

Explanation: Alkanes are saturated hydrocarbons with single bonds, having general formula CₙH₂ₙ₊₂.

5. What is the general formula for Alkenes (containing one double bond)?

  • A. CₙH₂ₙ₊₂
  • B. CₙH₂ₙ
  • C. CₙH₂ₙ₋₂
  • D. CₙH₂ₙ₊₁OH

Answer: B. CₙH₂ₙ

Explanation: Unsaturated hydrocarbons containing a carbon-carbon double bond are called alkenes (CₙH₂ₙ).

6. What is the general formula for Alkynes (containing one triple bond)?

  • A. CₙH₂ₙ₊₂
  • B. CₙH₂ₙ
  • C. CₙH₂ₙ₋₂
  • D. CₙH₂ₙ₊₂O

Answer: C. CₙH₂ₙ₋₂

Explanation: Unsaturated hydrocarbons containing a carbon-carbon triple bond are called alkynes (CₙH₂ₙ₋₂).

7. How many single covalent bonds are present in a molecule of Ethane (C₂H₆)?

  • A. 6
  • B. 7
  • C. 8
  • D. 9

Answer: B. 7

Explanation: Ethane has 1 C-C single bond and 6 C-H single bonds, making a total of 7 covalent bonds.

8. Buckminsterfullerene is an allotrope of carbon consisting of how many carbon atoms arranged in a football shape?

  • A. 20
  • B. 40
  • C. 60
  • D. 100

Answer: C. 60

Explanation: Buckminsterfullerene (C-60) contains 60 carbon atoms joined together in spherical geodesic structure.

9. Which functional group is present in aldehydes?

  • A. -OH
  • B. -CHO
  • C. -COOH
  • D. >C=O

Answer: B. -CHO

Explanation: The functional group -CHO represents an aldehyde group.

10. What is the functional group present in carboxylic acids?

  • A. -OH
  • B. -CHO
  • C. -COOH
  • D. -CO-

Answer: C. -COOH

Explanation: Carboxylic acids contain the carboxyl group (-COOH).

11. What is the functional group present in Ketones?

  • A. -OH
  • B. -CHO
  • C. >C=O
  • D. -COOH

Answer: C. >C=O

Explanation: Ketones contain a carbonyl group (>C=O) attached to two carbon chains.

12. Two consecutive members of a homologous series differ by a mass of:

  • A. 12 u
  • B. 14 u
  • C. 16 u
  • D. 18 u

Answer: B. 14 u

Explanation: Consecutive members differ by a -CH₂- group, which has a molecular mass of 12 + (2×1) = 14 u.

13. Structural isomers are compounds that have the same molecular formula but different:

  • A. Physical states
  • B. Molecular masses
  • C. Structural arrangements
  • D. Percentage composition

Answer: C. Structural arrangements

Explanation: Isomers share identical molecular formulas but possess different carbon chain structures.

14. How many structural isomers are possible for Butane (C₄H₁₀)?

  • A. 2
  • B. 3
  • C. 4
  • D. 5

Answer: A. 2

Explanation: Butane has 2 isomers: n-butane and isobutane (2-methylpropane).

15. What catalyst is used during the hydrogenation of vegetable oils to form vegetable ghee?

  • A. Iron
  • B. Nickel or Palladium
  • C. Copper
  • D. Zinc

Answer: B. Nickel or Palladium

Explanation: Addition reaction of hydrogen to unsaturated vegetable oils uses Nickel (Ni) or Palladium (Pd) as catalyst.

16. Alkaline potassium permanganate (KMnO₄) or acidified potassium dichromate (K₂Cr₂O⇇) acts as a/an:

  • A. Reducing agent
  • B. Oxidizing agent
  • C. Dehydrating agent
  • D. Catalyst for substitution

Answer: B. Oxidizing agent

Explanation: They supply oxygen to convert alcohols into carboxylic acids, acting as strong oxidizing agents.

17. Reaction of ethanol with concentrated sulphuric acid at 443 K yields:

  • A. Ethane
  • B. Ethene
  • C. Ethyne
  • D. Ethanoic acid

Answer: B. Ethene

Explanation: Hot conc. H₂SO₄ acts as a dehydrating agent, converting ethanol (CH₃CH₂OH) to ethene (CH₂=CH₂) by removing water.

18. The sweet-smelling compounds formed when a carboxylic acid reacts with an alcohol in the presence of an acid catalyst are called:

  • A. Aldehydes
  • B. Esters
  • C. Ketones
  • D. Alkynes

Answer: B. Esters

Explanation: Carboxylic Acid + Alcohol → Ester + Water. Esters are sweet-smelling substances used in perfumes and flavorings.

19. Saponification is a process used for the preparation of:

  • A. Detergents
  • B. Soaps
  • C. Esters
  • D. Alcohols

Answer: B. Soaps

Explanation: Alkaline hydrolysis of esters (or fats/oils) with NaOH gives soap (sodium salts of fatty acids).

20. What gas is liberated with brisk effervescence when ethanoic acid reacts with sodium hydrogen carbonate?

  • A. Hydrogen
  • B. Oxygen
  • C. Carbon dioxide
  • D. Nitrogen

Answer: C. Carbon dioxide

Explanation: CH₃COOH + NaHCO₃ → CH₃COONa + H₂O + CO₂↑. CO₂ gas causes brisk effervescence.

21. 5% to 8% solution of acetic acid in water is known as:

  • A. Absolute alcohol
  • B. Rectified spirit
  • C. Vinegar
  • D. Formalin

Answer: C. Vinegar

Explanation: A 5-8% solution of ethanoic acid (acetic acid) in water is widely used as a preservative known as vinegar.

22. Why is pure ethanoic acid often called 'glacial acetic acid'?

  • A. It glows in the dark
  • B. It freezes into ice-like crystals at 290 K (17°C)
  • C. It is extracted from glaciers
  • D. It turns white on boiling

Answer: B. It freezes into ice-like crystals at 290 K (17°C)

Explanation: Because its melting point is 290 K, pure ethanoic acid often freezes during winters in cold climates.

23. In a soap molecule, the ionic end is:

  • A. Hydrophobic (water repelling)
  • B. Hydrophilic (water loving)
  • C. Lipophilic (oil loving)
  • D. Non-polar

Answer: B. Hydrophilic (water loving)

Explanation: The ionic head of a soap molecule interacts with water (hydrophilic), while the long hydrocarbon tail interacts with oil (hydrophobic).

24. Spherical clusters formed by soap molecules in water during cleaning action are called:

  • A. Emulsions
  • B. Micelles
  • C. Precipitates
  • D. Colloids

Answer: B. Micelles

Explanation: Soap molecules align with hydrophobic tails inwards toward oil droplets, forming structures called micelles.

25. What insoluble precipitate (scum) is formed when soap is used in hard water?

  • A. Calcium and Magnesium salts of fatty acids
  • B. Sodium and Potassium salts
  • C. Iron oxide
  • D. Sodium chloride

Answer: A. Calcium and Magnesium salts of fatty acids

Explanation: Soap reacts with Ca²⁺ and Mg²⁺ ions present in hard water to form an insoluble sticky precipitate called scum.

26. Detergents are ammonium or sulfonate salts of long chain carboxylic acids that:

  • A. Do not form scum with hard water
  • B. Form scum only in warm water
  • C. Cannot clean oil stains
  • D. Are non-synthetic

Answer: A. Do not form scum with hard water

Explanation: Detergents remain soluble and effective even in hard water because their charged ends do not form insoluble precipitates with Ca²⁺ or Mg²⁺.

27. What is the molecular formula of Cyclohexane?

  • A. C₆H₆
  • B. C₆H₁₂
  • C. C₆H₁₄
  • D. C₅H₁₀

Answer: B. C₆H₁₂

Explanation: Cyclohexane is a cyclic saturated hydrocarbon with formula C₆H₁₂.

28. What is the chemical formula of Benzene?

  • A. C₆H₁₂
  • B. C₆H₆
  • C. C₆H₁₄
  • D. C₂H₂

Answer: B. C₆H₆

Explanation: Benzene is a cyclic unsaturated aromatic hydrocarbon with formula C₆H₆ (alternating single and double bonds).

29. The IUPAC name of Propanone belongs to which family of organic compounds?

  • A. Alcohol
  • B. Aldehyde
  • C. Ketone
  • D. Carboxylic acid

Answer: C. Ketone

Explanation: Propanone (CH₃COCH₃) ends with suffix '-one', indicating it is a ketone.

30. Unsaturation in hydrocarbons can be tested by the discoloration of:

  • A. Lime water
  • B. Bromine water
  • C. Litmus solution
  • D. Phenolphthalein

Answer: B. Bromine water

Explanation: Unsaturated hydrocarbons (alkenes/alkynes) decolorize reddish-brown bromine water through addition reactions.

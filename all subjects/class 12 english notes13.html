NCERT Solutions for Class 12 English Chapter 13 Aunt Jennifer's Tigers

NCERT Solutions for Class 12 English Flamingo Chapter 13 Aunt Jennifer's Tigers by Adrienne Rich covers the theme of female oppression and creative freedom, poetic devices, and important questions and answers.

1. About the Poet and Poem

Short Note:

• Adrienne Rich was an influential American poet known for exploring feminist themes, and this poem portrays Aunt Jennifer, a woman who expresses her suppressed desires for freedom and confidence through her embroidery of bold, fearless tigers.

• Despite her creative, imaginative embroidery depicting tigers as fearless and free creatures, Aunt Jennifer herself leads a constrained, oppressed life, symbolised by her trembling hands and the heavy wedding ring she wears, representing the burdens of marriage and patriarchal control.

• The poem concludes by noting that even after Aunt Jennifer's death, the tigers she created will continue to prance boldly and fearlessly, symbolising the enduring nature of creative expression beyond the confines of oppressive circumstances.

2. Theme & Central Idea

Short Note:

• The poem powerfully explores themes of female oppression within patriarchal marriage structures, illustrating how creative expression can become an important outlet for expressing suppressed desires for freedom, confidence and independence.

• It also highlights the contrast between the physical, emotional constraints experienced by Aunt Jennifer in her own life and the fearless, liberated symbolism embodied by the tigers she creates through her art.

• The poem suggests that creative expression can outlast and transcend personal suffering, allowing an individual's true spirit and desires to endure symbolically, even beyond their own lifetime.

3. Poetic Devices

Short Note:

• Symbolism: The tigers symbolise freedom, confidence and fearlessness, standing in stark contrast to Aunt Jennifer's own constrained, oppressed reality.

• Imagery: Vivid, contrasting imagery, such as the bold, prancing tigers versus Aunt Jennifer's trembling hands and heavy wedding ring, powerfully conveys the poem's central themes.

• Irony: There's a poignant irony in Aunt Jennifer creating symbols of fearless freedom through embroidery, a traditionally domestic, submissive activity, highlighting the complexity of her suppressed identity.

Reading With Insight (Textbook Questions)

Q1: What do the tigers symbolise in the poem, and how do they contrast with Aunt Jennifer's real life?
Answer: The tigers symbolise courage, confidence, freedom, and fearlessness, qualities that starkly contrast with Aunt Jennifer's own oppressed, constrained existence, marked by her trembling hands and the heavy burden symbolised by her wedding ring, highlighting the gap between her imaginative expression and her lived reality.
Q2: What does the imagery of 'the massive weight of Uncle's wedding band' suggest about Aunt Jennifer's marriage?
Answer: This imagery suggests that Aunt Jennifer's marriage is experienced as oppressive and burdensome, symbolising patriarchal control and the suppression of her individual freedom and identity within the traditional constraints of marriage.
Q3: What does the poem suggest will happen to the tigers even after Aunt Jennifer's death?
Answer: The poem suggests that even after Aunt Jennifer's death, the tigers she embroidered will continue to 'prance', unaffected by mortality, symbolising the lasting, enduring nature of creative expression and the triumphant liberation of her true spirit through art, beyond the limitations of her own oppressed lifetime.

Extract-Based / Short Answer Questions

Q4: How does the poem use the act of embroidery to comment on gender roles and creative freedom?
Answer: The poem uses embroidery, traditionally viewed as a submissive, domestic activity associated with women, ironically as a medium through which Aunt Jennifer expresses powerful, liberating imagery, subtly critiquing traditional gender roles by showing how creative expression can subvert and transcend imposed societal limitations.
Q5: What broader feminist message does the poem convey?
Answer: The poem conveys a broader feminist message about the oppressive nature of traditional patriarchal marriage structures, highlighting how women's individual identities, desires and creative expressions are often suppressed, yet can still find powerful, enduring outlets through art, symbolising hope for eventual liberation and self-expression beyond societal constraints.

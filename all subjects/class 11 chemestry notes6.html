1. Equilibrium in Physical & Chemical Processes and Dynamic Nature

Short Note:

• Equilibrium State: A state in a reversible reaction where the rate of the forward reaction equals the rate of the reverse reaction, making the concentrations of reactants and products constant over time.

• Dynamic Nature of Equilibrium: Reactions do not stop at equilibrium; instead, both forward and backward reactions continue at equal speeds.

• Physical Equilibrium Examples:

  - Solid-Liquid: Ice ⇌ Water at 0°C.

  - Liquid-Vapor: Water ⇌ Water Vapor in a sealed container.

  - Solid-Vapor: Iodine (s) ⇌ Iodine (v).

2. Law of Mass Action & Equilibrium Constants (Kc and Kp)

Short Note:

• Law of Chemical Equilibrium: For a reversible reaction aA + bB ⇌ cC + dD, the equilibrium expression is:

  - In terms of concentration: Kc = [C]c[D]d / ([A]a[B]b).

  - In terms of partial pressure: Kp = (PCc · PDd) / (PAa · PBb).

• Relationship between Kp and Kc:

  - Kp = Kc (RT)Δng, where Δng = (moles of gaseous products) - (moles of gaseous reactants).

• Properties of K:

  - If a reaction is reversed, the new equilibrium constant is K' = 1/K.

  - If a reaction is multiplied by a factor n, the new equilibrium constant becomes K' = Kn.

3. Factors Affecting Equilibrium – Le Chatelier's Principle

Short Note:

• Le Chatelier's Principle: If a system at equilibrium is subjected to a change in concentration, pressure, temperature, or volume, the system adjusts itself to counteract the effect of the change.

• Effect of Changes:

  - Concentration: Adding reactant shifts equilibrium forward; adding product shifts it backward.

  - Pressure: Increasing pressure shifts equilibrium toward the side with fewer gaseous moles.

  - Temperature: Increasing temperature favors endothermic reactions (ΔH > 0); decreasing temperature favors exothermic reactions (ΔH < 0).

  - Catalyst: Increases the rates of both forward and reverse reactions equally; does not alter the equilibrium position or value of K.

4. Ionic Equilibrium: Acids, Bases, pH, Buffers & Solubility Product (Ksp)

Short Note:

• Acids and Bases Concepts:

  - Arrhenius: Acids give H+ ions; Bases give OH- ions in aqueous solution.

  - Brønsted-Lowry: Acids are proton (H+) donors; Bases are proton acceptors.

  - Lewis: Acids are electron-pair acceptors (e.g., BF3, AlCl3); Bases are electron-pair donors (e.g., NH3, H2O).

• pH Scale: pH = -log[H+] and pOH = -log[OH-]. At 25°C, pH + pOH = 14.

• Buffer Solutions: Solutions that resist changes in pH upon addition of small amounts of acid or base (e.g., CH3COOH + CH3COONa).

• Solubility Product Constant (Ksp): For a sparingly soluble salt AxBy(s) ⇌ xAy+(aq) + yBx-(aq), Ksp = [Ay+]x [Bx-]y.

Intext & Conceptual Questions

Q1: Write the expression for the equilibrium constant Kc for the reaction: N2(g) + 3H2(g) ⇌ 2NH3(g). Find the relationship between Kp and Kc for this reaction.
Answer:
1. Concentration Equilibrium Constant Expression (Kc):
Kc = [NH3]2 / ([N2] [H2]3)

2. Relationship between Kp and Kc:
Formula: Kp = Kc (RT)Δng
Calculate Δng (moles of gaseous products - moles of gaseous reactants):
Δng = 2 - (1 + 3) = 2 - 4 = -2.

Therefore, Kp = Kc (RT)-2 or Kp = Kc / (RT)2.

Textbook Chapter End Exercise Questions

Q1: What will be the conjugate base for the Brønsted acids HF, H2SO4, and HCO3-?
Answer:
A conjugate base is formed when a Brønsted acid donates a proton (H+):

• HF: HF - H+ = F- (Fluoride ion)
• H2SO4: H2SO4 - H+ = HSO4- (Hydrogen sulfate ion)
• HCO3-: HCO3- - H+ = CO32- (Carbonate ion)
Q2: The concentration of hydrogen ion in a sample of soft drink is 3.8 × 10-3 M. What is its pH?
Answer:
Given:
[H+] = 3.8 × 10-3 M

Formula:
pH = -log[H+]
pH = -log(3.8 × 10-3)
pH = -[log(3.8) + log(10-3)]
Since log(3.8) ≈ 0.58 and log(10-3) = -3:
pH = -[0.58 - 3]
pH = -[-2.42] = 2.42.

The pH of the soft drink is 2.42 (Acidic).

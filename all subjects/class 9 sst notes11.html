NCERT Solutions for Class 9 Geography Chapter 6 Population

NCERT Solutions for Class 9 Geography Chapter 6 covers Population Size and Distribution, Population Growth and Processes of Change, Characteristics of Population, and National Population Policy (NPP).

Overview of Topics & Subtopics (Click to Jump)

  • 1. Population Size, Distribution & Density
  • 2. Population Growth & Processes of Change
  • 3. Age Composition, Literacy, Health & NPP

1. Population Size, Distribution & Density

Short Note:

• Census: An official enumeration of population done periodically (every 10 years in India).

• Population Density: Number of persons per unit area (calculated per sq. km). India is one of the most densely populated countries in the world.

• Uneven Distribution: Uttar Pradesh is the most populous state, while Sikkim has a small population. Densely populated areas include the Northern Plains and Kerala, whereas hilly and arid regions (e.g., Rajasthan, Arunachal Pradesh) have low density.

2. Population Growth & Processes of Change

Short Note:

• Processes of Population Change: Birth Rate, Death Rate, and Migration.

• Birth Rate ($BR$): Number of live births per 1,000 people in a year.

• Death Rate ($DR$): Number of deaths per 1,000 people in a year.

• Natural Growth Rate: Difference between birth rate and death rate ($Natural \ Growth = BR - DR$).

• Migration: Movement of people across regions/territories. Can be Internal (within country, shifts demographic distribution) or International (between countries, alters population size).

3. Age Composition, Literacy, Health & NPP

Short Note:

• Age Composition: Grouped into Children (<15 yrs, dependent), Working Age (15–59 yrs, economically active), and Aged (60+ yrs, dependent).

• Sex Ratio: Number of females per 1,000 males in the population.

• Literacy Rate: According to Census, a person aged 7 and above who can read and write with understanding in any language is considered literate.

• National Population Policy (NPP 2000): Aims at free and compulsory education up to age 14, reducing infant mortality rate, achieving universal immunization, and promoting delayed marriage for girls.

Intext & Conceptual Questions

Q1: Distinguish between Population Growth and Population Change.
Population Growth Population Change
Refers specifically to the increase in the number of inhabitants of a region during a specific period. Refers to the overall modification in the size, structure, and distribution of population (increase or decrease).
Expressed in absolute numbers or as a percentage per annum. Influenced by Birth rate, Death rate, and Migration patterns.
Q2: What are the primary factors responsible for the uneven distribution of population in India?
Answer:
1. Topography & Terrain: Flat fertile plains (like Ganga plains) attract high density, whereas rugged mountains (like Himalayas) have low density.
2. Climate: Moderate rainfall and pleasant temperatures favor settlement, whereas extreme hot or cold deserts hinder population growth.
3. Economic Development: Industrial hubs, urban centers, and availability of jobs attract higher populations due to migration.

Textbook Chapter End Exercise Questions

Q1: Choose the correct answer from the options given below:
(i) A person aged 7 years and above who can read and write with understanding in any language is treated as:
(a) Literate   (b) Graduate   (c) Intelligent   (d) Educated
Answer: (a) Literate

(ii) Migrations change the number, distribution and composition of the population in:
(a) the area of departure   (b) the area of arrival   (c) both the area of departure and arrival   (d) none of the above
Answer: (c) both the area of departure and arrival

(iii) A large proportion of children in a population is a result of:
(a) high birth rates   (b) high life expectancies   (c) high death rates   (d) more married couples
Answer: (a) high birth rates

(iv) The magnitude of population growth refers to:
(a) total population of an area   (b) the number of persons added each year   (c) the rate at which population increases   (d) number of females per 1000 males
Answer: (b) the number of persons added each year
Q2: What are the significant features of the National Population Policy 2000?
Answer:
1. Free & Compulsory Education: Imparting school education free up to 14 years of age.
2. Healthcare Targets: Reducing Infant Mortality Rate (IMR) to below 30 per 1,000 live births.
3. Universal Immunization: Achieving 100% immunization of children against all vaccine-preventable diseases.
4. Adolescent Focus: Promoting delayed marriage for girls and addressing nutritional needs and risks of adolescents.
Q3: How does internal migration affect the population structure of a region?
Answer:
1. No Change in Overall Size: Internal migration does not alter the total population of the country.
2. Redistribution: It alters the geographic distribution of population across urban and rural sectors.
3. Demographic Shift: Rural-to-urban migration often increases the working-age male population in cities while leaving more dependent populations in rural origin areas.

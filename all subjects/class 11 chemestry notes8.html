NCERT Solutions for Class 11 Chemistry Chapter 8 Organic Chemistry - Some Basic Principles and Techniques

NCERT Solutions for Class 11 Chemistry Chapter 8 Organic Chemistry - Some Basic Principles and Techniques covers tetravalence of carbon, shapes of organic molecules, classification of organic compounds, IUPAC nomenclature, isomerism (structural and stereoisomerism), fundamental concepts in organic reaction mechanisms (inductive, electromeric, resonance, and hyperconjugation effects), bond cleavage, reactive intermediates, and methods for purification and quantitative analysis of organic compounds.

1. Classification, IUPAC Nomenclature & Isomerism of Organic Compounds

Short Note:

• Tetravalence of Carbon: Carbon forms four covalent bonds using sp3 (tetrahedral, 109.5°), sp2 (trigonal planar, 120°), or sp (linear, 180°) hybridization.

• IUPAC Rules:

  - Identify the longest continuous carbon chain (Parent Chain).

  - Number the chain to give the principal functional group the lowest locant number.

  - Format: Prefix (Substituents) + Word Root (Chain length) + Primary Suffix (Saturation) + Secondary Suffix (Functional Group).

• Isomerism: Compounds having the same molecular formula but different structural arrangements or spatial configurations.

  - Structural Isomerism: Chain, Position, Functional group, and Metamerism.

  - Stereoisomerism: Geometrical (cis/trans) and Optical isomerism.

2. Electronic Effects in Organic Molecules

Short Note:

• Inductive Effect (I-Effect): Permanent displacement of sigma (σ) electrons along a carbon chain due to electronegativity differences. (+I: alkyl groups; -I: -NO2, -COOH, -X, -CN).

• Electromeric Effect (E-Effect): Temporary displacement of pi (π) electrons in a multiple bond under the influence of an attacking reagent (+E and -E).

• Resonance / Mesomeric Effect (R/M Effect): Delocalization of pi (π) or non-bonding electrons across conjugated systems.

  - +R Effect: Electron-donating groups (-OH, -NH2, -OR).

  - -R Effect: Electron-withdrawing groups (-NO2, -COOH, -CHO, -CN).

• Hyperconjugation (No-Bond Resonance): Delocalization of sigma (σ) C-H electrons of an alkyl group directly attached to an unsaturated system or carbocation (stability order: 3° > 2° > 1° carbocation).

3. Cleavage of Covalent Bonds & Reactive Intermediates

Short Note:

• Homolytic Cleavage: Symmetric bond breaking producing neutral species with an unpaired electron called Free Radicals (e.g., A:B → A• + B•).

• Heterolytic Cleavage: Unsymmetric bond breaking where the more electronegative atom takes both bonding electrons, forming ions:

  - Carbocation (C+): Positively charged carbon, sp2 hybridized, planar geometry (Stability: 3° > 2° > 1° > CH3+).

  - Carbanion (C-): Negatively charged carbon, sp3 hybridized, pyramidal (Stability: CH3- > 1° > 2° > 3°).

• Attacking Reagents:

  - Electrophiles (E+): Electron-deficient, Lewis acids (e.g., H+, NO2+, AlCl3, BF3).

  - Nucleophiles (Nu-): Electron-rich, electron-pair donors, Lewis bases (e.g., OH-, CN-, NH3, H2O).

4. Methods of Purification & Quantitative Analysis of Organic Compounds

Short Note:

• Purification Techniques: Sublimation, Crystallisation, Simple Distillation, Fractional Distillation, Steam Distillation, Vacuum Distillation, and Chromatography.

• Qualitative Analysis (Lassaigne's Test): Detects Nitrogen (Prussian blue color), Sulfur (Black PbS or Violet complex), and Halogens (AgX precipitate).

• Quantitative Estimation Methods:

  - Carbon and Hydrogen: Liebig's Combustion Method (CO2 absorbed in KOH, H2O in anhydrous CaCl2).

  - Nitrogen: Dumas Method (N2 gas measured) and Kjeldahl's Method (converted to NH3, titrated with standard acid).

  - Halogens: Carius Method (precipitated as AgX).

  - Sulfur: Carius Method (precipitated as BaSO4).

Intext & Conceptual Questions

Q1: Write the IUPAC names of the following organic compounds: (a) CH3-CH2-CH(OH)-CH3, (b) CH3-CH=CH-COOH, (c) CH3-CO-CH2-CH2-CH3.
Answer:
(a) CH3-CH2-CH(OH)-CH3:
Chain length = 4 carbons (Butan). Functional group -OH at C-2.
IUPAC Name: Butan-2-ol.

(b) CH3-CH=CH-COOH:
Chain length = 4 carbons (But). Double bond at C-2, carboxylic acid group at C-1.
IUPAC Name: But-2-enoic acid.

(c) CH3-CO-CH2-CH2-CH3:
Chain length = 5 carbons (Pentan). Ketone group (-CO-) at C-2.
IUPAC Name: Pentan-2-one.

Textbook Chapter End Exercise Questions

Q1: What are electrophiles and nucleophiles? Explain with suitable examples.
Answer:
1. Electrophiles (Electron-loving species):
Electrophiles are electron-deficient chemical species that tend to accept a pair of electrons during a chemical reaction. They act as Lewis acids.
• Positively Charged Electrophiles: H+, NO2+, Cl+, CH3+.
• Neutral Electrophiles (Vacant d or p orbitals): BF3, AlCl3, FeCl3, SO3.

2. Nucleophiles (Nucleus-loving species):
Nucleophiles are electron-rich chemical species containing at least one unshared electron pair that can be donated to an electron-deficient center. They act as Lewis bases.
• Negatively Charged Nucleophiles: OH-, CN-, Cl-, Br-, CH3COO-.
• Neutral Nucleophiles (Lone pairs present): H2O, NH3, R-OH, R-NH2.
Q2: Explain the term 'Hyperconjugation' and discuss why tertiary butyl carbocation (CH3)3C+ is more stable than secondary ethyl carbocation CH3-CH2+.
Answer:
Hyperconjugation (No-Bond Resonance):
Hyperconjugation is the delocalization of sigma (σ) electrons of a C-H bond of an alkyl group directly attached to an unsaturated carbon or a carbon with an unshared p-orbital (like carbocations or free radicals). Greater the number of alpha-hydrogen (α-H) atoms, greater the number of hyperconjugative structures, and higher the stability.

Comparison of Stability:
• Tertiary Butyl Carbocation (CH3)3C+:
The positively charged carbon is attached to 3 methyl groups, giving a total of 9 alpha-hydrogen atoms (3 × 3 = 9 α-H). Thus, it forms 9 hyperconjugative canonical structures.

• Ethyl Carbocation CH3-CH2+:
The positively charged carbon is attached to 1 methyl group, giving only 3 alpha-hydrogen atoms (3 α-H), producing 3 hyperconjugative structures.

Conclusion: Since (CH3)3C+ has 9 α-hydrogens compared to only 3 α-hydrogens in CH3-CH2+, the tertiary butyl carbocation experiences much greater delocalization of positive charge and is significantly more stable.

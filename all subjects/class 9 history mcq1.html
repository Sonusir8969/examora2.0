Chapter 1: The French Revolution 30 Questions

1. In which year did the French Revolution begin?

  • A. 1776
  • B. 1789
  • C. 1792
  • D. 1804

Answer: B. 1789

Explanation: The French Revolution officially began with the storming of the Bastille fortress on 14 July 1789.

2. Who was the king of France at the time of the revolution?

  • A. Louis XIV
  • B. Louis XV
  • C. Louis XVI
  • D. Louis XVII

Answer: C. Louis XVI

Explanation: Louis XVI was the last king of France before the revolution and was later executed by guillotine in 1793.

3. What was the name of the tax levied by the Church in France?

  • A. Taille
  • B. Tithe
  • C. Gabelle
  • D. Vingtieme

Answer: B. Tithe

Explanation: The Tithe was a tax collected by the Church from the common people, which constituted about one-tenth of their produce.

4. Which philosopher wrote the book 'The Social Contract'?

  • A. Montesquieu
  • B. John Locke
  • C. Jean-Jacques Rousseau
  • D. Voltaire

Answer: C. Jean-Jacques Rousseau

Explanation: Rousseau proposed that the social contract was the basis of society and that the government should be based on the consent of the people.

5. Which event is considered the starting point of the French Revolution?

  • A. Tennis Court Oath
  • B. Meeting of Estates General
  • C. Storming of Bastille
  • D. Abolition of Monarchy

Answer: C. Storming of Bastille

Explanation: The storming of the Bastille prison by the angry mob on 14 July 1789 marked the beginning of the French Revolution.

6. Which political club became the most powerful during the French Revolution?

  • A. Girondins
  • B. Jacobins
  • C. Feuillants
  • D. Cordeliers

Answer: B. Jacobins

Explanation: The Jacobins, led by Maximilien Robespierre, were the most radical and powerful political club during the revolution.

7. Who was the leader of the Jacobins and the Reign of Terror?

  • A. Georges Danton
  • B. Maximilien Robespierre
  • C. Jean-Paul Marat
  • D. Lafayette

Answer: B. Maximilien Robespierre

Explanation: Maximilien Robespierre led the Jacobins and implemented the Reign of Terror, executing thousands of enemies of the revolution.

8. When was the monarchy abolished in France?

  • A. 1789
  • B. 1790
  • C. 1792
  • D. 1793

Answer: C. 1792

Explanation: The National Convention declared the abolition of the monarchy and established the French First Republic on 21 September 1792.

9. What was the main slogan of the French Revolution?

  • A. Peace, Bread, and Land
  • B. Liberty, Equality, and Fraternity
  • C. Justice for All
  • D. Freedom and Prosperity

Answer: B. Liberty, Equality, and Fraternity

Explanation: The revolutionaries popularized the slogan 'Liberty, Equality, and Fraternity' as the guiding principle for the new French Republic.

10. In which year did Napoleon Bonaparte become the Emperor of France?

  • A. 1799
  • B. 1800
  • C. 1802
  • D. 1804

Answer: D. 1804

Explanation: Napoleon Bonaparte crowned himself Emperor of France in 1804, officially ending the revolutionary period and starting the Napoleonic era.

11. What was the name of the Civil Code introduced by Napoleon?

  • A. French Civil Code
  • B. Napoleonic Code
  • C. National Code
  • D. Revolutionary Code

Answer: B. Napoleonic Code

Explanation: The Napoleonic Code established a uniform legal system in France, abolishing the feudal legal divisions and promoting equality before the law.

12. Who wrote the famous book 'The Spirit of the Laws'?

  • A. Rousseau
  • B. Voltaire
  • C. Montesquieu
  • D. Diderot

Answer: C. Montesquieu

Explanation: Montesquieu proposed the concept of separation of powers among the executive, legislative, and judicial branches in 'The Spirit of the Laws'.

13. The Taille was a tax collected directly from which group?

  • A. Clergy
  • B. Nobility
  • C. Third Estate
  • D. The King

Answer: C. Third Estate

Explanation: The Taille was a direct land tax levied on the common people of the Third Estate, while the clergy and nobility were exempt.

14. Who was the queen of France executed during the revolution?

  • A. Mary I
  • B. Marie Antoinette
  • C. Catherine de Medici
  • D. Anne of Austria

Answer: B. Marie Antoinette

Explanation: Queen Marie Antoinette, the wife of Louis XVI, was executed by guillotine in 1793 during the Reign of Terror.

15. What was the primary cause that compelled the Third Estate to revolt?

  • A. Lack of political rights
  • B. High taxes and feudal privileges
  • C. Poor harvests and famine
  • D. All of the above

Answer: D. All of the above

Explanation: The Third Estate faced high taxes, feudal burdens, food shortages, and lacked political power, leading to widespread discontent.

16. Which event marked the beginning of the Reign of Terror?

  • A. Execution of Louis XVI
  • B. Formation of the National Convention
  • C. Establishment of the Committee of Public Safety
  • D. The Jacobin uprising

Answer: C. Establishment of the Committee of Public Safety

Explanation: The Reign of Terror began in 1793 when the Committee of Public Safety was established to deal with threats to the revolution.

17. What right did the Declaration of Rights of Man and Citizen guarantee?

  • A. Right to vote
  • B. Right to property
  • C. Freedom of speech
  • D. All of the above

Answer: D. All of the above

Explanation: The declaration guaranteed the rights to liberty, property, security, and resistance to oppression for all citizens.

18. Who introduced the reforms in the French church?

  • A. The Pope
  • B. Louis XVI
  • C. The National Assembly
  • D. The Clergy

Answer: C. The National Assembly

Explanation: The National Assembly introduced the Civil Constitution of the Clergy, bringing the church under state control and reducing papal influence.

19. The term 'Sans-culottes' referred to which group of people?

  • A. Nobles
  • B. Professional soldiers
  • C. Working-class revolutionaries
  • D. Royal guards

Answer: C. Working-class revolutionaries

Explanation: Sans-culottes were the common people who wore long trousers instead of the knee breeches worn by the nobility, symbolizing their revolutionary spirit.

20. How many estates were there in the French social hierarchy?

  • A. 1
  • B. 2
  • C. 3
  • D. 4

Answer: C. 3

Explanation: The French society was divided into three estates: the Clergy (First), the Nobility (Second), and the commoners (Third Estate).

21. Who represented the Third Estate in the Estates General meeting of 1789?

  • A. The King's ministers
  • B. Peasants and merchants
  • C. Nobles from the court
  • D. High-ranking bishops

Answer: B. Peasants and merchants

Explanation: Members of the Third Estate were elected representatives from the common people, including merchants, peasants, and professionals.

22. Which philosopher proposed the idea that the king is the sole sovereign power?

  • A. Rousseau
  • B. Hobbes
  • C. Locke
  • D. Montesquieu

Answer: B. Hobbes

Explanation: Thomas Hobbes advocated for absolute sovereignty of the ruler in his work 'Leviathan', while French philosophers challenged this idea.

23. The National Assembly decided to draft a constitution in which year?

  • A. 1789
  • B. 1791
  • C. 1792
  • D. 1793

Answer: B. 1791

Explanation: The National Assembly successfully drafted a constitution in 1791, which limited the power of the king and gave more rights to citizens.

24. What was the outcome of the Tennis Court Oath taken by the Third Estate?

  • A. Abolition of taxes
  • B. Formation of the National Assembly
  • C. Declaration of war
  • D. Execution of nobles

Answer: B. Formation of the National Assembly

Explanation: The Third Estate took the oath not to disperse until a new constitution was framed, which led to the formation of the National Assembly.

25. Why did the King of France call the Estates General meeting in 1789?

  • A. To declare war
  • B. To increase taxes and solve financial crisis
  • C. To abdicate the throne
  • D. To abolish the feudal system

Answer: B. To increase taxes and solve financial crisis

Explanation: Louis XVI called the Estates General meeting to approve new taxes to solve the severe financial crisis in France caused by years of warfare.

26. Which group of people was responsible for the practice of feudalism in France?

  • A. The clergy
  • B. The nobility
  • C. The merchants
  • D. The peasants

Answer: B. The nobility

Explanation: The nobility held feudal rights, extracting dues and obligations from the common peasants who worked on their lands.

27. Who took over the rule of France after the fall of Robespierre?

  • A. The Directory
  • B. Napoleon
  • C. The Committee of Public Safety
  • D. Louis XVIII

Answer: A. The Directory

Explanation: After Robespierre's execution in 1794, the Directory, a five-member executive body, took over the rule of France in 1795.

28. How did the peasants react to the feudal dues before the revolution?

  • A. They paid happily
  • B. They revolted and refused to pay
  • C. They requested the king to reduce them
  • D. They moved to cities

Answer: B. They revolted and refused to pay

Explanation: Peasants often refused to pay heavy feudal dues and frequently attacked the estates of the nobles, leading to widespread rural revolts.

29. What principle did John Locke advocate in his 'Two Treatises of Government'?

  • A. Divine right of kings
  • B. Absolute monarchy
  • C. Right to life, liberty, and property
  • D. Dictatorship of the proletariat

Answer: C. Right to life, liberty, and property

Explanation: John Locke argued that people have natural rights to life, liberty, and property, and governments must protect these rights to be legitimate.

30. Which symbol was used to represent the broken feudal system in France?

  • A. The guillotine
  • B. The red cap
  • C. The broken chains
  • D. The tricolor flag

Answer: C. The broken chains

Explanation: Broken chains were used as a symbol in French iconography to represent the liberation of the people from feudal oppression and tyranny.

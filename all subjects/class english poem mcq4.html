Poem-04: Childhood 25 Questions

1. Who is the poet of the poem 'Childhood'?

  • A. Markus Natten
  • B. Walt Whitman
  • C. Shirley Toulson
  • D. Ted Hughes

Answer: A. Markus Natten

Explanation: The poem 'Childhood' is written by Markus Natten.

2. What central question does the poet repeatedly ask throughout the poem?

  • A. When did my childhood go?
  • B. Where did my childhood go?
  • C. Why did my childhood go?
  • D. Who took my childhood away?

Answer: A. When did my childhood go?

Explanation: The poet starts the first three stanzas with the question "When did my childhood go?".

3. What age did the poet mention in the first stanza regarding losing his childhood?

  • A. Eleven years old
  • B. Twelve years old
  • C. Ten years old
  • D. Fifteen years old

Answer: A. Eleven years old

Explanation: The poem states: "Was it the day I ceased to be eleven?"

4. Which two places could not be found in Geography books?

  • A. Hell and Heaven
  • B. Earth and Moon
  • C. Past and Future
  • D. Eden and Utopia

Answer: A. Hell and Heaven

Explanation: The poet realized that Hell and Heaven are imaginary concepts because they are not on maps or geography books.

5. What realization did the poet gain regarding Hell and Heaven?

  • A. They are non-existent physical places
  • B. They are located on islands
  • C. They exist underground
  • D. They are visible at night

Answer: A. They are non-existent physical places

Explanation: Since geography books do not show them, the poet developed rational thinking that they do not exist physically.

6. What hypocrisy of adults did the poet notice in the second stanza?

  • A. They talked of love and preached of love, but did not act lovingly
  • B. They never ate healthy food
  • C. They told children not to study
  • D. They disliked playing games

Answer: A. They talked of love and preached of love, but did not act lovingly

Explanation: Adults advised children to be loving and kind, but their own actions were double-standard and hypocritical.

7. What quality did the poet discover about his own mind in the third stanza?

  • A. His mind was really his own, capable of producing independent thoughts
  • B. His mind was weak and dependent
  • C. His mind could memorize entire books
  • D. His mind was easily controlled by others

Answer: A. His mind was really his own, capable of producing independent thoughts

Explanation: The poet realized individuality when he found he could think independently without just following others.

8. What shift occurs in the final stanza of the poem?

  • A. The question changes from 'When' to 'Where' did my childhood go
  • B. The poet decides to become a child again
  • C. The poet starts talking about his school
  • D. The poet praises adults

Answer: A. The question changes from 'When' to 'Where' did my childhood go

Explanation: After pondering 'When', the poet shifts focus in the last stanza to 'Where' childhood went.

9. According to the poet, where has his childhood gone?

  • A. To some forgotten place, hidden in an infant's face
  • B. To his old school classroom
  • C. To the mountains
  • D. Into a magical kingdom

Answer: A. To some forgotten place, hidden in an infant's face

Explanation: The poet concludes: "It went to some forgotten place, That’s hidden in an infant’s face..."

10. What does 'an infant's face' symbolize in the poem?

  • A. Innocence and purity
  • B. Ignorance and foolishness
  • C. Weakness
  • D. Mischief

Answer: A. Innocence and purity

Explanation: An infant's face represents untainted innocence, where childhood truly resides.

11. What process is described through the stages of losing childhood?

  • A. Growing up / Process of maturation
  • B. Physical illness
  • C. Forgetfulness in old age
  • D. Loss of memory

Answer: A. Growing up / Process of maturation

Explanation: The poem outlines rationalism, hypocrisy-awareness, and individuality as steps of growing into adulthood.

12. What concept is highlighted when the poet rejects Hell and Heaven?

  • A. Rationalism
  • B. Idealism
  • C. Hypocrisy
  • D. Materialism

Answer: A. Rationalism

Explanation: Rationalism means logical thinking based on facts rather than blind acceptance of mythical ideas.

13. What feature of adulthood is highlighted when analyzing adults' behavior?

  • A. Hypocrisy
  • B. Kindness
  • C. Truthfulness
  • D. Innocence

Answer: A. Hypocrisy

Explanation: Adults do not practice what they preach to children, reflecting hypocrisy.

14. What stage is marked by realizing "My mind was really mine"?

  • A. Individuality / Self-awareness
  • B. Childhood confusion
  • C. Total dependency
  • D. Disobedience

Answer: A. Individuality / Self-awareness

Explanation: Developing personal opinions independent of others marks the attainment of individuality.

15. What refrain is repeated at the end of the first three stanzas?

  • A. Was that the day?
  • B. Where did it go?
  • C. Is that all I know?
  • D. Oh childhood, come back!

Answer: A. Was that the day?

Explanation: "Was that the day?" is repeated at the end of the first three stanzas as a refrain.

16. What poetic device is used in repeating lines like "Was that the day?" and "When did my childhood go?"

  • A. Refrain / Repetition
  • B. Simile
  • C. Metaphor
  • D. Oxymoron

Answer: A. Refrain / Repetition

Explanation: A line or phrase repeated at regular intervals in a poem is called a Refrain.

17. What figure of speech is present in "Hell and Heaven"?

  • A. Antithesis / Alliteration
  • B. Simile
  • C. Personification
  • D. Hyperbole

Answer: A. Antithesis / Alliteration

Explanation: Opposite ideas placed together (Antithesis) and repetition of 'h' sound (Alliteration).

18. Which word in the poem means 'stopped' or 'ended'?

  • A. Ceased
  • B. Preached
  • C. Produced
  • D. Hidden

Answer: A. Ceased

Explanation: 'Ceased' means to come to an end or stop (e.g., ceased to be eleven).

19. How does the poet view the transition from childhood to adulthood?

  • A. As an inevitable loss of innocence balanced by gain of wisdom
  • B. As a sudden accident
  • C. As a joyful celebration without loss
  • D. As a punishable crime

Answer: A. As an inevitable loss of innocence balanced by gain of wisdom

Explanation: Growing up involves losing innocent beliefs while gaining logic and individual thought.

20. What feeling does the poet express towards his lost childhood?

  • A. Nostalgia and longing
  • B. Anger and hatred
  • C. Indifference
  • D. Amusement

Answer: A. Nostalgia and longing

Explanation: The poet ponders deeply and fondly misses the innocence of his childhood days.

21. What does the poet mean by "Whichever way I choose"?

  • A. Making decisions according to his own free will
  • B. Choosing a road on a map
  • C. Selecting clothes to wear
  • D. Picking up a book to read

Answer: A. Making decisions according to his own free will

Explanation: It highlights freedom of choice and autonomous decision-making in adulthood.

22. What quality makes a child different from an adult according to the poem?

  • A. Genuine simplicity and lack of hypocrisy
  • B. Physical height and weight
  • C. Ability to earn money
  • D. Formal education

Answer: A. Genuine simplicity and lack of hypocrisy

Explanation: Children are pure and genuine, unlike adults who often pretend and act double-faced.

23. Is childhood permanent or temporary according to Markus Natten?

  • A. Temporary phase of life
  • B. Permanent state of mind
  • C. Never-ending cycle
  • D. Optional stage

Answer: A. Temporary phase of life

Explanation: Childhood is a fleeting phase that disappears as rationalism and adulthood step in.

24. What is the tone of the poem 'Childhood'?

  • A. Reflective, nostalgic, and introspective
  • B. Humorous and funny
  • C. Aggressive and hostile
  • D. Highly dramatic

Answer: A. Reflective, nostalgic, and introspective

Explanation: The poet deeply reflects and analyzes his past to understand the mystery of growing up.

25. What is the main message of the poem 'Childhood'?

  • A. Loss of childhood innocence is a natural step towards gaining rational thinking and maturity
  • B. Adults should be punished for hypocrisy
  • C. Children should not read geography books
  • D. People should never grow up

Answer: A. Loss of childhood innocence is a natural step towards gaining rational thinking and maturity

Explanation: The poem explains that losing childhood innocence is an inevitable trade-off for developing logic and individuality.

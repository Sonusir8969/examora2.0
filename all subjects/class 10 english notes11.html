NCERT Solutions for Class 10 English Chapter 11 The Proposal

NCERT Solutions for Class 10 English First Flight Chapter 11 The Proposal covers Anton Chekhov's comic play about a marriage proposal gone hilariously wrong, and important questions and answers.

1. Play Summary

Short Note:

• The play by Anton Chekhov is a farcical comedy about Ivan Lomov, a nervous, hypochondriac landowner, who visits his neighbour Stepan Chubukov to propose marriage to his daughter, Natalya.

• Before he can even make the proposal, Lomov and Natalya become embroiled in a fierce, comic argument over the ownership of a piece of land called the Oxen Meadows, completely forgetting the actual purpose of his visit.

• The couple then argues about whose dog, Squeezer or Guess, is the better hunting dog, leading to further chaos, fainting fits, and dramatic exclamations, until Chubukov finally reveals the true purpose of Lomov's visit and pushes the couple towards a comic, almost accidental engagement.

2. Character Sketch

Short Note:

• Ivan Lomov: A wealthy but extremely nervous and hypochondriac landowner, prone to exaggerated anxiety and physical complaints, especially during moments of stress or conflict.

• Natalya Stepanovna: Chubukov's argumentative, strong-willed daughter, who is quick to anger and enjoys debating fiercely, even over trivial matters.

• Stepan Chubukov: Natalya's excitable, dramatic father who is eager to see his daughter married and repeatedly tries to smooth over the couple's ridiculous arguments to secure the match.

3. Theme & Message

Short Note:

• The play humorously critiques the pettiness and materialism of the landowning class, showing how trivial disputes over property and pride can overshadow important life decisions like marriage.

• It uses exaggerated, comic characters and situational irony to entertain while gently mocking human vanity and the absurdity of needless conflict.

Reading With Insight (Textbook Questions)

Q1: Why did Lomov visit Chubukov's house at the beginning of the play?
Answer: Lomov visited Chubukov's house with the serious intention of formally proposing marriage to Chubukov's daughter, Natalya, though he approaches the visit with great nervousness and hesitation.
Q2: What causes the first major argument between Lomov and Natalya?
Answer: The first major argument arises when Lomov mentions that the Oxen Meadows, a piece of land bordering both their properties, belongs to him, while Natalya insists that it has always belonged to her family, leading to a heated dispute over ownership and historical claims.
Q3: How does the play finally end, and what is comic about this resolution?
Answer: The play ends with Chubukov forcibly joining Lomov and Natalya's hands and declaring them engaged, even as the couple continues arguing about their hunting dogs, making the resolution comic because their engagement happens almost accidentally amidst continued bickering, without either explicitly expressing romantic feelings.

Extract-Based / Short Answer Questions

Q4: What does Lomov's hypochondriac nature add to the humour of the play?
Answer: Lomov's exaggerated anxiety about his health, including his fear of heart palpitations and numbness during stressful arguments, adds comic exaggeration to the play, highlighting the absurd contrast between his trivial physical complaints and the larger farce of the marriage proposal.
Q5: What social commentary does Chekhov offer through this comic play?
Answer: Chekhov humorously critiques the obsession of the landed gentry with property, pride and status, suggesting that such petty concerns often overshadow more meaningful human connections and decisions, such as marriage.

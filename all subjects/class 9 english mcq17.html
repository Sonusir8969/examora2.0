Poem-7: On Killing a Tree 10 Questions

1. Who is the poet of 'On Killing a Tree'?

  • A. Gieve Patel
  • B. James Kirkup
  • C. Robert Frost
  • D. Coates Kinney

Answer: A. Gieve Patel

Explanation: 'On Killing a Tree' is written by the renowned Indian poet and physician Dr. Gieve Patel.

2. Can a simple jab of the knife kill a tree?

  • A. Yes, very easily
  • B. No, it takes much time to kill a tree
  • C. Yes, within seconds
  • D. It kills the tree partially

Answer: B. No, it takes much time to kill a tree

Explanation: "It takes much time to kill a tree, / Not a simple jab of the knife / Will do it." A tree has strong deep roots and cannot be killed by just a blow.

3. How does a tree grow over years?

  • A. By consuming the earth
  • B. By feeding upon its crust
  • C. By absorbing sunlight, air, and water
  • D. All of the above

Answer: D. All of the above

Explanation: The tree grows slowly by feeding on the earth's crust and absorbing years of sunlight, air, and water.

4. What happens when the tree is hacked and chopped?

  • A. It dies instantly
  • B. Its bleeding bark heals, and green twigs rise again
  • C. It turns into dust
  • D. It dries up completely

Answer: B. Its bleeding bark heals, and green twigs rise again

Explanation: Hacking and chopping cause pain, but "the bleeding bark will heal" and miniature boughs will regrow if left unchecked.

5. What must be pulled out to kill the tree permanently?

  • A. The leaves
  • B. The branches
  • C. The root
  • D. The outer bark

Answer: C. The root

Explanation: "No, / The root is to be pulled out / Out of the anchoring earth." The root contains the tree's vital strength.

6. What is the most sensitive part of the tree?

  • A. Its leaves
  • B. Its flowers
  • C. Its root (white and wet)
  • D. Its trunk

Answer: C. Its root (white and wet)

Explanation: The poet describes the root as "white and wet, the most sensitive, hidden / For years inside the earth."

7. What happens to the pulled-out root in the sun and air?

  • A. It grows bigger
  • B. It rots, scorches, browning, hardening, and withering
  • C. It blossoms
  • D. It turns green again

Answer: B. It rots, scorches, browning, hardening, and withering

Explanation: Once exposed, the sun and air scorch and choke it, leading to browning, hardening, withering, and finally death.

8. What phrase is used to describe the earth holding the tree's roots firmly?

  • A. Earth cave
  • B. Anchoring earth
  • C. Deep earth
  • D. Green earth

Answer: B. Anchoring earth

Explanation: The phrase "anchoring earth" implies that the earth supports and holds the tree securely like an anchor holds a ship.

9. What literary tone or style does the poet use in this poem?

  • A. Irony / Sarcasm
  • B. Humor
  • C. Romance
  • D. Fantasy

Answer: A. Irony / Sarcasm

Explanation: The poet uses irony by presenting a step-by-step guide on how to kill a tree to highlight human cruelty towards nature.

10. When is the process of killing a tree complete ("and then it is done")?

  • A. When the leaves fall off
  • B. When the bark turns brown
  • C. When the root is completely dried, withered, and dead
  • D. When the branches are chopped off

Answer: C. When the root is completely dried, withered, and dead

Explanation: Only when the root goes through scorching, browning, hardening, and withering is the tree completely killed.

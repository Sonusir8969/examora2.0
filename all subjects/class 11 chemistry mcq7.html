Ch-7: Redox Reactions 30 Questions

1. Oxidation is defined as a process involving:

  • A. Loss of electrons
  • B. Gain of electrons
  • C. Gain of hydrogen
  • D. Decrease in oxidation number

Answer: A. Loss of electrons

Explanation: According to the electronic concept, oxidation involves loss of electrons leading to an increase in oxidation state.

2. What is the oxidation number of Manganese (Mn) in KMnO₄?

  • A. +7
  • B. +6
  • C. +4
  • D. +2

Answer: A. +7

Explanation: In KMnO₄: (+1) + x + 4(-2) = 0 ⇒ x - 7 = 0 ⇒ x = +7.

3. What is the oxidation number of Chromium (Cr) in K₂Cr₂O₇?

  • A. +6
  • B. +3
  • C. +7
  • D. +12

Answer: A. +6

Explanation: In K₂Cr₂O₇: 2(+1) + 2x + 7(-2) = 0 ⇒ 2x - 12 = 0 ⇒ x = +6.

4. Reduction involves which of the following changes?

  • A. Gain of electrons
  • B. Loss of electrons
  • C. Increase in oxidation number
  • D. Loss of hydrogen

Answer: A. Gain of electrons

Explanation: Reduction is defined as the process in which an atom or ion gains one or more electrons.

5. An oxidizing agent is a substance that:

  • A. Accepts electrons and gets reduced
  • B. Loses electrons and gets oxidized
  • C. Increases its own oxidation number
  • D. Donates protons

Answer: A. Accepts electrons and gets reduced

Explanation: An oxidizing agent causes another species to lose electrons by accepting those electrons itself.

6. What is the oxidation number of Sulfur (S) in H₂SO₄?

  • A. +6
  • B. +4
  • C. -2
  • D. +2

Answer: A. +6

Explanation: In H₂SO₄: 2(+1) + x + 4(-2) = 0 ⇒ x - 6 = 0 ⇒ x = +6.

7. Oxidation state of Oxygen in Hydrogen Peroxide (H₂O₂) is:

  • A. -1
  • B. -2
  • C. 0
  • D. +2

Answer: A. -1

Explanation: Peroxides contain an O-O single bond, assigning an oxidation state of -1 to each oxygen atom.

8. A reaction in which the same species undergoes oxidation as well as reduction is called:

  • A. Disproportionation reaction
  • B. Displacement reaction
  • C. Combination reaction
  • D. Decomposition reaction

Answer: A. Disproportionation reaction

Explanation: In a disproportionation reaction, an element in one oxidation state is simultaneously oxidized and reduced.

9. What is the oxidation number of Phosphorus (P) in H₃PO₄?

  • A. +5
  • B. +3
  • C. +1
  • D. -3

Answer: A. +5

Explanation: In H₃PO₄: 3(+1) + x + 4(-2) = 0 ⇒ x - 5 = 0 ⇒ x = +5.

10. What is the oxidation state of Oxygen in OF₂ (Oxygen Difluoride)?

  • A. +2
  • B. -2
  • C. -1
  • D. +1

Answer: A. +2

Explanation: Fluorine is more electronegative than oxygen (-1 each), forcing oxygen to exhibit a +2 oxidation state.

11. In the reaction CuO + H₂ → Cu + H₂O, the reducing agent is:

  • A. H₂
  • B. CuO
  • C. Cu
  • D. H₂O

Answer: A. H₂

Explanation: H₂ gains oxygen (loses electrons) and gets oxidized from 0 to +1 state, making it the reducing agent.

12. What is the oxidation number of Iron (Fe) in Fe₃O₄?

  • A. +8/3
  • B. +2
  • C. +3
  • D. +6

Answer: A. +8/3

Explanation: Fe₃O₄ is a mixed oxide (FeO·Fe₂O₃); average oxidation state is 3x + 4(-2) = 0 ⇒ x = +8/3.

13. In a Galvanic cell, oxidation takes place at the:

  • A. Anode
  • B. Cathode
  • C. Salt bridge
  • D. Electrolyte solution

Answer: A. Anode

Explanation: In all electrochemical cells, oxidation always occurs at the anode electrode.

14. What is the main function of a salt bridge in an electrochemical cell?

  • A. Maintains electrical neutrality of both half-cells
  • B. Allows movement of electrons
  • C. Increases voltage output
  • D. Prevents mixing of gases

Answer: A. Maintains electrical neutrality of both half-cells

Explanation: A salt bridge allows ion migration between half-cells to complete the circuit and maintain charge neutrality.

15. What is the oxidation state of Carbon in C₆H₁₂O₆ (Glucose)?

  • A. 0
  • B. +2
  • C. +4
  • D. -2

Answer: A. 0

Explanation: 6x + 12(+1) + 6(-2) = 0 ⇒ 6x = 0 ⇒ x = 0.

16. Standard Hydrogen Electrode (SHE) is assigned a standard electrode potential of:

  • A. 0.00 V
  • B. 1.00 V
  • C. -1.00 V
  • D. 0.059 V

Answer: A. 0.00 V

Explanation: By convention, standard reduction potential of SHE is defined as zero volts at all temperatures.

17. Which of the following elements exhibits only a single negative oxidation state in its compounds?

  • A. Fluorine
  • B. Oxygen
  • C. Chlorine
  • D. Nitrogen

Answer: A. Fluorine

Explanation: Fluorine is the most electronegative element and always shows an oxidation state of -1 in its compounds.

18. What is the oxidation number of Chlorine (Cl) in CaOCl₂ (Bleaching powder)?

  • A. -1 and +1
  • B. +2 and -2
  • C. 0
  • D. +1 only

Answer: A. -1 and +1

Explanation: CaOCl₂ is composed of Ca²⁺, Cl⁻ (-1 state), and OCl⁻ (+1 state for chlorine).

19. In acidic medium, KMnO₄ acts as a strong oxidizing agent and reduces to:

  • A. Mn²⁺
  • B. MnO₂
  • C. MnO₄²⁻
  • D. Mn³⁺

Answer: A. Mn²⁺

Explanation: In acidic solution, MnO₄⁻ (+7) accepts 5 electrons to form colorless Mn²⁺ (+2).

20. Which of the following is NOT a redox reaction?

  • A. AgNO₃ + NaCl → AgCl + NaNO₃
  • B. 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O
  • C. Zn + 2HCl → ZnCl₂ + H₂
  • D. CH₄ + 2O₂ → CO₂ + 2H₂O

Answer: A. AgNO₃ + NaCl → AgCl + NaNO₃

Explanation: Double displacement/precipitation reactions involve no change in oxidation numbers of any element.

21. What is the oxidation state of Nitrogen in NH₄NO₃ (Ammonium Nitrate)?

  • A. -3 and +5
  • B. -3 only
  • C. +5 only
  • D. +1 and +3

Answer: A. -3 and +5

Explanation: NH₄NO₃ contains NH₄⁺ (Nitrogen = -3) and NO₃⁻ (Nitrogen = +5).

22. The species having the highest reducing power has the:

  • A. Most negative reduction potential
  • B. Most positive reduction potential
  • C. Zero reduction potential
  • D. Highest oxidation state

Answer: A. Most negative reduction potential

Explanation: A more negative reduction potential means a stronger tendency to lose electrons and act as a reducing agent.

23. What is the oxidation state of Chlorine in HClO₄ (Perchloric acid)?

  • A. +7
  • B. +5
  • C. +1
  • D. -1

Answer: A. +7

Explanation: In HClO₄: (+1) + x + 4(-2) = 0 ⇒ x - 7 = 0 ⇒ x = +7.

24. What is the oxidation state of Hydrogen in Metal Hydrides like NaH?

  • A. -1
  • B. +1
  • C. 0
  • D. +2

Answer: A. -1

Explanation: Hydrogen is more electronegative than alkali metals, accepting an electron to adopt a -1 oxidation state.

25. In neutral or weakly alkaline medium, KMnO₄ reduces to:

  • A. MnO₂
  • B. Mn²⁺
  • C. MnO₄²⁻
  • D. Mn₂O₃

Answer: A. MnO₂

Explanation: In neutral/faintly alkaline media, MnO₄⁻ accepts 3 electrons to form dark brown insoluble MnO₂ (+4 state).

26. Oxidation number of Sulfur in Sodium Thiosulfate (Na₂S₂O₃) is:

  • A. +2
  • B. +2.5
  • C. +6
  • D. +4

Answer: A. +2

Explanation: In Na₂S₂O₃: 2(+1) + 2x + 3(-2) = 0 ⇒ 2x - 4 = 0 ⇒ x = +2 (average oxidation state).

27. Which method is commonly used to balance complex redox equations?

  • A. Ion-electron (Half-reaction) method
  • B. Trial and error method
  • C. Gravimetric method
  • D. Volumetric method

Answer: A. Ion-electron (Half-reaction) method

Explanation: The ion-electron method splits redox reactions into oxidation and reduction half-reactions to balance mass and charge systematically.

28. What is the oxidation state of Xe in XeF₄?

  • A. +4
  • B. +2
  • C. +6
  • D. 0

Answer: A. +4

Explanation: In XeF₄: x + 4(-1) = 0 ⇒ x = +4.

29. When H₂S gas is passed through acidic K₂Cr₂O₇ solution, color changes from orange to green due to formation of:

  • A. Cr³⁺ ions
  • B. Cr⁶⁺ ions
  • C. SO₂ gas
  • D. S²⁻ ions

Answer: A. Cr³⁺ ions

Explanation: Dichromate ions (Cr₂O₇²⁻, orange) get reduced to green hydrated chromium ions (Cr³⁺).

30. What is the oxidation state of Nickel in Ni(CO)₄?

  • A. 0
  • B. +2
  • C. +4
  • D. -2

Answer: A. 0

Explanation: Carbon monoxide (CO) is a neutral ligand (charge = 0), so the oxidation state of Nickel is 0.

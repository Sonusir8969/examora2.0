NCERT Solutions for Class 12 Biology Chapter 2 Human Reproduction

NCERT Solutions for Class 12 Biology Chapter 2 Human Reproduction covers the structure and functioning of human male and female reproductive systems. Key concepts include Gametogenesis (Spermatogenesis & Oogenesis), Menstrual Cycle, Fertilization, Implantation, Pregnancy, Placenta functions, Parturition, and Lactation.

1. Male and Female Reproductive Systems

Short Note:

• Male Reproductive System: Located in pelvic region. Includes paired Testes situated inside the Scrotum (maintains temperature 2-2.5 °C lower than normal body temperature for spermatogenesis). Accessory ducts: Rete testis, Vasa efferentia, Epididymis, Vas deferens. Accessory glands: Seminal vesicles, Prostate gland, Bulbourethral glands (produce seminal plasma rich in fructose, calcium, and enzymes).

• Female Reproductive System: Includes paired Ovaries, Oviducts (Fallopian tubes), Uterus, Cervix, Vagina, and External Genitalia. Fallopian tube parts: Infundibulum (with fimbriae), Ampulla (site of fertilization), and Isthmus. Uterus wall consists of Perimetrium, Myometrium (smooth muscle layer for contractions), and Endometrium (glandular layer that undergoes cyclic changes during menstrual cycle).

• Mammary Glands: Paired structures containing glandular tissue and variable amount of fat. Divided into 15-20 mammary lobes containing alveoli which secrete milk.

2. Gametogenesis & Menstrual Cycle

Short Note:

• Spermatogenesis: Formation of spermatozoa from spermatogonia in seminiferous tubules under influence of LH and FSH. Spermatogonia (2n) → Primary spermatocyte (2n) → Secondary spermatocytes (n) → Spermatids (n) → Spermatozoa (n). Process of conversion of spermatids into spermatozoa is called Spermiogenesis, and release of sperms from Sertoli cells is called Spermiation.

• Oogenesis: Formation of mature female gamete. Begins during embryonic development. Primary oocytes remain arrested in Prophase-I of meiosis until puberty. First meiotic division is unequal, producing a large Secondary oocyte (n) and a small 1st Polar body.

• Menstrual Cycle (28/29 Days):

  - Menstrual Phase (Days 1-5): Breakdown of endometrial lining due to decline in progesterone.

  - Follicular/Proliferative Phase (Days 6-13): Primary follicles grow into Graafian follicle under FSH & Estrogen influence.

  - Ovulatory Phase (Day 14): Rapid surge of LH (LH Surge) induces rupture of Graafian follicle and release of ovum.

  - Luteal/Secretory Phase (Days 15-28): Ruptured follicle transforms into Corpus Luteum, which secretes large amounts of Progesterone to maintain endometrium for pregnancy.

3. Fertilization, Implantation, Parturition & Lactation

Short Note:

• Fertilization & Implantation: Fertilization occurs at the ampullary-isthmic junction of Fallopian tube. Acrosome of sperm releases enzymes to penetrate Corona Radiata and Zona Pellucida. Zygote undergoes cleavage to form Morula → Blastocyst. Blastocyst embeds into endometrium (Implantation).

• Placenta & Hormones: Placenta is an intimate structural and functional unit between developing embryo (foetus) and maternal body. Functions: Supply O2 and nutrients, remove CO2 and excretory wastes. Secretes hormones: hCG (human Chorionic Gonadotropin), hPL (human Placental Lactogen), Estrogens, Progestogens, and Relaxin (secreted by ovary during late pregnancy).

• Parturition & Lactation: Process of delivery of foetus (childbirth) induced by complex neuroendocrine mechanism involving fetal ejection reflex and secretion of Oxytocin from maternal pituitary. Colostrum is the yellowish milk produced during initial days of lactation containing essential antibodies (IgA).

Intext & Conceptual Questions

Q1: Differentiate between Spermatogenesis and Oogenesis.
Answer:

Feature Spermatogenesis Oogenesis
Location Occurs in Seminiferous tubules of Testes. Occurs in Ovaries.
Onset Time Starts at puberty and continues throughout life. Starts during embryonic development stage.
Equal/Unequal Division Equal meiotic divisions producing 4 functional sperms from 1 spermatogonium. Unequal meiotic divisions producing 1 functional ovum and polar bodies.
Motility of Gamete Produces small, motile gametes (spermatozoa). Produces large, non-motile gamete (ovum).
Q2: What is LH Surge? State its role in the human menstrual cycle.
Answer:
LH Surge: Both LH (Luteinizing Hormone) and FSH reach peak levels in the middle of the menstrual cycle (around the 14th day). The rapid secretion of LH leading to its maximum level during the mid-cycle is called LH Surge.

Role: LH Surge induces the rupture of the mature Graafian follicle, thereby causing the release of the ovum (secondary oocyte) into the pelvic cavity—a process known as Ovulation.

Textbook Chapter End Exercise Questions

Q1: What is placenta? Mention the major endocrine functions of placenta during human pregnancy.
Answer:
Placenta: Placenta is an intimate temporary organ and physical connection established between the developing fetal tissue (chorionic villi) and maternal uterine wall for physiological exchange.

Endocrine Functions of Placenta:
Placenta acts as an endocrine gland and secretes several hormones necessary for maintaining pregnancy:
1. human Chorionic Gonadotropin (hCG): Maintains corpus luteum to secrete progesterone.
2. human Placental Lactogen (hPL): Stimulates development of mammary glands for lactation.
3. Estrogens and Progestogens: Maintain endometrial lining and support fetal growth.
4. Relaxin: (Secreted in late pregnancy) Helps relax pelvic ligaments during parturition.
Q2: Write short notes on: (a) Spermiogenesis vs Spermiation, (b) Colostrum and its significance.
Answer:
(a) Spermiogenesis vs Spermiation:
• Spermiogenesis: The process of differentiation and transformation of non-motile haploid spermatids into functional motile spermatozoa.
• Spermiation: The process of release of mature spermatozoa from Sertoli cells into the lumen of seminiferous tubules.

(b) Colostrum & Its Significance:
Colostrum is the yellowish fluid milk secreted by mammary glands during the first few days following child delivery.
• Significance: It is rich in proteins and contains abundant IgA antibodies that provide passive immunity to protect the newborn infant against various infectious diseases.

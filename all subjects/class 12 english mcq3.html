Ch-3: Deep Water 8 Questions

1. Who is the author of 'Deep Water'?

  • A. William O. Douglas
  • B. Mark Twain
  • C. Ernest Hemingway
  • D. Jack London

Answer: A. William O. Douglas

Explanation: 'Deep Water' is an excerpt from the autobiography of William O. Douglas, who served as a justice of the United States Supreme Court.

2. How did the author develop his fear of water?

  • A. He nearly drowned after being thrown into deep water by another boy at a pool
  • B. He read about a shark attack
  • C. He fell from a boat as a baby
  • D. He watched a friend drown

Answer: A. He nearly drowned after being thrown into deep water by another boy at a pool

Explanation: The author's fear of water began at age ten or eleven, when a bigger boy at the YMCA pool threw him into the deep end, causing him to nearly drown in terror and panic.

3. Where did the traumatic near-drowning incident of the author's childhood take place?

  • A. A river
  • B. The sea
  • C. A YMCA swimming pool in Yakima
  • D. A lake near his farmhouse

Answer: C. A YMCA swimming pool in Yakima

Explanation: The frightening incident that instilled a deep phobia of water in the young author occurred at the YMCA pool in Yakima.

4. How did the author eventually decide to overcome his fear of water years later?

  • A. He avoided water for the rest of his life
  • B. He hired a professional instructor and practiced rigorously, piece by piece, to master swimming
  • C. He never managed to overcome it
  • D. He asked a friend to push him into water again

Answer: B. He hired a professional instructor and practiced rigorously, piece by piece, to master swimming

Explanation: As an adult, determined not to let fear control his life, the author hired an instructor who patiently trained him in individual swimming movements over months until his fear was conquered.

5. What method did the instructor use to help the author overcome his fear systematically?

  • A. Breaking down swimming into separate parts: kicking, arm movement, breathing, and combining them slowly over weeks
  • B. Throwing him into the pool repeatedly
  • C. Only teaching him to float
  • D. Making him watch others swim

Answer: A. Breaking down swimming into separate parts: kicking, arm movement, breathing, and combining them slowly over weeks

Explanation: The instructor used a careful, methodical approach, teaching the author each individual component of swimming separately before gradually combining them, spread over about five months of daily practice.

6. Where did the author finally test whether he had completely overcome his fear of water?

  • A. In a bathtub
  • B. In Lake Wentworth, New Hampshire
  • C. In a swimming pool only
  • D. In a river near his home

Answer: B. In Lake Wentworth, New Hampshire

Explanation: To truly confirm that his old terror was gone, the author swam alone across Lake Wentworth and then further out into the deep parts of Lake Kettle, both in New Hampshire.

7. What is the central theme of 'Deep Water'?

  • A. The dangers of swimming pools
  • B. Confronting and conquering one's deepest fears through determination and systematic effort
  • C. The biography of a judge
  • D. The history of the YMCA

Answer: B. Confronting and conquering one's deepest fears through determination and systematic effort

Explanation: The essay centers on the theme of overcoming deep-seated fear through willpower, patience, and a disciplined, step-by-step approach, illustrating that fear can be defeated with the right method and resolve.

8. What emotion does the author describe feeling each time he tried to enter the pool after his traumatic experience, even years later?

  • A. Excitement
  • B. Sheer terror and paralyzing fear
  • C. Boredom
  • D. Curiosity

Answer: B. Sheer terror and paralyzing fear

Explanation: Even years after the incident, whenever the author attempted to swim, the old terror would return in full force, leaving him paralyzed with fear despite his attempts to overcome it.

NCERT Solutions for Class 12 Biology Chapter 6 Evolution

NCERT Solutions for Class 12 Biology Chapter 6 Evolution provides detailed study notes and answers covering Origin of Life, Evidences for Evolution, Darwinism, Adaptive Radiation, Hardy-Weinberg Principle, Mechanisms of Evolution, and Human Evolution.

Overview of Topics & Subtopics (Click to Jump)

  • 1. Origin of Life & Evidences for Evolution
  • 2. Theories of Evolution & Adaptive Radiation
  • 3. Hardy-Weinberg Principle & Human Evolution

1. Origin of Life & Evidences for Evolution

Short Note:

• Origin of Life: Universe originated around 20 billion years ago (Big Bang Theory). Earth formed 4.5 billion years ago. Life appeared 500 million years after formation of Earth (~4 billion years ago).

• Miller Experiment (1953): S.L. Miller created primitive Earth conditions in spark discharge apparatus containing CH4, NH3, H2, and H2O vapor at 800°C. Formation of amino acids proved chemical evolution hypothesis of Oparin and Haldane.

• Evidences of Evolution:

  1. Homologous Organs: Same structure/origin but different function (Divergent Evolution). E.g., Forelimbs of Whales, Bats, Cheetah, and Humans.

  2. Analogous Organs: Different structure/origin but same function (Convergent Evolution). E.g., Wings of Butterflies and Birds, Eyes of Octopus and Mammals.

2. Theories of Evolution & Adaptive Radiation

Short Note:

• Theories of Evolution:

  - Lamarckism: Theory of inheritance of acquired characters (use and disuse of organs, e.g., Giraffe neck).

  - Darwinism: Natural Selection (Branching Descent and Survival of the Fittest based on natural variations).

  - Mutation Theory: Proposed by Hugo de Vries working on Evening Primrose (Oenothera lamarckiana). Mutations are random, directionless, and cause speciation in single step (Saltation).

• Adaptive Radiation: Process of evolution of different species in a given geographical area starting from a point and radiating to other habitats. E.g., Darwin's Finches in Galapagos Islands, Australian Marsupials.

3. Hardy-Weinberg Principle & Human Evolution

Short Note:

• Hardy-Weinberg Principle: Allele frequencies in a population are stable and constant from generation to generation in absence of evolutionary factors. Algebraic equation: p² + 2pq + q² = 1.

  - Factors Affecting Equilibrium: Gene migration/gene flow, Genetic drift (Founder effect), Mutation, Genetic recombination, and Natural Selection.

• Human Evolution Stages:

  1. Dryopithecus & Ramapithecus: 15 mya (Ramapithecus was more man-like).

  2. Australopithecines: 2 mya in East African grasslands (used stone weapons, ate fruit).

  3. Homo habilis: First human-like hominid (Brain capacity 650–800 cc, did not eat meat).

  4. Homo erectus: 1.5 mya (Brain capacity 900 cc, ate meat).

  5. Neanderthal Man: 100,000–40,000 years ago (Brain capacity 1400 cc, used hides, buried dead).

  6. Homo sapiens: Arose in Africa 75,000–10,000 years ago during Ice Age.

Intext & Conceptual Questions

Q1: Differentiate between Homologous structures and Analogous structures.
Answer:

Feature Homologous Structures Analogous Structures
Anatomy / Origin Same basic anatomical structure and embryonic origin. Different anatomical structure and embryonic origin.
Function Adapted to perform different functions. Adapted to perform similar functions.
Pattern of Evolution Indicates Divergent Evolution. Indicates Convergent Evolution.
Example Forelimbs of Whales, Bats, Cheetah, and Humans. Wings of Birds and Insects; Eyes of Octopus and Mammals.
Q2: What is Adaptive Radiation? Give an example.
Answer:
Adaptive Radiation: It is the evolutionary process in which different species evolve from a common ancestral stock and diversify to adapt to different ecological niches/habitats.

Examples:
1. Darwin's Finches: On the Galapagos Islands, Darwin observed many varieties of small black birds. From an original seed-eating ancestor, different varieties evolved with altered beak shapes adapted to eating seeds, insects, cactus, etc.
2. Australian Marsupials: A number of distinct marsupials (e.g., Kangaroo, Koala, Tasmanian wolf) evolved from a single ancestral stock within the Australian island continent.

Textbook Chapter End Exercise Questions

Q1: Describe Stanley Miller's experiment and state its significance.
Answer:
Miller's Experiment (1953): Stanley Miller created experimental conditions similar to primitive Earth atmosphere in a laboratory setup:

1. He used a closed glass apparatus containing methane (CH4), ammonia (NH3), hydrogen (H2), and water vapor.
2. Electric sparks were discharged using electrodes at 800°C to simulate lightning.
3. After running the experiment for a week, he observed the formation of simple organic compounds including amino acids (such as glycine, alanine, aspartic acid).

Significance: It provided experimental proof for Oparin-Haldane's theory of Chemical Evolution (Abiogenesis followed by Biogenesis).
Q2: State the Hardy-Weinberg Principle. What factors disturb the Hardy-Weinberg Equilibrium?
Answer:
Hardy-Weinberg Principle: States that allele frequencies in a large, randomly mating population remain constant from generation to generation, provided no evolutionary influences act on it. Represented as:
p² + 2pq + q² = 1 (where p and q are frequencies of dominant and recessive alleles).

Factors Disturbance Equilibrium:
1. Gene Migration / Gene Flow: Movement of genes into or out of a population.
2. Genetic Drift: Random changes in allele frequency by chance events (e.g., Founder Effect).
3. Mutation: New alleles introduced by genetic mutations.
4. Genetic Recombination: Crossing over during meiosis.
5. Natural Selection: Differential reproduction favoring certain genotypes.
Q3: Trace the evolutionary stages of Modern Humans (Homo sapiens).
Answer:
The sequential stages of human evolution are:
1. Dryopithecus & Ramapithecus (15 mya): Ape-like and man-like hominids.
2. Australopithecines (2 mya): Walked upright, lived in grasslands, used stone tools.
3. Homo habilis (2 mya): First human-like hominid, brain capacity 650–800 cc.
4. Homo erectus (1.5 mya): Walked fully erect, brain capacity 900 cc, ate meat.
5. Neanderthal Man (100,000–40,000 years ago): Brain capacity 1400 cc, used hides, buried dead.
6. Homo sapiens (75,000–10,000 years ago): Modern humans, developed agriculture, art, and civilization.

Ch-2: Sectors of the Indian Economy 30 Questions

1. Which sector involves activities that directly exploit natural resources?

  • A. Primary Sector
  • B. Secondary Sector
  • C. Tertiary Sector
  • D. Quaternary Sector

Answer: A. Primary Sector

Explanation: The Primary sector produces goods directly by utilizing natural resources, such as agriculture, fishing, forestry, and mining.

2. Converting raw cotton into yarn and cloth is an activity belonging to which sector?

  • A. Secondary Sector
  • B. Primary Sector
  • C. Tertiary Sector
  • D. Information Technology Sector

Answer: A. Secondary Sector

Explanation: The Secondary sector covers activities in which natural products are changed into other forms through manufacturing or industrial processes.

3. Transport, storage, communication, banking, and trade are examples of activities in which sector?

  • A. Tertiary Sector
  • B. Secondary Sector
  • C. Primary Sector
  • D. Agricultural Sector

Answer: A. Tertiary Sector

Explanation: The Tertiary sector generates services rather than physical goods, aiding the production process of the primary and secondary sectors.

4. Why is the Primary sector also called the Agriculture and Related sector?

  • A. Most natural products obtained are from agriculture, dairy, fishing, and forestry
  • B. It employs only agricultural laborers
  • C. It is managed entirely by the Ministry of Agriculture
  • D. It produces machinery for farming

Answer: A. Most natural products obtained are from agriculture, dairy, fishing, and forestry

Explanation: Since agriculture, dairy, forestry, and fishing form the major base of primary production, this sector is commonly termed the agriculture and related sector.

5. What does GDP stand for in economics?

  • A. Gross Domestic Product
  • B. Gross Development Production
  • C. General Domestic Price
  • D. Government Development Plan

Answer: A. Gross Domestic Product

Explanation: Gross Domestic Product (GDP) is the total monetary value of all final goods and services produced within a country during a specific year.

6. To avoid double counting while calculating GDP, economists count only the value of:

  • A. Final goods and services
  • B. Intermediate goods
  • C. Raw materials
  • D. Exported items

Answer: A. Final goods and services

Explanation: The value of final goods already includes the value of all intermediate goods used in production, avoiding double counting.

7. Which organization in India is responsible for measuring Gross Domestic Product (GDP)?

  • A. Central Statistics Office (CSO) under a Central Government Ministry
  • B. Reserve Bank of India (RBI)
  • C. NITI Aayog
  • D. State Bank of India (SBI)

Answer: A. Central Statistics Office (CSO) under a Central Government Ministry

Explanation: In India, the task of measuring GDP is undertaken by the Central Government Ministry with the help of various state and central government departments.

8. Which sector has emerged as the largest producing sector in India in recent decades, replacing agriculture?

  • A. Tertiary Sector
  • B. Secondary Sector
  • C. Primary Sector
  • D. Handloom Sector

Answer: A. Tertiary Sector

Explanation: Over the past few decades, the tertiary sector has grown fastest and contributes the largest share to India's GDP.

9. Which sector continues to be the largest employer in India despite contributing less to GDP?

  • A. Primary Sector
  • B. Secondary Sector
  • C. Tertiary Sector
  • D. IT Sector

Answer: A. Primary Sector

Explanation: The primary sector continues to employ nearly half of India's workforce because not enough jobs were created in the secondary and tertiary sectors.

10. What is the situation called when more people are working on a job than required, and removing some workers does not reduce total production?

  • A. Disguised Unemployment
  • B. Seasonal Unemployment
  • C. Structural Unemployment
  • D. Friction Unemployment

Answer: A. Disguised Unemployment

Explanation: Disguised unemployment (or underemployment) occurs when people appear to be working, but their marginal contribution to output is zero.

11. Where is disguised unemployment most commonly observed in India?

  • A. Agriculture sector in rural areas
  • B. Large multinational corporations
  • C. Government defense sector
  • D. Banking sector

Answer: A. Agriculture sector in rural areas

Explanation: Disguised unemployment is widely prevalent in rural smallholder farming, where entire families work on small plots of land.

12. In which year was the Mahatma Gandhi National Rural Employment Guarantee Act (MGNREGA) passed?

  • A. 2005
  • B. 2000
  • C. 2010
  • D. 1995

Answer: A. 2005

Explanation: MGNREGA was enacted in 2005 to guarantee wage employment and livelihood security in rural India.

13. How many days of guaranteed employment in a year does MGNREGA 2005 promise to rural households?

  • A. 100 days
  • B. 150 days
  • C. 200 days
  • D. 365 days

Answer: A. 100 days

Explanation: MGNREGA guarantees 100 days of manual wage employment per fiscal year to adult members of rural households.

14. What happens under MGNREGA if the government fails to provide employment within 15 days of application?

  • A. An unemployment allowance is paid to the applicant
  • B. The applicant is offered land
  • C. The government pays a heavy fine to court
  • D. Free food grains are provided

Answer: A. An unemployment allowance is paid to the applicant

Explanation: Under MGNREGA, if employment is not provided within 15 days, the applicant is legally entitled to a daily unemployment allowance.

15. How are economic enterprises classified based on working conditions and legal registration?

  • A. Organised and Unorganised Sectors
  • B. Public and Private Sectors
  • C. Primary and Secondary Sectors
  • D. Domestic and Foreign Sectors

Answer: A. Organised and Unorganised Sectors

Explanation: Based on terms of employment, rules, regulations, and security of job, enterprises are divided into organized and unorganized sectors.

16. Which of the following is a characteristic feature of the Organised Sector?

  • A. Job security, fixed working hours, and paid leave benefits
  • B. Low and irregular wages without formal contracts
  • C. Absence of government regulation and labor laws
  • D. No social security benefits like provident fund

Answer: A. Job security, fixed working hours, and paid leave benefits

Explanation: The organized sector is registered with the government and provides employment security, regulated working hours, paid leave, and provident fund benefits.

17. Which of the following workers belongs to the Unorganised Sector?

  • A. A daily wage construction worker
  • B. A doctor working in a government hospital
  • C. A bank manager in State Bank of India
  • D. An engineer in a registered private firm

Answer: A. A daily wage construction worker

Explanation: Daily wage laborers, street vendors, and landless agricultural workers belong to the unorganized sector due to lack of job security and benefits.

18. On what basis are economic activities classified into Public and Private sectors?

  • A. Ownership of assets and responsibility for delivery of services
  • B. Terms of employment
  • C. Nature of economic activity
  • D. Number of workers employed

Answer: A. Ownership of assets and responsibility for delivery of services

Explanation: The division between public and private sectors depends on whether the government or private individuals own assets and control services.

19. Which of the following is an example of a Public Sector enterprise in India?

  • A. Indian Railways and Bharat Heavy Electricals Limited (BHEL)
  • B. Tata Iron and Steel Company (TISCO)
  • C. Reliance Industries Limited
  • D. Infosys

Answer: A. Indian Railways and Bharat Heavy Electricals Limited (BHEL)

Explanation: Indian Railways and BHEL are owned and operated by the government, making them public sector organizations.

20. What is the primary motive of private sector enterprises?

  • A. To earn profits
  • B. To promote social welfare
  • C. To provide free education
  • D. To generate public employment regardless of revenue

Answer: A. To earn profits

Explanation: Private sector activities are driven guided primarily by profit motives for private owners or shareholders.

21. Why does the government undertake large infrastructure projects like building roads, railways, and dams?

  • A. They require huge capital investments with long gestation periods, which private sectors may not find profitable
  • B. Private companies are legally banned from constructing roads
  • C. Infrastructure projects do not require technology
  • D. To eliminate private businesses

Answer: A. They require huge capital investments with long gestation periods, which private sectors may not find profitable

Explanation: Mega-infrastructure requires immense expenditure that private entities cannot easily finance or monetize at affordable public rates.

22. Wheat flour sold to a bakery to bake bread is an example of what type of good?

  • A. Intermediate good
  • B. Final good
  • C. Capital good
  • D. Consumer good

Answer: A. Intermediate good

Explanation: Flour used by a bakery is processed further into bread, making it an intermediate good in the production chain.

23. Sugar produced from sugarcane in a sugar mill is an activity of which sector?

  • A. Secondary Sector
  • B. Primary Sector
  • C. Tertiary Sector
  • D. Service Sector

Answer: A. Secondary Sector

Explanation: Processing sugarcane into refined sugar is a manufacturing transformation, placing it in the secondary sector.

24. What type of unemployment occurs when farmers are employed only during sowing and harvesting seasons?

  • A. Seasonal Unemployment
  • B. Disguised Unemployment
  • C. Technical Unemployment
  • D. Voluntary Unemployment

Answer: A. Seasonal Unemployment

Explanation: Seasonal unemployment happens when people find work only during specific months or seasons of the year.

25. Which sector helps in the development of the Primary and Secondary sectors?

  • A. Tertiary Sector
  • B. Agricultural Sector
  • C. Manufacturing Sector
  • D. Unorganized Sector

Answer: A. Tertiary Sector

Explanation: Tertiary services (transport, credit, storage, marketing) support primary and secondary production activities.

26. Which of the following is a key reason for the rapid growth of the tertiary sector in India?

  • A. Expansion of basic services like hospitals, schools, police stations, and banking
  • B. Decline in agricultural productivity
  • C. Complete closure of manufacturing units
  • D. Reduction in population

Answer: A. Expansion of basic services like hospitals, schools, police stations, and banking

Explanation: The state's responsibility to provide essential services, along with rising income levels, has driven demand for tertiary sector expansion.

27. Underemployment can also be referred to as:

  • A. Hidden unemployment
  • B. Open unemployment
  • C. Structural unemployment
  • D. Cyclic unemployment

Answer: A. Hidden unemployment

Explanation: Underemployment is also called hidden unemployment because people appear employed, yet work below their potential.

28. Which of the following measures can create more employment in rural areas?

  • A. Providing easy credit, irrigation, cold storage, and rural roads
  • B. Stopping agricultural subsidies
  • C. Closing down agro-based industries
  • D. Banning machine use in farming

Answer: A. Providing easy credit, irrigation, cold storage, and rural roads

Explanation: Expanding irrigation, infrastructure, credit, and agro-processing creates viable self-employment and wage jobs in rural areas.

29. What type of protection do vulnerable workers in the unorganised sector need?

  • A. Minimum wages, fixed working hours, and social security
  • B. High income tax rates
  • C. Banning labor unions
  • D. Immediate migration to cities

Answer: A. Minimum wages, fixed working hours, and social security

Explanation: Unorganized sector workers need legislative support for minimum wages, occupational safety, job security, and social safety nets.

30. TISCO (Tata Iron and Steel Company Limited) is an example of which sector?

  • A. Private Sector
  • B. Public Sector
  • C. Cooperative Sector
  • D. Joint Sector

Answer: A. Private Sector

Explanation: TISCO is owned by private shareholders and managed privately, making it a private sector company.

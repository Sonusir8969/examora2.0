1. Electric Current and Circuit

Short Note:

• Electric Current (I): The rate of flow of electric charges per unit time through a conductor cross-section (I = Q / t). Its SI unit is Ampere (A).

• Electric Circuit: A continuous and closed path along which electric current flows.

• Ammeter: An instrument used to measure electric current, always connected in series in a circuit.

2. Electric Potential and Potential Difference

Short Note:

• Potential Difference (V): The work done to move a unit positive charge from one point to another in an electric circuit (V = W / Q). Its SI unit is Volt (V).

• Voltmeter: Device used to measure potential difference, connected in parallel across points.

• 1 Volt is defined as 1 Joule of work done in moving a charge of 1 Coulomb from one point to another.

3. Ohm's Law and Resistance Factors

Short Note:

• Ohm's Law: At constant temperature, electric current (I) flowing through a metallic conductor is directly proportional to potential difference (V) across its ends (V = IR).

• Resistance (R): Property of a conductor to oppose the flow of electric current. SI unit: Ohm (Ω).

• Factors Affecting Resistance: Resistance depends directly on length (L), inversely on cross-sectional area (A), temperature, and nature of material (R = ρL / A, where ρ is resistivity).

4. Resistance in Series and Parallel Combinations

Short Note:

• Series Combination: Resistors connected end-to-end. Total resistance Rs = R1 + R2 + R3. Current remains constant across all resistors.

• Parallel Combination: Resistors connected across common nodes. Total resistance 1 / Rp = 1 / R1 + 1 / R2 + 1 / R3. Potential difference remains constant across all branches.

5. Heating Effect of Electric Current & Power

Short Note:

• Joule's Law of Heating: Heat produced in a resistor is proportional to the square of current, resistance, and time duration (H = I2Rt).

• Electric Power (P): Rate at which electrical energy is consumed (P = VI = I2R = V2 / R). SI unit: Watt (W).

• Commercial Unit: Kilowatt-hour (kWh). 1 kWh = 3.6 × 106 Joules.

Intext Questions - Page Number: 200

Q1: What does an electric circuit mean?
Answer: An electric circuit is a continuous and closed conductive loop containing a power source (cell/battery), conducting wires, switches, and electrical loads through which electric current can flow.
Q2: Define the unit of current.
Answer: The SI unit of current is Ampere (A). One Ampere is defined as the flow of one Coulomb of electric charge per second through any cross-section of a conductor (1 A = 1 C / 1 s).

Intext Questions - Page Number: 202

Q1: Name a device that helps to maintain a potential difference across a conductor.
Answer: An electric cell or a battery.
Q2: How much energy is given to each coulomb of charge passing through a 6 V battery?
Answer:
Given: Charge (Q) = 1 C, Potential Difference (V) = 6 V
Formula: Energy (W) = Q × V
Calculation: W = 1 × 6 = 6 Joules.

Textbook Chapter End Exercise Questions

Q1: A piece of wire of resistance R is cut into five equal parts. These parts are then connected in parallel. If the equivalent resistance of this combination is R', then the ratio R/R' is:

(a) 1/25
(b) 1/5
(c) 5
(d) 25

Correct Answer: (d) 25
Explanation: Each part has resistance R/5. When connected in parallel, 1/R' = 5/(R/5) = 25/R. Therefore, R/R' = 25.

Q2: Which of the following terms does not represent electrical power in a circuit?

(a) I2R
(b) IR2
(c) VI
(d) V2/R

Correct Answer: (b) IR2.

Q3: An electric bulb is rated 220 V and 100 W. When it is operated on 110 V, the power consumed will be:

(a) 100 W
(b) 75 W
(c) 50 W
(d) 25 W

Correct Answer: (d) 25 W
Explanation: Resistance R = V2 / P = 2202 / 100 = 484 Ω. When operated at 110 V, new power P' = V'2 / R = 1102 / 484 = 25 W.

Q4: Why are copper and aluminium wires usually employed for electricity transmission?
Answer: Copper and aluminium have extremely low electrical resistivity and high conductivity. This minimizes power loss as heat during long-distance transmission. Additionally, they are ductile and cost-effective.

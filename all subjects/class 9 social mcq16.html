Chapter 16: Democratic Rights 30 Questions

1. Why are rights considered essential in a democracy?

  • A. They protect citizens from arbitrary use of power by the state and others
  • B. They apply only to government officials
  • C. They are unnecessary once elections are held
  • D. They restrict citizens from questioning the government

Answer: A. They protect citizens from arbitrary use of power by the state and others

Explanation: Rights are entitlements that protect citizens from misuse of power and allow them to lead a life of dignity and freedom.

2. How many categories of Fundamental Rights are guaranteed by the Indian Constitution?

  • A. Four
  • B. Five
  • C. Six
  • D. Seven

Answer: C. Six

Explanation: The Indian Constitution guarantees six categories of Fundamental Rights, including the Right to Equality, Right to Freedom, and Right to Constitutional Remedies.

3. Which right ensures that the state cannot discriminate against citizens on grounds of religion, race, caste, sex or place of birth?

  • A. Right to Freedom
  • B. Right to Equality
  • C. Right against Exploitation
  • D. Cultural and Educational Rights

Answer: B. Right to Equality

Explanation: The Right to Equality guarantees that all citizens are equal before the law and prohibits discrimination on the grounds mentioned.

4. Which Fundamental Right includes freedom of speech and expression?

  • A. Right to Equality
  • B. Right to Freedom
  • C. Right to Constitutional Remedies
  • D. Right against Exploitation

Answer: B. Right to Freedom

Explanation: The Right to Freedom includes several freedoms such as speech and expression, assembly, association, movement, and residence, subject to reasonable restrictions.

5. Which right bans human trafficking, forced labour and child labour in hazardous jobs?

  • A. Right against Exploitation
  • B. Right to Freedom of Religion
  • C. Right to Equality
  • D. Cultural and Educational Rights

Answer: A. Right against Exploitation

Explanation: The Right against Exploitation prohibits forced labour, trafficking of human beings, and employment of children below 14 years in hazardous work.

6. Which Fundamental Right allows citizens to practise, profess and propagate any religion of their choice?

  • A. Right to Equality
  • B. Right to Freedom of Religion
  • C. Right to Constitutional Remedies
  • D. Right against Exploitation

Answer: B. Right to Freedom of Religion

Explanation: This right guarantees every citizen the freedom to practise, profess and propagate any religion, in keeping with India's secular character.

7. Which right protects the interests of minority communities to preserve their language, script and culture?

  • A. Right to Equality
  • B. Right against Exploitation
  • C. Cultural and Educational Rights
  • D. Right to Freedom

Answer: C. Cultural and Educational Rights

Explanation: Cultural and Educational Rights allow minorities to conserve their distinct language, script and culture, and to establish and administer their own educational institutions.

8. Which Fundamental Right is often described as the 'heart and soul' of the Constitution?

  • A. Right to Equality
  • B. Right to Constitutional Remedies
  • C. Right to Freedom
  • D. Right against Exploitation

Answer: B. Right to Constitutional Remedies

Explanation: Dr. B.R. Ambedkar described the Right to Constitutional Remedies as the heart and soul of the Constitution because it allows citizens to approach courts if any of their Fundamental Rights are violated.

9. Which Article of the Indian Constitution empowers citizens to move the Supreme Court if their Fundamental Rights are violated?

  • A. Article 14
  • B. Article 21
  • C. Article 32
  • D. Article 370

Answer: C. Article 32

Explanation: Article 32 grants citizens the right to directly approach the Supreme Court for enforcement of their Fundamental Rights.

10. Which body was set up in India to look into complaints of human rights violations?

  • A. Election Commission
  • B. National Human Rights Commission
  • C. Finance Commission
  • D. Law Commission

Answer: B. National Human Rights Commission

Explanation: The National Human Rights Commission was established to investigate complaints regarding violation of human rights and to recommend appropriate action.

11. What is the term for rights that belong to every human being simply by virtue of being human?

  • A. Legal rights only
  • B. Human rights
  • C. Property rights
  • D. Civic duties

Answer: B. Human rights

Explanation: Human rights are basic rights and freedoms that are believed to belong to every person, regardless of nationality, gender, or any other status.

12. In which year did the United Nations adopt the Universal Declaration of Human Rights (UDHR)?

  • A. 1945
  • B. 1948
  • C. 1950
  • D. 1966

Answer: B. 1948

Explanation: The Universal Declaration of Human Rights was adopted by the United Nations General Assembly in 1948 as a global standard for human rights.

13. The detention facility at Guantanamo Bay is cited in the chapter as an example of what?

  • A. Proper application of human rights standards
  • B. Alleged violation of prisoners' basic rights, including detention without trial
  • C. A model prison for rehabilitating criminals
  • D. A UN human rights headquarters

Answer: B. Alleged violation of prisoners' basic rights, including detention without trial

Explanation: The chapter cites Guantanamo Bay as an example where detainees were held without trial for long periods, raising concerns about violation of basic human rights.

14. Which Fundamental Right guarantees protection of life and personal liberty?

  • A. Article 14
  • B. Article 19
  • C. Article 21
  • D. Article 25

Answer: C. Article 21

Explanation: Article 21 guarantees that no person shall be deprived of their life or personal liberty except according to a legally established procedure.

15. Which Fundamental Right was abolished in India along with untouchability?

  • A. Right to Property (as a fundamental right)
  • B. Practice of untouchability, abolished under the Right to Equality
  • C. Right to Freedom of Religion
  • D. Right to Constitutional Remedies

Answer: B. Practice of untouchability, abolished under the Right to Equality

Explanation: The Right to Equality specifically abolishes untouchability and forbids its practice in any form, making it a punishable offence.

16. Which of the following is NOT one of the six Fundamental Rights in the Indian Constitution?

  • A. Right to Property
  • B. Right to Equality
  • C. Right to Freedom
  • D. Right to Constitutional Remedies

Answer: A. Right to Property

Explanation: The Right to Property was originally a Fundamental Right but was later removed from the list of Fundamental Rights and made a legal right through a constitutional amendment.

17. Why are rights considered necessary even when a country holds free elections?

  • A. Because elections alone do not guarantee protection of individual freedoms and dignity
  • B. Because elections are unrelated to rights
  • C. Because rights automatically disappear after elections
  • D. Because only the winning party needs rights

Answer: A. Because elections alone do not guarantee protection of individual freedoms and dignity

Explanation: Even in a democracy with elections, rights are needed to protect citizens against possible misuse of power by the majority or the government itself.

18. Which right allows citizens to form associations, unions or political parties?

  • A. Right to Equality
  • B. Right to Freedom
  • C. Right against Exploitation
  • D. Cultural and Educational Rights

Answer: B. Right to Freedom

Explanation: The Right to Freedom includes the freedom to form associations or unions, which is essential for political and civic participation.

19. Are Fundamental Rights in India absolute and without any limits?

  • A. Yes, they can never be restricted under any circumstances
  • B. No, they are subject to reasonable restrictions imposed by law
  • C. They apply only to government employees
  • D. They apply only during elections

Answer: B. No, they are subject to reasonable restrictions imposed by law

Explanation: Fundamental Rights are not absolute; the state can impose reasonable restrictions in the interest of public order, morality, security and other stated grounds.

20. What remedy can a court provide if it finds that a person's Fundamental Right has been violated by the government?

  • A. It can issue directions or writs to stop the violation and provide relief
  • B. It can only issue a warning with no legal effect
  • C. It has no power to intervene in such cases
  • D. It must wait for parliamentary approval before acting

Answer: A. It can issue directions or writs to stop the violation and provide relief

Explanation: Courts, especially the Supreme Court and High Courts, can issue writs and directions to enforce Fundamental Rights and provide remedies to affected citizens.

21. What is meant by 'civil liberties'?

  • A. Freedoms and protections given to citizens against undue interference by the state
  • B. Duties imposed on citizens by the state
  • C. Rights given only to public officials
  • D. Restrictions placed only on foreigners

Answer: A. Freedoms and protections given to citizens against undue interference by the state

Explanation: Civil liberties refer to basic freedoms, such as speech, movement and association, that protect individuals from unwarranted state interference.

22. In the example of the United States discussed in the chapter, what raised concerns about rights after the 9/11 attacks?

  • A. New laws expanded citizens' voting rights
  • B. New anti-terrorism laws increased government powers, raising concerns over civil liberties
  • C. All existing laws on rights were repealed
  • D. No changes were made to any laws

Answer: B. New anti-terrorism laws increased government powers, raising concerns over civil liberties

Explanation: After the 9/11 attacks, expanded government powers under new security laws raised concerns about potential infringement on individual civil liberties.

23. Which right protects citizens against being exploited through practices like bonded labour?

  • A. Right to Equality
  • B. Right against Exploitation
  • C. Right to Freedom of Religion
  • D. Right to Constitutional Remedies

Answer: B. Right against Exploitation

Explanation: The Right against Exploitation specifically prohibits forced and bonded labour, protecting vulnerable citizens from being exploited.

24. Why is judicial protection of rights considered important in a democracy?

  • A. Because courts have no power over rights cases
  • B. Because it allows citizens to seek redress when their rights are violated by the state or others
  • C. Because it eliminates the need for a constitution
  • D. Because judicial protection applies only to criminals

Answer: B. Because it allows citizens to seek redress when their rights are violated by the state or others

Explanation: Judicial protection ensures that if a right is violated, citizens have an accessible and enforceable mechanism to seek justice through the courts.

25. Which of the following situations, discussed in the chapter, illustrates the abuse of rights in Saudi Arabia?

  • A. Full freedom of religion for all groups
  • B. Restrictions faced by non-Muslims in publicly practising their religion
  • C. Complete freedom for workers of all nationalities
  • D. Free and fair multi-party elections

Answer: B. Restrictions faced by non-Muslims in publicly practising their religion

Explanation: The chapter notes restrictions on non-Muslims openly practising their religion in Saudi Arabia as an example of curbs on religious freedom.

26. What broader idea does the chapter suggest about rights and democracy?

  • A. Rights and democracy are unrelated concepts
  • B. Rights are an essential part of the idea of democracy, not something separate from it
  • C. Rights can exist only in non-democratic countries
  • D. Democracy makes rights unnecessary

Answer: B. Rights are an essential part of the idea of democracy, not something separate from it

Explanation: The chapter emphasises that rights are not just legal add-ons but a core component of what makes a political system genuinely democratic.

27. What kind of protection does the Right to Freedom of Religion NOT provide?

  • A. It does not allow the state to promote or favour any particular religion
  • B. It bans all religious practices in the country
  • C. It only allows the majority religion to be practised
  • D. It prevents citizens from changing their religion under any circumstance

Answer: A. It does not allow the state to promote or favour any particular religion

Explanation: In line with India's secular character, this right ensures the state does not favour any single religion while allowing citizens freedom of worship.

28. Which of these best explains why minorities are given special cultural and educational rights?

  • A. To help preserve their distinct identity and prevent domination by the majority
  • B. To give them more political power than the majority
  • C. To eliminate their culture over time
  • D. To exclude them from mainstream education

Answer: A. To help preserve their distinct identity and prevent domination by the majority

Explanation: These rights protect minority communities' language, culture and educational institutions from being overridden by the majority community.

29. What is the significance of describing rights as 'guarantees' rather than mere 'promises'?

  • A. Guarantees are legally enforceable, unlike promises which may not be
  • B. There is no difference between the two terms
  • C. Promises are stronger than guarantees
  • D. Guarantees apply only to the government, not citizens

Answer: A. Guarantees are legally enforceable, unlike promises which may not be

Explanation: Because Fundamental Rights are guaranteed by the Constitution, they are legally enforceable in court, unlike mere political promises that carry no legal obligation.

30. Overall, what is the central message of the chapter regarding rights in a democracy?

  • A. Rights limit democracy and should be minimised
  • B. Rights empower citizens, protect their dignity, and strengthen democratic governance
  • C. Rights are only symbolic and rarely enforced
  • D. Rights are needed only in times of war

Answer: B. Rights empower citizens, protect their dignity, and strengthen democratic governance

Explanation: The chapter concludes that rights are central to democracy, empowering citizens, safeguarding their dignity, and helping ensure the government remains accountable and just.

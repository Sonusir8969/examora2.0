NCERT Solutions for Class 12 Biology Chapter 7 Human Health and Disease

NCERT Solutions for Class 12 Biology Chapter 7 Human Health and Disease covers common infectious diseases, the immune system, AIDS, cancer, and the harmful effects of drug and alcohol abuse.

1. Common Diseases in Humans

Short Note:

• Diseases can be caused by pathogens such as bacteria (e.g. typhoid, pneumonia), viruses (e.g. common cold), protozoans (e.g. malaria caused by Plasmodium, transmitted by female Anopheles mosquitoes), and helminths (e.g. ascariasis, elephantiasis).

• Understanding the mode of transmission (airborne, waterborne, vector-borne) and life cycle of pathogens is essential for designing effective prevention and control strategies, such as vaccination, sanitation, and vector control.

2. The Immune System

Short Note:

• The human immune system provides innate immunity (non-specific, present from birth, including physical barriers, phagocytic cells) and acquired immunity (specific, pathogen-directed, and possessing memory).

• Acquired immunity can be active (antibodies produced by the individual's own immune system, as in natural infection or vaccination) or passive (ready-made antibodies given directly, as in a mother's antibodies passed to a baby through breast milk).

3. AIDS and Cancer

Short Note:

• AIDS (Acquired Immuno Deficiency Syndrome) is caused by the Human Immunodeficiency Virus (HIV), which attacks and destroys helper T-lymphocytes, severely weakening the immune system and making the body vulnerable to opportunistic infections.

• Cancer results from uncontrolled cell division caused by genetic mutations affecting proto-oncogenes and tumour suppressor genes; cancer cells can invade surrounding tissue and, in malignant cases, spread to other parts of the body through metastasis.

4. Drug and Alcohol Abuse

Short Note:

• Drug and alcohol abuse refers to the use of substances that lead to adverse effects on the individual's physical, mental and social wellbeing, often resulting in addiction and dependence.

• Commonly abused substances include opioids, cannabinoids, and coca alkaloids (like cocaine), and preventive measures include avoiding peer pressure, seeking professional counselling, and being aware of warning signs of substance abuse.

NCERT Textbook Exercise Questions

Q1: Differentiate between innate immunity and acquired immunity.
Answer: Innate immunity is the non-specific defence mechanism present in the body from birth, providing immediate but general protection through physical barriers (skin, mucus), physiological barriers (acidic stomach pH), and cellular barriers (phagocytic cells, natural killer cells). Acquired immunity, on the other hand, is pathogen-specific, develops over the lifetime of an individual, is slower to act initially, and possesses memory, meaning the body can respond more rapidly upon subsequent exposure to the same pathogen.
Q2: How does HIV cause AIDS?
Answer: HIV primarily infects and destroys helper T-lymphocytes (a type of white blood cell crucial for coordinating the immune response). As the virus replicates within these cells and causes their gradual depletion, the body's immune system becomes progressively weaker, making the infected person increasingly susceptible to opportunistic infections and certain cancers, a condition which is diagnosed as AIDS.
Q3: What is metastasis, and why is it dangerous in cancer?
Answer: Metastasis is the process by which cancer cells break away from the primary tumour, travel through the blood or lymphatic system, and establish secondary tumours in other parts of the body. It is dangerous because it allows cancer to spread beyond its original site, making the disease much harder to treat and significantly worsening the prognosis for the patient.
Q4: What are the harmful effects of drug and alcohol abuse on the human body?
Answer: Drug and alcohol abuse can cause severe damage to vital organs such as the liver, heart, and brain, impair coordination and cognitive function, weaken the immune system, and lead to psychological dependence and addiction. It can also result in significant social consequences, including strained relationships, poor academic or work performance, and increased risk-taking behaviour that endangers both the individual and others.

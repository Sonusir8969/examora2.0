NCERT Solutions for Class 9 Maths Chapter 1 Number Systems

NCERT Solutions for Class 9 Maths Chapter 1 Number Systems covers rational and irrational numbers, real numbers and their decimal expansions, representing real numbers on the number line, and the laws of exponents for real numbers.

1. Rational and Irrational Numbers

Short Note:

• Rational Number: A number that can be written in the form p/q, where p and q are integers and q ≠ 0.

• Irrational Number: A number that cannot be written in the form p/q; examples include √2, √3, π.

• Every integer, whole number and natural number is a rational number. Zero is also a rational number (0 = 0/1).

• Between any two rational numbers, there exist infinitely many rational numbers as well as infinitely many irrational numbers.

2. Real Numbers and Decimal Expansions

Short Note:

• The collection of rational and irrational numbers together forms the set of Real Numbers.

• A rational number has either a terminating decimal expansion or a non-terminating repeating (recurring) decimal expansion.

• An irrational number has a non-terminating, non-repeating decimal expansion.

• Every real number can be represented by a unique point on the number line, and every point on the number line represents a unique real number.

3. Representing Real Numbers on the Number Line

Short Note:

• Irrational numbers such as √2, √3 can be located on the number line using the Pythagoras theorem construction.

• Process of Successive Magnification: visualising a real number with a non-terminating decimal expansion by zooming into smaller and smaller intervals on the number line.

4. Operations on Real Numbers & Laws of Exponents

Short Note:

• Sum or difference of a rational and an irrational number is always irrational.

• Product or quotient of a non-zero rational number with an irrational number is always irrational.

• For positive real numbers a, b: √a · √b = √(ab); √a / √b = √(a/b); (√a + √b)(√a − √b) = a − b.

• Laws of Exponents: ap · aq = ap+q; (ap)q = apq; ap/aq = ap−q; apbp = (ab)p, for a, b > 0 and p, q rational numbers.

NCERT Textbook Exercise Questions

Is zero a rational number? Can you write it in the form p/q, where p and q are integers and q ≠ 0?
Answer: Yes, zero is a rational number. It can be written as 0/1, 0/2, 0/(−5), etc., where p = 0 and q is any non-zero integer, so it satisfies the p/q form.
Find six rational numbers between 3 and 4.
Answer: 3 = 21/7 and 4 = 28/7. So six rational numbers between 3 and 4 are 22/7, 23/7, 24/7, 25/7, 26/7 and 27/7.
State whether the following statement is true or false: Every rational number is a real number. Justify.
Answer: True. The collection of real numbers is made up of all rational numbers together with all irrational numbers, so every rational number is indeed a real number.
Show how √5 can be represented on the number line.
Answer: Draw a right triangle OAB with OA = 2 units and AB = 1 unit at right angles, so OB = √(2² + 1²) = √5. With O as centre and OB as radius, draw an arc cutting the number line; the point where it cuts represents √5.
Classify 1.101001000100001... as rational or irrational.
Answer: The decimal expansion is non-terminating and non-repeating (the pattern of zeros keeps increasing without settling into a repeating block), so the number is irrational.
Rationalise the denominator of 1/(7 + 3√2).
Answer: Multiply numerator and denominator by (7 − 3√2): 1/(7+3√2) × (7−3√2)/(7−3√2) = (7−3√2)/(49−18) = (7−3√2)/31.

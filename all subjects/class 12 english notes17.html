NCERT Solutions for Class 12 English Chapter 17 The Enemy

NCERT Solutions for Class 12 English Vistas Chapter 17 The Enemy covers Pearl S. Buck's story of a Japanese doctor's moral dilemma during wartime, the theme of humanity over nationalism, and important questions and answers.

1. Story Summary

Short Note:

• Set during World War II, the story follows Dr. Sadao Hoki, a respected Japanese surgeon, who discovers a severely wounded American prisoner of war, Tom, washed up near his home, and faces an intense moral dilemma about whether to help or abandon his enemy.

• Despite significant personal risk, including potential accusations of treason from his own community, Dr. Sadao and his wife, Hana, choose to secretly treat and nurse the wounded American soldier back to health, driven by their deep sense of medical duty and humanity.

• Eventually, fearing discovery and the danger it poses to his family and reputation, Dr. Sadao arranges for Tom's safe escape, ultimately prioritising his sense of professional and moral integrity over narrow nationalistic loyalty.

2. Character Sketch

Short Note:

• Dr. Sadao Hoki: A skilled, morally conscientious surgeon who grapples deeply with the ethical dilemma of treating an enemy soldier, ultimately choosing humanity and professional duty over nationalistic prejudice.

• Hana: Dr. Sadao's supportive and courageous wife, who initially struggles with fear and revulsion but ultimately assists her husband in caring for the wounded soldier, embodying compassion despite personal risk.

• Tom: The wounded American soldier, whose vulnerability and humanity challenge the rigid nationalistic and racial prejudices prevalent during wartime.

3. Theme & Message

Short Note:

• The story powerfully explores the theme of humanity transcending national boundaries and wartime prejudice, emphasising the importance of compassion, medical ethics, and moral integrity even amid intense political and social pressures.

• It also highlights the psychological complexity and personal courage required to act according to one's conscience, especially when such actions directly conflict with prevailing societal expectations and nationalistic loyalty.

Reading With Insight (Textbook Questions)

Q1: Why does Dr. Sadao initially hesitate to help the wounded American soldier?
Answer: Dr. Sadao initially hesitates because helping an enemy soldier during wartime could be seen as treasonous by his own community and government, potentially endangering his reputation, career, and even the safety of his family, creating a significant conflict between his professional duty as a doctor and his loyalty to his nation.
Q2: How does Hana's attitude towards the wounded soldier change throughout the story?
Answer: Hana initially reacts with fear and disgust towards the wounded enemy soldier, reflecting the deep-seated wartime prejudice and anxiety of the time, but gradually, through her husband's example and her own sense of compassion and duty, she overcomes her initial reluctance to actively assist in caring for and nursing Tom back to health.
Q3: Why does Dr. Sadao ultimately decide to help Tom escape rather than turn him over to authorities?
Answer: Dr. Sadao ultimately decides to help Tom escape because he recognises the moral complexity of the situation, prioritising his sense of humanity, gratitude for Tom's survival under his care, and personal integrity over blind national loyalty, despite the potential risks and consequences of this decision.

Extract-Based / Short Answer Questions

Q4: What broader commentary does the story offer about nationalism and prejudice during wartime?
Answer: The story critiques the dehumanising effects of nationalism and wartime prejudice, illustrating through Dr. Sadao's moral struggle how deeply ingrained political and social loyalties can conflict with fundamental human compassion, ultimately advocating for prioritising universal humanity over narrow, divisive nationalistic ideologies.
Q5: What does the story suggest about the role of personal conscience in moral decision-making?
Answer: The story suggests that personal conscience and individual moral integrity should ultimately guide decision-making, even when such choices conflict with broader societal expectations or nationalistic pressures, highlighting the courage required to act ethically and compassionately amid challenging, high-stakes circumstances.

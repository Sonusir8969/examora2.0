Ch-11: Political Parties 15 Questions

1. Which of the following is NOT a component of a political party?

  • A. The Leaders
  • B. The Active Members
  • C. The Followers
  • D. The Election Commission

Answer: D. The Election Commission

Explanation: A political party has three main components: the leaders, the active members, and the followers. The Election Commission is an independent body that regulates elections.

2. A country that has a one-party system is:

  • A. China
  • B. India
  • C. United States of America
  • D. United Kingdom

Answer: A. China

Explanation: In China, only the Communist Party is allowed to rule, making it a one-party system.

3. Which system of government features a two-party system?

  • A. USA and UK
  • B. India and Pakistan
  • C. China and North Korea
  • D. France and Germany

Answer: A. USA and UK

Explanation: The United States of America and the United Kingdom are prominent examples of a two-party system.

4. What is an alliance or front in political party terms?

  • A. When several parties join hands for the purpose of contesting elections and winning power
  • B. When two candidates from the same party fight each other
  • C. When a party gets split into smaller factions
  • D. An agreement between the ruling and opposition parties

Answer: A. When several parties join hands for the purpose of contesting elections and winning power

Explanation: When several parties in a multi-party system join hands for contesting elections and holding power, it is called an alliance or a front (e.g., NDA, UPA in India).

5. Which body in India registers political parties and allocates symbols to them?

  • A. Election Commission of India
  • B. Supreme Court of India
  • C. Parliament of India
  • D. NITI Aayog

Answer: A. Election Commission of India

Explanation: All political parties in India must register with the Election Commission, which treats all parties equally and offers unique election symbols to recognized parties.

6. To become a recognized State Party, a party must secure at least what percentage of the total votes in an election to the Legislative Assembly?

  • A. 6% of total votes and win at least 2 seats
  • B. 10% of total votes and win at least 5 seats
  • C. 4% of total votes and win at least 1 seat
  • D. 8% of total votes and win at least 3 seats

Answer: A. 6% of total votes and win at least 2 seats

Explanation: A party that secures at least 6 per cent of the total votes in an election to the Legislative Assembly of a State and wins at least two seats is recognized as a State Party.

7. To become a recognized National Party, a party must secure at least what percentage of total votes in Lok Sabha or Assembly elections in four states?

  • A. 6% of total votes and win at least 4 seats in Lok Sabha
  • B. 10% of total votes and win at least 10 seats in Lok Sabha
  • C. 5% of total votes and win at least 2 seats in Lok Sabha
  • D. 12% of total votes and win at least 6 seats in Lok Sabha

Answer: A. 6% of total votes and win at least 4 seats in Lok Sabha

Explanation: A party that secures at least 6% of total votes in Lok Sabha elections or Assembly elections in four states and wins at least 4 seats in the Lok Sabha is recognized as a National Party.

8. What is 'Defection' in politics?

  • A. Changing party allegiance from the party on whose ticket one was elected to another party
  • B. Refusing to participate in elections
  • C. Forming a new political party before elections
  • D. Voting against the government during a policy debate

Answer: A. Changing party allegiance from the party on whose ticket one was elected to another party

Explanation: Defection means changing party allegiance from the political party on whose ticket a person got elected to another political party.

9. What requirement has been made mandatory by the Supreme Court to reduce the influence of money and muscle power in elections?

  • A. Filing an affidavit giving details of property and criminal cases
  • B. Mandatory retirement age for politicians
  • C. Banning television campaigns
  • D. Direct public funding for every candidate

Answer: A. Filing an affidavit giving details of property and criminal cases

Explanation: The Supreme Court made it mandatory for every candidate contesting elections to file an Affidavit giving details of their property and criminal cases pending against them.

10. What is a signed document submitted to an officer containing personal information or statements under oath called?

  • A. Affidavit
  • B. Manifesto
  • C. Nominee Form
  • D. Charter

Answer: A. Affidavit

Explanation: An affidavit is a signed document submitted to an officer, where a person makes a sworn statement regarding their personal information.

11. Which of the following is a major challenge faced by political parties?

  • A. Lack of internal democracy
  • B. Dynastic succession
  • C. Growing role of money and muscle power
  • D. All of the above

Answer: D. All of the above

Explanation: Political parties face multiple challenges, including lack of internal democracy, dynastic succession, money/muscle power, and lack of meaningful choices to voters.

12. Which function is performed by political parties in a democracy?

  • A. Contest elections and form government
  • B. Shape public opinion
  • C. Play the role of opposition when not in power
  • D. All of the above

Answer: D. All of the above

Explanation: Political parties perform key functions: contesting elections, putting forward policies, making laws, forming governments, playing the opposition role, and shaping public opinion.

13. The party that loses elections plays which critical role in a democracy?

  • A. Role of Opposition
  • B. Ruling Coalition
  • C. Election Observer
  • D. Independent Judiciary

Answer: A. Role of Opposition

Explanation: Parties that lose in the elections play the role of opposition by voicing different views and criticizing the government for its failures or wrong policies.

14. What is a 'partisan' person?

  • A. A person who is strongly committed to a party, group, or faction
  • B. A person who does not support any political party
  • C. An official appointed by the Election Commission
  • D. A voter who changes parties frequently

Answer: A. A person who is strongly committed to a party, group, or faction

Explanation: A partisan is a person who is strongly committed to a party, group, or faction, and is often unable to take a balanced view on an issue.

15. Why is a multi-party system considered suitable for a diverse country like India?

  • A. It allows various social and geographical interests to get political representation
  • B. It ensures faster decision-making than single-party systems
  • C. It eliminates election expenditures
  • D. It prevents the formation of coalition governments

Answer: A. It allows various social and geographical interests to get political representation

Explanation: In a large and socially diverse country like India, a multi-party system ensures that different regional, social, and cultural interests get fair representation in governance.

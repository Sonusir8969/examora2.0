Ch-3: Money and Credit 30 Questions

1. What is the system called where goods are directly exchanged for other goods without using money?

  • A. Barter System
  • B. Credit System
  • C. Monetary System
  • D. Banking System

Answer: A. Barter System

Explanation: Under the barter system, trade occurs directly through the exchange of goods and services without the medium of money.

2. What essential condition must be met for a barter system transaction to occur successfully?

  • A. Double coincidence of wants
  • B. High liquidity
  • C. Availability of paper currency
  • D. Government regulation

Answer: A. Double coincidence of wants

Explanation: Double coincidence of wants means both parties involved in trade must desire the exact commodity that the other person offers.

3. Why is money accepted as a medium of exchange in modern economies?

  • A. It is authorized by the country's government
  • B. It is made of precious metals like gold and silver
  • C. It can be easily manufactured by anyone
  • D. It has intrinsic value equal to its face value

Answer: A. It is authorized by the country's government

Explanation: Money acts as a universally accepted medium of exchange because legal laws and state authority back its validity for transactions.

4. Which authority issues currency notes in India on behalf of the Central Government?

  • A. Reserve Bank of India (RBI)
  • B. State Bank of India (SBI)
  • C. Ministry of Finance
  • D. NITI Aayog

Answer: A. Reserve Bank of India (RBI)

Explanation: Under Indian law, only the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) is empowered to issue legal tender currency notes on behalf of the central government.

5. What are bank deposits that can be withdrawn by depositors on demand called?

  • A. Demand Deposits
  • B. Fixed Deposits
  • C. Recurring Deposits
  • D. Term Deposits

Answer: A. Demand Deposits

Explanation: Deposits held in bank savings or current accounts that account holders can withdraw at any time on demand are known as demand deposits.

6. What paper instrument instructs a bank to pay a specific amount from a person's account to another person?

  • A. Cheque
  • B. Promissory Note
  • C. Bond
  • D. Collateral Paper

Answer: A. Cheque

Explanation: A cheque is a written instrument instructing a bank to transfer a specified amount from the payer's account to the named recipient.

7. Approximately what percentage of their total cash deposits do commercial banks in India keep as cash reserves for daily withdrawals?

  • A. 15%
  • B. 50%
  • C. 75%
  • D. 100%

Answer: A. 15%

Explanation: Banks in India retain about 15% of total deposits as liquid cash reserves to satisfy everyday withdrawal demands of depositors.

8. How do commercial banks earn their main source of income?

  • A. By charging higher interest rates on loans than what they pay on deposits
  • B. By charging high entrance fees for opening accounts
  • C. From direct grants provided by the central government
  • D. By charging transaction fees for issuing paper cheques

Answer: A. By charging higher interest rates on loans than what they pay on deposits

Explanation: The difference between the interest rate charged on loans given to borrowers and the interest paid to depositors forms the primary source of income for banks.

9. What is an asset that a borrower owns and pledges as a guarantee to a lender until a loan is fully repaid?

  • A. Collateral
  • B. Principal
  • C. Dividend
  • D. Credit Guarantee

Answer: A. Collateral

Explanation: Collateral is security (land, house, vehicle, livestock, bank deposits) offered by a borrower to secure loan repayment.

10. Interest rate, collateral, documentation requirements, and the mode of repayment together constitute what?

  • A. Terms of Credit
  • B. Terms of Trade
  • C. Monetary Policy Terms
  • D. Banking Norms

Answer: A. Terms of Credit

Explanation: Every credit agreement specifies conditions including interest rate, security deposit (collateral), documentation, and payment schedule, collectively known as terms of credit.

11. Which of the following is an example of a formal source of credit in India?

  • A. Commercial Banks and Cooperatives
  • B. Moneylenders and Traders
  • C. Friends and Relatives
  • D. Landlords

Answer: A. Commercial Banks and Cooperatives

Explanation: Formal sources include financial institutions regulated legally by the state or central authority, like commercial banks and cooperative societies.

12. Which regulatory institution supervises the functioning and lending activities of formal credit sources in India?

  • A. Reserve Bank of India (RBI)
  • B. Ministry of Finance
  • C. Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI)
  • D. State Governments

Answer: A. Reserve Bank of India (RBI)

Explanation: The RBI oversees commercial banks and formal financial institutions to ensure compliance with lending regulations, minimum reserve balances, and fair interest rates.

13. Which group relies predominantly on informal sources of credit in India?

  • A. Poor rural households
  • B. Large industrial corporations
  • C. Well-off urban households
  • D. Government contractors

Answer: A. Poor rural households

Explanation: Due to lack of collateral assets, formal bank presence, and proper documentation, poor households depend heavily on informal sources like local moneylenders.

14. What risk occurs when credit pushes a borrower into a situation where repayment is impossible, forcing them to sell assets or borrow further?

  • A. Debt Trap
  • B. Liquidity Crunch
  • C. Credit Default Swap
  • D. Asset Depreciation

Answer: A. Debt Trap

Explanation: A debt trap occurs when high interest rates or crop failures make credit repayment impossible, driving the borrower into worsening debt spirals.

15. What is the typical number of members in a standard Self-Help Group (SHG)?

  • A. 15 to 20 members
  • B. 50 to 100 members
  • C. 2 to 5 members
  • D. 200 to 500 members

Answer: A. 15 to 20 members

Explanation: A typical Self-Help Group consists of 15-20 members, usually neighborhood women, who meet regularly to pool small savings and extend micro-loans.

16. Who founded the Grameen Bank of Bangladesh, which pioneered micro-credit for the poor?

  • A. Muhammad Yunus
  • B. Amartya Sen
  • C. Mahbub ul Haq
  • D. Abhijit Banerjee

Answer: A. Muhammad Yunus

Explanation: Nobel Laureate Prof. Muhammad Yunus founded Grameen Bank in the 1970s to provide affordable small-scale credit to poor rural women.

17. Why do informal lenders charge extremely high interest rates compared to formal banks?

  • A. There is no supervising body to control their lending practices
  • B. They are mandated by law to collect high rates
  • C. They face heavy corporate taxation from state governments
  • D. Their operational expenses are supervised by RBI

Answer: A. There is no supervising body to control their lending practices

Explanation: Informal lenders operate outside legal regulatory oversight, allowing them to exploit borrowers by demanding exorbitant interest charges.

18. Which modern forms of money have replaced historical currency mediums like grain, cattle, and metal coins?

  • A. Currency notes and coins
  • B. Commodities and bartered livestock
  • C. Gold and silver bullion standard
  • D. Agricultural produce vouchers

Answer: A. Currency notes and coins

Explanation: Modern forms of money consist primarily of paper notes and metallic currency coins authorized legally by national monetary authorities.

19. Why do informal moneylenders grant loans to poor borrowers without demanding collateral?

  • A. They personally know the borrowers and can enforce repayment through social pressure
  • B. They are mandated by government welfare schemes
  • C. They collect zero interest from poor families
  • D. The RBI guarantees insurance for their unpaid loans

Answer: A. They personally know the borrowers and can enforce repayment through social pressure

Explanation: Local moneylenders often rely on personal familiarity and coercive pressure to grant credit without requiring formal collateral property assets.

20. What is a major advantage of Self-Help Groups (SHGs) for rural poor women?

  • A. They help borrowers overcome the lack of collateral assets
  • B. They provide free housing to members
  • C. They guarantee lifelong employment in government posts
  • D. They grant interest-free international loans

Answer: A. They help borrowers overcome the lack of collateral assets

Explanation: Banks lend to registered SHGs as a collective unit based on group saving performance, enabling individual members to obtain loans without personal collateral.

21. Modern currency notes differ from historical commodity money because modern paper notes:

  • A. Have no intrinsic value of their own as a commodity
  • B. Are made from pure silver sheets
  • C. Can be consumed as food during emergencies
  • D. Possess intrinsic value equal to their printed denomination

Answer: A. Have no intrinsic value of their own as a commodity

Explanation: Unlike gold or silver coins, modern paper notes have no direct utility or intrinsic commodity worth; their value rests on government authorization.

22. Which of the following is an informal source of credit?

  • A. Agricultural Trader
  • B. State Cooperative Bank
  • C. Regional Rural Bank
  • D. Urban Commercial Bank

Answer: A. Agricultural Trader

Explanation: Agricultural traders, agricultural landlords, and village moneylenders operate outside regulated banking channels, making them informal credit providers.

23. What role does the Reserve Bank of India play regarding bank loan distribution?

  • A. It ensures banks extend credit to small cultivators, small industries, and priority sectors, not just profit-making businesses
  • B. It mandates that banks keep 100% of deposits in cash reserves
  • C. It sets prices for consumer goods in retail markets
  • D. It fixes salary structures for private corporate employees

Answer: A. It ensures banks extend credit to small cultivators, small industries, and priority sectors, not just profit-making businesses

Explanation: The RBI monitors commercial banks to ensure credit flow extends equitably to small farmers, small scale industries, and weaker sections of society.

24. What primary advantage do formal loans offer compared to informal credit?

  • A. Lower interest rates and transparent, fair repayment terms
  • B. No requirement for identity proof or documentation
  • C. Total immunity from loan repayment if crops fail
  • D. Unrestricted instant cash delivery without application evaluation

Answer: A. Lower interest rates and transparent, fair repayment terms

Explanation: Formal loans feature regulated, reasonable interest rates and defined terms, preventing predatory practices common in informal lending.

25. Why do rich urban households receive a higher share of formal credit than poor rural households?

  • A. They possess necessary collateral documents and have better banking access
  • B. Poor rural families are legally banned from opening bank accounts
  • C. Formal banks are forbidden from opening rural branches
  • D. Rich urban households pay zero interest on loans

Answer: A. They possess necessary collateral documents and have better banking access

Explanation: Rich urban households easily satisfy bank prerequisites like property collateral, steady salary documentation, and credit histories.

26. In credit transactions, what does the term 'principal' represent?

  • A. The original amount of money borrowed
  • B. The extra interest amount charged by the lender
  • C. The fee paid for banking processing
  • D. The value of property pledged as security

Answer: A. The original amount of money borrowed

Explanation: The principal refers to the base sum of money lent or invested, excluding accumulated interest or additional processing fees.

27. Which organization provides cheap loans to member farmers for purchasing seeds, fertilizers, and agricultural implements?

  • A. Cooperative Societies
  • B. Foreign Institutional Investors
  • C. Private Money Syndicates
  • D. Multi-National Corporations

Answer: A. Cooperative Societies

Explanation: Agricultural cooperatives accept deposits from farmer members and utilize pooled resources to offer low-interest agricultural loans.

28. What key problem prevents poor farmers from obtaining formal loans from commercial banks?

  • A. Lack of asset collateral and formal documentation
  • B. Refusal to pay interest charges
  • C. Excess savings deposited in bank accounts
  • D. Availability of interest-free informal credit

Answer: A. Lack of asset collateral and formal documentation

Explanation: Formal banking institutions require asset collateral security and documentation, which many landless and poor farmers lack.

29. Why is cheap and affordable credit considered crucial for a nation's development?

  • A. It enables people to invest in agriculture, business, and industries without severe debt burden
  • B. It eliminates the need for paper currency
  • C. It automatically raises government tax revenues
  • D. It reduces overall agricultural production costs to zero

Answer: A. It enables people to invest in agriculture, business, and industries without severe debt burden

Explanation: Affordable credit allows entrepreneurs, farmers, and small business owners to expand operations and generate employment without getting trapped in debt.

30. How do Self-Help Groups promote self-employment among poor rural women?

  • A. By providing small, affordable loans for purchasing raw materials, seeds, cattle, and machinery
  • B. By offering government civil service jobs directly
  • C. By buying all products manufactured in villages at fixed prices
  • D. By eliminating all formal banking branches in rural districts

Answer: A. By providing small, affordable loans for purchasing raw materials, seeds, cattle, and machinery

Explanation: SHGs extend micro-loans that enable women to buy working assets (sewing machines, livestock, raw materials), fostering economic self-reliance.

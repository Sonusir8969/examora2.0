NCERT Solutions for Class 10 Economics Chapter 4 Globalisation and the Indian Economy

NCERT Solutions for Class 10 Economics Chapter 4 covers Multinational Corporations (MNCs), Integration of Production and Markets across countries, Factors Enabling Globalisation, World Trade Organisation (WTO), Foreign Investment Policy, Trade Barriers, and Fair Globalisation.

1. Multinational Corporations (MNCs) & Production Across Countries

Short Note:

• MNC (Multinational Corporation): A company that owns or controls production in more than one nation to lower costs and maximize profits.

• Interlinking Production: MNCs set up production jointly with local companies, buy local firms, or place orders with small producers in developing nations.

2. Foreign Trade and Integration of Markets

Short Note:

• Foreign Trade: Creates an opportunity for producers to reach markets beyond domestic boundaries and provides consumers with wider choices.

• Market Integration: Foreign trade connects markets in different countries through the movement of goods, capital, and technology.

3. What is Globalisation & Factors Enabling It

Short Note:

• Globalisation: The process of rapid integration or interconnection between countries through greater trade, investment, and movement of people.

• Enabling Factors: Rapid improvements in Telecommunication, Internet, and Containerized Transport technology along with trade liberalisation policies.

4. World Trade Organisation (WTO) & Liberalisation in India

Short Note:

• Liberalisation: Removing barriers or restrictions set by the government on trade and foreign investments (adopted in India in 1991).

• WTO: An international organization aimed at liberalising international trade and enforcing trade agreements among member nations.

5. Impact of Globalisation & The Struggle for Fair Globalisation

Short Note:

• Impact: Benefited urban consumers, large companies, and skilled workers, but posed severe challenges to small-scale industries and unorganized workers.

• Fair Globalisation: Ensures that benefits are shared equally and government policy protects the interests of workers and small producers alongside big investors.

Intext & Conceptual Questions

Q1: What are trade barriers? Why did the Indian government put trade barriers after Independence?
1. Trade Barriers: Restrictions imposed by governments on international trade, such as import duties or quotas.
2. Reason: Post-independence, trade barriers were necessary to protect domestic infant industries from fierce foreign competition until they grew strong enough.
Q2: What was the New Economic Policy of 1991 in India?
1. In 1991, India removed major restrictions on foreign trade and investment to liberalise the economy.
2. The objective was to make Indian producers compete globally to improve product quality and efficiency.

Textbook Chapter End Exercise Questions

Q1: What do you understand by globalisation? Explain in your own words.
Answer:
Globalisation is the process of rapid integration and interconnection among various countries of the world. It involves the free flow of goods, services, technology, capital, and labor across international borders, driven primarily by Multinational Corporations (MNCs) and supported by modern communication and transport systems.
Q2: What is the role of MNCs in the globalisation process?
Answer:
1. Foreign Direct Investment: MNCs invest capital in multiple countries, bringing advanced production technologies.
2. Global Production Networks: They manufacture parts in low-cost countries and assemble them elsewhere, linking global economies.
3. Market Expansion: MNCs increase global trade volumes and offer greater choice of quality goods at competitive prices worldwide.
Q3: How can the government ensure that globalisation becomes fairer?
Answer:
1. Labor Laws: Enforce strict labor laws to protect workers' rights and ensure fair wages and job security.
2. Support Small Producers: Provide financial credit, subsidies, and infrastructure to small-scale industries to help them compete against MNCs.
3. Use Trade Barriers Responsibly: Use targeted trade and investment barriers when necessary to safeguard vulnerable domestic sectors.
4. Negotiations at WTO: Join forces with other developing nations to negotiate fairer rules at international forums like the WTO.

1. Alpha-Particle Scattering Experiment & Rutherford's Atomic Model

Short Note:

• Geiger-Marsden Experiment: Alpha particles (He²⁺) directed at a thin gold foil. Observations showed most α-particles pass un-deviated, while a tiny fraction (~1 in 8000) bounce back by > 90°.

• Key Inferences: Entire positive charge and almost all mass of the atom are concentrated in a tiny central region called the nucleus (size ~ 10⁻¹⁵ m to 10⁻¹⁴ m), while atom size is ~ 10⁻¹⁰ m.

• Distance of Closest Approach (r₀): Estimated size of nucleus: r₀ = (1 / 4πε₀) × (2 Z e² / K), where K is the initial kinetic energy of the α-particle.

• Impact Parameter (b): Perpendicular distance of the velocity vector of α-particle from the central nucleus line. Small b leads to large deflection angle θ.

• Limitations of Rutherford Model: Could not explain the electrical stability of an orbiting accelerating electron radiating continuous energy, nor the discrete line spectrum of atoms.

2. Bohr's Model of the Hydrogen Atom

Short Note:

• Bohr's Postulates:

  1. Stationary Orbits: Electrons revolve around the nucleus only in certain non-radiating, stable circular orbits.

  2. Quantization of Angular Momentum: Orbital angular momentum is an integral multiple of h / (2π): L = m v r = (n h) / (2π), where n = 1, 2, 3... is the principal quantum number.

  3. Frequency Condition: Energy is emitted or absorbed as a photon only during transition between orbits: h ν = E₂ - E₁.

• Radius of n-th Orbit: r_n = (n² h² ε₀) / (π m e²) = n² × r₁ (Bohr radius a₀ = r₁ ≈ 0.53 Å = 0.053 nm).

• Velocity of Electron in n-th Orbit: v_n = e² / (2 ε₀ n h) ≈ c / (137 n).

• Energy of Electron in n-th Orbit:

  - Kinetic Energy: K = -E_total = 13.6 / n² eV

  - Potential Energy: U = 2 E_total = -27.2 / n² eV

  - Total Energy: E_n = -13.6 / n² eV (Negative sign confirms electron is bound to nucleus).

3. Line Spectra of Hydrogen & De Broglie's Explanation

Short Note:

• Rydberg Formula for Hydrogen Wavelength: 1 / λ = R × (1 / n₁² - 1 / n₂²), where R is Rydberg constant (R ≈ 1.097 × 10⁷ m⁻¹).

• Spectral Series Summary:

  - Lyman Series: n₁ = 1, n₂ = 2, 3, 4... (Ultraviolet Region)

  - Balmer Series: n₁ = 2, n₂ = 3, 4, 5... (Visible Region)

  - Paschen Series: stream n₁ = 3, n₂ = 4, 5, 6... (Near Infrared Region)

  - Brackett Series: n₁ = 4, n₂ = 5, 6, 7... (Infrared Region)

  - Pfund Series: n₁ = 5, n₂ = 6, 7, 8... (Far Infrared Region)

• De Broglie's Explanation of Quantization: Standing wave condition for an electron wave along a circular orbit requires circumference to equal integer number of wavelengths: 2π r = n λ = n (h / m v) => m v r = (n h) / (2π).

Intext & Conceptual Questions

Q1: State Bohr's quantization condition for angular momentum of an electron. How did de Broglie explain this postulate?
Answer:
• Bohr's Postulate: Bohr postulated that electrons in an atom revolve only in those non-radiating stationary orbits for which the total orbital angular momentum (L) is an integral multiple of h / (2π):
L = m v r = (n h) / (2π), where n = 1, 2, 3...

• De Broglie's Explanation: De Broglie proposed that an electron revolving in a circular orbit behaves as a matter wave. For a stable stationary orbit, the electron wave must form a constructive standing wave around the circular path, meaning the total perimeter of the orbit must equal an integral number of de Broglie wavelengths:
2π r = n λ
Substituting de Broglie wavelength λ = h / (m v) into the equation:
2π r = n (h / m v) => m v r = (n h) / (2π)
Thus, de Broglie's wave hypothesis provided a theoretical derivation for Bohr's empirical quantization rule.
Q2: Distinguish between the Lyman series and Balmer series of the hydrogen spectrum.
Answer:
Feature Lyman Series Balmer Series
Lower Orbit (n₁) n₁ = 1 (Ground State) n₁ = 2 (First Excited State)
Upper Orbits (n₂) n₂ = 2, 3, 4, 5... n₂ = 3, 4, 5, 6...
Spectral Region Ultraviolet (UV) Region Visible Light Region
Energy Transition Higher energy transitions (larger frequency) Moderate energy transitions

Textbook Chapter End Exercise Questions

Q1: Calculate the shortest and longest wavelengths present in the Balmer series of the hydrogen spectrum. (Rydberg constant R = 1.097 × 10⁷ m⁻¹)
Answer:
For Balmer series, the lower quantum level is n₁ = 2.
Rydberg Formula: 1 / λ = R × (1 / 2² - 1 / n₂²) = R × (1 / 4 - 1 / n₂²)

Part (a): Longest Wavelength (λ_max)
Longest wavelength corresponds to the minimum energy photon transition, which occurs when n₂ = 3:
1 / λ_max = R × (1 / 4 - 1 / 9) = R × (5 / 36)
λ_max = 36 / (5 × R) = 36 / (5 × 1.097 × 10⁷) ≈ 656.3 nm

Part (b): Shortest Wavelength (λ_min)
Shortest wavelength corresponds to the maximum energy photon transition (series limit), which occurs when n₂ = ∞:
1 / λ_min = R × (1 / 4 - 0) = R / 4
λ_min = 4 / R = 4 / (1.097 × 10⁷) ≈ 364.6 nm

• Longest wavelength = 656.3 nm (H-alpha line)
• Shortest wavelength = 364.6 nm (Balmer limit)
Q2: According to Bohr's model, calculate the speed of an electron in a hydrogen atom in the ground state level (n = 1) and find its orbital time period.
Answer:
Given for Hydrogen (Z = 1, n = 1):
• Charge of electron (e) = 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ C
• Planck's constant (h) = 6.63 × 10⁻³⁴ J s
• Permittivity of free space (ε₀) = 8.854 × 10⁻¹² F/m

Step 1: Calculate Electron Velocity (v₁)
v₁ = e² / (2 ε₀ h)
v₁ = (1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹)² / (2 × 8.854 × 10⁻¹² × 6.63 × 10⁻³⁴)
v₁ ≈ 2.18 × 10⁶ m/s (roughly 1/137 of light speed c)

Step 2: Calculate Radius of Ground Orbit (r₁)
r₁ = 0.53 Å = 0.53 × 10⁻¹⁰ m

Step 3: Calculate Orbital Period (T)
T = (2π r₁) / v₁ = (2 × 3.1416 × 0.53 × 10⁻¹⁰) / (2.18 × 10⁶) ≈ 1.53 × 10⁻¹⁶ s

• Speed of electron in n = 1 level = 2.18 × 10⁶ m/s
• Orbital time period = 1.53 × 10⁻¹⁶ s

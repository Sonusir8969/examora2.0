NCERT Solutions for Class 12 Physics Chapter 4 Moving Charges and Magnetism

NCERT Solutions for Class 12 Physics Chapter 4 Moving Charges and Magnetism provides comprehensive notes, important formulas, derivations, and step-by-step solutions covering Lorentz force, Biot-Savart law, Ampere's circuital law, solenoid, force between current-carrying conductors, and moving coil galvanometer.

1. Magnetic Force, Lorentz Force & Motion in Magnetic Field

Short Note:

• Lorentz Force: Total force experienced by a charge q moving with velocity v in region with both electric (E) and magnetic (B) fields: F = q(E + v × B).

• Magnetic Force: F_m = q(v × B) = q v B sin θ. Magnetic force acts perpendicular to both velocity and magnetic field, so it does no work on the charge.

• Motion in Uniform Magnetic Field:

  - Perpendicular Entry (θ = 90°): Charge follows a circular path. Radius r = (m v) / (q B); Time period T = (2π m) / (q B) (independent of v and r).

  - Oblique Entry (0° < θ < 90°): Charge follows a helical path with pitch p = v_parallel × T = (2π m v cos θ) / (q B).

2. Biot-Savart Law & Ampere's Circuital Law

Short Note:

• Biot-Savart Law: Magnetic field dB produced by a current element I dl at distance r is: dB = (μ₀ / 4π) × (I dl sin θ / r²), where μ₀ = 4π × 10⁻⁷ T·m/A.

• Applications of Biot-Savart Law:

  - Center of Circular Current Loop (radius R, N turns): B = (μ₀ N I) / (2 R).

  - Axis of Circular Loop at distance x: B = (μ₀ N I R²) / [2(R² + x²)^(3/2)].

• Ampere's Circuital Law: The line integral of magnetic field B along any closed loop equals μ₀ times net current enclosed: ∮ B · dl = μ₀ I_enclosed.

  - Infinitely Long Straight Wire: B = (μ₀ I) / (2π r).

3. Solenoid & Force Between Parallel Current-Carrying Conductors

Short Note:

• Magnetic Field of Long Solenoid: Inside a long solenoid with n turns per unit length carrying current I: B = μ₀ n I (uniform field along the axis; field outside is zero).

• Force on a Current-Carrying Conductor: A straight conductor of length L carrying current I in magnetic field B experiences force: F = I (L × B) = I L B sin θ.

• Force Between Two Parallel Current-Carrying Wires: Force per unit length between two long parallel conductors separated by distance d carrying currents I₁ and I₂:

  - Formula: f = F / L = (μ₀ I₁ I₂) / (2π d).

  - Nature of Force: Currents in same direction attract; currents in opposite directions repel.

• Definition of 1 Ampere: Constant current which, when flowing through two parallel conductors of infinite length placed 1 meter apart in vacuum, produces force of 2 × 10⁻⁷ N/m per unit length.

4. Torque on Current Loop & Moving Coil Galvanometer

Short Note:

• Magnetic Dipole Moment of Loop (m): m = N I A (vector normal to plane of loop). SI unit is A·m² or J/T.

• Torque on Current Loop: When placed in uniform magnetic field: τ = m × B = N I A B sin θ.

• Moving Coil Galvanometer (MCG): Instrument used to detect and measure small currents.

  - Working Principle: Torque acts on current-carrying coil placed in radial magnetic field: τ_deflecting = N I A B.

  - Restoring Torque: τ_restoring = k θ => Deflection θ = (N A B / k) × I.

  - Current Sensitivity (S_i): Deflection per unit current: S_i = θ / I = (N A B) / k.

  - Voltage Sensitivity (S_v): Deflection per unit voltage: S_v = θ / V = (N A B) / (k R).

• Conversion of Galvanometer:

  - Into Ammeter: Connect a small resistance (Shunt, S) in parallel: S = (I_g G) / (I - I_g).

  - Into Voltmeter: Connect a high resistance (R) in series: R = (V / I_g) - G.

Intext & Conceptual Questions

Q1: Why is a radial magnetic field employed in a moving coil galvanometer? How is it produced?
Answer:
• Purpose: A radial magnetic field ensures that the magnetic field lines are always parallel to the plane of the coil (θ = 90° between normal to coil plane and magnetic field) regardless of coil rotation. This keeps deflecting torque maximum and constant (τ = N I A B), leading to a linear scale where deflection is directly proportional to current (θ ∝ I).
• Production: It is produced by using concave pole pieces of a permanent magnet along with a soft iron core placed at the center of the coil.
Q2: Explain why two parallel conductors carrying current in the same direction attract each other, whereas two static point charges of the same sign repel each other.
Answer:
• Static charges interact via electrostatic forces described by Coulomb's Law, where like charges repel each other.
• Parallel current-carrying wires interact via magnetic forces. Wire 1 produces a magnetic field at Wire 2 given by Right-Hand Thumb Rule. Applying Fleming's Left-Hand Rule to Wire 2 shows that the magnetic force points toward Wire 1. By symmetry, Wire 1 is also pulled toward Wire 2. Thus, parallel currents flowing in the same direction exert mutual attractive magnetic forces.

Textbook Chapter End Exercise Questions

Q1: A circular coil of wire consisting of 100 turns, each of radius 8.0 cm carries a current of 0.40 A. What is the magnitude of the magnetic field B at the centre of the coil?
Answer:
Given:
• Number of turns (N) = 100
• Radius of coil (R) = 8.0 cm = 0.08 m
• Current (I) = 0.40 A
• Permittivity constant (μ₀) = 4π × 10⁻⁷ T·m/A

Formula for magnetic field at center of circular coil:
B = (μ₀ N I) / (2 R)

Calculation:
B = (4π × 10⁻⁷ × 100 × 0.40) / (2 × 0.08)
B = (4 × 3.1416 × 10⁻⁷ × 40) / 0.16
B = (502.65 × 10⁻⁷) / 0.16 = 3.14 × 10⁻⁴ T

The magnitude of the magnetic field at the centre of the coil is 3.14 × 10⁻⁴ T.
Q2: Distinguish between Conversion of Galvanometer into Ammeter and Voltmeter.
Answer:
Feature Conversion to Ammeter Conversion to Voltmeter
Added Resistance Very small resistance (Shunt, S). Very high resistance (R).
Connection Type Connected in parallel with coil. Connected in series with coil.
Formula S = (I_g G) / (I - I_g) R = (V / I_g) - G
Circuit Connection Connected in series in main circuit. Connected in parallel across component.
Ideal Value Ideal Ammeter resistance = 0. Ideal Voltmeter resistance = ∞.

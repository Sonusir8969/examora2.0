Ch-3: Discovering Tut: the Saga Continues 30 Questions

1. Who is the author of 'Discovering Tut: the Saga Continues'?

  • A. A. R. Williams
  • B. Gordon Cook
  • C. Shirley Toulson
  • D. Nick Middleton

Answer: A. A. R. Williams

Explanation: The chapter was written by A. R. Williams detailing the forensic examination of King Tutankhamun.

2. Who was King Tutankhamun?

  • A. The last heir of a powerful Egyptian pharaoh family
  • B. A famous Greek philosopher
  • C. A Roman emperor
  • D. A British archaeologist

Answer: A. The last heir of a powerful Egyptian pharaoh family

Explanation: King Tut was the last ruler of a powerful dynasty that had ruled Egypt for centuries.

3. How old was King Tut approximately when he died?

  • A. A teenager
  • B. An old man in his 70s
  • C. A middle-aged ruler in his 40s
  • D. A young child of 5 years

Answer: A. A teenager

Explanation: Tut was just a teenager when he died, bringing an end to his famous royal lineage.

4. Who discovered King Tut's tomb in 1922?

  • A. Howard Carter
  • B. Zahi Hawass
  • C. Amenhotep III
  • D. Lord Carnarvon

Answer: A. Howard Carter

Explanation: British archaeologist Howard Carter discovered King Tut's intact tomb in 1922.

5. In which year was King Tut's mummy taken for a CT scan?

  • A. 2005
  • B. 1922
  • C. 1968
  • D. 2000

Answer: A. 2005

Explanation: On January 5, 2005, Tut's body was taken for a CT scan to resolve mysteries surrounding his death.

6. What was unique about King Tut's CT scan in Egyptian history?

  • A. He was the first mummy to be CT scanned
  • B. He was scanned using underwater technology
  • C. He was scanned inside a royal palace
  • D. He was the oldest mummy ever found

Answer: A. He was the first mummy to be CT scanned

Explanation: King Tut was the first mummy undergo a CT scan under the Egyptian Mummy Project.

7. Who was Zahi Hawass in the context of the story?

  • A. Secretary General of Egypt's Supreme Council of Antiquities
  • B. The British archaeologist who found the tomb
  • C. The driver of the CT scan trailer
  • D. A descendant of Amenhotep IV

Answer: A. Secretary General of Egypt's Supreme Council of Antiquities

Explanation: Zahi Hawass oversaw the 2005 CT scanning project of King Tut's mummy.

8. What major discovery was made by an anatomy professor in 1968 regarding Tut's mummy?

  • A. His breastbone and front ribs were missing
  • B. His skull was fractured
  • C. He had a golden heart
  • D. His left leg was artificial

Answer: A. His breastbone and front ribs were missing

Explanation: An X-ray in 1968 revealed that Tut's breastbone and front ribs were missing under the resin layer.

9. Why was Howard Carter forced to cut Tut's mummy into pieces?

  • A. The ritual resins had hardened, cementing Tut to the solid gold coffin
  • B. The coffin was too small
  • C. To steal the golden artifacts easily
  • D. The mummy was too heavy to carry whole

Answer: A. The ritual resins had hardened, cementing Tut to the solid gold coffin

Explanation: The hardened ritual resins fused the mummy solidly to the bottom of his gold coffin.

10. What method did Carter first attempt to loosen the hardened resins?

  • A. Setting the mummy in scorching desert sun
  • B. Washing it with warm water
  • C. Melting it with fire
  • D. Using chemical solvents

Answer: A. Setting the mummy in scorching desert sun

Explanation: Carter placed the mummy outside in the sun, heating it to 149 degrees Fahrenheit, but it failed to loosen the resin.

11. Where is King Tut's tomb located?

  • A. Valley of the Kings
  • B. Valley of the Queens
  • C. Giza Plateau
  • D. Alexandria

Answer: A. Valley of the Kings

Explanation: Tut's tomb lies deep underground in the ancient royal cemetery known as the Valley of the Kings.

12. What items were buried with King Tut for his afterlife?

  • A. Gold artifacts, board games, bronze razor, and fresh linen garments
  • B. Steel weapons and iron tools
  • C. Only religious books
  • D. Silver coins and paper documents

Answer: A. Gold artifacts, board games, bronze razor, and fresh linen garments

Explanation: Egyptians believed the dead needed everyday luxuries in the afterlife, so gold, games, food, and clothes were buried.

13. What flowers found in the shroud suggested that Tut was buried in March or April?

  • A. Willow, olive leaves, celery, lotus, and cornflowers
  • B. Roses and lilies
  • C. Sunflowers and marigolds
  • D. Jasmine and orchids

Answer: A. Willow, olive leaves, celery, lotus, and cornflowers

Explanation: These seasonal flora blossom in spring, indicating a burial in March or April.

14. What original name did King Tut hold before changing it?

  • A. Tutankhaten
  • B. Akhenaten
  • C. Amenhotep III
  • D. Smenkhkare

Answer: A. Tutankhaten

Explanation: His original name was Tutankhaten, meaning "living image of Aten".

15. What does the name 'Tutankhamun' mean?

  • A. Living image of Amun
  • B. Son of the Sun God
  • C. King of Gold
  • D. Ruler of Egypt

Answer: A. Living image of Amun

Explanation: Tutankhamun translates to "living image of Amun", reflecting his restoration of the traditional god Amun.

16. Which pharaoh initiated one of the strangest periods in ancient Egyptian history?

  • A. Amenhotep IV (Akhenaten)
  • B. Amenhotep III
  • C. Tutankhamun
  • D. Smenkhkare

Answer: A. Amenhotep IV (Akhenaten)

Explanation: Amenhotep IV promoted the worship of Aten, changed his name to Akhenaten, and caused religious upheaval.

17. Which god's temples were smashed and closed during Akhenaten's reign?

  • A. Amun
  • B. Osiris
  • C. Ra
  • D. Anubis

Answer: A. Amun

Explanation: Akhenaten shocked the nation by attacking Amun, a major god, smashing his images and closing temples.

18. What new religious capital did Akhenaten establish?

  • A. Akhetaten (Amarna)
  • B. Thebes
  • C. Cairo
  • D. Memphis

Answer: A. Akhetaten (Amarna)

Explanation: He moved the religious capital from Thebes to the new city of Akhetaten, known now as Amarna.

19. How did Ray Johnson describe Akhenaten's reign?

  • A. Wacky and turbulent
  • B. Peaceful and golden
  • C. Weak and insignificant
  • D. Highly prosperous

Answer: A. Wacky and turbulent

Explanation: Ray Johnson, director of the University of Chicago research centre, described the period as "wacky".

20. What technical snag happened during the CT scan of King Tut's mummy?

  • A. The scanner's cooling fan stopped working due to sand
  • B. Power outage in the entire town
  • C. The scanner bed broke down
  • D. The computer monitor failed

Answer: A. The scanner's cooling fan stopped working due to sand

Explanation: Sand got into a cooler fan, temporarily stopping the scanner until spare plastic fans were brought in.

21. What did workers jokingly say when the CT scanner fan stopped?

  • A. "Curse of the Pharaoh"
  • B. "Tut's revenge"
  • C. "Bad luck of Egypt"
  • D. "Mummy's power"

Answer: A. "Curse of the Pharaoh"

Explanation: A guard nervously joked about the "curse of the pharaoh" falling upon those who disturbed his peace.

22. How many digital X-ray images were created during Tut's CT scan?

  • A. 1,700 digital X-ray images
  • B. 500 digital X-ray images
  • C. 2,500 digital X-ray images
  • D. 100 digital X-ray images

Answer: A. 1,700 digital X-ray images

Explanation: The CT machine generated 1,700 cross-sectional digital X-ray images of Tut's body.

23. Who donated the portable CT scanner machine for the project?

  • A. National Geographic Society and Siemens
  • B. Oxford University
  • C. Egyptian Antiquities Department
  • D. British Museum

Answer: A. National Geographic Society and Siemens

Explanation: The portable CT scanner was manufactured and donated by Siemens along with National Geographic.

24. What constellation watched over King Tut's resting place after the scan ended?

  • A. Orion
  • B. Ursa Major
  • C. Cassiopeia
  • D. Scorpius

Answer: A. Orion

Explanation: Orion, known to ancient Egyptians as the soul of Osiris (god of the afterlife), shone in the sky above his tomb.

25. What was Zahi Hawass's reaction after the CT scan was successfully completed?

  • A. He felt relieved and stated he could finally sleep
  • B. He felt disappointed with results
  • C. He ordered more scans
  • D. He criticized Howard Carter

Answer: A. He felt relieved and stated he could finally sleep

Explanation: Hawass admitted he hadn't slept for nights worrying about the mummy, but relieved that nothing went wrong.

26. How long did the actual CT scan process take inside the trailer?

  • A. Less than 3 hours
  • B. 12 hours
  • C. Whole day
  • D. 30 minutes

Answer: A. Less than 3 hours

Explanation: Less than three hours after he was removed from his coffin, Tut was returned to his tomb.

27. What are the two biggest unresolved questions regarding King Tut?

  • A. How did he die and how old was he at death?
  • B. Where was he born and who was his mother?
  • C. How much gold was in his tomb and where was it sold?
  • D. Who built his pyramid and when?

Answer: A. How did he die and how old was he at death?

Explanation: Modern science aims to answer two key questions: his exact cause of death and his precise age at death.

28. What was King Tut's coffin made of?

  • A. Solid gold
  • B. Pure silver
  • C. Carved teak wood
  • D. Polished copper

Answer: A. Solid gold

Explanation: King Tut's innermost coffin was crafted entirely from solid gold.

29. What shift occurred in archaeology over the decades as illustrated in the chapter?

  • A. From focusing on treasure to focusing on details of life and mysteries of death
  • B. From computer technology to manual digging
  • C. From scientific testing to treasure hunting
  • D. From studying mummies to ignoring them

Answer: A. From focusing on treasure to focusing on details of life and mysteries of death

Explanation: Modern archaeology relies on sophisticated diagnostic technology like CT scans rather than just hunting treasures.

30. Why is King Tut's death considered a grand event?

  • A. He was the last of his family line, ending a major royal dynasty
  • B. He died in a battle against Romans
  • C. He was the oldest king of Egypt
  • D. He ruled for 100 years

Answer: A. He was the last of his family line, ending a major royal dynasty

Explanation: Tut's demise marked the end of a powerful dynasty that ruled Egypt for centuries.

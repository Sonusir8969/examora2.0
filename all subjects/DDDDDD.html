Ch-9: Manufacturing Industries 30 Questions

1. Production of goods in large quantities after processing from raw materials to more valuable products is called:

  • A. Manufacturing
  • B. Agriculture
  • C. Mining
  • D. Service

Answer: A. Manufacturing

Explanation: Manufacturing belongs to the secondary sector, converting raw materials into valuable finished goods in large quantities.

2. Manufacturing sector is considered the backbone of development in general and economic development in particular because it belongs to which economic sector?

  • A. Secondary Sector
  • B. Primary Sector
  • C. Tertiary Sector
  • D. Quaternary Sector

Answer: A. Secondary Sector

Explanation: Manufacturing processes raw materials into finished products and is the primary component of the secondary sector.

3. When many industries tend to come together to make use of the advantages offered by the urban centers, it is known as:

  • A. Agglomeration economies
  • B. Scale of production
  • C. Industrial estate
  • D. Joint venture

Answer: A. Agglomeration economies

Explanation: Agglomeration economies occur when industries group near cities to benefit from banking, transport, labor, and infrastructure.

4. Which of the following is an agro-based industry?

  • A. Cotton textile
  • B. Iron and steel
  • C. Cement
  • D. Petrochemicals

Answer: A. Cotton textile

Explanation: Agro-based industries rely on agricultural raw materials such as cotton, wool, jute, silk, rubber, sugar, tea, and coffee.

5. Industries that use minerals and metals as raw materials are known as:

  • A. Mineral-based industries
  • B. Agro-based industries
  • C. Pastoral industries
  • D. Forest-based industries

Answer: A. Mineral-based industries

Explanation: Mineral-based industries use metallic and non-metallic minerals like iron ore, limestone, and bauxite as raw materials.

6. Industries which supply their products as raw materials to manufacture other goods are called:

  • A. Basic or Key industries
  • B. Consumer industries
  • C. Small-scale industries
  • D. Cottage industries

Answer: A. Basic or Key industries

Explanation: Basic industries (like iron and steel, copper smelting, and aluminium smelting) provide basic inputs to build other machines and products.

7. Which of the following is a basic or heavy industry?

  • A. Iron and Steel industry
  • B. Sugar industry
  • C. Toothpaste manufacturing
  • D. Paper industry

Answer: A. Iron and Steel industry

Explanation: The Iron and Steel industry is the basic industry since all other industries—heavy, medium, and light—depend on it for machinery.

8. Industries owned and operated by government agencies, such as BHEL and SAIL, are classified as:

  • A. Public sector industries
  • B. Private sector industries
  • C. Joint sector industries
  • D. Cooperative sector industries

Answer: A. Public sector industries

Explanation: Public sector enterprises are owned and operated by state or central government organizations (e.g., BHEL, SAIL).

9. Oil India Limited (OIL) is an example of which type of industry classification based on ownership?

  • A. Joint sector industry
  • B. Private sector industry
  • C. Public sector industry
  • D. Cooperative sector industry

Answer: A. Joint sector industry

Explanation: Joint sector industries are jointly run by the state and individuals or a group of individuals (e.g., Oil India Ltd.).

10. Amul and sugar mills in Maharashtra are examples of which type of sector industry?

  • A. Cooperative sector
  • B. Private sector
  • C. Public sector
  • D. Joint sector

Answer: A. Cooperative sector

Explanation: Cooperative sector industries are owned and operated by the producers or suppliers of raw materials, workers, or both.

11. Where was the first successful textile mill established in India in 1854?

  • A. Mumbai
  • B. Kolkata
  • C. Ahmedabad
  • D. Chennai

Answer: A. Mumbai

Explanation: The first successful modern textile mill in India was established in Mumbai in 1854.

12. Where was the first jute mill established near Kolkata in 1855 at Rishra?

  • A. Rishra
  • B. Howrah
  • C. Hooghly
  • D. Murshidabad

Answer: A. Rishra

Explanation: The first jute mill was set up near Kolkata at Rishra in 1855.

13. Most of the jute mills in India are concentrated along the banks of which river in West Bengal?

  • A. Hooghly River
  • B. Damodar River
  • C. Padma River
  • D. Brahmaputra River

Answer: A. Hooghly River

Explanation: The jute industry is concentrated along the Hooghly river belt due to raw material availability, abundant water, and port facilities.

14. India is the second-largest producer of sugar in the world, but it ranks first in the production of:

  • A. Gur and Khandsari
  • B. Ethanol
  • C. Molasses
  • D. Refined White Sugar

Answer: A. Gur and Khandsari

Explanation: India stands second in world sugar production, but holds the first position in Gur and Khandsari production.

15. Why has there been a recent shift of sugar mills from North India to Southern and Western states like Maharashtra?

  • A. Higher sucrose content in cane and longer crushing season
  • B. Cheap land availability
  • C. Government compulsion
  • D. High rainfall throughout the year

Answer: A. Higher sucrose content in cane and longer crushing season

Explanation: Sugarcane produced in southern/western regions has higher sucrose content and the cooler climate provides a longer crushing season.

16. What ratio of Iron Ore, Coking Coal, and Limestone is required in the proportion to produce steel?

  • A. 4 : 2 : 1
  • B. 2 : 4 : 1
  • C. 1 : 2 : 4
  • D. 4 : 1 : 2

Answer: A. 4 : 2 : 1

Explanation: Production of steel requires iron ore, coking coal, and limestone in approximately 4 : 2 : 1 ratio.

17. Which region in India has the maximum concentration of iron and steel industries?

  • A. Chota Nagpur Plateau region
  • B. Malwa Plateau
  • C. Deccan Plateau
  • D. Northern Plains

Answer: A. Chota Nagpur Plateau region

Explanation: Chota Nagpur plateau region has the maximum concentration of iron and steel plants due to low-cost iron ore, high-grade raw materials, and proximity to markets.

18. Most public sector iron and steel plants market their steel through which public sector authority?

  • A. SAIL (Steel Authority of India Limited)
  • B. BHEL
  • C. TISCO
  • D. GAIL

Answer: A. SAIL (Steel Authority of India Limited)

Explanation: Almost all public sector steel plants market their product through Steel Authority of India Limited (SAIL).

19. What is the second most important metallurgical industry in India, light in weight, resistant to corrosion, and a good conductor of heat?

  • A. Aluminium Smelting
  • B. Copper Smelting
  • C. Zinc Smelting
  • D. Lead Smelting

Answer: A. Aluminium Smelting

Explanation: Aluminium smelting is the second most important metallurgical industry in India after steel.

20. What is the main raw material used in the aluminium smelting industry?

  • A. Bauxite
  • B. Hematite
  • C. Dolomite
  • D. Magnetite

Answer: A. Bauxite

Explanation: Bauxite is a bulky, dark reddish ore used as the primary raw material in aluminium smelters.

21. Inorganic chemicals industry includes manufacturing of Sulphuric Acid. What is Sulphuric acid used for?

  • A. Manufacturing fertilizers, synthetic fibers, and paints
  • B. Refining water
  • C. Cooking oils
  • D. Food preservation

Answer: A. Manufacturing fertilizers, synthetic fibers, and paints

Explanation: Sulphuric acid is used to produce fertilizers, synthetic fibers, plastics, paints, and dyestuffs.

22. The Fertilizer Industry in India is centered around the production of which primary nutrient that India lacks reserves for in terms of potash?

  • A. Nitrogenous fertilizers (Urea)
  • B. Phosphate fertilizers
  • C. Ammonium phosphate
  • D. Potassic fertilizers

Answer: A. Nitrogenous fertilizers (Urea)

Explanation: India is the third-largest producer of nitrogenous fertilizers. India imports all of its potash requirements as there are no commercial reserves.

23. Where was the first cement plant set up in India in 1904?

  • A. Chennai
  • B. Mumbai
  • C. Gujarat
  • D. Kolkata

Answer: A. Chennai

Explanation: The first cement plant in India was established in Chennai (then Madras) in 1904.

24. What raw materials are required for the Cement Industry?

  • A. Limestone, Silica, Alumina, and Gypsum
  • B. Iron ore, Coal, and Manganese
  • C. Copper, Lead, and Zinc
  • D. Bauxite, Mica, and Salt

Answer: A. Limestone, Silica, Alumina, and Gypsum

Explanation: Cement requires bulky raw materials like limestone, silica, alumina, and gypsum along with coal and electric power.

25. Which city has emerged as the 'Electronic Capital' of India?

  • A. Bengaluru
  • B. Hyderabad
  • C. Pune
  • D. Noida

Answer: A. Bengaluru

Explanation: Bengaluru has emerged as the electronic and IT capital of India.

26. Which type of pollution is caused by the presence of high proportion of undesirable gases like sulphur dioxide and carbon monoxide?

  • A. Air pollution
  • B. Water pollution
  • C. Noise pollution
  • D. Thermal pollution

Answer: A. Air pollution

Explanation: Air pollution is caused by high concentrations of toxic gases like sulfur dioxide and carbon monoxide released by smoke stacks.

27. Thermal pollution of water occurs when:

  • A. Hot water from factories and thermal plants is drained into rivers before cooling
  • B. Rainwater mixes with sewage
  • C. Oil spills happen in oceans
  • D. Garbage is dumped in canals

Answer: A. Hot water from factories and thermal plants is drained into rivers before cooling

Explanation: Thermal pollution occurs when hot water from factories and power stations is discharged into water bodies before cooling, damaging aquatic life.

28. Treatment of industrial effluents can be done in how many phases?

  • A. Three phases (Primary, Secondary, and Tertiary)
  • B. Two phases
  • C. Four phases
  • D. Five phases

Answer: A. Three phases (Primary, Secondary, and Tertiary)

Explanation: Effluent treatment includes Primary (mechanical), Secondary (biological), and Tertiary (biological, chemical, and physical recycling) processes.

29. Which organization in India has ISO certification for Environmental Management System (EMS 14001) for its power plants?

  • A. NTPC
  • B. BHEL
  • C. SAIL
  • D. ONGC

Answer: A. NTPC

Explanation: NTPC (National Thermal Power Corporation) has ISO certification 14001 for environmental management and sustainable power generation.

30. How can industrial noise pollution be effectively reduced inside factories?

  • A. Fitting generators with silencers and using earplugs/earphones
  • B. Increasing machine speed
  • C. Opening factory doors and windows
  • D. Running machines only at night

Answer: A. Fitting generators with silencers and using earplugs/earphones

Explanation: Fitting silencers, re-designing machinery, and providing ear protection to workers reduces noise pollution and health hazards.

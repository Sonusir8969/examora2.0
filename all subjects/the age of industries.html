Class 9 History

Chapter 7: The Age of Industrialisation 30 Questions

1. In which country did the Industrial Revolution first begin?

  • A. France
  • B. Germany
  • C. England
  • D. USA

Answer: C. England

Explanation: The Industrial Revolution began in Britain in the late 18th century due to abundant coal, iron ore, and political stability.

2. Who invented the Spinning Jenny?

  • A. James Hargreaves
  • B. James Watt
  • C. Richard Arkwright
  • D. George Stephenson

Answer: A. James Hargreaves

Explanation: James Hargreaves invented the Spinning Jenny in 1764, allowing a single worker to spin multiple threads at once.

3. Which energy source was the primary driver of the Industrial Revolution?

  • A. Water
  • B. Coal
  • C. Oil
  • D. Solar

Answer: B. Coal

Explanation: Coal was used to power the steam engines that drove factories, railways, and ships throughout the Industrial Revolution.

4. Who built the first successful steam engine?

  • A. James Watt
  • B. Thomas Newcomen
  • C. George Stephenson
  • D. Richard Trevithick

Answer: A. James Watt

Explanation: James Watt's improved steam engine in 1769 was highly efficient and widely adopted to run factory machines.

5. The 'Cottage Industry' was replaced by what?

  • A. Large factories
  • B. International trade
  • C. Small workshops
  • D. Agricultural farms

Answer: A. Large factories

Explanation: As machinery became expensive, production moved from small rural cottages to large centralized factories powered by steam.

6. Which of the following was a direct impact of industrialisation on workers?

  • A. Increased leisure time
  • B. Long working hours and poor wages
  • C. Free education
  • D. Better health care

Answer: B. Long working hours and poor wages

Explanation: Factory workers faced grueling shifts lasting up to 14-16 hours a day with extremely low wages and unsafe conditions.

7. The construction of railways in England was greatly aided by the invention of which engine?

  • A. The Steam Locomotive
  • B. The Diesel Engine
  • C. The Combustion Engine
  • D. The Electric Motor

Answer: A. The Steam Locomotive

Explanation: George Stephenson's steam locomotive "Rocket" in 1829 revolutionized railway transport, sparking a huge railway boom.

8. Who wrote the 'Communist Manifesto' in response to the industrial social conditions?

  • A. Karl Marx and Engels
  • B. Adam Smith
  • C. John Maynard Keynes
  • D. Max Weber

Answer: A. Karl Marx and Engels

Explanation: Witnessing the harsh exploitation of factory workers, Marx and Engels published the Communist Manifesto in 1848.

9. What were the main products of the 'First Industrial Revolution'?

  • A. Steel and chemicals
  • B. Textiles and iron
  • C. Petroleum and plastics
  • D. Electronics and computers

Answer: B. Textiles and iron

Explanation: The earliest phase of the revolution was heavily focused on the mechanization of cotton textiles and the mass production of iron.

10. Which act was passed in England to improve the horrid conditions of factories?

  • A. The Factory Act of 1819
  • B. The Reform Act of 1832
  • C. The Factory Act of 1833
  • D. The Education Act of 1870

Answer: C. The Factory Act of 1833

Explanation: The 1833 Factory Act was a landmark legislation that limited child labor to 9 hours a day and set safety standards.

11. The Industrial Revolution helped Britain become the world's first what?

  • A. Agricultural nation
  • B. Industrial power
  • C. Military dictatorship
  • D. Socialist state

Answer: B. Industrial power

Explanation: The massive industrial capacity gave Britain the nickname the "Workshop of the World" in the 19th century.

12. What was the main source of labor in early British factories?

  • A. Skilled craftsmen
  • B. Women and children
  • C. University graduates
  • D. Immigrants

Answer: B. Women and children

Explanation: Early factories heavily exploited women and children due to their cheap labor and small, nimble fingers for delicate textile work.

13. Which invention improved the production of iron in the 18th century?

  • A. The Spinning Mule
  • B. The Steam Hammer
  • C. The Blast Furnace
  • D. The Power Loom

Answer: C. The Blast Furnace

Explanation: The use of coke (refined coal) in blast furnaces allowed for the mass production of cheap, high-quality iron.

14. The process of urbanization during the Industrial Revolution meant what?

  • A. Movement of people to rural areas
  • B. Movement of people to cities
  • C. Decline of trade
  • D. Increase in agricultural jobs

Answer: B. Movement of people to cities

Explanation: Urbanization was the massive shift of population from rural agricultural villages to bustling industrial cities in search of work.

15. What was the purpose of the 'Luddite' movement?

  • A. To promote machinery
  • B. To destroy textile machines
  • C. To establish trade unions
  • D. To demand higher wages

Answer: B. To destroy textile machines

Explanation: The Luddites were skilled textile workers who smashed the machines they believed were replacing their jobs and lowering their wages.

16. Which country became the leading industrial power after Britain by the late 19th century?

  • A. France
  • B. USA and Germany
  • C. Russia
  • D. Japan

Answer: B. USA and Germany

Explanation: By the late 1800s, the USA and Germany had rapidly industrialized, surpassing Britain in steel and chemical production.

17. What major social class emerged during the Industrial Revolution?

  • A. The Aristocracy
  • B. The Middle Class
  • C. The Clergy
  • D. The Peasantry

Answer: B. The Middle Class

Explanation: A new industrial middle class, consisting of factory owners, bankers, and merchants, gained great wealth and social prominence.

18. Why was the location of early factories often near rivers?

  • A. For transportation of goods
  • B. To harness water power for machines
  • C. To obtain drinking water
  • D. To dispose of waste

Answer: B. To harness water power for machines

Explanation: Before widespread steam engines, factories relied on water wheels driven by river currents to power their textile machinery.

19. What was the impact of the Industrial Revolution on the global trade market?

  • A. Decreased global trade
  • B. Massively increased global trade
  • C. Created local barter systems
  • D. Ended international commerce

Answer: B. Massively increased global trade

Explanation: Mass-produced cheap goods flooded international markets, while raw materials were imported from colonies, creating global economic links.

20. Who wrote the influential book 'The Wealth of Nations' advocating free trade?

  • A. Thomas Malthus
  • B. Adam Smith
  • C. David Ricardo
  • D. John Locke

Answer: B. Adam Smith

Explanation: Adam Smith's 'The Wealth of Nations' (1776) argued for laissez-faire economics and the freedom of trade and industry.

21. The use of child labor in factories was finally restricted by legislation in which century?

  • A. 18th century
  • B. 19th century
  • C. 20th century
  • D. 17th century

Answer: B. 19th century

Explanation: The Factory Acts of the 19th century (starting in 1833) progressively restricted the working hours and age for child laborers.

22. The industrial revolution contributed to a huge expansion of which mineral?

  • A. Copper
  • B. Gold
  • C. Coal
  • D. Silver

Answer: C. Coal

Explanation: The rapid expansion of steam engines and railways created a massive demand for coal, making it the driving fuel of the era.

23. A major environmental consequence of early industrialisation was what?

  • A. Cleaner rivers
  • B. Severe air and water pollution
  • C. Decreased forest fires
  • D. Increased wildlife population

Answer: B. Severe air and water pollution

Explanation: Factories belched black smoke into the air and dumped toxic chemicals and waste into rivers, creating highly polluted urban centers.

24. What type of manufacturing process did 'Mass Production' rely on?

  • A. Custom handcrafted goods
  • B. Standardized interchangeable parts
  • C. Local artisan workshops
  • D. Subsistence farming

Answer: B. Standardized interchangeable parts

Explanation: Mass production utilized standardized, interchangeable parts to make items quickly, cheaply, and uniformly in large quantities.

25. Who invented the Safety Lamp for coal miners?

  • A. James Watt
  • B. Humphrey Davy
  • C. George Stephenson
  • D. Richard Trevithick

Answer: B. Humphrey Davy

Explanation: Humphrey Davy invented the safety lamp in 1815, which prevented the flammable gas in coal mines from causing fatal explosions.

26. How did the Industrial Revolution impact the class divide in England?

  • A. It decreased the class divide
  • B. It created a sharp divide between the rich industrialists and the poor workers
  • C. It established a middle-only society
  • D. It removed the monarchy

Answer: B. It created a sharp divide between the rich industrialists and the poor workers

Explanation: The immense wealth of the bourgeoisie contrasted starkly with the squalid poverty of the industrial working class (proletariat).

27. The first successful steamboat was invented by whom?

  • A. James Watt
  • B. Robert Fulton
  • C. George Stephenson
  • D. Thomas Savery

Answer: B. Robert Fulton

Explanation: Robert Fulton successfully launched the first commercially viable steamboat, the Clermont, on the Hudson River in 1807.

28. What key factor turned Britain into a wealthy industrial leader?

  • A. Extensive agricultural lands
  • B. Huge reserves of coal and iron ore
  • C. Skilled painters
  • D. Vast gold mines

Answer: B. Huge reserves of coal and iron ore

Explanation: Abundant domestic coal and iron deposits allowed Britain to fuel its factories and build massive machinery without relying on imports.

29. What is the term for the movement of workers organizing themselves for better rights?

  • A. Feudalism
  • B. Trade Unionism
  • C. Slavery
  • D. Mercantilism

Answer: B. Trade Unionism

Explanation: Trade Unions were formal associations formed by workers to collectively bargain for better wages, shorter hours, and safer conditions.

30. The textile industry heavily relied on raw cotton imported from which country?

  • A. Canada
  • B. India
  • C. Australia
  • D. South Africa

Answer: B. India

Explanation: British textile mills imported vast quantities of raw cotton from colonial India to produce cheap cloth for global markets.

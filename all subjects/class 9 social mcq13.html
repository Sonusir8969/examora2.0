Chapter 13: Constitutional Design 30 Questions

1. Who became the first democratically elected President of South Africa in 1994?

  • A. Desmond Tutu
  • B. Nelson Mandela
  • C. F.W. de Klerk
  • D. Steve Biko

Answer: B. Nelson Mandela

Explanation: Nelson Mandela, leader of the African National Congress, became South Africa's first democratically elected President in 1994 after decades of struggle against apartheid.

2. What was the racist system of discrimination practised in South Africa before 1994 called?

  • A. Segregation Act
  • B. Apartheid
  • C. Jim Crow Law
  • D. Colonial Rule

Answer: B. Apartheid

Explanation: Apartheid was the official policy of racial discrimination followed by the white minority government of South Africa until it ended in the early 1990s.

3. What is a 'constitution'?

  • A. A set of unwritten customs only
  • B. The supreme law that lays down the fundamental principles for governing a country
  • C. A political party manifesto
  • D. A single act passed by parliament each year

Answer: B. The supreme law that lays down the fundamental principles for governing a country

Explanation: A constitution is the fundamental law of a country that determines the relationship between the government and its citizens and among different organs of government.

4. Who is regarded as the Chairman of the Drafting Committee of the Indian Constitution?

  • A. Jawaharlal Nehru
  • B. Dr. B.R. Ambedkar
  • C. Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel
  • D. Rajendra Prasad

Answer: B. Dr. B.R. Ambedkar

Explanation: Dr. B.R. Ambedkar chaired the Drafting Committee responsible for preparing the draft of the Indian Constitution.

5. The body that drafted the Constitution of India is known as what?

  • A. The Constituent Assembly
  • B. The Parliament
  • C. The Supreme Court Bench
  • D. The Union Cabinet

Answer: A. The Constituent Assembly

Explanation: The Constituent Assembly, elected for the purpose of drafting a constitution, framed the Constitution of India between 1946 and 1949.

6. On what date did the Indian Constitution come into effect?

  • A. 15 August 1947
  • B. 26 January 1950
  • C. 26 November 1949
  • D. 2 October 1950

Answer: B. 26 January 1950

Explanation: Although the Constitution was adopted on 26 November 1949, it came into force on 26 January 1950, celebrated today as Republic Day.

7. Which word in the Preamble means that the government does not favour any particular religion?

  • A. Sovereign
  • B. Secular
  • C. Republic
  • D. Socialist

Answer: B. Secular

Explanation: 'Secular' in the Preamble means the Indian state treats all religions equally and does not officially promote any one religion.

8. What does the term 'Sovereign' in the Preamble signify?

  • A. India is ruled by a foreign power
  • B. India is internally and externally free to take its own decisions
  • C. India is governed by the United Nations
  • D. India has no independent army

Answer: B. India is internally and externally free to take its own decisions

Explanation: Sovereign means India is fully independent, with the authority to govern itself without external interference.

9. What does 'Republic' mean as stated in the Preamble to the Indian Constitution?

  • A. The head of state is a hereditary monarch
  • B. The head of state is elected, not hereditary
  • C. The country has no head of state
  • D. The head of state is appointed by the UN

Answer: B. The head of state is elected, not hereditary

Explanation: A republic means the head of the state, the President, is elected either directly or indirectly for a fixed term rather than being a hereditary post.

10. Which document guarantees Fundamental Rights to citizens of India?

  • A. The Preamble alone
  • B. The Constitution of India
  • C. State legislative acts only
  • D. Executive orders of the Prime Minister

Answer: B. The Constitution of India

Explanation: The Constitution of India, in Part III, guarantees a set of Fundamental Rights to all citizens.

11. Who is often called the 'Father of the Indian Constitution'?

  • A. Mahatma Gandhi
  • B. Dr. B.R. Ambedkar
  • C. Jawaharlal Nehru
  • D. Sardar Patel

Answer: B. Dr. B.R. Ambedkar

Explanation: Dr. B.R. Ambedkar's crucial role as Chairman of the Drafting Committee has led to him being widely referred to as the Father of the Indian Constitution.

12. What did South Africa's new constitution guarantee to protect the rights of the white minority after apartheid ended?

  • A. Denial of all rights to the minority
  • B. Extensive rights and safeguards to the white minority along with the black majority
  • C. Exile of the white population
  • D. A separate parallel government for whites

Answer: B. Extensive rights and safeguards to the white minority along with the black majority

Explanation: The new South African constitution guaranteed extensive rights to all citizens including the white minority, as part of a broader reconciliation process.

13. Why is a constitution generally considered necessary for a country?

  • A. It has no real use
  • B. It provides a set of shared norms that citizens broadly agree upon
  • C. It benefits only the ruling party
  • D. It replaces the need for any government

Answer: B. It provides a set of shared norms that citizens broadly agree upon

Explanation: A constitution establishes the fundamental rules of governance that most citizens accept, providing stability and a shared basis for political life.

14. Which of the following is one of the functions a constitution performs?

  • A. It specifies how the government will be constituted
  • B. It bans all political parties
  • C. It removes the need for elections
  • D. It eliminates the judiciary

Answer: A. It specifies how the government will be constituted

Explanation: One of the key functions of a constitution is to lay down who will have the power to make decisions in a society and how the government will be formed.

15. What is the process called by which changes are made to the Constitution?

  • A. Repeal
  • B. Amendment
  • C. Nullification
  • D. Suspension

Answer: B. Amendment

Explanation: An amendment is the formal process through which provisions of the Constitution can be added, changed or removed as per laid-down procedures.

16. Which is regarded as the guiding spirit or soul of the Indian Constitution?

  • A. The Preamble
  • B. The Seventh Schedule
  • C. Article 370
  • D. The Directive Principles alone

Answer: A. The Preamble

Explanation: The Preamble is often described as the soul of the Constitution because it outlines its fundamental values and objectives.

17. The phrase 'We, the people of India' in the Preamble signifies what?

  • A. That the Constitution was imposed by a foreign ruler
  • B. That the ultimate authority to frame and adopt the Constitution rests with the people of India
  • C. That only the government drafted the Constitution
  • D. That the Constitution belongs to a single political party

Answer: B. That the ultimate authority to frame and adopt the Constitution rests with the people of India

Explanation: This phrase reflects the idea that the Constitution derives its authority from the people of India, who adopted it through their representatives.

18. Why did the makers of the Indian Constitution choose universal adult franchise?

  • A. To give voting rights only to property owners
  • B. To ensure that every adult citizen, regardless of caste, religion, or gender, has the right to vote
  • C. To restrict voting to men only
  • D. To restrict voting rights to the educated class

Answer: B. To ensure that every adult citizen, regardless of caste, religion, or gender, has the right to vote

Explanation: Universal adult franchise was adopted to guarantee political equality by granting every adult citizen the right to vote irrespective of background.

19. The Indian Constitution declares India to be a 'Union of States' meaning what?

  • A. States can leave the Union at will
  • B. India is a federation of states that together form the nation and cannot separate from it
  • C. States have no relation with the central government
  • D. Only one state governs the entire country

Answer: B. India is a federation of states that together form the nation and cannot separate from it

Explanation: Describing India as a 'Union of States' emphasises that the states form an indestructible union and cannot secede from the country.

20. Which value in the Preamble refers to reducing inequality and ensuring social and economic justice?

  • A. Socialist
  • B. Secular
  • C. Sovereign
  • D. Republic

Answer: A. Socialist

Explanation: The term 'Socialist' in the Preamble reflects the commitment to reducing inequality and ensuring social and economic justice for all citizens.

21. In the South African case, what was significant about how the new constitution was made?

  • A. It was drafted by only one political party without consultation
  • B. It was drafted through negotiation and consensus among various political groups after apartheid
  • C. It was written entirely by a foreign country
  • D. It excluded the black majority from participation

Answer: B. It was drafted through negotiation and consensus among various political groups after apartheid

Explanation: South Africa's constitution was framed after long negotiations among various political parties and groups, symbolising the country's transition to a democratic society.

22. What role did the African National Congress (ANC) play in South Africa's history?

  • A. It supported apartheid
  • B. It led the freedom struggle against apartheid
  • C. It was a foreign colonial power
  • D. It had no involvement in politics

Answer: B. It led the freedom struggle against apartheid

Explanation: The African National Congress, along with other organisations, led the long struggle against apartheid, eventually helping to establish a democratic South Africa.

23. Which of these best explains why constitutions typically cannot be changed easily by a simple majority?

  • A. Because they must remain flexible for everyday decisions
  • B. Because they lay down fundamental rules meant to have long-term stability
  • C. Because it is illegal to ever amend a constitution
  • D. Because constitutions do not need any amendment procedure

Answer: B. Because they lay down fundamental rules meant to have long-term stability

Explanation: Special and often more rigorous procedures are used for constitutional amendments to ensure that fundamental rules are not changed casually.

24. The Indian Constitution provides for which type of government structure?

  • A. Unitary government only
  • B. Federal government with a division of powers between the Centre and states
  • C. A purely presidential system with no Parliament
  • D. Direct rule by the judiciary

Answer: B. Federal government with a division of powers between the Centre and states

Explanation: India follows a federal structure where powers are constitutionally divided between the central government and the state governments.

25. The idea that laws and the government must operate within limits set by the constitution is best described as what?

  • A. Constitutionalism
  • B. Autocracy
  • C. Absolutism
  • D. Colonialism

Answer: A. Constitutionalism

Explanation: Constitutionalism refers to the practice of a government limiting and exercising its power in accordance with the provisions of a written constitution.

26. Which of the following was a key demand of the freedom movement regarding future governance of India?

  • A. Continuation of colonial rule
  • B. A democratic constitution based on principles of freedom and equality
  • C. Restoration of monarchy
  • D. Rule by a foreign appointed council

Answer: B. A democratic constitution based on principles of freedom and equality

Explanation: The Indian freedom struggle envisioned an independent nation governed by a democratic constitution guaranteeing liberty and equality to all citizens.

27. Which of the following best defines 'Fundamental Rights' as provided in the Indian Constitution?

  • A. Rights that can be withdrawn at any time without procedure
  • B. Basic rights guaranteed to citizens that are enforceable by courts
  • C. Rights given only to government officials
  • D. Rights applicable only during emergencies

Answer: B. Basic rights guaranteed to citizens that are enforceable by courts

Explanation: Fundamental Rights are basic rights enshrined in the Constitution that citizens can enforce through the courts if violated.

28. Why is it important that the process of constitution-making involves wide participation and consensus?

  • A. To make the constitution acceptable and legitimate to most sections of society
  • B. To ensure it benefits only one community
  • C. To keep the process secretive
  • D. To avoid involving elected representatives

Answer: A. To make the constitution acceptable and legitimate to most sections of society

Explanation: Broad participation helps ensure the constitution reflects diverse viewpoints and is widely accepted as legitimate by different sections of society.

29. Which body in India has the power to interpret the Constitution?

  • A. The Election Commission
  • B. The judiciary, headed by the Supreme Court
  • C. The Council of Ministers
  • D. The Speaker of the Lok Sabha alone

Answer: B. The judiciary, headed by the Supreme Court

Explanation: The Supreme Court and the judiciary have the authority to interpret the provisions of the Constitution and settle disputes about its meaning.

30. What overall lesson does the South African example teach about constitution-making?

  • A. Constitutions work only in wealthy nations
  • B. Even deeply divided societies can create a shared constitution through negotiation and mutual trust
  • C. A constitution is unnecessary once independence is achieved
  • D. Constitutions should be imposed by force

Answer: B. Even deeply divided societies can create a shared constitution through negotiation and mutual trust

Explanation: South Africa's transition shows that even a society divided by decades of racial conflict could arrive at a widely accepted constitution through dialogue and compromise.

NCERT Solutions for Class 11 Biology Chapter 7 Structural Organisation in Animals

NCERT Solutions for Class 11 Biology Chapter 7 Structural Organisation in Animals provides detailed notes and answers on animal tissues (Epithelial, Connective, Muscular, Neural) and the organ system morphology and anatomy of representative organisms like the Frog (Rana tigrina).

Overview of Topics & Subtopics (Click to Jump)

  • 2. Connective Tissue (Loose, Dense & Specialized)

1. Epithelial Tissue

Short Note:

• Epithelial tissue forms protective coverings and inner linings of body surfaces, cavities, and organs with tightly packed cells and minimal intercellular matrix.

• Simple Epithelium: Single layer of cells functioning in absorption, secretion, and filtration.

  - Squamous Epithelium: Flattened cells with irregular boundaries (e.g., blood vessel walls, air sacs of lungs).

  - Cuboidal Epithelium: Cube-like cells (e.g., ducts of glands, nephrons of kidneys for secretion/absorption).

  - Columnar Epithelium: Tall and slender cells with basally located nuclei (e.g., lining of stomach and intestine).

  - Ciliated Epithelium: Columnar/cuboidal cells bearing cilia on free surface to move particles/mucus in a specific direction (e.g., bronchioles, fallopian tubes).

• Compound Epithelium: Multi-layered epithelium providing protection against mechanical and chemical stress (e.g., skin surface, pharynx, esophagus lining).

• Cell Junctions: Tight junctions (prevent leakage), Adhering junctions (cement neighboring cells), Gap junctions (facilitate rapid intercellular transport).

2. Connective Tissue

Short Note:

• Most abundant and widely distributed tissue in complex animals; binds, supports, and connects body organs.

• Loose Connective Tissue: Cells (fibroblasts, macrophages, mast cells) and fibers arranged loosely in ground substance.

  - Areolar Tissue: Serves as a support framework beneath the skin.

  - Adipose Tissue: Specialized for fat storage beneath skin and around internal organs.

• Dense Connective Tissue: Compactly packed fibers and fibroblasts.

  - Dense Regular: Parallel bundle fibers, e.g., Tendons (connect muscle to bone) and Ligaments (connect bone to bone).

  - Dense Irregular: Fibers oriented in various directions, found in the skin dermis.

• Specialized Connective Tissue:

  - Cartilage: Solid, pliable matrix containing chondrocytes (e.g., tip of nose, outer ear, intervertebral joints).

  - Bone: Hard, non-pliable matrix rich in calcium salts and collagen fibers containing osteocytes in lacunae.

  - Blood: Fluid matrix (plasma) containing RBCs, WBCs, and platelets.

3. Muscular and Neural Tissues

Short Note:

• Muscular Tissue: Made of long, contractile muscle fibers containing myofibrils (actin and myosin filaments) that enable movement.

  - Skeletal Muscle: Striated, voluntary, multinucleated; attached to skeletal bones.

  - Smooth Muscle: Non-striated, involuntary, spindle-shaped (fusiform); found in walls of internal organs like gut and blood vessels.

  - Cardiac Muscle: Striated, involuntary, branched cells joined by intercalated discs; found exclusively in the heart wall.

• Neural Tissue: Exerts greatest control over body response to changing environments.

  - Neurons: Excitable functional units that transmit electrical impulses.

  - Neuroglial Cells: Non-excitable supporting cells that protect, nourish, and form more than half the volume of neural tissue.

4. Structural Organisation of Organisms (Frog - Rana tigrina)

Short Note:

• Morphology: Amphibian adapted for water and land. Body divided into Head and Trunk (neck and tail absent). Skin is smooth, moist, and scaleless with cutaneous respiration capability.

• Anatomy & Systems:

  - Digestive System: Complete alimentary canal; short intestine (carnivorous diet); liver produces bile, pancreas produces digestive enzymes.

  - Respiration: Cutaneous (skin) in water; Pulmonary (lungs) and Buccopharyngeal cavity on land.

  - Circulatory System: Closed, 3-chambered heart (2 atria + 1 ventricle) with Sinus Venosus and Truncus Arteriosus. RBCs are nucleated.

  - Excretory System: Pair of kidneys, ureters, urinary bladder, opening into Cloaca. Ureotelic animal.

  - Reproduction: External fertilization in water. Development includes a indirect larval stage (Tadpole larva undergoing metamorphosis).

Intext & Conceptual Questions

Q1: What are the main types of cell junctions present in epithelial tissues, and what are their functions?
Answer:
Three main types of cell junctions are present in epithelial and other animal tissues:

1. Tight Junctions: Prevent substances from leaking across a tissue layer by tightly sealing adjacent cell membranes.
2. Adhering Junctions: Perform cementing functions to keep neighboring cells firmly anchored together.
3. Gap Junctions: Facilitate rapid communication between cells by connecting the cytoplasm of adjoining cells for quick transport of ions, small molecules, and second messengers.

Textbook Chapter End Exercise Questions

Q1: Differentiate between Skeletal, Smooth, and Cardiac muscle fibers based on structural and functional characteristics.
Answer:
Feature Skeletal Muscle Smooth Muscle Cardiac Muscle
Shape & Structure Cylindrical, unbranched fibers. Spindle-shaped (fusiform), unbranched. Cylindrical, branched fibers.
Striations Present (alternate dark & light bands). Absent (smooth appearance). Present (faint striations).
Nucleus Multinucleated (peripherally located). Uninucleated (centrally located). Uninucleated (centrally located).
Control Voluntary. Involuntary. Involuntary.
Intercalated Discs Absent. Absent. Present (communication junctions).
Q2: Distinguish between Tendon and Ligament.
Answer:
Feature Tendon Ligament
Connection Attaches skeletal muscle to bone. Attaches one bone to another bone.
Fiber Composition Made primarily of tough white collagen fibers. Made of elastic yellow fibers and collagen.
Flexibility & Strength Inelastic with high tensile strength. Flexible and elastic in nature.

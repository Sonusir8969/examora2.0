NCERT Solutions for Class 11 Chemistry Chapter 8 Organic Chemistry: Basic Principles & Techniques

NCERT Solutions for Class 11 Chemistry Chapter 8 Organic Chemistry: Some Basic Principles and Techniques covers classification and IUPAC nomenclature of organic compounds, electronic effects, types of organic reactions, and methods of purification and qualitative analysis.

1. Classification and Nomenclature of Organic Compounds

Short Note:

• Organic compounds are classified based on structure into acyclic (open chain) and cyclic (closed chain) compounds, and further into homologous series based on functional groups.

• IUPAC nomenclature follows systematic rules using a root word (indicating number of carbon atoms), primary suffix (indicating saturation), and secondary suffix/prefix (indicating functional groups).

2. Electronic Displacement Effects

Short Note:

• Inductive Effect: the permanent displacement of a sigma bond electron pair towards a more electronegative atom, decreasing with distance along the chain.

• Resonance Effect: delocalisation of pi electrons or lone pairs, represented by multiple contributing structures (resonance structures) of a molecule.

• Electromeric Effect: temporary effect seen in multiple bonds in the presence of an attacking reagent. Hyperconjugation: delocalisation of electrons in a C–H sigma bond adjacent to a positively charged carbon or a multiple bond.

3. Types of Organic Reactions and Reactive Intermediates

Short Note:

• Common reaction types: substitution, addition, elimination, and rearrangement reactions.

• Key reactive intermediates: carbocations (positively charged carbon), carbanions (negatively charged carbon), and free radicals (unpaired electron species).

4. Purification and Qualitative Analysis Techniques

Short Note:

• Purification methods include crystallisation, sublimation, distillation, differential extraction, and chromatography (for separating mixtures based on differing physical/chemical properties).

• Qualitative tests: Lassaigne's Test is used to detect nitrogen, sulphur, and halogens in an organic compound by converting them into ionic forms via fusion with sodium metal.

NCERT Textbook Exercise Questions

Write the IUPAC name of CH3-CH2-CHO.
Answer: This compound is propanal (an aldehyde with 3 carbon atoms), named using the suffix '-al' for aldehydes.
Explain the inductive effect with the example of chloroacetic acid being a stronger acid than acetic acid.
Answer: The chlorine atom in chloroacetic acid (ClCH2COOH) is electronegative and exerts a −I (electron-withdrawing) inductive effect, which stabilises the conjugate base (carboxylate ion) by dispersing the negative charge, making it easier to lose H+. This makes chloroacetic acid a stronger acid than acetic acid, which lacks this effect.
What is the principle behind the technique of steam distillation?
Answer: Steam distillation is used to purify substances that are steam-volatile and immiscible with water; the substance co-distils with steam at a temperature below the boiling point of water because the total vapour pressure (sum of the two components' vapour pressures) reaches atmospheric pressure sooner than either component alone would.
How is nitrogen detected in an organic compound by Lassaigne's Test?
Answer: The organic compound is fused with sodium metal to convert nitrogen into sodium cyanide (NaCN). This extract is then treated with freshly prepared FeSO4, followed by acidification with dilute H2SO4; a Prussian blue colouration confirms the presence of nitrogen.
Differentiate between homolytic and heterolytic fission of a covalent bond.
Answer: In homolytic fission, the bonded electron pair is shared equally between the two fragments, producing free radicals. In heterolytic fission, the electron pair goes entirely to one fragment, producing a cation and an anion (e.g., a carbocation and a nucleophile).

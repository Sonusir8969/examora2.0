NCERT Solutions for Class 10 English Chapter 7 Glimpses of India

NCERT Solutions for Class 10 English First Flight Chapter 7 Glimpses of India covers all three parts - A Baker from Goa, Coorg, and Tea from Assam - along with important questions and answers.

  • 1. Part I: A Baker from Goa
  • 2. Part II: Coorg
  • 3. Part III: Tea from Assam
  • 4. Theme & Message

1. Part I: A Baker from Goa

Short Note:

• This section describes the traditional Goan baker, known as the 'pader', who was once an essential and much-loved figure in every Goan household, bringing fresh bread daily.

• The chapter nostalgically recalls how the jingling of the baker's bamboo bells announced his arrival, and how bread was central to important occasions and celebrations in Goan life.

• It also notes how, despite modernisation, the tradition of the loafer bread and the sweet bread called 'bol' associated with the baker still holds cultural significance.

2. Part II: Coorg

Short Note:

• This section describes Coorg, a scenic hill region in Karnataka, known for its coffee plantations, spice estates, and stunning natural beauty.

• It highlights the martial history and traditions of the Coorgi people, who are known for their bravery and are said to be descendants of a mix of ancient Arab, Greek and Kurdish soldiers according to local legend.

• The chapter also mentions adventure sports available in the region, such as river rafting and trekking to the Brahmagiri hills, along with the wildlife found in its dense forests.

3. Part III: Tea from Assam

Short Note:

• This section, structured as a conversation on a train journey, explains the history and origins of tea drinking, tracing it back to ancient China before it spread to India.

• It describes how the British established large tea estates in Assam after discovering wild tea plants growing naturally there, leading to Assam becoming one of the world's largest tea-growing regions.

• The chapter also explains the detailed process of tea processing, including plucking, withering, rolling, fermenting and drying the tea leaves before they are ready for sale.

4. Theme & Message

Short Note:

• This chapter as a whole celebrates India's rich cultural, regional and culinary diversity, offering glimpses into distinct traditions from different parts of the country.

• It also touches on themes of changing traditions with modernisation, regional pride, and the fascinating history behind everyday things like bread and tea.

Reading With Insight (Textbook Questions)

Q1: What was the importance of the baker in traditional Goan society?
Answer: The baker played a central role in Goan society, delivering fresh bread daily to homes, and his bread, especially the special sweet bol, was an essential part of celebrations like weddings and festivals, making him a beloved and important figure in the community.
Q2: What makes Coorg an attractive destination, according to the chapter?
Answer: Coorg is attractive due to its scenic beauty, misty hills, coffee and spice plantations, rich martial history and traditions, adventurous trekking and rafting opportunities, and its diverse wildlife, making it appealing to both nature lovers and history enthusiasts.
Q3: How did tea cultivation develop in Assam?
Answer: Tea cultivation developed in Assam after the British discovered wild tea plants growing naturally in the region, leading them to establish large-scale tea gardens and plantations, which eventually made Assam one of the most significant tea-producing regions in the world.

Extract-Based / Short Answer Questions

Q4: Why does the writer describe the sound of the baker's bells with nostalgia?
Answer: The writer describes the jingling bells nostalgically because they symbolise a cherished, simpler way of life in old Goa, where the baker's daily visit was a comforting and anticipated part of the community's routine, a tradition that has become less common with modernisation.
Q5: What legend explains the martial traditions of the Coorgi people?
Answer: According to local legend, the Coorgi people are believed to be descendants of Arab or Kurdish soldiers, possibly linked to Alexander the Great's army, which is offered as an explanation for their strong martial traditions and history of bravery.

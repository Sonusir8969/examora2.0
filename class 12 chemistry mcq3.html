Ch-3: Chemical Kinetics 12 Questions

1. The order of a chemical reaction is determined:

  • A. Experimentally from the rate law
  • B. From the stoichiometry of the balanced equation
  • C. From molecularity
  • D. From theoretical calculation only

Answer: A. Experimentally from the rate law

Explanation: Order is an experimental quantity found from how rate depends on concentration; it need not match stoichiometric coefficients.

2. The unit of the rate constant for a first order reaction is:

  • A. s⁻¹
  • B. mol L⁻¹ s⁻¹
  • C. L mol⁻¹ s⁻¹
  • D. mol⁻¹ L² s⁻¹

Answer: A. s⁻¹

Explanation: For a first order reaction, rate = k[A], so k must have units of s⁻¹ to keep rate in mol L⁻¹ s⁻¹.

3. The half-life of a first order reaction is:

  • A. Independent of the initial concentration
  • B. Directly proportional to the initial concentration
  • C. Inversely proportional to the initial concentration
  • D. Proportional to the square of concentration

Answer: A. Independent of the initial concentration

Explanation: For a first order reaction, t½ = 0.693/k, which does not depend on initial concentration.

4. The rate of a reaction generally increases with a rise in temperature mainly because:

  • A. The number of effective collisions increases
  • B. The activation energy decreases
  • C. The molecularity increases
  • D. The order of reaction changes

Answer: A. The number of effective collisions increases

Explanation: Higher temperature increases the fraction of molecules with energy greater than the activation energy, increasing effective collisions.

5. The Arrhenius equation is correctly written as:

  • A. k = A e^(-Ea/RT)
  • B. k = A + Ea/RT
  • C. k = A - RT/Ea
  • D. k = A e^(Ea/RT)

Answer: A. k = A e^(-Ea/RT)

Explanation: The Arrhenius equation relates the rate constant k to the activation energy Ea, temperature T, and pre-exponential factor A.

6. Molecularity of a reaction can never be:

  • A. Zero or a fraction
  • B. One
  • C. Two
  • D. Three

Answer: A. Zero or a fraction

Explanation: Molecularity is the number of reacting species in an elementary step, so it must be a whole number and cannot be zero or fractional (unlike order).

7. A zero order reaction has a rate that is:

  • A. Independent of the concentration of reactants
  • B. Directly proportional to concentration
  • C. Inversely proportional to concentration
  • D. Proportional to the square of concentration

Answer: A. Independent of the concentration of reactants

Explanation: In a zero order reaction, rate = k, so it does not depend on reactant concentration.

8. The slowest step in a multistep reaction mechanism is called the:

  • A. Rate determining step
  • B. Elementary step
  • C. Transition step
  • D. Equilibrium step

Answer: A. Rate determining step

Explanation: The overall rate of a multistep reaction is governed by its slowest step, called the rate determining step.

9. Pseudo first order reactions appear to follow first order kinetics because:

  • A. One of the reactants is present in large excess
  • B. Both reactants have equal concentration
  • C. The reaction is catalyzed
  • D. Temperature remains constant

Answer: A. One of the reactants is present in large excess

Explanation: When one reactant (e.g. water in hydrolysis) is in large excess, its concentration stays effectively constant, making the reaction appear first order.

10. According to collision theory, the rate of a reaction depends mainly on:

  • A. The frequency of effective collisions between reactant molecules
  • B. The total number of collisions only
  • C. The molecular mass of reactants
  • D. The number of moles present only

Answer: A. The frequency of effective collisions between reactant molecules

Explanation: Only collisions with proper orientation and sufficient energy (effective collisions) lead to product formation.

11. If the rate of a reaction doubles when the concentration of a reactant is doubled, the order with respect to that reactant is:

  • A. 1
  • B. 2
  • C. 0
  • D. 3

Answer: A. 1

Explanation: Rate ∝ [A]ⁿ; doubling [A] doubles the rate only when n = 1.

12. A catalyst increases the rate of a reaction mainly by:

  • A. Providing an alternate pathway with lower activation energy
  • B. Increasing the activation energy
  • C. Changing the equilibrium constant
  • D. Increasing the enthalpy of reaction

Answer: A. Providing an alternate pathway with lower activation energy

Explanation: Catalysts lower the activation energy barrier, allowing more molecules to react per unit time, without altering the equilibrium position.

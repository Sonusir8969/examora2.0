Ch-2: Nelson Mandela: Long Walk to Freedom 30 Questions

1. On which date did the inauguration ceremony take place?

  • A. 10th May
  • B. 10th March
  • C. 20th May
  • D. 15th April

Answer: A. 10th May

Explanation: The inauguration ceremony took place on 10th May, which dawned bright and clear.

2. Where did the inauguration ceremony take place?

  • A. Union Buildings amphitheatre in Pretoria
  • B. Parliament House in Cape Town
  • C. Stadium in Johannesburg
  • D. City Hall in Durban

Answer: A. Union Buildings amphitheatre in Pretoria

Explanation: The ceremony took place in the lovely sandstone amphitheatre formed by the Union Buildings in Pretoria.

3. Who accompanied Nelson Mandela on the day of the ceremony?

  • A. His daughter Zenani
  • B. His wife Evelyn
  • C. His mother
  • D. His sister

Answer: A. His daughter Zenani

Explanation: Mandela was accompanied on the podium by his daughter Zenani.

4. Who was sworn in as the First Deputy President?

  • A. Thabo Mbeki
  • B. Mr. de Klerk
  • C. Oliver Tambo
  • D. Walter Sisulu

Answer: A. Thabo Mbeki

Explanation: Thabo Mbeki was sworn in as the First Deputy President after Mr. de Klerk was sworn in as Second Deputy President.

5. Who was sworn in as the Second Deputy President?

  • A. Mr. de Klerk
  • B. Thabo Mbeki
  • C. Nelson Mandela
  • D. Chief Luthuli

Answer: A. Mr. de Klerk

Explanation: Mr. de Klerk was the first to be sworn in as Second Deputy President.

6. What system of government was replaced in South Africa on 10th May 1994?

  • A. Apartheid regime
  • B. Monarchical rule
  • C. Military dictatorship
  • D. Communist rule

Answer: A. Apartheid regime

Explanation: The racial system of Apartheid was replaced by South Africa's first democratic, non-racial government.

7. How many national anthems were sung on the inauguration day?

  • A. Two
  • B. One
  • C. Three
  • D. Four

Answer: A. Two

Explanation: Two national anthems were sung: 'Nkosi Sikelel’ iAfrika' (by whites) and 'Die Stem' (by blacks).

8. What was the name of the old national anthem sung by the black population?

  • A. Die Stem
  • B. Nkosi Sikelel' iAfrika
  • C. Star-Spangled Banner
  • D. Freedom Song

Answer: A. Die Stem

Explanation: 'Die Stem' was the old anthem of the republic sung by the black population.

9. According to Mandela, what is the greatest wealth of a nation?

  • A. Its people
  • B. Its minerals
  • C. Its diamonds
  • D. Its gold

Answer: A. Its people

Explanation: Mandela states that the country's greatest wealth is its people, finer and truer than the purest diamonds.

10. What did Mandela learn about courage from his comrades?

  • A. Triumph over fear
  • B. Absence of fear
  • C. Physical strength
  • D. Lack of emotions

Answer: A. Triumph over fear

Explanation: Mandela learned that courage was not the absence of fear, but the triumph over it.

11. According to Mandela, what comes more naturally to the human heart?

  • A. Love
  • B. Hate
  • C. Jealousy
  • D. Fear

Answer: A. Love

Explanation: Mandela asserts that love comes more naturally to the human heart than its opposite (hate).

12. What are the "twin obligations" mentioned by Nelson Mandela?

  • A. Obligation to family and obligation to community/country
  • B. Obligation to work and obligation to self
  • C. Obligation to God and obligation to government
  • D. Obligation to society and obligation to education

Answer: A. Obligation to family and obligation to community/country

Explanation: Mandela states every man has twin obligations: to family/parents/wife/children, and to his people/community/country.

13. Why was it impossible for a man of color in South Africa to fulfill both obligations?

  • A. He was punished and isolated if he tried to live as a human
  • B. He lacked money
  • C. He had no time
  • D. He was not educated

Answer: A. He was punished and isolated if he tried to live as a human

Explanation: In South Africa, a black man attempting to fulfill his duty to his people was ripped from his family and forced to live an isolated life.

14. What political party did Nelson Mandela join?

  • A. African National Congress (ANC)
  • B. National Party
  • C. Inkatha Freedom Party
  • D. Democratic Alliance

Answer: A. African National Congress (ANC)

Explanation: Mandela joined the African National Congress (ANC) when he realized his freedom and his people's freedom were curtailed.

15. How did Mandela's understanding of freedom change as he grew older?

  • A. He realized his boyhood freedom was an illusion
  • B. He thought freedom was only about running in fields
  • C. He stopped caring about freedom
  • D. He believed freedom meant having lots of money

Answer: A. He realized his boyhood freedom was an illusion

Explanation: As a young man, Mandela realized that his boyhood freedom was an illusion and that real freedom was denied to his people.

16. What transformed a frightened young man into a bold one, according to Mandela?

  • A. The desire for freedom of his people
  • B. His law degree
  • C. Joining a political election
  • D. His fear of prison

Answer: A. The desire for freedom of his people

Explanation: It was the desire for the dignity and freedom of his people that transformed a timid man into a courageous leader.

17. A man who takes away another man's freedom is a prisoner of what?

  • A. Hatred
  • B. Crime
  • C. Fear
  • D. Ignorance

Answer: A. Hatred

Explanation: Mandela states that an oppressor who takes away freedom is a prisoner of hatred, locked behind bars of prejudice.

18. What spectrum of colors was left by the Impala jets over the Union Buildings?

  • A. Black, red, green, gold, and blue
  • B. Red, yellow, blue, and white
  • C. Silver, gold, black, and white
  • D. Orange, green, white, and blue

Answer: A. Black, red, green, gold, and blue

Explanation: The Impala jets left a smoke trail of the colors of the new South African flag: black, red, green, gold, and blue.

19. How many years did Nelson Mandela spend in prison?

  • A. 30 years
  • B. 20 years
  • C. 27 years
  • D. 15 years

Answer: A. 30 years

Explanation: Mandela spent nearly three decades (30 years) in prison before becoming South Africa's first black president.

20. What created "a deep and lasting wound" in South Africa?

  • A. The policy of Apartheid
  • B. World War II
  • C. The economic recession
  • D. Tribal wars

Answer: A. The policy of Apartheid

Explanation: Mandela mentions that the policy of Apartheid created a deep and lasting wound in his country and his people.

21. Oliver Tambo, Walter Sisulu, and Chief Luthuli were examples of men of extraordinary:

  • A. Courage, wisdom, and generosity
  • B. Wealth and power
  • C. Military tactics
  • D. Scientific knowledge

Answer: A. Courage, wisdom, and generosity

Explanation: Mandela credited these freedom fighters as men of extraordinary courage, wisdom, and generosity created by decades of oppression.

22. What does Mandela mean by "an extraordinary human disaster"?

  • A. The practice of racial discrimination (Apartheid)
  • B. A devastating earthquake
  • C. The outbreak of a disease
  • D. A famine in South Africa

Answer: A. The practice of racial discrimination (Apartheid)

Explanation: "Extraordinary human disaster" refers to the long-lasting inhumane treatment and suppression under the Apartheid system.

23. What phrase did Mandela use to describe the new non-racial government?

  • A. A human society of which all humanity will be proud
  • B. A rich financial empire
  • C. A powerful military nation
  • D. A kingdom of peace

Answer: A. A human society of which all humanity will be proud

Explanation: Mandela pledged to give birth to a society of which all humanity would be proud.

24. What did the military generals do to demonstrate their loyalty during the ceremony?

  • A. Saluted Mandela and pledged their loyalty
  • B. Fired rifles into the air
  • C. Gave speech on defense
  • D. Marched away

Answer: A. Saluted Mandela and pledged their loyalty

Explanation: Highest military generals saluted Nelson Mandela and swore their allegiance to the democratic state.

25. What was Mandela's reaction when the generals saluted him?

  • A. He remembered that not many years ago they would have arrested him
  • B. He felt proud and arrogant
  • C. He was afraid of them
  • D. He ignored them

Answer: A. He remembered that not many years ago they would have arrested him

Explanation: Mandela reflected on the irony that years earlier, those same generals would have arrested him as an outlaw.

26. In the first decade of the 20th century, white South Africans erected a system of racial domination against:

  • A. The dark-skinned peoples of their own land
  • B. Immigrants from Europe
  • C. Asian traders
  • D. Foreign soldiers

Answer: A. The dark-skinned peoples of their own land

Explanation: Following the Anglo-Boer war, white South Africans established Apartheid against native dark-skinned citizens.

27. What did Mandela feel he was "simply the sum of"?

  • A. All those African patriots who had gone before him
  • B. All the world leaders present
  • C. His family ancestors
  • D. His political colleagues in ANC

Answer: A. All those African patriots who had gone before him

Explanation: Mandela felt that he stood on the shoulders of all African freedom fighters who sacrificed their lives earlier.

28. What is the meaning of 'transitory freedoms' that Mandela desired as a student?

  • A. Temporary freedoms like staying out late and reading what he pleased
  • B. Permanent voting rights
  • C. Freedom to travel abroad
  • D. Financial independence

Answer: A. Temporary freedoms like staying out late and reading what he pleased

Explanation: As a student, Mandela wanted personal, momentary freedoms like staying out late or reading books of his choice.

29. Complete the sentence: "The oppressed and the oppressor alike are robbed of ______."

  • A. Their humanity
  • B. Their wealth
  • C. Their freedom of speech
  • D. Their strength

Answer: A. Their humanity

Explanation: Mandela wrote: "The oppressed and the oppressor alike are robbed of their humanity."

30. What was the ANC's victory percentage in South Africa's first democratic elections?

  • A. 252 out of 400 seats
  • B. 300 out of 400 seats
  • C. 150 out of 400 seats
  • D. 200 out of 400 seats

Answer: A. 252 out of 400 seats

Explanation: Nelson Mandela’s ANC won 252 of the 400 seats in the first democratic elections in South Africa's history.

Chapter 19: Poverty as a Challenge 30 Questions

1. What is the 'poverty line' used to measure?

  • A. The income tax paid by citizens
  • B. A minimum level of income or consumption needed to meet basic needs
  • C. The literacy rate of a region
  • D. The population density of an area

Answer: B. A minimum level of income or consumption needed to meet basic needs

Explanation: The poverty line is a benchmark used to identify individuals or households whose income or consumption falls below the minimum level needed for basic subsistence.

2. On what basis is the poverty line in India typically calculated?

  • A. Land ownership only
  • B. Minimum calorie requirement and corresponding monetary expenditure
  • C. Number of family members alone
  • D. Religion of the family

Answer: B. Minimum calorie requirement and corresponding monetary expenditure

Explanation: India's poverty line has traditionally been based on a minimum calorie intake requirement, converted into the monetary expenditure needed to buy that food along with other basic needs.

3. What is meant by 'social exclusion' in the context of poverty?

  • A. Being denied basic amenities and opportunities due to social factors such as caste
  • B. Being excluded from a family gathering
  • C. Choosing to live away from society voluntarily
  • D. Excess income compared to others

Answer: A. Being denied basic amenities and opportunities due to social factors such as caste

Explanation: Social exclusion refers to the process by which certain groups are systematically denied access to resources, opportunities, or social participation, often due to caste or community identity.

4. What is meant by 'vulnerability' in the context of poverty analysis?

  • A. The likelihood that certain groups face a higher risk of falling into poverty due to shocks like illness or disasters
  • B. The total income of a household
  • C. The number of children in a family
  • D. The educational qualification of the head of a household

Answer: A. The likelihood that certain groups face a higher risk of falling into poverty due to shocks like illness or disasters

Explanation: Vulnerability measures how susceptible certain individuals or communities are to falling into poverty due to unforeseen events, such as natural disasters, illness, or unemployment.

5. Which social groups are identified in the chapter as being at the highest risk of poverty in India?

  • A. Scheduled Castes, Scheduled Tribes and casual wage labourers
  • B. Government employees
  • C. Large landowners
  • D. Software professionals

Answer: A. Scheduled Castes, Scheduled Tribes and casual wage labourers

Explanation: The chapter highlights that Scheduled Castes, Scheduled Tribes and casual wage labourers face a disproportionately higher risk of poverty in India.

6. What is the 'poverty line basket' typically composed of?

  • A. A hypothetical set of essential goods considered necessary for basic subsistence
  • B. A list of luxury goods
  • C. Only clothing items
  • D. Financial assets owned by a household

Answer: A. A hypothetical set of essential goods considered necessary for basic subsistence

Explanation: The poverty line basket includes essential food and non-food items considered necessary to satisfy basic needs, and its cost helps determine the poverty line.

7. Which term describes people who move in and out of poverty over different years, such as farm labourers with unstable incomes?

  • A. Chronic poor
  • B. Transient poor
  • C. Never poor
  • D. Absolute rich

Answer: B. Transient poor

Explanation: Transient poor are those who move in and out of poverty periodically, often due to fluctuating incomes, unlike the chronic poor who remain persistently poor.

8. Which of the following is generally considered a cause of poverty in India, as discussed in the chapter?

  • A. High economic growth for all sections
  • B. Unequal distribution of resources and low levels of economic development historically
  • C. Universal access to quality education
  • D. Absence of any population growth

Answer: B. Unequal distribution of resources and low levels of economic development historically

Explanation: Historical factors such as low economic growth, unequal distribution of land and resources, and social inequalities have contributed significantly to persistent poverty in India.

9. What poverty ratio trend has India generally shown since the early 1970s according to the chapter?

  • A. It has remained exactly the same with no change
  • B. The percentage of people below the poverty line has generally declined over the decades
  • C. It has consistently increased every year
  • D. It reached zero before 2000

Answer: B. The percentage of people below the poverty line has generally declined over the decades

Explanation: Although the absolute number remains significant, the overall percentage of people living below the poverty line in India has shown a declining trend over the decades.

10. What is the 'Below Poverty Line' (BPL) census used for?

  • A. To identify households that qualify for government poverty alleviation schemes
  • B. To count the total population of India
  • C. To measure literacy rates only
  • D. To calculate GDP growth

Answer: A. To identify households that qualify for government poverty alleviation schemes

Explanation: The BPL census helps the government identify poor households so that targeted welfare and anti-poverty programmes can reach those most in need.

11. Which government scheme guarantees a minimum number of days of wage employment to rural households, as referenced in poverty alleviation efforts?

  • A. MGNREGA (Mahatma Gandhi National Rural Employment Guarantee Act)
  • B. Digital India
  • C. Make in India
  • D. Beti Bachao Beti Padhao

Answer: A. MGNREGA (Mahatma Gandhi National Rural Employment Guarantee Act)

Explanation: MGNREGA guarantees a minimum of 100 days of wage employment in a year to rural households, aiming to reduce poverty by providing income security.

12. What method has India traditionally used to define the poverty line for rural and urban areas?

  • A. Same fixed amount for the entire country regardless of area
  • B. Separate minimum income/consumption levels calculated for rural and urban areas
  • C. Based only on the size of the family
  • D. Based only on the state government's discretion

Answer: B. Separate minimum income/consumption levels calculated for rural and urban areas

Explanation: Because the cost of living differs, separate poverty lines have generally been calculated for rural and urban regions in India.

13. What is one of the main criticisms of using only income or calorie intake to define poverty?

  • A. It fully captures every dimension of deprivation
  • B. It may not capture broader deprivations such as lack of access to health, education, and clean water
  • C. It is the only accurate way to measure poverty
  • D. It applies only to urban poverty

Answer: B. It may not capture broader deprivations such as lack of access to health, education, and clean water

Explanation: Critics argue that income-based poverty lines may overlook other important aspects of deprivation, such as poor access to healthcare, education, sanitation and housing.

14. Which of the following countries, as discussed in a global comparison, has historically had a very high number of people in poverty due to its large population?

  • A. Sub-Saharan African countries and South Asian countries including India
  • B. Only Scandinavian countries
  • C. Only Western European countries
  • D. Only countries in North America

Answer: A. Sub-Saharan African countries and South Asian countries including India

Explanation: The chapter notes that a large proportion of the world's poor live in Sub-Saharan Africa and South Asia, including India, due to a combination of high population and slower development.

15. What is the term for poverty that persists across generations within the same family?

  • A. Seasonal poverty
  • B. Chronic poverty
  • C. Occasional poverty
  • D. Random poverty

Answer: B. Chronic poverty

Explanation: Chronic poverty refers to a persistent, long-term state of poverty that often continues across generations within the same family or community.

16. Why are women, elderly people, and female-headed households often considered particularly vulnerable to poverty?

  • A. Because they tend to earn higher wages than others
  • B. Because they often face fewer income-earning opportunities and social disadvantages
  • C. Because they always own the most land
  • D. Because they receive special exemption from all hardships

Answer: B. Because they often face fewer income-earning opportunities and social disadvantages

Explanation: Social and economic disadvantages, such as unequal wages and limited access to resources, make groups like women, the elderly, and female-headed households more vulnerable to poverty.

17. What is one effective long-term strategy for reducing poverty mentioned in the chapter?

  • A. Promoting economic growth combined with human development, such as education and health
  • B. Ignoring the issue completely
  • C. Reducing all government welfare schemes
  • D. Preventing any form of employment generation

Answer: A. Promoting economic growth combined with human development, such as education and health

Explanation: The chapter highlights that sustained economic growth, along with investments in human development like education and healthcare, is key to reducing poverty over the long term.

18. What role do government anti-poverty programmes generally aim to play?

  • A. To increase income inequality further
  • B. To provide direct support, employment or subsidies to help the poor meet basic needs
  • C. To eliminate the need for economic growth
  • D. To only benefit wealthy citizens

Answer: B. To provide direct support, employment or subsidies to help the poor meet basic needs

Explanation: Anti-poverty programmes are designed to directly assist poor households through employment generation, subsidised food, or other support to improve their standard of living.

19. Which of the following best defines 'absolute poverty'?

  • A. Poverty measured only relative to the richest in society
  • B. A condition where individuals cannot meet a minimum defined standard of basic necessities
  • C. Poverty that exists only in urban areas
  • D. Poverty measured by educational qualification alone

Answer: B. A condition where individuals cannot meet a minimum defined standard of basic necessities

Explanation: Absolute poverty refers to a fixed minimum standard of living, below which a person is considered unable to meet their basic survival needs.

20. What does the chapter suggest about the relationship between poverty and inequality?

  • A. Inequality has no relation to poverty
  • B. High levels of inequality can worsen the impact and persistence of poverty in society
  • C. Poverty always leads to greater equality
  • D. Inequality only affects rich people

Answer: B. High levels of inequality can worsen the impact and persistence of poverty in society

Explanation: Unequal distribution of income and resources tends to deepen and prolong poverty for disadvantaged groups within a society.

21. Why is unemployment considered one of the major causes of poverty in India?

  • A. It has no impact on household income
  • B. It leaves households without a stable source of income to meet basic needs
  • C. It only affects a small number of families
  • D. It automatically leads to higher literacy

Answer: B. It leaves households without a stable source of income to meet basic needs

Explanation: Without regular employment, households often lack sufficient income to afford basic necessities, making unemployment a major driver of poverty.

22. What is the significance of measuring the 'poverty gap' or 'depth of poverty'?

  • A. It measures only the number of people above the poverty line
  • B. It indicates how far below the poverty line poor households fall, on average
  • C. It has no practical use for policymakers
  • D. It measures the literacy rate of the poor

Answer: B. It indicates how far below the poverty line poor households fall, on average

Explanation: The poverty gap measures the average shortfall of poor households' income from the poverty line, giving a sense of the severity, not just the extent, of poverty.

23. Which of the following global institutions is mentioned as tracking and comparing poverty across countries?

  • A. The World Bank
  • B. The Election Commission of India
  • C. NITI Aayog alone
  • D. The Reserve Bank of India

Answer: A. The World Bank

Explanation: The World Bank uses international poverty lines to compare and track poverty levels across different countries around the world.

24. What contribution can education make towards reducing poverty, according to the chapter?

  • A. Education increases job opportunities and skills, helping people escape the poverty cycle
  • B. Education has no effect on employment opportunities
  • C. Education always increases poverty
  • D. Education is unrelated to income levels

Answer: A. Education increases job opportunities and skills, helping people escape the poverty cycle

Explanation: Access to quality education improves employability and income-earning potential, making it an important tool for breaking the cycle of poverty.

25. Why might a natural disaster, such as a drought or flood, push additional households into poverty?

  • A. It has no effect on household income or assets
  • B. It can destroy crops, homes and livelihoods, pushing vulnerable families further into deprivation
  • C. It always increases a household's savings
  • D. It benefits farmers by improving soil quality instantly

Answer: B. It can destroy crops, homes and livelihoods, pushing vulnerable families further into deprivation

Explanation: Natural disasters can wipe out crops, assets and sources of income, making already vulnerable households even more susceptible to falling into poverty.

26. What is one reason why urban poverty has been increasing in certain contexts, as discussed in the chapter?

  • A. Migration of the rural poor to cities in search of work, often ending up in low-paid informal jobs
  • B. Complete absence of any migration to cities
  • C. Cities have unlimited high-paying jobs for everyone
  • D. Urban areas have no informal sector

Answer: A. Migration of the rural poor to cities in search of work, often ending up in low-paid informal jobs

Explanation: Many poor rural migrants move to cities hoping for better opportunities but often end up in insecure, low-paying informal jobs, contributing to urban poverty.

27. What does the term 'human poverty' emphasise, as opposed to purely income-based measures?

  • A. Deprivation in areas like health, education, and access to resources, beyond just income
  • B. Only the amount of money a person earns
  • C. Only the number of assets a person owns
  • D. Only agricultural productivity

Answer: A. Deprivation in areas like health, education, and access to resources, beyond just income

Explanation: Human poverty broadens the concept beyond income, capturing deprivations in health, education, and overall standard of living.

28. Which of these could be considered a non-income indicator of poverty?

  • A. Lack of access to clean drinking water and sanitation
  • B. The exact monthly salary of a household
  • C. The stock market performance of a country
  • D. The exchange rate of the national currency

Answer: A. Lack of access to clean drinking water and sanitation

Explanation: Non-income indicators such as access to clean water, sanitation, healthcare and education help provide a fuller picture of poverty beyond just monetary measures.

29. According to the chapter, why is poverty considered a 'multi-dimensional' problem?

  • A. Because it involves only one single factor
  • B. Because it involves multiple interconnected factors like income, health, education and social exclusion
  • C. Because it can be solved by a single government scheme alone
  • D. Because it applies only to a specific religion

Answer: B. Because it involves multiple interconnected factors like income, health, education and social exclusion

Explanation: Poverty is described as multi-dimensional because it results from and reinforces various interconnected deprivations, not just a lack of income alone.

30. What is the overall message of the chapter regarding poverty as a national challenge?

  • A. Poverty has already been completely eliminated in India
  • B. Poverty remains a serious and complex challenge requiring sustained economic growth and targeted government action
  • C. Poverty is not a significant issue for policymakers
  • D. Poverty can be solved instantly without any effort

Answer: B. Poverty remains a serious and complex challenge requiring sustained economic growth and targeted government action

Explanation: The chapter concludes that despite progress, poverty remains a major challenge in India, requiring continued economic growth alongside focused efforts in education, health and social protection.

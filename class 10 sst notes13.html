NCERT Solutions for Class 10 Political Science Chapter 1 Power Sharing

NCERT Solutions for Class 10 Civics Chapter 1 covers Case Studies of Belgium and Sri Lanka, Majoritarianism vs Accommodation, Prudential and Moral Reasons for Power Sharing, and Forms of Power Sharing.

1. Case Studies: Belgium and Sri Lanka

Short Note:

• Belgium Composition: 59% Dutch-speakers (Flemish region), 40% French-speakers (Wallonia region), 1% German-speakers. In capital Brussels: 80% French, 20% Dutch.

• Sri Lanka Composition: 74% Sinhala-speakers (mostly Buddhist), 18% Tamil-speakers (13% Sri Lankan Tamils, 5% Indian Tamils; Hindu/Muslim), 7% Christians (both Sinhala and Tamil).

2. Majoritarianism in Sri Lanka vs Accommodation in Belgium

Short Note:

• Majoritarianism in Sri Lanka: In 1956, an Act was passed making Sinhala the sole official language. Preferences given to Sinhalas for government jobs and university admissions. Resulted in political alienation among Tamils and led to a violent Civil War.

• Belgian Model of Accommodation: Equal number of Dutch and French ministers in central government. Power handed to state governments. Separate government for Brussels with equal representation. Introduction of a 3rd tier: 'Community Government' elected by people belonging to one language community.

3. Why Power Sharing is Desirable? (Prudential vs Moral)

Short Note:

• Prudential Reasons: Reduces the possibility of conflict between social groups, ensures political stability, and prevents violence.

• Moral Reasons: Power sharing is the very spirit of democracy. A legitimate government is one where citizens participate to acquire a stake in the system.

4. Forms of Power Sharing in Modern Democracies

Short Note:

• Horizontal Distribution: Power shared among different organs of government at the same level (Legislature, Executive, Judiciary). Known as a system of Checks and Balances.

• Vertical Distribution: Power shared among governments at different levels (Central, State, Local). Known as Federal Power Sharing.

• Among Social Groups: Power shared among linguistic or religious groups (e.g., Reserved Constituencies in India, Community Government in Belgium).

• Among Political Parties & Pressure Groups: Coalition governments and policy influence by interest groups.

Intext & Conceptual Questions

Q1: What was the main cause of conflict between the Sinhala and Tamil communities in Sri Lanka?
1. Adoption of Majoritarian policies favoring the Sinhala majority after independence in 1948.
2. Making Sinhala the only official language, ignoring Tamil culture and language.
3. Denial of equal political rights and career opportunities to Tamils led to distrust and Civil War.
Q2: What is the system of 'Checks and Balances' in power sharing?
1. It is a form of horizontal distribution where power is shared among Legislature, Executive, and Judiciary.
2. Even though each organ works at the same level, none of them can exercise unlimited power.
3. Each organ checks the functioning of the other, maintaining a balance of power within the state.

Textbook Chapter End Exercise Questions

Q1: Compare the ways in which Belgium and Sri Lanka handled their ethnic diversity.
Answer:
1. Sri Lanka: Followed Majoritarianism. Forced majority rules on minority Tamils, leading to widespread distrust, violent clashes, and a long Civil War.
2. Belgium: Followed Power Sharing & Accommodation. Modified their constitution four times to ensure equal rights for Dutch and French citizens, avoiding civil strife.
Q2: Differentiate between Prudential and Moral reasons for Power Sharing.
Answer:
1. Prudential Reasons: Based on careful calculation of gains and losses. Power sharing prevents social conflicts and ensures political stability.
2. Moral Reasons: Based on ethical principles. Power sharing is the fundamental spirit and intrinsic value of a true democracy.
Q3: Describe any four forms of Power Sharing in modern democracies with suitable examples.
Answer:
1. Horizontal Division: Power divided among Legislature, Executive, and Judiciary (System of Checks and Balances).
2. Vertical Division: Power shared between Central, State, and Local governments (Federalism).
3. Social Groups: Power shared among diverse social or religious groups (e.g., Reserved Constituencies in India, Community Govt in Belgium).
4. Political Parties & Alliances: Power shared when two or more parties form a Coalition Government.

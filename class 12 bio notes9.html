NCERT Solutions for Class 12 Biology Chapter 9 Biotechnology: Principles and Processes

NCERT Solutions for Class 12 Biology Chapter 9 Biotechnology: Principles and Processes covers the principles of genetic engineering, tools like restriction enzymes and vectors, and the process of recombinant DNA technology.

1. Principles of Biotechnology

Short Note:

• Modern biotechnology relies on two core techniques: genetic engineering, which involves altering the chemistry of genetic material (DNA and RNA) to introduce desired traits into an organism, and bioprocess engineering, which involves maintaining sterile, favourable conditions for large-scale production of biotechnological products using microbes or other cells.

2. Tools of Recombinant DNA Technology

Short Note:

• Restriction enzymes (molecular scissors), such as EcoRI, recognise specific palindromic DNA sequences and cut the DNA at those points, allowing scientists to isolate specific genes of interest.

• Vectors, commonly plasmids or bacteriophages, are used to carry and introduce foreign DNA into a host cell; they must have an origin of replication, a selectable marker, and a limited number of recognition sites for restriction enzymes (cloning sites).

• DNA ligase is used to join or 'paste' the foreign DNA fragment with the vector DNA to form recombinant DNA.

3. Process of Recombinant DNA Technology

Short Note:

• The key steps include isolation of genetic material (DNA), cutting it at specific locations using restriction enzymes, amplification of the desired gene (often using PCR - Polymerase Chain Reaction), ligation of the amplified DNA into a vector, and introduction of the recombinant DNA into a suitable host cell.

• Methods of introducing recombinant DNA into host cells include transformation (using calcium chloride to make bacterial cells competent to take up DNA), micro-injection, biolistics (gene gun, used mainly for plant cells), and using disarmed pathogen vectors like Agrobacterium tumefaciens.

• Finally, the desired protein product is obtained through downstream processing, which includes separation, purification and quality control before the product can be marketed.

NCERT Textbook Exercise Questions

Q1: What are restriction enzymes, and how do they function in genetic engineering?
Answer: Restriction enzymes, also known as molecular scissors, are enzymes that recognise and bind to specific palindromic sequences of DNA, called recognition sequences, and cut the DNA strand at or near this site. In genetic engineering, they are used to precisely cut out a gene of interest from a larger DNA molecule and to cut open a vector DNA at a specific point, allowing the foreign gene to be inserted into the vector to form recombinant DNA.
Q2: What are the essential features required for a molecule to act as an effective cloning vector?
Answer: An effective cloning vector must have an origin of replication (ori), which allows it to replicate independently within the host cell; a selectable marker gene, which helps identify and eliminate host cells that have not been successfully transformed; and a limited number of unique recognition sites for commonly used restriction enzymes, allowing convenient insertion of foreign DNA without disrupting the vector's essential functions.
Q3: Explain the process and significance of the Polymerase Chain Reaction (PCR).
Answer: PCR is a technique used to amplify a single or few copies of a specific DNA segment into millions of copies, using repeated cycles of denaturation (separating DNA strands using heat), annealing (attaching short primers to the target sequence), and extension (synthesising new DNA strands using a heat-stable DNA polymerase, such as Taq polymerase). PCR is significant because it allows a small amount of DNA to be rapidly amplified for use in gene cloning, diagnostic testing, and forensic analysis.
Q4: How is Agrobacterium tumefaciens used as a vector in plant genetic engineering?
Answer: Agrobacterium tumefaciens is a plant pathogen that naturally has the ability to transfer a part of its DNA (T-DNA) into the genome of infected plant cells, causing crown gall disease. Scientists have exploited this natural ability by disarming the harmful, disease-causing genes and replacing them with a gene of interest, using the modified Agrobacterium as a vector to deliver and integrate desired genes into plant cells, forming the basis of many transgenic plant technologies.

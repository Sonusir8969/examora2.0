NCERT Solutions for Class 9 Science Chapter 15 Improvement in Food Resources

NCERT Solutions for Class 9 Science Chapter 15, "Improvement in Food Resources," covers crop variety improvement, crop production management (nutrients, manure, fertilizers, irrigation, cropping patterns), crop protection management, and animal husbandry (cattle farming, poultry farming, fish production, and apiculture).

1. Improvement in Crop Yields & Variety Improvement

Short Note:

• Types of Crops:

  - Kharif Crops: Grown in the rainy season (June–October), e.g., Paddy, Soyabean, Pigeon pea, Maize, Cotton.

  - Rabi Crops: Grown in the winter season (November–April), e.g., Wheat, Gram, Peas, Mustard, Linseed.

• Crop Variety Improvement: Aims to develop high-yielding, disease-resistant crop varieties through hybridisation (crossing between genetically different plants) and genetic modification.

• Objectives: Higher yield, improved quality, biotic and abiotic resistance, wider adaptability, desirable agronomic characteristics.

2. Crop Production Management: Nutrients & Cropping Patterns

Short Note:

• Nutrient Management: Plants require 16 essential nutrients (Air supplies C and O; Water supplies H; Soil supplies 13 nutrients divided into 6 Macronutrients and 7 Micronutrients).

• Manure vs Fertilizers:

  - Manure: Organic matter prepared by decomposition of animal waste and plant waste. Enriches soil with humus, improves soil structure, but provides small amounts of nutrients.

  - Fertilizers: Commercially produced chemical compounds (rich in N, P, K). High nutrient supply, but excessive use degrades soil fertility and causes water pollution.

• Cropping Patterns:

  - Mixed Cropping: Growing two or more crops simultaneously on the same piece of land (e.g., Wheat + Gram).

  - Inter-cropping: Growing two or more crops in alternating definite row patterns (e.g., Soyabean + Maize).

  - Crop Rotation: Growing different crops on a piece of land in a pre-planned succession to maintain soil fertility.

3. Crop Protection & Storage of Grains

Short Note:

• Weed Control: Weeds are unwanted plants in cultivated fields (e.g., Xanthium, Parthenium, Cyperinus) that compete for nutrients, light, and space. Controlled by herbicides, manual weeding, or proper seed-bed preparation.

• Insect Pests & Diseases: Pests attack crops by cutting roots/stems, sucking cell sap, or boring into fruits/stems. Controlled using pesticides/insecticides, resistant varieties, and biological methods.

• Storage Loss Factors:

  - Biotic factors: Insects, rodents, fungi, mites, and bacteria.

  - Abiotic factors: Inappropriate moisture and temperature in storage places.

  - Prevention: Proper drying before storage, cleaning, and chemical fumigation.

4. Animal Husbandry: Cattle, Poultry, Fish & Apiculture

Short Note:

• Cattle Farming: Undertaken for milk production (milch animals) and agricultural draught labour (draught animals). Cross-breeding indigenous breeds (e.g., Sahiwal, Red Sindhi) with exotic breeds (e.g., Jersey, Brown Swiss) produces high-yield, disease-resistant offspring.

• Poultry Farming: Raised for egg production (layers) and meat production (broilers). Cross-breeding of Indian (e.g., Aseel) and Foreign (e.g., Leghorn) breeds improves chick quality and temperature tolerance.

• Fish Production (Pisciculture & Aquaculture):

  - Capture Fishing: Catching fish from natural water sources (marine or freshwater).

  - Culture Fishery: Farming fish in controlled water bodies.

  - Composite Fish Culture: Combination of 5–6 fish species in a single pond using different feeding zones (e.g., Catlas feed on surface, Rohus in middle zone, Mrigals/Common Carps on bottom).

• Apiculture (Bee-keeping): Practice of keeping honeybees for honey and wax production. Italian bee species (Apis mellifera) is commonly used due to high honey collection capacity, fast breeding, and less stinging nature.

Intext Questions & Textbook Exercise Answers

Q1: What do we get from cereals, pulses, fruits and vegetables?
Answer:

• Cereals (like wheat, rice, maize) provide carbohydrates for energy requirements.

• Pulses (like gram, pea, lentil) provide proteins for body growth and repair.

• Fruits and Vegetables provide a range of vitamins, minerals, along with small amounts of proteins, carbohydrates, and fats.

Q2: How do biotic and abiotic factors affect crop production?
Answer:

• Biotic factors: Diseases, insects, pests, and nematodes attack crops, damage plant parts, reduce growth, and lower crop yields.

• Abiotic factors: Drought, salinity, waterlogging, heat, cold, and frost adversely affect germination, plant growth, flowering, and overall crop productivity.

Q3: Compare the use of manure and fertilizers in maintaining soil fertility.
Answer:

• Manure: Organic and rich in humus. It improves soil texture, aeration, and water-retaining capacity. It restores soil fertility safely over long periods without causing environmental harm.

• Fertilizers: Inorganic chemical salts rich in specific plant nutrients (N, P, K). Continuous and excessive usage damages soil structure, kills soil microbes, lowers long-term soil fertility, and pollutes nearby water bodies.

Q4: Explain any one method of crop production which ensures high yield.
Answer: Inter-cropping is a crop production method that ensures higher yield and crop protection.

In inter-cropping, two or more crops are grown simultaneously on the same field in a definite row pattern (e.g., 1 row of Soyabean alternating with 1 row of Maize). It prevents pests and diseases from spreading to all plants of a single crop and ensures maximum utilization of applied nutrients and sunlight.
Q5: What are the advantages of composite fish culture?
Answer:

• A combination of five or six different fish species (both indigenous and exotic) are used in a single fish pond.

• Species are selected so that they have different food habits and do not compete for food (e.g., Catlas feed on surface, Rohus in middle zone, Mrigals at the bottom).

• As a result, food available in all parts of the pond is completely utilized, increasing the total fish yield from the pond significantly.

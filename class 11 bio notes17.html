NCERT Solutions for Class 11 Biology Chapter 17 Locomotion and Movement

NCERT Solutions for Class 11 Biology Chapter 17 Locomotion and Movement covers the types of movement, the structure of skeletal muscle and the mechanism of muscle contraction, and the human skeletal system and joints.

Overview of Topics & Subtopics (Click to Jump)

  • 1. Types of Movement
  • 2. Muscle Structure and Contraction
  • 3. Skeletal System and Joints

1. Types of Movement

Short Note:

• Body movements can be amoeboid (e.g., macrophages, using pseudopodia), ciliary (e.g., movement of ova in fallopian tubes), or muscular (e.g., limb movement, powered by skeletal muscle contraction).

2. Muscle Structure and Contraction

Short Note:

• Skeletal muscle is composed of bundles of muscle fibres; each fibre contains myofibrils made of repeating units called sarcomeres, composed of thin (actin) and thick (myosin) filaments.

• Muscle contraction follows the Sliding Filament Theory: actin filaments slide over myosin filaments (powered by ATP and triggered by Ca²+ ions binding to troponin), shortening the sarcomere without a change in the length of individual filaments.

3. Skeletal System and Joints

Short Note:

• The human skeletal system is divided into the axial skeleton (skull, vertebral column, ribs, sternum — 80 bones) and appendicular skeleton (limb bones and girdles — 126 bones), totalling 206 bones.

• Joints facilitate movement, classified as fibrous (immovable, e.g. skull sutures), cartilaginous (slightly movable, e.g. vertebrae), and synovial (freely movable, e.g. ball-and-socket at shoulder/hip, hinge at knee/elbow).

NCERT Textbook Exercise Questions

Draw the diagrammatic representation of a sarcomere, showing different regions.
Answer: A sarcomere is the region between two successive Z-lines, containing thin actin filaments extending inward from each Z-line, and thick myosin filaments in the middle (forming the A-band). The I-band contains only thin filaments (near the Z-line), while the H-zone (in the middle of the A-band) contains only thick filaments where there is no overlap in a relaxed sarcomere.
Describe the sliding filament theory of muscle contraction.
Answer: According to the sliding filament theory, muscle contraction occurs when actin filaments slide over myosin filaments, without any change in the length of the filaments themselves. This is triggered by Ca²+ ions (released from the sarcoplasmic reticulum upon nerve stimulation) binding to troponin, which exposes myosin-binding sites on actin. Myosin heads then bind to actin, forming cross-bridges, and pull the actin filament inward using energy from ATP hydrolysis, shortening the sarcomere and the whole muscle fibre.
Name the different types of movement exhibited by the cells of the human body.
Answer: The three main types are amoeboid movement (e.g., white blood cells like macrophages and leucocytes, using pseudopodia), ciliary movement (e.g., cells lining the respiratory tract or fallopian tubes, using cilia to move particles/ova), and muscular movement (e.g., limb movement, heartbeat, powered by contraction of muscle fibres).
What is tetanus (in the context of muscle physiology, not the disease)? How is it caused?
Answer: In muscle physiology, tetanus refers to the sustained, fused contraction of a muscle that occurs when it receives a series of stimuli in such rapid succession that individual twitches merge together without any relaxation phase in between, resulting in a smooth, sustained contraction of maximal strength.

Poem-6: No Men Are Foreign 10 Questions

1. Who is the poet of 'No Men Are Foreign'?

  • A. James Kirkup
  • B. Robert Frost
  • C. W. B. Yeats
  • D. Phoebe Cary

Answer: A. James Kirkup

Explanation: 'No Men Are Foreign' is a poem composed by English poet James Kirkup emphasizing universal brotherhood.

2. What lies beneath all uniforms according to the poet?

  • A. Different hearts
  • B. A single body breathes
  • C. Strong armor
  • D. Different minds

Answer: B. A single body breathes

Explanation: "Beneath all uniforms, a single body breathes..." - regardless of national uniforms, all human beings breathe and live the same way.

3. What do all human beings share in common according to the poem?

  • A. Same sun, air, and water
  • B. Same earth upon which we walk
  • C. Same hunger during long winters of war
  • D. All of the above

Answer: D. All of the above

Explanation: The poet highlights that people all over the world share the same basic human needs, nature, and feelings.

4. What happens when we hate our brothers (people of other nations)?

  • A. We dispossess, betray, and condemn ourselves
  • B. We gain victory
  • C. We become strong
  • D. We protect our nation

Answer: A. We dispossess, betray, and condemn ourselves

Explanation: "It is ourselves that we shall dispossess, betray, condemn." Hating others ultimately hurts humanity and ourselves.

5. How do wars impact our earth?

  • A. They clean the environment
  • B. They defile and pollute the human earth
  • C. They bring prosperity
  • D. They expand green spaces

Answer: B. They defile and pollute the human earth

Explanation: "Our hells of fire and dust outrage the innocence..." War pollutes the pure air and soil shared by all people.

6. What message does the poem convey?

  • A. Universal brotherhood and peace
  • B. Importance of national borders
  • C. Glory of winning wars
  • D. Superiority of one culture over another

Answer: A. Universal brotherhood and peace

Explanation: The poet promotes global unity and harmony, urging humanity to see beyond geographical and cultural boundaries.

7. What is common about our hands and labor?

  • A. They look identical
  • B. Their lines show labor not different from our own
  • C. They cannot do hard work
  • D. They wear the same rings

Answer: B. Their lines show labor not different from our own

Explanation: "Their hands are ours, and in their lines we read / A labour not different from our own." All people work hard to live.

8. How can strength be won according to the poet?

  • A. By weapons
  • B. By love
  • C. By hatred
  • D. By deception

Answer: B. By love

Explanation: "...and strength that can be won by love." True strength comes from compassion and understanding rather than military force.

9. "Remember, no men are strange, no countries ______." Complete the line.

  • A. foreign
  • B. enemy
  • C. distant
  • D. dark

Answer: A. foreign

Explanation: The opening and closing lines of the poem state: "Remember, no men are strange, no countries foreign."

10. What lies ahead for all people after death?

  • A. Same earth where all shall lie
  • B. Different lands
  • C. Different seas
  • D. None of the above

Answer: A. Same earth where all shall lie

Explanation: "...like ours: the land our brothers walk upon / Is earth like this, in which we all shall lie." In the end, everyone returns to the same earth.

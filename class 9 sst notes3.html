NCERT Solutions for Class 9 History Chapter 3 Nazism and the Rise of Hitler

NCERT Solutions for Class 9 History Chapter 3 covers Weimar Republic, Effects of the War, Political Radicalism and Economic Crises, Hitler's Rise to Power, Destruction of Democracy, The Nazi Worldview, Youth in Nazi Germany, and Ordinary People and the Holocaust.

1. Weimar Republic, Treaty of Versailles & Economic Crisis

Short Note:

• Weimar Republic: Formed in Germany after defeat in WWI. It faced severe criticism from Germans for signing the humiliating Treaty of Versailles (loss of colonies, 13% territory, 75% iron reserves, and £6.6 billion war reparations).

• Hyperinflation (1923): Germany printed paper currency recklessly to pay reparations, leading to the collapse of the German Mark. Prices soared astronomically (a loaf of bread cost millions of Marks).

• Great Depression (1929): The crash of the Wall Street Exchange in the US led to withdrawal of short-term loans. Industrial production fell by 40%, leaving over 6 million Germans unemployed.

2. Rise of Adolf Hitler & Destruction of Democracy

Short Note:

• Hitler's Rise: Joined the German Workers' Party in 1919 and renamed it the National Socialist German Workers' Party (Nazi Party). Powerful speeches, red banners, Swastika symbol, and the Nazi salute attracted masses during economic distress.

• Fire Decree (Feb 28, 1933): Suspended civic rights like freedom of speech, press, and assembly following the Reichstag fire.

• Enabling Act (March 3, 1933): Established dictatorship in Germany, giving Hitler power to rule by decree without Parliament and banning all political parties except the Nazis.

3. Nazi Racial Worldview, Youth & Propaganda

Short Note:

• Racial Hierarchy: Based on Charles Darwin and Herbert Spencer's theories (misapplied). Nordic German Aryans were placed at the top while Jews were considered the 'undesirable' lowest race.

• Youth Organisations: Boys aged 10 joined Jungvolk, and at 14 joined Hitler Youth to learn war worship, aggression, and hatred for Jews.

• Mass Murder / Holocaust: Systematic extermination of Jews, Romani (Gypsies), Polish people, and disabled individuals in concentration camps equipped with gas chambers (e.g., Auschwitz).

Intext & Conceptual Questions

Q1: What were the main provisions of the Treaty of Versailles signed by Germany after World War I?
Answer:
1. Germany lost its overseas colonies, 10% of its population, and 13% of its European territory.
2. Germany lost 75% of its iron and 26% of its coal to France, Poland, Denmark, and Lithuania.
3. The Allied powers demilitarised Germany to weaken its strength.
4. The War Guilt Clause held Germany responsible for the war and imposed reparations of £6.6 billion.
Q2: How did the Nazi state use media and language to propagate its ideology?
Answer:
1. Coded Terminology: Mass killings were never termed 'murder'. Nazi officials used phrases like 'Special Treatment' / 'Final Solution' (for Jews), 'Euthanasia' (for disabled), and 'Evacuation' (deportation to gas chambers).
2. Mass Media Usage: Radio, posters, films, and slogans were deployed to stereotype Jews as subhuman 'vermin' or 'parasites'.
3. Popular Films: Propaganda films like The Eternal Jew were created to generate hatred and fear among citizens.

Textbook Chapter End Exercise Questions

Q1: Describe the problems faced by the Weimar Republic.
Answer:
1. Disgrace of Versailles Treaty: The Weimar Republic was held responsible for accepting the harsh terms of the Treaty of Versailles.
2. Economic Collapse & Hyperinflation: Forced to pay huge war indemnities, Germany faced severe financial collapse in 1923, destroying the value of the Mark.
3. Great Depression: After 1929, industrial production dropped, unemployment touched 6 million, and social unrest grew rapidly.
4. Political Instability: The Weimar Constitution had inherent flaws like Proportional Representation and Article 48 (Emergency Powers), leading to 20 short-lived cabinets in 14 years.
Q2: Discuss why Nazism became popular in Germany by 1932.
Answer:
Nazism became a mass movement during the Great Depression due to the following reasons:
1. Economic Distress: Widespread unemployment, poverty, and business closures left people desperate for strong leadership.
2. Hitler's Oratory Skills: Hitler promised to rebuild national pride, restore employment for youth, and undo the humiliation of Versailles.
3. Effective Propaganda: Joseph Goebbels and Nazi propaganda portrayed Hitler as a savior who would pull Germany out of crisis.
4. Fear of Communism: Middle-class citizens feared a communist takeover, viewing Nazism as a strong anti-communist defense.
Q3: Explain what role women played in Nazi society. How was it different from women's roles in the French Revolution?
Answer:
• Role in Nazi Society: Women were taught that they were radically different from men. Their primary duty was to be good mothers and rear pure-blooded Aryan children. They were awarded medals (Bronze for 4, Silver for 6, Gold for 8+ children) and given discounts. Deviation from racial norms led to public humiliation and imprisonment.
• Difference from French Revolution: During the French Revolution, women actively formed political clubs, published newspapers, demanded equal political rights, and fought for active citizenship. In contrast, Nazi ideology forced women strictly back into domestic roles, stripping away equal political rights.

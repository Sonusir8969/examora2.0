Chapter 4: Forest Society and Colonialism 30 Questions

1. When was the first Indian Forest Act passed by the British?

  • A. 1850
  • B. 1865
  • C. 1878
  • D. 1900

Answer: B. 1865

Explanation: The first Indian Forest Act was passed in 1865 to assert control over forests and limit the rights of local communities.

2. What was the main purpose of building railways by the British in India?

  • A. Transporting cotton and wood
  • B. Connecting villages for trade
  • C. Moving tourists
  • D. Establishing mail routes

Answer: A. Transporting cotton and wood

Explanation: The British built vast railway networks to transport raw materials like cotton and timber from inland India to ports for export.

3. In which region of India did the famous 'Bastar Rebellion' take place?

  • A. Punjab
  • B. Bengal
  • C. Madhya Pradesh
  • D. Maharashtra

Answer: C. Madhya Pradesh

Explanation: The Bastar rebellion took place in the Bastar region of Madhya Pradesh in 1910 against the colonial forest laws.

4. What does the term 'Swidden' agriculture refer to?

  • A. Terrace farming
  • B. Shifting cultivation
  • C. Permanent farming
  • D. Monsoon farming

Answer: B. Shifting cultivation

Explanation: Swidden agriculture involves clearing a patch of forest, farming it for a few years, and then moving to a new area to let the land regenerate.

5. Who were the 'Dangs' in the context of Indian forests?

  • A. A tribal group
  • B. The local forests
  • C. British officers
  • D. Hilly grasslands

Answer: D. Hilly grasslands

Explanation: 'Dangs' were the hilly grasslands situated in the western ghats, significant for their rich biodiversity and resources.

6. What tree was heavily harvested by the British for shipbuilding?

  • A. Banyan
  • B. Oak
  • C. Teak
  • D. Mahogany

Answer: C. Teak

Explanation: Teak wood was in high demand for shipbuilding and railway sleepers, leading to massive deforestation by the colonial rulers.

7. Which item became a luxury in the colonial market due to widespread forest destruction?

  • A. Tobacco
  • B. Sandalwood
  • C. Silk
  • D. Tea

Answer: B. Sandalwood

Explanation: Due to overexploitation by the British, sandalwood became a highly prized and rare luxury in the colonial period.

8. What was the 'Scientific Forestry' introduced by the British?

  • A. Cutting all trees for profit
  • B. Planting single species in rows
  • C. Seasonal tree planting
  • D. Organic farming methods

Answer: B. Planting single species in rows

Explanation: Scientific forestry involved planting single-species plantations like pine and teak in rows, which replaced the diverse natural forests.

9. Why did the British prohibit shifting cultivation?

  • A. It destroyed valuable timber
  • B. It used too much water
  • C. It required fewer workers
  • D. It caused soil erosion

Answer: A. It destroyed valuable timber

Explanation: The British viewed shifting cultivation as destructive to the valuable timber trees they wanted for commercial use.

10. What was the response of the tribal people to the Forest Acts?

  • A. They moved to towns
  • B. They revolted frequently
  • C. They accepted the laws
  • D. They joined the British army

Answer: B. They revolted frequently

Explanation: The restrictions imposed by forest laws sparked numerous tribal uprisings across India, such as the Bastar and Santhal rebellions.

11. The 'Forest Satyagraha' movement occurred in which year?

  • A. 1920
  • B. 1930
  • C. 1942
  • D. 1945

Answer: B. 1930

Explanation: In 1930, large-scale protests known as the Forest Satyagraha took place in the Central Provinces against the British forest laws.

12. Which tribal community is primarily associated with the Bastar rebellion?

  • A. Bhils
  • B. Gonds
  • C. Murias
  • D. Santhals

Answer: C. Murias

Explanation: The Muria tribal community played a leading role in the 1910 Bastar rebellion against the British colonial forest policies.

13. What was the primary product used by the British to build railway sleepers in India?

  • A. Bamboo
  • B. Teak and Sal
  • C. Neem
  • D. Coconut

Answer: B. Teak and Sal

Explanation: The massive expansion of the Indian railway network led to a huge demand for sleepers made from sturdy Teak and Sal woods.

14. What was the main objective of the Dutch 'Forest Law' in Java?

  • A. To protect wildlife
  • B. To provide timber for shipbuilding
  • C. To clear land for plantations
  • D. To promote tourism

Answer: C. To clear land for plantations

Explanation: The Dutch enacted forest laws to clear forests in Java to make space for profitable colonial plantations like coffee and sugarcane.

15. Who were the 'Konds' in India?

  • A. A nomadic tribe
  • B. A farming community in Odisha
  • C. A hill tribe in Northeast India
  • D. A fishing community

Answer: B. A farming community in Odisha

Explanation: The Kondhs were an indigenous tribal farming community residing in the forested hills of Odisha, often impacted by colonial laws.

16. How did the British view the Indian forests?

  • A. As a spiritual reserve
  • B. As a source of great commercial wealth
  • C. As a place for hunting
  • D. As a buffer against invasions

Answer: B. As a source of great commercial wealth

Explanation: The British viewed Indian forests as a massive untapped resource for timber and raw materials to fuel their industrial economy.

17. What was the 'Van Mahotsav' introduced by the British?

  • A. An annual tree plantation festival
  • B. A timber trading fair
  • C. A forest clearing operation
  • D. A tribal cultural event

Answer: A. An annual tree plantation festival

Explanation: The British introduced Van Mahotsav as an annual tree-planting festival, though it was often used to promote single-species plantations.

18. Which animal was primarily hunted by the British during their rule in Indian forests?

  • A. Lion
  • B. Tiger
  • C. Elephant
  • D. Rhinoceros

Answer: B. Tiger

Explanation: The British colonial elite frequently hunted tigers for sport, contributing significantly to the decline of the tiger population.

19. What was the primary complaint of the Bastar people against the British?

  • A. High taxes on land
  • B. Ban on shifting cultivation
  • C. Strict hunting laws
  • D. Forced military service

Answer: B. Ban on shifting cultivation

Explanation: The colonial ban on traditional shifting cultivation destroyed the livelihood and food supply of the Bastar tribal people, sparking the rebellion.

20. The 1878 Indian Forest Act gave the British absolute control over which forests?

  • A. Reserved forests
  • B. Protected forests
  • C. Village forests
  • D. Unclassified forests

Answer: A. Reserved forests

Explanation: The 1878 Forest Act classified forests into Reserved, Protected, and Village forests, with the British taking strict control over Reserved ones.

21. What is the primary reason for the deforestation of India's forests during the colonial period?

  • A. Population growth
  • B. Urbanization
  • C. Expansion of railways and plantations
  • D. Forest fires

Answer: C. Expansion of railways and plantations

Explanation: The massive expansion of railway networks and commercial plantations were the main drivers of wide-scale deforestation under British rule.

22. Who introduced the scientific forestry system in India?

  • A. Lord Curzon
  • B. Dietrich Brandis
  • C. Robert Clive
  • D. Warren Hastings

Answer: B. Dietrich Brandis

Explanation: Dietrich Brandis was a German forester appointed by the British to introduce scientific forest management and the forest acts in India.

23. What is the major difference between shifting and sedentary agriculture?

  • A. Shifting requires permanent land
  • B. Shifting involves moving farms periodically
  • C. Sedentary uses no water
  • D. Sedentary requires forests

Answer: B. Shifting involves moving farms periodically

Explanation: Shifting cultivation involves clearing a forest patch, farming it, and moving to a new patch, while sedentary farming uses the same land permanently.

24. In which part of India is the 'Sunderbans' forest located?

  • A. Eastern coast (Bengal)
  • B. Western Ghats
  • C. Himalayas
  • D. Central India

Answer: A. Eastern coast (Bengal)

Explanation: The Sunderbans, the largest mangrove forest in the world, is located along the delta of the Ganges, Brahmaputra, and Meghna rivers in Bengal.

25. What was the impact of the railway expansion on timber prices?

  • A. Prices dropped
  • B. Prices rose sharply
  • C. Prices stayed the same
  • D. Timber became free

Answer: B. Prices rose sharply

Explanation: The huge demand for wooden sleepers for the railways caused timber prices to skyrocket, making the forest department a profitable venture for the British.

26. What was the main forest product exploited by the British to build warships?

  • A. Bamboo
  • B. Deodar
  • C. Sal
  • D. Teak

Answer: D. Teak

Explanation: The British Royal Navy utilized high-quality teak wood from Indian forests to build strong, durable warships and commercial vessels.

27. How did the British usually dispose of the confiscated wood from forests?

  • A. Gave it to tribals
  • B. Auctioned it to European traders
  • C. Burned it
  • D. Used it in local housing

Answer: B. Auctioned it to European traders

Explanation: Wood harvested from forests under British control was auctioned off to European merchants and companies to generate maximum revenue.

28. The forest rules restricted the local people from doing what?

  • A. Grazing animals
  • B. Collecting firewood
  • C. Hunting
  • D. All of the above

Answer: D. All of the above

Explanation: The colonial forest laws imposed harsh bans on grazing livestock, collecting fuel wood, and hunting for the local and tribal communities.

29. The Dutch controlled the forests of which major Southeast Asian island?

  • A. Sumatra
  • B. Borneo
  • C. Java
  • D. Sulawesi

Answer: C. Java

Explanation: The Dutch colonial administration controlled Java and enacted strict forest laws to clear land for monoculture plantations.

30. Which movement was an example of tribal resistance against the Forest Acts?

  • A. Non-cooperation
  • B. Bastar Rebellion
  • C. Khilafat Movement
  • D. Quit India Movement

Answer: B. Bastar Rebellion

Explanation: The Bastar Rebellion of 1910 was a major tribal uprising against the British colonial Forest Acts, led by the local tribal people.

NCERT Solutions for Class 10 Geography Chapter 4 Agriculture

NCERT Solutions for Class 10 Geography Chapter 4 covers Types of Farming, Cropping Patterns (Rabi, Kharif, Zaid), Major Crops (Rice, Wheat, Millets, Commercial Crops), Technological and Institutional Reforms, and Bhoodan-Gramdan Movement.

1. Types of Farming

Short Note:

• Primitive Subsistence Farming: Slash-and-burn agriculture (Jhumming in North-Eastern states) using primitive tools like hoes and digging sticks depending on natural monsoon and soil fertility.

• Intensive Subsistence Farming: Practiced in high population density areas using high doses of biochemical inputs and irrigation for maximum production.

• Commercial Farming: Uses high yielding variety (HYV) seeds, chemical fertilizers, and pesticides for market sale. Plantation farming (tea, coffee, rubber) is a single crop commercial farming system.

2. Cropping Pattern (Rabi, Kharif & Zaid)

Short Note:

• Rabi Season: Sown in winter (Oct-Dec) and harvested in summer (April-June). Key crops: Wheat, Barley, Peas, Gram, Mustard.

• Kharif Season: Sown with the onset of monsoon (June-July) and harvested in Sep-Oct. Key crops: Paddy (Rice), Maize, Jowar, Bajra, Cotton, Jute.

• Zaid Season: Short summer season between Rabi and Kharif (March-June). Key crops: Watermelon, Muskmelon, Cucumber, Vegetables.

3. Major Food & Cash Crops of India

Short Note:

• Rice: Staple food crop; requires high temperature (>25°C) and heavy rainfall (>100 cm).

• Wheat: Second most important cereal; requires cool growing season, bright sunshine, and 50–75 cm annual rainfall.

• Millets: Coarse grains rich in nutrients (Jowar, Bajra, Ragi).

• Commercial Crops: Sugarcane (hot/humid climate), Tea (slopes with humus rich soil), Coffee (Arabica variety from Yemen), Cotton (black soil, 210 frost-free days), Jute (golden fibre).

4. Technological & Institutional Reforms

Short Note:

• Institutional Reforms: Collectivization, consolidation of holdings, abolition of Zamindari system, and land reform policies.

• Technological Reforms: Green Revolution (HYV seeds) and White Revolution (Operation Flood).

• Government Schemes: Kisaan Credit Card (KCC), Personal Accident Insurance Scheme (PAIS), Minimum Support Price (MSP), and crop insurance against weather disasters.

• Bhoodan-Gramdan: Initiated by Vinoba Bhave, also known as the Bloodless Revolution, encouraging voluntary donation of land to landless farmers.

Intext & Conceptual Questions

Q1: What is 'Slash and Burn' agriculture? Mention its different local names in India.
1. Farmers clear a patch of land by burning vegetation, cultivate crops for a few seasons, and move to a new patch when soil fertility decreases.
2. Jhumming: North-Eastern states (Assam, Meghalaya, Mizoram).
3. Pamlou: Manipur; Dipa: Bastar (Chhattisgarh) and Andaman & Nicobar Islands.
Q2: Name the three major cropping seasons of India with suitable examples.
1. Rabi: Sown in winter, harvested in summer (e.g., Wheat, Mustard).
2. Kharif: Sown with monsoon, harvested in autumn (e.g., Paddy, Maize).
3. Zaid: Short summer crop season between Rabi and Kharif (e.g., Watermelon, Cucumber).

Textbook Chapter End Exercise Questions

Q1: Describe the geographical conditions required for the growth of Rice.
Answer:
1. Temperature: Requires high temperature (above 25°C) with high humidity.
2. Rainfall: Requires annual rainfall above 100 cm; grown with canal and tube-well irrigation in drier areas.
3. Soil: Deep fertile clayey and alluvial soils that can retain water.
Q2: What are the technological and institutional reforms introduced by the Indian government to rescue agriculture?
Answer:
1. Institutional Reforms: Abolition of Zamindari, consolidation of small land holdings, and establishment of Grameen Banks/Cooperative societies for easy credit.
2. Technological Reforms: Introduction of High Yielding Varieties (HYV) of seeds, chemical fertilizers, modern farm machinery, and drip irrigation.
3. Financial & Social Safety: Provision of Kisaan Credit Card (KCC), crop insurance against droughts and floods, and announcement of Minimum Support Price (MSP).
Q3: Differentiate between Commercial Farming and Intensive Subsistence Farming.
Answer:
1. Commercial Farming: Practiced on large land holdings using high doses of modern inputs (HYV seeds, fertilizers) primarily for profit and sale in markets.
2. Intensive Subsistence Farming: Practiced on small, fragmented holdings in densely populated areas using labor-intensive methods to meet family consumption requirements.

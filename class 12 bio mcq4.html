Ch-4: Principles of Inheritance and Variation 12 Questions

1. Gregor Mendel conducted his classical genetic experiments primarily on:

  • A. Garden pea (Pisum sativum)
  • B. Fruit fly (Drosophila)
  • C. Maize
  • D. Human pedigrees

Answer: A. Garden pea (Pisum sativum)

Explanation: Mendel chose the garden pea for his experiments due to its easily observable contrasting traits and short generation time.

2. Mendel's Law of Segregation states that:

  • A. The two alleles of a gene separate from each other during gamete formation
  • B. Genes located on different chromosomes assort independently
  • C. All traits observed are always dominant
  • D. Genetic traits always blend together in offspring

Answer: A. The two alleles of a gene separate from each other during gamete formation

Explanation: This law explains that each gamete receives only one allele of a gene pair, ensuring the two alleles segregate cleanly during meiosis.

3. In a dihybrid cross between two heterozygous parents, the typical phenotypic ratio observed in the F2 generation is:

  • A. 9:3:3:1
  • B. 3:1
  • C. 1:2:1
  • D. 1:1:1:1

Answer: A. 9:3:3:1

Explanation: This characteristic ratio arises from the independent assortment of two different gene pairs, each following Mendelian segregation.

4. A cross between an F1 heterozygote and a homozygous recessive parent is specifically called a:

  • A. Test cross
  • B. Dihybrid cross
  • C. Reciprocal cross
  • D. Back cross to the dominant parent

Answer: A. Test cross

Explanation: A test cross is used to determine the genotype of an individual showing a dominant phenotype, by crossing it with a homozygous recessive individual.

5. Sex determination in humans is primarily based on the presence of:

  • A. X and Y chromosomes
  • B. Only autosomes
  • C. Only X chromosomes
  • D. Hormone levels alone

Answer: A. X and Y chromosomes

Explanation: In humans, XX individuals typically develop as female and XY individuals typically develop as male, based on the sex chromosome combination.

6. Haemophilia, a well-known bleeding disorder, is an example of a:

  • A. Sex-linked recessive disorder
  • B. Autosomal dominant disorder
  • C. Autosomal recessive disorder
  • D. Y-linked disorder

Answer: A. Sex-linked recessive disorder

Explanation: The gene for haemophilia is located on the X chromosome and behaves as a recessive trait, making it more common in males.

7. Down's syndrome in humans is primarily caused by:

  • A. Trisomy of chromosome 21
  • B. Deletion of chromosome 21
  • C. Trisomy of the X chromosome
  • D. Monosomy of chromosome 21

Answer: A. Trisomy of chromosome 21

Explanation: Down's syndrome results from the presence of an extra copy of chromosome 21, giving affected individuals a total of 47 chromosomes.

8. Pedigree analysis is a technique primarily used to study the:

  • A. Inheritance pattern of a specific trait within a family
  • B. DNA sequence of an organism
  • C. Overall process of gamete formation
  • D. Physical structure of a chromosome

Answer: A. Inheritance pattern of a specific trait within a family

Explanation: By tracing a trait through several generations of a family, pedigree analysis helps determine whether it is dominant, recessive, or sex-linked.

9. Incomplete dominance results in a hybrid phenotype that is:

  • A. Intermediate between the two parental phenotypes
  • B. Identical to only one of the two parents
  • C. A blend that eventually reverses over generations
  • D. Never actually observed in nature

Answer: A. Intermediate between the two parental phenotypes

Explanation: In incomplete dominance, neither allele is fully dominant, resulting in offspring showing a blended intermediate trait.

10. The ABO blood group system in humans is a classic example of:

  • A. Multiple allelism
  • B. Sex-linked inheritance
  • C. Incomplete dominance exclusively
  • D. Polygenic inheritance exclusively

Answer: A. Multiple allelism

Explanation: The ABO blood group is controlled by three alleles (IA, IB, and i) present at a single gene locus, illustrating multiple allelism.

11. Linked genes are genes that are located:

  • A. On the same chromosome, and tend to be inherited together
  • B. On entirely different chromosomes
  • C. Only within mitochondrial DNA
  • D. Only on the sex chromosomes

Answer: A. On the same chromosome, and tend to be inherited together

Explanation: Since linked genes lie close together on the same chromosome, they tend to be transmitted together during inheritance, deviating from independent assortment.

12. A mutation is best described as a:

  • A. Sudden, heritable change occurring in the genetic material
  • B. Non-heritable change limited to the phenotype only
  • C. Change that can only result from crossing over
  • D. A type of dominant trait exclusively

Answer: A. Sudden, heritable change occurring in the genetic material

Explanation: Mutations are permanent alterations in the DNA sequence that can be passed on to offspring and serve as a source of genetic variation.

Ch-6: Equilibrium 30 Questions

1. Which relation holds true for equilibrium constants Kp and Kc?

  • A. Kp = Kc (RT)^Δn
  • B. Kc = Kp (RT)^Δn
  • C. Kp = Kc / (RT)^Δn
  • D. Kp = Kc + RT

Answer: A. Kp = Kc (RT)^Δn

Explanation: Kp and Kc are related by Kp = Kc (RT)^Δn, where Δn is the difference in moles of gaseous products and reactants.

2. According to Bronsted-Lowry concept, an acid is a substance which:

  • A. Donates a proton (H⁺)
  • B. Accepts a proton (H⁺)
  • C. Accepts an electron pair
  • D. Donates an OH⁻ ion

Answer: A. Donates a proton (H⁺)

Explanation: Bronsted-Lowry theory defines an acid as a proton donor and a base as a proton acceptor.

3. What is the conjugate base of H₂SO₄?

  • A. HSO₄⁻
  • B. SO₄²⁻
  • C. H₃O⁺
  • D. SO₃

Answer: A. HSO₄⁻

Explanation: Removing one proton (H⁺) from H₂SO₄ yields its conjugate base, HSO₄⁻.

4. Which of the following acts as a Lewis acid?

  • A. BF₃
  • B. NH₃
  • C. H₂O
  • D. Cl⁻

Answer: A. BF₃

Explanation: BF₃ has an incomplete octet on boron, making it an electron pair acceptor (Lewis acid).

5. The pH of a neutral aqueous solution at 25 °C (298 K) is:

  • A. 7
  • B. 0
  • C. 14
  • D. 1

Answer: A. 7

Explanation: At 298 K, [H⁺] = [OH⁻] = 10⁻⁷ M, so pH = -log(10⁻⁷) = 7.

6. According to Le Chatelier's principle, adding an inert gas at constant volume to a dynamic equilibrium system will:

  • A. Have no effect on equilibrium
  • B. Shift equilibrium forward
  • C. Shift equilibrium backward
  • D. Double the reaction rate

Answer: A. Have no effect on equilibrium

Explanation: Adding inert gas at constant volume does not change the partial pressures or concentrations of the reacting species.

7. The ionic product of pure water (Kw) at 298 K is:

  • A. 1.0 × 10⁻¹⁴ M²
  • B. 1.0 × 10⁻⁷ M²
  • C. 1.0 × 10⁻¹⁰ M²
  • D. 1.0 × 10⁻¹ M²

Answer: A. 1.0 × 10⁻¹⁴ M²

Explanation: Kw = [H⁺][OH⁻] = (10⁻⁷)(10⁻⁷) = 1.0 × 10⁻¹⁴ M² at 25 °C.

8. A buffer solution is a solution that resists changes in:

  • A. pH value
  • B. Temperature
  • C. Volume
  • D. Pressure

Answer: A. pH value

Explanation: Buffer solutions maintain a nearly constant pH upon the addition of small amounts of acid or base.

9. Which of the following forms an acidic buffer solution?

  • A. CH₃COOH + CH₃COONa
  • B. NH₄OH + NH₄Cl
  • C. HCl + NaCl
  • D. NaOH + NaCl

Answer: A. CH₃COOH + CH₃COONa

Explanation: An acidic buffer contains a weak acid (CH₃COOH) and its salt with a strong base (CH₃COONa).

10. If reaction quotient Q < Kc, the reaction will proceed in:

  • A. Forward direction
  • B. Backward direction
  • C. Reverse direction only
  • D. Neither direction (at equilibrium)

Answer: A. Forward direction

Explanation: When Q < Kc, reactants convert to products to reach equilibrium, shifting the reaction forward.

11. What is the expression for solubility product (Ksp) of AgCl?

  • A. [Ag⁺][Cl⁻]
  • B. [Ag⁺] / [Cl⁻]
  • C. [Ag⁺]²[Cl⁻]
  • D. [Ag⁺][Cl⁻]²

Answer: A. [Ag⁺][Cl⁻]

Explanation: For AgCl(s) ⇌ Ag⁺(aq) + Cl⁻(aq), the solubility product constant Ksp = [Ag⁺][Cl⁻].

12. For an exothermic reaction, increasing temperature shifts equilibrium to the:

  • A. Left (Backward)
  • B. Right (Forward)
  • C. Remains unchanged
  • D. Complete conversion

Answer: A. Left (Backward)

Explanation: Heat is a product in exothermic reactions; adding heat causes the equilibrium to shift left to absorb excess energy.

13. The pH of 0.01 M HCl solution is:

  • A. 2
  • B. 1
  • C. 12
  • D. 7

Answer: A. 2

Explanation: Strong acid HCl fully dissociates: [H⁺] = 0.01 M = 10⁻² M. Thus, pH = -log(10⁻²) = 2.

14. Common ion effect causes the degree of dissociation of a weak electrolyte to:

  • A. Decrease
  • B. Increase
  • C. Remain constant
  • D. Become zero completely

Answer: A. Decrease

Explanation: Adding a strong electrolyte containing a common ion shifts equilibrium backward, suppressing ionization of the weak electrolyte.

15. An aqueous solution of Sodium Acetate (CH₃COONa) is:

  • A. Basic
  • B. Acidic
  • C. Neutral
  • D. Amphoteric

Answer: A. Basic

Explanation: CH₃COONa is a salt of a weak acid and strong base; anion hydrolysis yields excess OH⁻ ions (pH > 7).

16. The units of Kc for the reaction N₂(g) + 3H₂(g) ⇌ 2NH₃(g) are:

  • A. L² mol⁻²
  • B. mol L⁻¹
  • C. mol² L⁻²
  • D. Dimensionless

Answer: A. L² mol⁻²

Explanation: Unit = (mol L⁻¹)^Δn = (mol L⁻¹)^(2-4) = (mol L⁻¹)⁻² = L² mol⁻².

17. Which of the following is amphoteric in nature?

  • A. H₂O
  • B. HCl
  • C. NaOH
  • D. NH₄⁺

Answer: A. H₂O

Explanation: Water can donate a proton to form OH⁻ (acting as an acid) or accept a proton to form H₃O⁺ (acting as a base).

18. Salt hydrolysis of a strong acid and strong base salt (e.g., NaCl) produces a solution with pH:

  • A. Equal to 7
  • B. Greater than 7
  • C. Less than 7
  • D. Equal to 0

Answer: A. Equal to 7

Explanation: Neither cation nor anion hydrolyzes in water for a salt of a strong acid and strong base, keeping pH neutral at 7.

19. If the solubility of AB₂ salt is 'S' mol/L, its solubility product Ksp is:

  • A. 4S³
  • B. S²
  • C. 27S⁴
  • D. 2S²

Answer: A. 4S³

Explanation: AB₂(s) ⇌ A²⁺ + 2B⁻. Ksp = [A²⁺][B⁻]² = (S)(2S)² = 4S³.

20. In Haber's process (N₂ + 3H₂ ⇌ 2NH₃ + heat), high yield of ammonia is favored by:

  • A. High pressure and low temperature
  • B. Low pressure and high temperature
  • C. Low pressure and low temperature
  • D. High pressure and high temperature

Answer: A. High pressure and low temperature

Explanation: High pressure shifts equilibrium toward fewer gas moles (NH₃), and low temperature favors the exothermic reaction.

21. The relationship between pH and pOH at 298 K is:

  • A. pH + pOH = 14
  • B. pH - pOH = 14
  • C. pH × pOH = 14
  • D. pH / pOH = 14

Answer: A. pH + pOH = 14

Explanation: Taking -log on both sides of Kw = [H⁺][OH⁻] = 10⁻¹⁴ gives pH + pOH = 14.

22. What happens to the pH of water when temperature is increased?

  • A. Decreases
  • B. Increases
  • C. Remains constant
  • D. Becomes 14

Answer: A. Decreases

Explanation: Dissociation of water is endothermic, so Kw increases with temperature, raising [H⁺] and lowering pH (though water remains neutral).

23. Henderson-Hasselbalch equation for an acidic buffer is:

  • A. pH = pKa + log([Salt] / [Acid])
  • B. pH = pKa - log([Salt] / [Acid])
  • C. pH = pKb + log([Salt] / [Base])
  • D. pH = pKa + log([Acid] / [Salt])

Answer: A. pH = pKa + log([Salt] / [Acid])

Explanation: The Henderson-Hasselbalch equation relates pH to pKa and logarithmic ratio of conjugate base to acid.

24. Catalyst added to a reversible reaction at equilibrium:

  • A. Increases rates of both forward and backward reactions equally
  • B. Increases only forward reaction rate
  • C. Shifts equilibrium toward products
  • D. Changes equilibrium constant value

Answer: A. Increases rates of both forward and backward reactions equally

Explanation: A catalyst lowers activation energy for both directions equally without shifting equilibrium position or altering K.

25. Precipitation of a salt takes place when ionic product (Qsp) is:

  • A. Greater than solubility product (Ksp)
  • B. Less than solubility product (Ksp)
  • C. Equal to solubility product (Ksp)
  • D. Zero

Answer: A. Greater than solubility product (Ksp)

Explanation: When ionic product exceeds Ksp, the solution becomes supersaturated and excess solute precipitates out.

26. Which of the following is a strong electrolyte?

  • A. NaCl
  • B. CH₃COOH
  • C. NH₄OH
  • D. H₂CO₃

Answer: A. NaCl

Explanation: Sodium chloride completely dissociates into Na⁺ and Cl⁻ ions in aqueous solution.

27. Ostwald's Dilution Law is applicable to:

  • A. Weak electrolytes only
  • B. Strong electrolytes only
  • C. Non-electrolytes only
  • D. Both strong and weak electrolytes

Answer: A. Weak electrolytes only

Explanation: Ostwald's law applies to weak electrolytes where partial dissociation equilibrium is established.

28. The conjugate acid of NH₃ is:

  • A. NH₄⁺
  • B. NH₂⁻
  • C. N³⁻
  • D. NH₄OH

Answer: A. NH₄⁺

Explanation: Addition of a proton (H⁺) to NH₃ gives its conjugate acid NH₄⁺.

29. The value of equilibrium constant for a reaction depends on:

  • A. Temperature
  • B. Initial concentrations
  • C. Pressure
  • D. Presence of catalyst

Answer: A. Temperature

Explanation: For a given chemical reaction, the equilibrium constant K is a constant that changes only with temperature.

30. An aqueous solution of Ammonium Chloride (NH₄Cl) is acidic because of:

  • A. Cationic hydrolysis
  • B. Anionic hydrolysis
  • C. Both cationic and anionic hydrolysis
  • D. No hydrolysis

Answer: A. Cationic hydrolysis

Explanation: NH₄⁺ from weak base hydrolyzes with water to release H⁺ ions, making the solution acidic (pH < 7).

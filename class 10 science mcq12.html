Ch-12: Magnetic Effects of Electric Current 30 Questions

1. Who first discovered the magnetic effect of electric current in 1820?

  • A. Michael Faraday
  • B. Hans Christian Oersted
  • C. James Clerk Maxwell
  • D. André-Marie Ampère

Answer: B. Hans Christian Oersted

Explanation: Oersted noticed that a magnetic compass needle deflected when an electric current flowed through a nearby metallic wire.

2. Magnetic field lines emerge from which pole and enter at which pole outside a bar magnet?

  • A. Emerge from South Pole, enter at North Pole
  • B. Emerge from North Pole, enter at South Pole
  • C. Emerge from Center, enter at Poles
  • D. Emerge from East Pole, enter at West Pole

Answer: B. Emerge from North Pole, enter at South Pole

Explanation: Outside a magnet, field lines emerge from the North pole and merge at the South pole, forming continuous closed loops.

3. Inside a bar magnet, the direction of magnetic field lines is from:

  • A. North Pole to South Pole
  • B. South Pole to North Pole
  • C. Center to edges
  • D. Top to bottom

Answer: B. South Pole to North Pole

Explanation: Since magnetic field lines form continuous closed loops, inside the magnet they travel from the South pole to the North pole.

4. The degree of closeness of magnetic field lines indicates the:

  • A. Direction of magnetic field
  • B. Temperature of magnet
  • C. Relative strength of the magnetic field
  • D. Speed of electric current

Answer: C. Relative strength of the magnetic field

Explanation: Closer field lines mean a stronger magnetic field. That is why the field is strongest near the poles of a magnet.

5. Why do two magnetic field lines never intersect each other?

  • A. Because magnetic forces cancel out
  • B. Because at the point of intersection, a compass needle would point in two directions simultaneously, which is impossible
  • C. Because field lines are straight parallel lines
  • D. Because magnetic field is a scalar quantity

Answer: B. Because at the point of intersection, a compass needle would point in two directions simultaneously, which is impossible

Explanation: Tangent to a field line gives magnetic direction. Intersection implies two distinct directions at one single point.

6. The shape of magnetic field lines around a straight current-carrying conductor consists of:

  • A. Parallel straight lines
  • B. Concentric circles
  • C. Elliptical loops
  • D. Hyperbolic curves

Answer: B. Concentric circles

Explanation: Magnetic field lines around a long straight current-carrying wire form concentric circles centered on the wire.

7. Which rule is used to find the direction of magnetic field lines around a straight current-carrying conductor?

  • A. Fleming's Left-Hand Rule
  • B. Fleming's Right-Hand Rule
  • C. Right-Hand Thumb Rule
  • D. Ampere's Swimming Rule

Answer: C. Right-Hand Thumb Rule

Explanation: Pointing the right thumb along electric current causes curled fingers to show the circular magnetic field's direction.

8. The magnetic field strength near a current-carrying circular loop increases when:

  • A. The current is decreased
  • B. The radius of the loop is increased
  • C. The number of turns in the coil is increased
  • D. The loop is placed in vacuum

Answer: C. The number of turns in the coil is increased

Explanation: Magnetic field produced by an n-turn coil is n times as large as that produced by a single turn, as fields add up.

9. A long cylindrical coil of many insulated copper wire turns wrapped closely in the shape of a cylinder is called a:

  • A. Solenoid
  • B. Rheostat
  • C. Galvanometer
  • D. Commutator

Answer: A. Solenoid

Explanation: A current-carrying solenoid produces a magnetic field pattern identical to that of a bar magnet.

10. The magnetic field inside a long straight current-carrying solenoid is:

  • A. Zero
  • B. Uniform (same at all points)
  • C. Decreases towards the center
  • D. Different at every point

Answer: B. Uniform (same at all points)

Explanation: Inside a solenoid, field lines are parallel straight lines, indicating a uniform magnetic field.

11. A soft iron core placed inside a current-carrying solenoid forms a temporary magnet known as an:

  • A. Permanent magnet
  • B. Electromagnet
  • C. Bar magnet
  • D. Isotope

Answer: B. Electromagnet

Explanation: Soft iron easily magnetizes when current flows and demagnetizes quickly when current stops, forming an electromagnet.

12. The magnitude of force experienced by a current-carrying conductor in a magnetic field is maximum when the angle between conductor and field is:

  • A. 0°
  • B. 45°
  • C. 90°
  • D. 180°

Answer: C. 90°

Explanation: Magnetic force F = I L B sin(θ). Maximum force occurs when current is perpendicular (90°) to the magnetic field.

13. Which rule gives the direction of mechanical force acting on a current-carrying conductor in a magnetic field?

  • A. Fleming's Left-Hand Rule
  • B. Fleming's Right-Hand Rule
  • C. Right-Hand Thumb Rule
  • D. Maxwell's Corkscrew Rule

Answer: A. Fleming's Left-Hand Rule

Explanation: Forefinger = Field, Middle finger = Current, Thumb = Motion/Force (FBI / Father-Mother-Child rule).

14. In Fleming's Left-Hand Rule, the forefinger points in the direction of the:

  • A. Electric Current
  • B. Motion of conductor
  • C. Magnetic Field
  • D. Induced Current

Answer: C. Magnetic Field

Explanation: Forefinger represents Field, Middle finger represents Current, and Thumb represents Force (Motion).

15. An electron enters a magnetic field at right angles to it. The direction of force acting on the electron will be:

  • A. In direction of motion of electron
  • B. Opposite to motion of electron
  • C. Into the page / perpendicular to both field and conventional current
  • D. Out of the page

Answer: C. Into the page / perpendicular to both field and conventional current

Explanation: Conventional current direction is taken opposite to electron flow. Applying Fleming's left-hand rule yields force directed into the page.

16. The SI unit of magnetic field strength (magnetic flux density) is:

  • A. Weber
  • B. Tesla (T)
  • C. Henry
  • D. Gauss

Answer: B. Tesla (T)

Explanation: Named in honor of Nikola Tesla, the SI unit for magnetic field intensity is Tesla (T).

17. In domestic electric circuits, the live wire is usually covered with insulation of color:

  • A. Green
  • B. Black
  • C. Red or Brown
  • D. Yellow

Answer: C. Red or Brown

Explanation: According to standard wire color coding, live wire is red/brown, neutral wire is black/blue, and earth wire is green/yellow.

18. In India, the potential difference between live wire and neutral wire in domestic AC supply is:

  • A. 110 V
  • B. 220 V
  • C. 440 V
  • D. 12 V

Answer: B. 220 V

Explanation: Domestic mains electricity supply in India is alternating current (AC) at 220 V and 50 Hz frequency.

19. What is the frequency of alternating current (AC) supplied for domestic use in India?

  • A. 60 Hz
  • B. 50 Hz
  • C. 100 Hz
  • D. 0 Hz

Answer: B. 50 Hz

Explanation: 50 Hz frequency means the alternating current reverses its direction 100 times every second.

20. Earth wire is connected to metallic bodies of high power appliances to prevent:

  • A. Overheating of wires
  • B. Severe electric shock due to current leakage
  • C. Short circuiting
  • D. High energy consumption

Answer: B. Severe electric shock due to current leakage

Explanation: Earthing provides a low-resistance path to the ground, safely diverting leak current and protecting users.

21. A condition when live wire and neutral wire come in direct contact with each other due to damaged insulation is called:

  • A. Overloading
  • B. Earthing
  • C. Short Circuiting
  • D. Electromagnetic Induction

Answer: C. Short Circuiting

Explanation: Direct contact causes resistance to drop to near zero, causing a dangerously high surge of current known as short circuiting.

22. Connecting too many heavy electrical appliances to a single socket simultaneously causes:

  • A. Short circuit
  • B. Overloading
  • C. Induction
  • D. High voltage

Answer: B. Overloading

Explanation: Overloading happens when total drawn current exceeds the safe capacity limit of the circuit wiring.

23. An electric fuse wire must possess:

  • A. High resistance and high melting point
  • B. Low resistance and low melting point
  • C. High resistance and low melting point
  • D. Low resistance and high melting point

Answer: C. High resistance and low melting point

Explanation: High resistance produces heating quickly during excess current, while low melting point allows it to melt and sever the circuit.

24. What happens to the force on a current-carrying wire in a magnetic field if the magnetic field strength is doubled?

  • A. Force is halved
  • B. Force remains unchanged
  • C. Force is doubled
  • D. Force becomes quadrupled

Answer: C. Force is doubled

Explanation: Magnetic force F is directly proportional to magnetic field strength B (F ∝ B).

25. A current flowing vertically downwards in a straight conductor produces a magnetic field around it in which direction (seen from above)?

  • A. Clockwise
  • B. Anti-clockwise
  • C. Upward
  • D. Downward

Answer: A. Clockwise

Explanation: By Right-Hand Thumb Rule, pointing thumb downwards curls fingers clockwise as viewed from top.

26. A positively charged particle (alpha particle) projected towards west is deflected towards north by a magnetic field. The direction of magnetic field is:

  • A. Towards South
  • B. Towards East
  • C. Downward
  • D. Upward

Answer: D. Upward

Explanation: Current is West (+ve particle direction), Force is North. Applying Fleming's Left-Hand Rule, magnetic field points upward.

27. Which of the following material cannot be magnetized easily to make a permanent magnet?

  • A. Steel
  • B. Alnico
  • C. Soft Iron
  • D. Nipermag

Answer: C. Soft Iron

Explanation: Soft iron loses its magnetism quickly when current is removed, making it suitable for electromagnets rather than permanent magnets.

28. The magnetic field at the center of a circular current-carrying coil is:

  • A. Circular
  • B. Straight parallel lines perpendicular to the plane of loop
  • C. Zero
  • D. Elliptical

Answer: B. Straight parallel lines perpendicular to the plane of loop

Explanation: As we move towards the center of a circular current loop, arcs of circular field lines appear as straight parallel lines.

29. An electric fuse works on which effect of electric current?

  • A. Magnetic effect
  • B. Chemical effect
  • C. Heating effect
  • D. Optical effect

Answer: C. Heating effect

Explanation: Fuse relies on Joule heating (H = I^2 Rt) to melt the lead-tin alloy wire when current exceeds safe limit.

30. Commercial electric motors do not use:

  • A. An electromagnet to rotate the armature
  • B. Effectively large number of turns of conducting wire
  • C. Permanent magnets to rotate the armature
  • D. A soft iron core on which coil is wound

Answer: C. Permanent magnets to rotate the armature

Explanation: Commercial motors use powerful electromagnets instead of permanent magnets to achieve much higher rotational power.

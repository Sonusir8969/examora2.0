Ch-9: Hydrocarbons 30 Questions

1. What is the general formula of open-chain alkanes?

  • A. CₙH₂ₙ₊₂
  • B. CₙH₂ₙ
  • C. CₙH₂ₙ₋₂
  • D. CₙH₂ₙ₋₆

Answer: A. CₙH₂ₙ₊₂

Explanation: Alkanes are saturated acyclic hydrocarbons with the general molecular formula CₙH₂ₙ₊₂.

2. Wurtz reaction involves heating an alkyl halide with sodium metal in dry ether to form:

  • A. Higher alkane containing even number of carbon atoms
  • B. Alkenes
  • C. Alkynes
  • D. Alcohols

Answer: A. Higher alkane containing even number of carbon atoms

Explanation: 2R-X + 2Na → R-R + 2NaX; Wurtz reaction couples two alkyl halides to synthesize symmetrical higher alkanes.

3. Which of the following alkanes cannot be prepared in good yield by Wurtz reaction?

  • A. Methane (CH₄)
  • B. Ethane (C₂H₆)
  • C. n-Butane (C₄H₁₀)
  • D. n-Hexane (C₆H₁₄)

Answer: A. Methane (CH₄)

Explanation: Wurtz reaction requires coupling of at least two alkyl groups, so methane (1 carbon) cannot be prepared.

4. Heating sodium ethanoate (CH₃COONa) with soda lime (NaOH + CaO) produces:

  • A. Methane
  • B. Ethane
  • C. Propane
  • D. Ethene

Answer: A. Methane

Explanation: Decarboxylation of sodium ethanoate yields methane: CH₃COONa + NaOH → CH₄ + Na₂CO₃.

5. According to Markovnikov's rule, addition of unsymmetrical reagent (like HBr) to unsymmetrical alkene adds the negative part to:

  • A. Carbon atom with fewer hydrogen atoms
  • B. Carbon atom with more hydrogen atoms
  • C. Any carbon atom randomly
  • D. Oxygen atom

Answer: A. Carbon atom with fewer hydrogen atoms

Explanation: Markovnikov's rule states that the negative addendum attaches to the double-bonded carbon containing fewer hydrogen atoms.

6. Anti-Markovnikov addition of HBr to alkenes occurs in the presence of:

  • A. Organic Peroxides
  • B. Anhydrous AlCl₃
  • C. Concentrated H₂SO₄
  • D. Nickel catalyst

Answer: A. Organic Peroxides

Explanation: Kharasch (peroxide) effect causes free radical addition of HBr to yield the anti-Markovnikov product.

7. Ozonolysis of ethene followed by reduction with Zn/H₂O yields:

  • A. Methanal (Formaldehyde)
  • B. Ethanal (Acetaldehyde)
  • C. Methanol
  • D. Formic acid

Answer: A. Methanal (Formaldehyde)

Explanation: CH₂=CH₂ + O₃ → Ozonide → 2 HCHO (methanal) upon reductive cleavage with Zn/H₂O.

8. What is the geometry around double-bonded carbon atoms in alkenes?

  • A. Trigonal planar
  • B. Tetrahedral
  • C. Linear
  • D. Octahedral

Answer: A. Trigonal planar

Explanation: Double-bonded carbon atoms are sp² hybridized with bond angles of ~120° and a planar structure.

9. Which of the following hydro-carbons displays acidic hydrogens capable of reacting with sodium metal?

  • A. Ethyne (Acetylene)
  • B. Ethane
  • C. Ethene
  • D. Benzene

Answer: A. Ethyne (Acetylene)

Explanation: Terminal alkynes contain sp-hybridized carbons with high s-character (50%), making attached hydrogens acidic.

10. Cyclic polymerization of ethyne (C₂H₂) by passing through a red-hot iron tube at 873 K forms:

  • A. Benzene
  • B. Cyclohexane
  • C. Toluene
  • D. Cyclooctatetraene

Answer: A. Benzene

Explanation: Three ethyne molecules polymerize cyclically to form benzene: 3 C₂H₂ → C₆H₆.

11. According to Huckel's rule, a planar monocyclic conjugated ring system is aromatic if it contains:

  • A. (4n + 2) π electrons
  • B. 4n π electrons
  • C. (2n + 2) π electrons
  • D. (n + 2) π electrons

Answer: A. (4n + 2) π electrons

Explanation: Huckel's rule defines aromaticity based on planar conjugated rings possessing (4n + 2) π electrons, where n is an integer.

12. Benzene reacts with CH₃Cl in the presence of anhydrous AlCl₃ to form toluene. This reaction is known as:

  • A. Friedel-Crafts Alkylation
  • B. Friedel-Crafts Acylation
  • C. Wurtz-Fittig Reaction
  • D. Reimer-Tiemann Reaction

Answer: A. Friedel-Crafts Alkylation

Explanation: Electrophilic aromatic substitution of benzene with an alkyl halide using Lewis acid (AlCl₃) is Friedel-Crafts alkylation.

13. What catalyst is used in Nitration of Benzene alongside concentrated HNO₃?

  • A. Concentrated H₂SO₄
  • B. Anhydrous AlCl₃
  • C. FeCl₃
  • D. Pt catalyst

Answer: A. Concentrated H₂SO₄

Explanation: A nitrating mixture consists of conc. HNO₃ and conc. H₂SO₄ to generate the nitronium electrophile (NO₂⁺).

14. Which functional group is ortho- and para-directing in aromatic electrophilic substitution?

  • A. -OH group
  • B. -NO₂ group
  • C. -COOH group
  • D. -CHO group

Answer: A. -OH group

Explanation: Electron-donating groups with lone pairs like -OH increase electron density at ortho/para positions via resonance.

15. Which substituent is strongly meta-directing and deactivating on a benzene ring?

  • A. -NO₂
  • B. -CH₃
  • C. -NH₂
  • D. -OCH₃

Answer: A. -NO₂

Explanation: Strong electron-withdrawing groups like -NO₂ deactivate the ring and direct incoming electrophiles to the meta position.

16. Conformations of ethane which have maximum and minimum potential energy are respectively:

  • A. Eclipsed and Staggered
  • B. Staggered and Eclipsed
  • C. Skew and Eclipsed
  • D. Chair and Boat

Answer: A. Eclipsed and Staggered

Explanation: Eclipsed conformation has maximum torsional strain (highest energy), while staggered conformation has minimal repulsion (lowest energy).

17. Addition of water to ethyne in the presence of H₂SO₄ and HgSO₄ at 333 K yields:

  • A. Ethanal (Acetaldehyde)
  • B. Ethanol
  • C. Methanal
  • D. Ethanoic acid

Answer: A. Ethanal (Acetaldehyde)

Explanation: Hydration of ethyne forms vinyl alcohol intermediate, which tautomerizes to ethanal (CH₃CHO).

18. Lindlar's catalyst used for partial hydrogenation of alkynes to cis-alkenes is:

  • A. Palladised charcoal deactivated with BaSO₄ or quinoline
  • B. Sodium in liquid ammonia
  • C. Raney Nickel
  • D. Pt in acidic medium

Answer: A. Palladised charcoal deactivated with BaSO₄ or quinoline

Explanation: Partially poisoned Pd/CaCO₃ (or BaSO₄) controls reduction of alkynes specifically to cis-alkenes.

19. Reduction of alkynes with Sodium in liquid Ammonia (Birch reduction) produces:

  • A. trans-Alkenes
  • B. cis-Alkenes
  • C. Alkanes
  • D. Aromatic compounds

Answer: A. trans-Alkenes

Explanation: Na/liquid NH₃ stereospecifically reduces non-terminal alkynes to trans-alkenes.

20. Ozonolysis of propene followed by Zn/H₂O gives:

  • A. Ethanal and Methanal
  • B. Two molecules of Ethanal
  • C. Propanone and Methanal
  • D. Propanal and Water

Answer: A. Ethanal and Methanal

Explanation: Cleavage of CH₃-CH=CH₂ double bond gives CH₃CHO (ethanal) and HCHO (methanal).

21. The chemical test used to distinguish between alkanes and alkenes is:

  • A. Bromine water test
  • B. Tollen's test
  • C. Fehling's test
  • D. Biuret test

Answer: A. Bromine water test

Explanation: Unsaturated alkenes decolorize reddish-brown bromine water due to addition reactions, whereas alkanes do not react in dark.

22. What product is formed when Benzene is treated with excess Cl₂ in presence of anhydrous AlCl₃ in dark?

  • A. Hexachlorobenzene (C₆Cl₆)
  • B. Benzene hexachloride (BHC)
  • C. Chlorobenzene
  • D. Dichlorobenzene

Answer: A. Hexachlorobenzene (C₆Cl₆)

Explanation: In presence of anhydrous AlCl₃ catalyst in dark, all six aromatic hydrogens are substituted by chlorine to give hexachlorobenzene.

23. Gammaxane (Lindane) is chemically known as:

  • A. Benzene hexachloride (1,2,3,4,5,6-hexachlorocyclohexane)
  • B. Hexachlorobenzene
  • C. Chlorobenzene
  • D. DDT

Answer: A. Benzene hexachloride (1,2,3,4,5,6-hexachlorocyclohexane)

Explanation: Addition of 3 molecules of Cl₂ to benzene in presence of UV light forms BHC (Gammaxane).

24. What is the major product of Dehydrohalogenation of 2-bromobutane with alcoholic KOH?

  • A. But-2-ene
  • B. But-1-ene
  • C. Butan-2-ol
  • D. Butane

Answer: A. But-2-ene

Explanation: According to Saytzeff's rule, elimination yields the more substituted, stable alkene (But-2-ene) as the major product.

25. Kolbe's electrolytic method of sodium acetate solution yields which gas at the anode?

  • A. Ethane and CO₂
  • B. Methane and O₂
  • C. Hydrogen only
  • D. Ethene and CO

Answer: A. Ethane and CO₂

Explanation: Electrolysis of acetate ions (CH₃COO⁻) produces CH₃-CH₃ and CO₂ at the anode via methyl radical dimerisation.

26. Which conformer of cyclohexane is the most stable?

  • A. Chair form
  • B. Boat form
  • C. Twist-boat form
  • D. Half-chair form

Answer: A. Chair form

Explanation: The chair conformation of cyclohexane is free of angle and torsional strain, rendering it most stable.

27. What is the number of sigma (σ) and pi (π) bonds present in a Benzene molecule?

  • A. 12 σ and 3 π
  • B. 6 σ and 3 π
  • C. 9 σ and 3 π
  • D. 12 σ and 6 π

Answer: A. 12 σ and 3 π

Explanation: C₆H₆ contains 6 C-C σ bonds, 6 C-H σ bonds (total 12 σ), and 3 delocalized C=C π bonds.

28. Which gas is liberated when calcium carbide (CaC₂) reacts with water?

  • A. Ethyne
  • B. Ethene
  • C. Methane
  • D. Ethane

Answer: A. Ethyne

Explanation: CaC₂ + 2H₂O → Ca(OH)₂ + C₂H₂ (ethyne gas).

29. Complete combustion of any hydrocarbon produces:

  • A. CO₂ and H₂O
  • B. CO and H₂
  • C. Carbon black and H₂O
  • D. CH₄ and O₂

Answer: A. CO₂ and H₂O

Explanation: Hydrocarbons burn completely in excess oxygen to yield carbon dioxide, water vapor, and heat energy.

30. Friedel-Crafts Acylation of benzene with acetyl chloride (CH₃COCl) in presence of AlCl₃ forms:

  • A. Acetophenone
  • B. Toluene
  • C. Benzoic acid
  • D. Chlorobenzene

Answer: A. Acetophenone

Explanation: C₆H₆ + CH₃COCl → C₆H₅COCH₃ (Acetophenone) + HCl.

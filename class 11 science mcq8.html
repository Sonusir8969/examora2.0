Phy-8: Gravitation 30 Questions

1. The force of attraction between any two masses in the universe is called:

  • A. Gravitational force
  • B. Electromagnetic force
  • C. Frictional force
  • D. Nuclear force

Answer: A. Gravitational force

Explanation: Gravitational force is the attractive force that exists between any two objects possessing mass, according to Newton's law of gravitation.

2. Newton's Law of Universal Gravitation states that the gravitational force between two masses is directly proportional to:

  • A. The product of their masses
  • B. The sum of their masses
  • C. The difference of their masses
  • D. The square of the distance between them

Answer: A. The product of their masses

Explanation: According to the law, gravitational attraction increases proportionally with the product of the two interacting masses.

3. Newton's Law of Universal Gravitation states that the gravitational force between two masses is inversely proportional to:

  • A. The square of the distance between them
  • B. The product of their masses
  • C. The sum of their masses
  • D. Time

Answer: A. The square of the distance between them

Explanation: The gravitational force decreases with the square of the distance separating the two masses, following an inverse-square relationship.

4. The mathematical formula for Newton's Law of Gravitation is:

  • A. F = Gm1m2/r²
  • B. F = Gm1m2r²
  • C. F = G(m1+m2)/r
  • D. F = Gm1m2/r

Answer: A. F = Gm1m2/r²

Explanation: The formula expresses gravitational force as the universal gravitational constant (G) multiplied by the product of the masses, divided by the square of their separation.

5. The universal gravitational constant (G) has an approximate value of:

  • A. 6.674 × 10⁻¹¹ N m²/kg²
  • B. 9.8 m/s²
  • C. 3 × 10⁸ m/s
  • D. 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ C

Answer: A. 6.674 × 10⁻¹¹ N m²/kg²

Explanation: G is a fundamental constant with an approximate value of 6.674 × 10⁻¹¹ N m²/kg², experimentally determined.

6. The acceleration produced in a body due to the Earth's gravitational pull is called:

  • A. Acceleration due to gravity
  • B. Centripetal acceleration
  • C. Angular acceleration
  • D. Tangential acceleration

Answer: A. Acceleration due to gravity

Explanation: Acceleration due to gravity refers specifically to the acceleration experienced by objects due to the Earth's gravitational attraction.

7. The value of acceleration due to gravity (g) generally decreases with:

  • A. Increasing altitude above the Earth's surface
  • B. Decreasing altitude
  • C. Increasing mass of the object
  • D. Decreasing mass of the object

Answer: A. Increasing altitude above the Earth's surface

Explanation: As altitude increases, the distance from Earth's centre increases, causing the value of g to decrease according to the inverse-square relationship.

8. The value of acceleration due to gravity also varies with depth below the Earth's surface, generally:

  • A. Decreasing as depth increases
  • B. Increasing as depth increases
  • C. Remaining constant regardless of depth
  • D. Becoming negative

Answer: A. Decreasing as depth increases

Explanation: As one moves deeper into the Earth, the effective mass contributing to gravitational pull decreases, causing g to decrease with depth.

9. Kepler's First Law states that planets move in elliptical orbits with the Sun at:

  • A. One of the foci
  • B. The exact centre
  • C. The circumference
  • D. No fixed point

Answer: A. One of the foci

Explanation: Kepler's First Law describes planetary orbits as ellipses, with the Sun positioned at one of the two focal points of the ellipse.

10. Kepler's Second Law states that a line joining a planet and the Sun sweeps out equal areas in equal:

  • A. Intervals of time
  • B. Distances
  • C. Angles
  • D. Masses

Answer: A. Intervals of time

Explanation: This law, also called the law of areas, indicates that planets move faster when closer to the Sun and slower when farther away, sweeping equal areas in equal time.

11. Kepler's Third Law relates the square of a planet's orbital period to the:

  • A. Cube of the semi-major axis of its orbit
  • B. Square of the semi-major axis
  • C. Mass of the planet only
  • D. Speed of the planet only

Answer: A. Cube of the semi-major axis of its orbit

Explanation: Kepler's Third Law establishes that T² is proportional to r³, connecting orbital period and orbital distance for planets around the Sun.

12. The minimum velocity required for an object to escape the Earth's gravitational pull permanently is called:

  • A. Escape velocity
  • B. Orbital velocity
  • C. Terminal velocity
  • D. Angular velocity

Answer: A. Escape velocity

Explanation: Escape velocity is the minimum speed an object must attain to break free from a planet's gravitational field without further propulsion.

13. The approximate escape velocity from the Earth's surface is:

  • A. 11.2 km/s
  • B. 9.8 km/s
  • C. 3 km/s
  • D. 1 km/s

Answer: A. 11.2 km/s

Explanation: The standard value for escape velocity from Earth's surface is approximately 11.2 kilometres per second.

14. The velocity required for a satellite to maintain a stable circular orbit around a planet is called:

  • A. Orbital velocity
  • B. Escape velocity
  • C. Terminal velocity
  • D. Free-fall velocity

Answer: A. Orbital velocity

Explanation: Orbital velocity is the specific speed required for a satellite to continuously fall around a planet in a stable circular orbit.

15. A satellite that orbits the Earth with the same time period as Earth's rotation is called a:

  • A. Geostationary satellite
  • B. Polar satellite
  • C. Low-orbit satellite
  • D. Escape satellite

Answer: A. Geostationary satellite

Explanation: A geostationary satellite orbits with a period matching Earth's rotation, appearing stationary relative to a fixed point on Earth's surface.

16. The gravitational potential energy between two masses is generally taken to be:

  • A. Negative, with zero potential energy at infinite separation
  • B. Always positive
  • C. Always zero
  • D. Undefined at large distances

Answer: A. Negative, with zero potential energy at infinite separation

Explanation: By convention, gravitational potential energy is negative for bound systems, approaching zero as the separation between masses becomes infinite.

17. The weight of an object is defined as the force of gravity acting on its:

  • A. Mass
  • B. Volume
  • C. Density
  • D. Surface area

Answer: A. Mass

Explanation: Weight is the gravitational force acting on an object's mass, calculated as W = mg.

18. Weightlessness experienced by astronauts in orbit occurs because they are in a state of:

  • A. Continuous free fall around the Earth
  • B. Zero gravity entirely
  • C. No gravitational force at all
  • D. Complete rest

Answer: A. Continuous free fall around the Earth

Explanation: Astronauts experience weightlessness because both they and their spacecraft are continuously falling freely under Earth's gravity while orbiting.

19. According to the principle of superposition, the net gravitational force on a mass due to several other masses is the:

  • A. Vector sum of individual gravitational forces
  • B. Scalar sum only
  • C. Average of all forces
  • D. Product of all forces

Answer: A. Vector sum of individual gravitational forces

Explanation: The principle of superposition states that the total gravitational force is found by vectorially adding the individual forces due to each mass.

20. The value of g at the poles compared to the equator is generally:

  • A. Slightly higher at the poles due to Earth's shape and rotation
  • B. Equal at both locations
  • C. Lower at the poles
  • D. Zero at the poles

Answer: A. Slightly higher at the poles due to Earth's shape and rotation

Explanation: Due to Earth's flattened shape (oblate spheroid) and rotational effects, g is slightly greater at the poles than at the equator.

21. The gravitational field intensity at a point is defined as the gravitational force experienced per unit:

  • A. Mass placed at that point
  • B. Distance from the source
  • C. Time
  • D. Volume

Answer: A. Mass placed at that point

Explanation: Gravitational field intensity represents the force exerted on a unit mass placed at a given point within the gravitational field.

22. For a satellite in a low Earth orbit compared to one in a higher orbit, the orbital velocity is generally:

  • A. Greater for the lower orbit
  • B. Greater for the higher orbit
  • C. Equal in both cases
  • D. Independent of orbital radius

Answer: A. Greater for the lower orbit

Explanation: Since orbital velocity depends inversely on the square root of the orbital radius, satellites closer to Earth require higher velocities to maintain orbit.

23. The time period of a geostationary satellite is approximately:

  • A. 24 hours
  • B. 12 hours
  • C. 90 minutes
  • D. 1 hour

Answer: A. 24 hours

Explanation: A geostationary satellite has an orbital period of approximately 24 hours, matching Earth's rotational period.

24. The gravitational force between two objects becomes four times weaker if the distance between them is:

  • A. Doubled
  • B. Halved
  • C. Tripled
  • D. Quadrupled

Answer: A. Doubled

Explanation: Since gravitational force follows an inverse-square law, doubling the distance reduces the force to one-fourth of its original value.

25. Which scientist first proposed the law of universal gravitation?

  • A. Isaac Newton
  • B. Johannes Kepler
  • C. Galileo Galilei
  • D. Albert Einstein

Answer: A. Isaac Newton

Explanation: Isaac Newton formulated the law of universal gravitation, describing the attractive force between any two masses in the universe.

26. The path of a projectile with very high velocity, when considering Earth's curvature, can become:

  • A. An orbital path around the Earth
  • B. A straight vertical line only
  • C. A stationary point
  • D. Impossible to determine

Answer: A. An orbital path around the Earth

Explanation: At sufficiently high velocity, a projectile's trajectory can curve to match the Earth's curvature, resulting in a stable orbital path.

27. The value of acceleration due to gravity on the Moon is approximately what fraction of Earth's gravity?

  • A. 1/6
  • B. 1/2
  • C. Equal to Earth's
  • D. Twice Earth's

Answer: A. 1/6

Explanation: Due to the Moon's smaller mass and radius, its surface gravity is approximately one-sixth that of Earth's.

28. If the mass of one object in a gravitational interaction is doubled while distance remains constant, the gravitational force:

  • A. Doubles
  • B. Halves
  • C. Remains unchanged
  • D. Quadruples

Answer: A. Doubles

Explanation: Since force is directly proportional to the product of masses, doubling one mass while keeping the other constant doubles the gravitational force.

29. The centripetal force required for a satellite's circular orbit around the Earth is provided by:

  • A. The gravitational force of attraction between the Earth and satellite
  • B. Air resistance
  • C. The satellite's own engine continuously
  • D. Magnetic forces

Answer: A. The gravitational force of attraction between the Earth and satellite

Explanation: Earth's gravitational pull acts as the centripetal force, continuously directing the satellite towards the centre and maintaining its circular orbit.

30. Which of the following best explains why astronauts float inside the International Space Station?

  • A. Both the station and astronauts are in continuous free fall, experiencing effective weightlessness
  • B. There is no gravity at all in that region of space
  • C. The astronauts have lost all mass
  • D. The station is stationary relative to Earth

Answer: A. Both the station and astronauts are in continuous free fall, experiencing effective weightlessness

Explanation: Since both the ISS and its occupants are falling freely under Earth's gravity at the same rate while orbiting, they experience apparent weightlessness.

Ch-6: Evolution 12 Questions

1. The theory of evolution by natural selection was proposed by:

  • A. Charles Darwin
  • B. Gregor Mendel
  • C. Jean-Baptiste Lamarck
  • D. Hugo de Vries

Answer: A. Charles Darwin

Explanation: Darwin's theory, presented in 'On the Origin of Species', proposed that natural selection acting on heritable variation drives evolution.

2. Lamarck's theory of evolution is based mainly on the concept of:

  • A. Inheritance of acquired characteristics
  • B. Natural selection
  • C. Survival of the fittest
  • D. The mutation theory

Answer: A. Inheritance of acquired characteristics

Explanation: Lamarck proposed that traits developed or modified through use or disuse during an organism's lifetime could be passed on to offspring.

3. Homologous organs are structures that share:

  • A. A similar basic structure but perform different functions, due to common ancestry
  • B. A different structure but similar functions, due to convergent evolution
  • C. No evolutionary relationship whatsoever
  • D. Identical functions across unrelated species

Answer: A. A similar basic structure but perform different functions, due to common ancestry

Explanation: Homologous organs, such as the forelimbs of vertebrates, illustrate divergent evolution from a common ancestral structure.

4. Analogous organs, which perform similar functions despite structural differences, arise as a result of:

  • A. Convergent evolution
  • B. Divergent evolution
  • C. Common ancestry
  • D. Genetic drift alone

Answer: A. Convergent evolution

Explanation: Analogous structures, such as the wings of insects and birds, evolve independently in unrelated groups facing similar environmental demands.

5. Industrial melanism, as observed in the peppered moth, is a well-documented example of:

  • A. Natural selection in action
  • B. Genetic drift
  • C. The founder effect
  • D. Artificial selection

Answer: A. Natural selection in action

Explanation: The shift toward darker moths in polluted, soot-covered environments illustrates how natural selection favors traits offering better camouflage.

6. The Hardy-Weinberg principle describes a theoretical population that is:

  • A. Not evolving, with constant allele frequencies across generations
  • B. Rapidly evolving under strong selection
  • C. Undergoing continuous, rapid mutation only
  • D. Always experiencing significant genetic drift

Answer: A. Not evolving, with constant allele frequencies across generations

Explanation: This principle serves as a null model, describing conditions under which allele and genotype frequencies remain stable in the absence of evolutionary forces.

7. Adaptive radiation refers to the:

  • A. Diversification of an ancestral species into various forms adapted to different ecological niches
  • B. Complete extinction of a species
  • C. Formation of analogous organs across species
  • D. Mutation occurring within a single gene

Answer: A. Diversification of an ancestral species into various forms adapted to different ecological niches

Explanation: Darwin's finches on the Galapagos Islands are a classic example of adaptive radiation from a common ancestral species.

8. Which of the following provides direct physical evidence supporting the process of evolution?

  • A. Fossils
  • B. Homologous organs alone
  • C. Vestigial organs alone
  • D. DNA sequencing alone

Answer: A. Fossils

Explanation: Fossils represent preserved remains or traces of ancient organisms, offering a direct historical record of evolutionary change over time.

9. Genetic drift, or random change in allele frequency, tends to be most pronounced and impactful in:

  • A. Small populations
  • B. Very large populations
  • C. Populations that do not reproduce at all
  • D. Asexually reproducing organisms exclusively

Answer: A. Small populations

Explanation: Random chance events have a proportionally larger effect on allele frequencies when the population size is small.

10. According to the theory proposed by Oparin and Haldane, the first forms of life originated from:

  • A. Simple inorganic and organic molecules present in a primitive atmosphere
  • B. Fully formed, complex cells appearing directly
  • C. Extraterrestrial sources exclusively
  • D. Complex proteins with no prior chemical evolution

Answer: A. Simple inorganic and organic molecules present in a primitive atmosphere

Explanation: Oparin and Haldane proposed that simple chemical compounds present in the early atmosphere gradually combined to form the first organic molecules of life.

11. Vestigial organs are structures that are:

  • A. Reduced and largely functionless remnants of organs that were functional in ancestral species
  • B. Newly evolved and highly functional in modern organisms
  • C. Found exclusively within the plant kingdom
  • D. Present only in modern humans, with no ancestral link

Answer: A. Reduced and largely functionless remnants of organs that were functional in ancestral species

Explanation: Vestigial organs, such as the human appendix, provide evidence of evolutionary history by hinting at structures once useful to ancestral species.

12. The modern evolutionary synthesis combines Darwin's theory of natural selection with:

  • A. The principles of population genetics
  • B. Lamarckism
  • C. The theory of special creation
  • D. The theory of spontaneous generation

Answer: A. The principles of population genetics

Explanation: The modern synthesis integrates Darwinian natural selection with Mendelian genetics and population genetics to explain evolutionary mechanisms more fully.

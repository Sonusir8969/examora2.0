Phy-6: Work, Energy and Power 30 Questions

1. Work done by a force is defined as the product of force and:

  • A. Displacement in the direction of the force
  • B. Time taken
  • C. Mass of the object
  • D. Velocity of the object

Answer: A. Displacement in the direction of the force

Explanation: Work is calculated as the component of force acting along the direction of displacement, multiplied by the displacement.

2. The SI unit of work is the:

  • A. Joule
  • B. Newton
  • C. Watt
  • D. Pascal

Answer: A. Joule

Explanation: The joule (J) is the SI unit of work, equivalent to one newton-metre.

3. Work done is zero when the force and displacement are:

  • A. Perpendicular to each other
  • B. Parallel to each other
  • C. In the same direction
  • D. Equal in magnitude

Answer: A. Perpendicular to each other

Explanation: Since work = force × displacement × cos(θ), when the angle θ between them is 90°, cos(90°) = 0, making the work done zero.

4. The capacity of a body to do work is called its:

  • A. Energy
  • B. Power
  • C. Force
  • D. Momentum

Answer: A. Energy

Explanation: Energy is defined as the capacity or ability of a body to perform work.

5. The energy possessed by a body due to its motion is called:

  • A. Kinetic energy
  • B. Potential energy
  • C. Mechanical energy only
  • D. Chemical energy

Answer: A. Kinetic energy

Explanation: Kinetic energy is specifically the energy an object possesses because of its motion.

6. The energy possessed by a body due to its position or configuration is called:

  • A. Potential energy
  • B. Kinetic energy
  • C. Thermal energy
  • D. Electrical energy

Answer: A. Potential energy

Explanation: Potential energy is stored energy associated with an object's position, such as height above the ground, or its configuration.

7. The formula for kinetic energy of an object with mass m and velocity v is:

  • A. 1/2 mv²
  • B. mv²
  • C. mv
  • D. 1/2 mv

Answer: A. 1/2 mv²

Explanation: Kinetic energy is calculated using the formula (1/2) × mass × velocity squared.

8. The formula for gravitational potential energy at height h is:

  • A. mgh
  • B. 1/2 mv²
  • C. mgh²
  • D. mg/h

Answer: A. mgh

Explanation: Gravitational potential energy is calculated as mass × gravitational acceleration (g) × height (h).

9. The law of conservation of energy states that energy can neither be created nor destroyed, but can only be:

  • A. Transformed from one form to another
  • B. Increased over time
  • C. Decreased over time
  • D. Completely lost

Answer: A. Transformed from one form to another

Explanation: This fundamental law asserts that total energy remains constant, though it may change forms, such as from potential to kinetic.

10. The rate at which work is done is called:

  • A. Power
  • B. Energy
  • C. Force
  • D. Momentum

Answer: A. Power

Explanation: Power specifically measures how quickly work is performed, calculated as work done divided by time taken.

11. The SI unit of power is the:

  • A. Watt
  • B. Joule
  • C. Newton
  • D. Pascal

Answer: A. Watt

Explanation: The watt (W) is the SI unit of power, equivalent to one joule per second.

12. A collision in which both momentum and kinetic energy are conserved is called a/an:

  • A. Elastic collision
  • B. Inelastic collision
  • C. Perfectly inelastic collision
  • D. Non-conservative collision

Answer: A. Elastic collision

Explanation: In an elastic collision, both the total momentum and total kinetic energy of the system remain unchanged before and after collision.

13. A collision in which momentum is conserved but kinetic energy is not conserved is called a/an:

  • A. Inelastic collision
  • B. Elastic collision
  • C. Frictionless collision
  • D. Reversible collision

Answer: A. Inelastic collision

Explanation: In an inelastic collision, some kinetic energy is converted into other forms (like heat or sound), while momentum remains conserved.

14. In a perfectly inelastic collision, the colliding objects:

  • A. Stick together and move with a common velocity after collision
  • B. Bounce apart with equal speeds
  • C. Conserve all kinetic energy
  • D. Do not interact at all

Answer: A. Stick together and move with a common velocity after collision

Explanation: A perfectly inelastic collision results in the two objects combining and moving together with a shared final velocity.

15. The work-energy theorem states that the work done by the net force on an object equals the:

  • A. Change in its kinetic energy
  • B. Change in its potential energy only
  • C. Total momentum
  • D. Total power

Answer: A. Change in its kinetic energy

Explanation: The work-energy theorem directly relates the net work done on an object to the resulting change in its kinetic energy.

16. A force that does work independent of the path taken, depending only on initial and final positions, is called a:

  • A. Conservative force
  • B. Non-conservative force
  • C. Frictional force always
  • D. Applied force only

Answer: A. Conservative force

Explanation: Conservative forces, such as gravity, perform work that depends solely on the starting and ending positions, not the specific path.

17. Friction is generally classified as a:

  • A. Non-conservative force
  • B. Conservative force
  • C. Gravitational force
  • D. Elastic force

Answer: A. Non-conservative force

Explanation: Since the work done against friction depends on the path length travelled, friction is classified as a non-conservative force.

18. The total mechanical energy of a system remains constant if only:

  • A. Conservative forces are acting on it
  • B. Non-conservative forces are acting
  • C. External forces are removed entirely
  • D. Friction is present

Answer: A. Conservative forces are acting on it

Explanation: Mechanical energy conservation applies specifically when only conservative forces, like gravity, act on the system, with no energy loss to friction or other non-conservative forces.

19. When a spring is stretched or compressed, it stores energy known as:

  • A. Elastic potential energy
  • B. Kinetic energy
  • C. Gravitational potential energy
  • D. Thermal energy

Answer: A. Elastic potential energy

Explanation: Elastic potential energy is the specific type of stored energy resulting from the deformation (stretching or compressing) of an elastic object like a spring.

20. The formula for elastic potential energy stored in a spring with spring constant k and extension x is:

  • A. 1/2 kx²
  • B. kx
  • C. 1/2 kx
  • D. kx²

Answer: A. 1/2 kx²

Explanation: The elastic potential energy stored in a spring is calculated as one-half the spring constant multiplied by the square of its extension or compression.

21. The dot product used to calculate work done by a constant force is defined mathematically as:

  • A. W = F·s = Fs cos θ
  • B. W = F + s
  • C. W = F/s
  • D. W = Fs sin θ

Answer: A. W = F·s = Fs cos θ

Explanation: Work is calculated as the scalar (dot) product of force and displacement vectors, given by Fs cos θ, where θ is the angle between them.

22. If the angle between the applied force and displacement is 0°, the work done is:

  • A. Maximum, equal to Fs
  • B. Zero
  • C. Negative
  • D. Undefined

Answer: A. Maximum, equal to Fs

Explanation: When force and displacement are in the same direction (θ = 0°), cos(0°) = 1, resulting in maximum possible work being done.

23. If the angle between the applied force and displacement is 180°, the work done is:

  • A. Negative
  • B. Positive maximum
  • C. Zero
  • D. Undefined

Answer: A. Negative

Explanation: When force acts opposite to displacement (θ = 180°), cos(180°) = -1, resulting in negative work being done.

24. A machine that has an efficiency of 100% would mean:

  • A. All input energy is converted to useful output work with none lost
  • B. It requires no input energy
  • C. It produces infinite energy
  • D. It cannot function at all

Answer: A. All input energy is converted to useful output work with none lost

Explanation: 100% efficiency, though practically unachievable, would mean all supplied energy converts entirely into useful work with no losses.

25. The power delivered by a machine doing work W in time t is calculated as:

  • A. P = W/t
  • B. P = Wt
  • C. P = W + t
  • D. P = t/W

Answer: A. P = W/t

Explanation: Power is defined as the rate of doing work, calculated by dividing the total work done by the time taken.

26. If a body of mass 2 kg moves with a velocity of 3 m/s, its kinetic energy is:

  • A. 9 J
  • B. 6 J
  • C. 18 J
  • D. 3 J

Answer: A. 9 J

Explanation: KE = 1/2 × m × v² = 1/2 × 2 × 3² = 1/2 × 2 × 9 = 9 J.

27. In an elastic collision between two identical masses, where one is initially at rest, after collision:

  • A. The moving mass comes to rest, and the initially stationary mass moves with the original velocity
  • B. Both masses move together
  • C. Neither mass moves
  • D. The initially stationary mass remains at rest

Answer: A. The moving mass comes to rest, and the initially stationary mass moves with the original velocity

Explanation: This is a special result of elastic collisions between equal masses: complete transfer of velocity occurs between the two identical masses.

28. The concept of negative work is applicable when the force acts:

  • A. Opposite to the direction of displacement
  • B. In the same direction as displacement
  • C. Perpendicular to displacement
  • D. At exactly zero magnitude

Answer: A. Opposite to the direction of displacement

Explanation: When the applied force has a component opposing the motion's direction, the resulting work done is considered negative.

29. In the absence of air resistance, a ball thrown upward and falling back demonstrates the interconversion between:

  • A. Kinetic energy and potential energy
  • B. Only thermal energy
  • C. Only sound energy
  • D. Only chemical energy

Answer: A. Kinetic energy and potential energy

Explanation: As the ball rises, kinetic energy converts to potential energy, and this process reverses as it falls back down, illustrating mechanical energy conservation.

30. 1 horsepower (hp) is approximately equal to:

  • A. 746 watts
  • B. 1000 watts
  • C. 100 watts
  • D. 7460 watts

Answer: A. 746 watts

Explanation: The commonly used conversion factor states that 1 horsepower is approximately equal to 746 watts.

Ch-14: Natural Resources 10 Questions

1. The life-supporting zone of the Earth where the atmosphere, hydrosphere, and lithosphere interact is called:

  • A. Stratosphere
  • B. Biosphere
  • C. Ionosphere
  • D. Troposphere

Answer: B. Biosphere

Explanation: The biosphere is the global ecological system integrating all living beings and their relationships with the lithosphere, hydrosphere, and atmosphere.

2. Which gas constitutes approximately 78% of Earth's atmosphere?

  • A. Oxygen
  • B. Carbon Dioxide
  • C. Nitrogen
  • D. Argon

Answer: C. Nitrogen

Explanation: Earth's atmosphere is composed of about 78% nitrogen, 21% oxygen, and small amounts of carbon dioxide, argon, and water vapor.

3. What causes wind movement across Earth's surface?

  • A. Uneven heating of land and water bodies by solar radiation
  • B. Rotation of Earth only
  • C. Gravitational pull of the Moon
  • D. High humidity levels

Answer: A. Uneven heating of land and water bodies by solar radiation

Explanation: Solar heating causes air over land to warm faster than over water, creating convection currents that generate winds.

4. Which organism plays a crucial role as a biological weathering agent in soil formation?

  • A. Earthworms
  • B. Lichens
  • C. Algae
  • D. Fungi

Answer: B. Lichens

Explanation: Lichens growing on rock surfaces release substances that cause the rock to powder down, aiding soil formation.

5. Which atmospheric layer contains the protective ozone layer that absorbs harmful UV radiation?

  • A. Troposphere
  • B. Stratosphere
  • C. Mesosphere
  • D. Exosphere

Answer: B. Stratosphere

Explanation: The ozone layer is located in the stratosphere and blocks harmful ultraviolet (UV) radiation from reaching Earth's surface.

6. Which man-made chemicals are primarily responsible for ozone layer depletion?

  • A. Methane
  • B. Chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs)
  • C. Carbon Monoxide
  • D. Sulfur Dioxide

Answer: B. Chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs)

Explanation: CFCs contain chlorine atoms that react with ozone molecules in the upper atmosphere, breaking them down into oxygen.

7. Nitrogen fixation in soil is primarily carried out by which symbiotic bacterium found in root nodules of leguminous plants?

  • A. Azotobacter
  • B. Rhizobium
  • C. Nitrosomonas
  • D. Nitrobacter

Answer: B. Rhizobium

Explanation: *Rhizobium* bacteria reside in the root nodules of pulses/legumes and convert atmospheric nitrogen into usable nitrates.

8. Which of the following gases is NOT considered a primary greenhouse gas?

  • A. Carbon dioxide (CO₂)
  • B. Methane (CH₄)
  • C. Water Vapor
  • D. Nitrogen (N₂)

Answer: D. Nitrogen (N₂)

Explanation: Nitrogen gas does not trap infrared radiation. CO₂, CH₄, N₂O, and water vapor are key greenhouse gases responsible for the greenhouse effect.

9. Acid rain is primarily caused by elevated atmospheric levels of oxides of:

  • A. Carbon and Nitrogen
  • B. Sulfur and Nitrogen
  • C. Phosphorus and Sulfur
  • D. Carbon and Hydrogen

Answer: B. Sulfur and Nitrogen

Explanation: Burning fossil fuels releases SO₂ and NO₂, which react with atmospheric water vapor to form sulfuric and nitric acids, falling as acid rain.

10. The process of conversion of atmospheric nitrogen into nitrates and nitrites is called:

  • A. Nitrification
  • B. Nitrogen Fixation
  • C. Ammonification
  • D. Denitrification

Answer: B. Nitrogen Fixation

Explanation: Nitrogen fixation converts inert N₂ gas into biologically useful forms like nitrates and nitrites via biological or industrial processes.

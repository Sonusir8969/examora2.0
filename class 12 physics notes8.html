1. Displacement Current & Maxwell's Equations

Short Note:

• Need for Displacement Current: Ampere's Circuital Law was found to be logically inconsistent for time-varying electric fields (such as charging a capacitor). Maxwell modified the law by adding displacement current.

• Displacement Current (I_d): Current arising due to time-varying electric field / electric flux: I_d = ε₀ (dΦ_E / dt).

• Ampere-Maxwell Law: Total current = Conduction current (I_c) + Displacement current (I_d). ∮ B · dl = μ₀ (I_c + ε₀ dΦ_E / dt).

• Maxwell's Four Fundamental Equations:

  1. Gauss's Law in Electrostatics: ∮ E · dA = q / ε₀

  2. Gauss's Law in Magnetism: ∮ B · dA = 0

  3. Faraday's Law of Electromagnetic Induction: ∮ E · dl = -dΦ_B / dt

  4. Ampere-Maxwell Law: ∮ B · dl = μ₀ I_c + μ₀ ε₀ (dΦ_E / dt)

2. Electromagnetic Waves & Their Characteristics

Short Note:

• Nature of EM Waves: Coupled sinusoidal electric and magnetic field vectors oscillating mutually perpendicular to each other and perpendicular to direction of propagation (Transverse Nature).

• Speed of EM Waves:

  - In Vacuum: c = 1 / √(μ₀ ε₀) ≈ 3 × 10⁸ m/s.

  - In Material Medium: v = 1 / √(μ ε) = c / n, where n is refractive index.

• Ratio of Amplitudes: Amplitude ratio of electric field E₀ to magnetic field B₀ equals speed of light: c = E₀ / B₀ = E_rms / B_rms.

• Energy Density: Energy is shared equally between electric and magnetic fields:

  - Average Energy Density: u = u_E + u_B = (1/2) ε₀ E_rms² + (1 / 2μ₀) B_rms² = ε₀ E_rms².

• Momentum & Radiation Pressure: EM wave transports linear momentum p = U / c (where U is energy). When absorbed by surface, it exerts pressure called Radiation Pressure.

3. Electromagnetic Spectrum & Its Applications

Short Note:

Classification of EM waves in order of increasing frequency (decreasing wavelength):

1. Radio Waves (λ > 0.1 m): Produced by accelerated motion of charges in conducting wires. Used in radio & TV broadcasting.

2. Microwaves (0.1 m to 1 mm): Produced by special vacuum tubes (klystrons, magnetrons). Used in radar systems and microwave ovens.

3. Infrared Waves (1 mm to 700 nm): Heat waves produced by hot bodies and molecules. Used in remote controls, night-vision devices, and greenhouse effect.

4. Visible Light (700 nm to 400 nm): Emitted by atomic excitation. Provides vision to human eyes.

5. Ultraviolet Rays (400 nm to 1 nm): Produced by high-voltage lamps and Sun. Used in water purifiers, sterilization, and LASIK eye surgery.

6. X-Rays (1 nm to 10⁻³ nm): Produced by bombarding metal target with high-energy electrons. Used in medical diagnosis and structure detection.

7. Gamma Rays (< 10⁻³ nm): Emitted by radioactive nuclei during nuclear transitions. Used in cancer radiotherapy.

Intext & Conceptual Questions

Q1: Why is displacement current needed in Ampere's circuital law? Explain how it restores continuity of current in a capacitor circuit.
Answer:
• Ampere's Circuital Law states ∮ B · dl = μ₀ I_c. During the charging of a capacitor, a conduction current flows in the wires, but no actual charge moves across the space between the capacitor plates. Applying Ampere's law to a flat surface yields a non-zero magnetic field, whereas applying it to a bulging surface passing between plates yields zero, causing a contradiction.
• Maxwell introduced the concept of displacement current (I_d = ε₀ dΦ_E / dt) arising from the changing electric flux between capacitor plates.
• Between the plates, I_c = 0 and I_d = I, while outside in wires, I_c = I and I_d = 0. Hence, total current I_total = I_c + I_d remains continuous throughout the circuit.
Q2: Summarize the Electromagnetic Spectrum with Frequency and Wavelength ranges along with Main Uses.
Answer:
Type of Wave Wavelength Range Frequency Range Primary Application
Radio Waves > 0.1 m < 3 × 10⁹ Hz Radio and television communication.
Microwaves 0.1 m – 1 mm 3 × 10⁹ Hz – 3 × 10¹¹ Hz Radar systems, satellite communication & ovens.
Infrared 1 mm – 700 nm 3 × 10¹¹ Hz – 4 × 10¹⁴ Hz Night vision, remote controls & heat lamps.
Visible Light 700 nm – 400 nm 4 × 10¹⁴ Hz – 7.5 × 10¹⁴ Hz Optical vision, photography.
Ultraviolet 400 nm – 1 nm 7.5 × 10¹⁴ Hz – 3 × 10¹⁶ Hz Water purification, sterilization, LASIK.

Textbook Chapter End Exercise Questions

Q1: A parallel plate capacitor with circular plates of radius 1 m has a capacitance of 1 nF. At t = 0, it is connected for charging in series with a resistor R = 1 MΩ across a 2 V battery. Calculate the magnetic field at a point P, halfway between the centre and the peripheral edge of the plates, at t = 10⁻³ s.
Answer:
Given:
• Radius of circular plates (R_p) = 1.0 m
• Distance from center (r) = 0.5 m (halfway)
• Capacitance (C) = 1 nF = 10⁻⁹ F
• Resistance (R) = 1 MΩ = 10⁶ Ω => Time constant (τ = R C) = 10⁶ × 10⁻⁹ = 10⁻³ s
• Voltage (V₀) = 2 V, Time (t) = 10⁻³ s = τ

Step 1: Charging Current formula
I(t) = (V₀ / R) × e^(-t / τ)
At t = τ:
I = (2 / 10⁶) × e⁻¹ = 2 × 10⁻⁶ × 0.368 = 0.736 × 10⁻⁶ A

Step 2: Magnetic Field inside capacitor at distance r
Using Ampere-Maxwell law inside circular plates:
B = (μ₀ r I_d) / (2π R_p²) = (4π × 10⁻⁷ × 0.5 × 0.736 × 10⁻⁶) / (2π × 1.0²)
B = 2 × 10⁻⁷ × 0.5 × 0.736 × 10⁻⁶ = 0.736 × 10⁻¹³ T

The magnetic field at point P at t = 10⁻³ s is 0.736 × 10⁻¹³ T.
Q2: In a plane electromagnetic wave, the electric field oscillates sinusoidally at a frequency of 2.0 × 10¹⁰ Hz and amplitude 48 V/m. (a) What is the wavelength of the wave? (b) What is the amplitude of the oscillating magnetic field?
Answer:
Given:
• Frequency (f) = 2.0 × 10¹⁰ Hz
• Electric field amplitude (E₀) = 48 V/m
• Speed of light in vacuum (c) = 3.0 × 10⁸ m/s

Part (a): Wavelength (λ)
λ = c / f = (3.0 × 10⁸) / (2.0 × 10¹⁰) = 1.5 × 10⁻² m = 1.5 cm

Part (b): Amplitude of Magnetic Field (B₀)
B₀ = E₀ / c = 48 / (3.0 × 10⁸) = 1.6 × 10⁻⁷ T

• The wavelength of the wave is 1.5 cm (or 1.5 × 10⁻² m).
• The amplitude of the oscillating magnetic field is 1.6 × 10⁻⁷ T.

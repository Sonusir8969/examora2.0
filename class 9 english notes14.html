NCERT Solutions for Class 9 English Poem 5 A Legend of the Northland

NCERT Solutions for Class 9 English Beehive Poem 5 A Legend of the Northland by Phoebe Cary covers the theme of selfishness and its consequences, poetic devices, and important questions and answers.

1. About the Poet and Poem

Short Note:

• Phoebe Cary was an American poet, and this poem retells a folk legend from the cold Northland about an old woman who refuses to share food with a hungry visitor.

• In the story, Saint Peter, disguised as a beggar, asks the old woman for a pancake, but she keeps making the cakes smaller out of greed each time she considers giving one away.

• As punishment for her selfishness, Saint Peter transforms her into a woodpecker, condemning her to forever search for food by tapping on trees.

2. Theme & Central Idea

Short Note:

• The poem's central theme is that selfishness and greed bring their own punishment, while generosity and kindness are truly valued.

• It uses a folk-tale format to teach a moral lesson about sharing with those in need, especially in a land of scarcity and harsh winters.

• The transformation into a woodpecker, a bird constantly digging for tiny insects in bark, symbolically reflects the old woman's endless, meagre search for food, similar to how she once meagrely measured her pancake.

3. Poetic Devices

Short Note:

• Narrative Style: The poem is written as a story-like ballad, making the moral lesson memorable and engaging.

• Symbolism: The shrinking pancake symbolises the old woman's shrinking generosity, and the woodpecker symbolises eternal, petty searching brought on by greed.

• Irony: The old woman ends up eternally hungry and searching for food, the very fate she tried to avoid by refusing to share.

Reading With Insight (Textbook Questions)

Q1: Why did the old woman keep making the pancake smaller?
Answer: Each time the old woman prepared a pancake to give to the hungry beggar, she felt it was too large to part with, so she kept reducing its size out of growing greed, until even the smallest cake felt too much to give away.
Q2: How did the beggar (Saint Peter) punish the old woman for her selfishness?
Answer: Saint Peter, angered by the old woman's refusal to share even a tiny cake with a starving visitor, declared that she was too selfish to remain human and turned her into a woodpecker, condemning her to peck endlessly at tree bark for food.
Q3: What does the woodpecker symbolise in the poem?
Answer: The woodpecker symbolises the eternal consequence of the old woman's selfishness; just as she once measured out food too meagrely, she is now doomed to search tirelessly and receive only tiny amounts of food from the trees.

Extract-Based / Short Answer Questions

Q4: What moral lesson does the poem convey?
Answer: The poem conveys that selfishness and unwillingness to share with those in need is morally wrong and can lead to severe, lasting consequences, while generosity is presented as an essential human virtue.
Q5: Why is the setting of the Northland significant to the story?
Answer: The harsh, cold climate of the Northland makes food and warmth especially precious, which heightens the significance of the old woman's refusal to share even a small pancake with a hungry traveller.

Ch-7: Alternating Current 20 Questions

1. What is Alternating Current (AC)?

  • A. Current that flows in one direction only
  • B. Current whose magnitude and direction change periodically with time
  • C. Current that remains constant with time
  • D. Current produced only by batteries

Answer: B. Current whose magnitude and direction change periodically with time

Explanation: AC is defined as electric current whose magnitude changes continuously and direction reverses periodically, typically following a sinusoidal pattern.

2. The RMS value of an alternating current I = I0 sin(ωt) is:

  • A. I0
  • B. I0/√2
  • C. I0/2
  • D. 2I0

Answer: B. I0/√2

Explanation: RMS (root mean square) value of a sinusoidal AC is Irms = I0/√2 ≈ 0.707 I0, representing the equivalent steady DC that produces the same heating effect.

3. The average value of alternating current over one complete cycle is:

  • A. I0
  • B. I0/√2
  • C. Zero
  • D. 2I0/π

Answer: C. Zero

Explanation: Over a full cycle, the positive and negative half cycles cancel out exactly, so the average value of a sinusoidal AC over one complete cycle is zero.

4. Capacitive reactance (Xc) of a capacitor of capacitance C in an AC circuit of angular frequency ω is:

  • A. Xc = ωC
  • B. Xc = 1/(ωC)
  • C. Xc = ω/C
  • D. Xc = ωC²

Answer: B. Xc = 1/(ωC)

Explanation: Capacitive reactance is given by Xc = 1/(ωC) = 1/(2πfC); it decreases as frequency increases, since a capacitor charges/discharges faster at higher frequency.

5. Inductive reactance (XL) of an inductor of inductance L in an AC circuit of angular frequency ω is:

  • A. XL = ωL
  • B. XL = 1/(ωL)
  • C. XL = ω/L
  • D. XL = L/ω

Answer: A. XL = ωL

Explanation: Inductive reactance is XL = ωL = 2πfL; it increases with frequency because a changing current induces a larger back-EMF at higher rates of change.

6. In a purely resistive AC circuit, the phase difference between voltage and current is:

  • A. 90°
  • B. 180°
  • C. Zero
  • D. 45°

Answer: C. Zero

Explanation: In a pure resistor, voltage and current are always in phase (phase difference = 0°) since V = IR holds instantaneously at every point of the cycle.

7. In a purely inductive AC circuit, the current:

  • A. Leads the voltage by 90°
  • B. Lags the voltage by 90°
  • C. Is in phase with voltage
  • D. Lags the voltage by 180°

Answer: B. Lags the voltage by 90°

Explanation: In a pure inductor, the induced back-EMF opposes change in current, causing the current to lag behind the applied voltage by a phase angle of 90° (π/2).

8. In a purely capacitive AC circuit, the current:

  • A. Leads the voltage by 90°
  • B. Lags the voltage by 90°
  • C. Is in phase with voltage
  • D. Leads the voltage by 180°

Answer: A. Leads the voltage by 90°

Explanation: In a pure capacitor, current must flow first to build up charge before voltage develops across it, so current leads the applied voltage by 90°.

9. The impedance (Z) of a series RLC circuit is given by:

  • A. Z = R + XL + XC
  • B. Z = √(R² + (XL − XC)²)
  • C. Z = R × XL × XC
  • D. Z = R² + XL² + XC²

Answer: B. Z = √(R² + (XL − XC)²)

Explanation: Since XL and XC act in opposite phase to R, impedance is found by combining them as a right-angle vector sum: Z = √(R² + (XL − XC)²).

10. Resonance occurs in a series LCR circuit when:

  • A. XL = 0
  • B. XC = 0
  • C. XL = XC
  • D. R = 0

Answer: C. XL = XC

Explanation: At resonance, inductive and capacitive reactances become equal (XL = XC) and cancel each other, leaving impedance purely resistive and minimum.

11. The resonant frequency (f0) of a series LCR circuit is:

  • A. f0 = 1/(2π√(LC))
  • B. f0 = 2π√(LC)
  • C. f0 = 1/(2πLC)
  • D. f0 = √(LC)/2π

Answer: A. f0 = 1/(2π√(LC))

Explanation: Setting XL = XC gives ωL = 1/(ωC), so ω0 = 1/√(LC), and hence resonant frequency f0 = ω0/2π = 1/(2π√(LC)).

12. The power factor of an AC circuit is defined as:

  • A. cos φ = R/Z
  • B. cos φ = Z/R
  • C. cos φ = XL/Z
  • D. cos φ = XC/R

Answer: A. cos φ = R/Z

Explanation: Power factor is the cosine of the phase angle between voltage and current, cos φ = R/Z, and determines what fraction of apparent power does real work.

13. At resonance in a series LCR circuit, the power factor is:

  • A. Zero
  • B. 0.5
  • C. One
  • D. Undefined

Answer: C. One

Explanation: At resonance Z = R (minimum, purely resistive), so cos φ = R/Z = 1, meaning voltage and current are in phase and power dissipation is maximum.

14. Average power dissipated in a pure inductor or pure capacitor over a complete cycle is:

  • A. Maximum
  • B. Zero
  • C. Equal to Vrms Irms
  • D. Negative

Answer: B. Zero

Explanation: Since current and voltage differ in phase by 90° in a pure L or C, the average of the power term (proportional to cos 90° = 0) over a cycle is zero — hence called wattless current.

15. A transformer works on the principle of:

  • A. Self-induction only
  • B. Mutual induction
  • C. Electrostatic induction
  • D. Thermoelectric effect

Answer: B. Mutual induction

Explanation: A transformer transfers electrical energy between two coils linked by a common changing magnetic flux, which is the phenomenon of mutual induction.

16. In a step-up transformer:

  • A. Number of turns in secondary is less than primary
  • B. Number of turns in secondary is greater than primary
  • C. Both coils have equal turns
  • D. It converts AC to DC

Answer: B. Number of turns in secondary is greater than primary

Explanation: A step-up transformer increases voltage, which requires the secondary coil to have a greater number of turns than the primary coil (Ns > Np).

17. Which of the following is NOT a source of energy loss in a real transformer?

  • A. Copper loss (I²R heating)
  • B. Iron loss due to hysteresis
  • C. Eddy current loss in core
  • D. Loss due to mutual induction

Answer: D. Loss due to mutual induction

Explanation: Mutual induction is the very principle by which a transformer operates and transfers energy — it is not a loss mechanism, unlike resistive, hysteresis, and eddy current losses.

18. A choke coil is used in AC circuits (instead of a resistor) to reduce current because:

  • A. It consumes large average power
  • B. It reduces current with negligible power loss due to its low resistance and high inductive reactance
  • C. It converts AC to DC
  • D. It has zero reactance

Answer: B. It reduces current with negligible power loss due to its low resistance and high inductive reactance

Explanation: A choke coil has high inductive reactance but very low ohmic resistance, so it limits AC current effectively while dissipating very little power, unlike a resistor.

19. The quality factor (Q) of a series LCR resonant circuit is given by:

  • A. Q = R/(ω0L)
  • B. Q = ω0L/R
  • C. Q = ω0RL
  • D. Q = 1/(ω0RL)

Answer: B. Q = ω0L/R

Explanation: Quality factor, which measures the sharpness of resonance, is defined as Q = ω0L/R = (1/R)√(L/C); a higher Q means a sharper, narrower resonance peak.

20. An AC voltage V = V0 sin(ωt) is applied to a series LCR circuit with XL > XC. The circuit behaves as:

  • A. Purely resistive
  • B. Predominantly inductive
  • C. Predominantly capacitive
  • D. Purely capacitive

Answer: B. Predominantly inductive

Explanation: When XL > XC, the net reactance is inductive in nature, so the current lags behind the voltage and the circuit behaves as a predominantly inductive circuit.

Phy-1: Physical World 30 Questions

1. Physics is the branch of science concerned with the study of:

  • A. The basic laws of nature and their manifestations in natural phenomena
  • B. Only living organisms
  • C. Only chemical reactions
  • D. Only mathematical equations

Answer: A. The basic laws of nature and their manifestations in natural phenomena

Explanation: Physics deals with understanding the fundamental laws governing nature and how they manifest in the physical world.

2. The branch of physics dealing with the motion of objects without considering the cause of motion is called:

  • A. Kinematics
  • B. Dynamics
  • C. Statics
  • D. Thermodynamics

Answer: A. Kinematics

Explanation: Kinematics studies motion purely in terms of displacement, velocity and acceleration, without considering forces.

3. The branch of physics dealing with heat and temperature is called:

  • A. Thermodynamics
  • B. Mechanics
  • C. Optics
  • D. Electrodynamics

Answer: A. Thermodynamics

Explanation: Thermodynamics is the branch of physics that studies heat, temperature, and their relation to energy and work.

4. The study of electric and magnetic phenomena associated with charged particles is called:

  • A. Electrodynamics
  • B. Optics
  • C. Mechanics
  • D. Thermodynamics

Answer: A. Electrodynamics

Explanation: Electrodynamics deals specifically with electricity, magnetism, and their interconnected phenomena.

5. The branch of physics dealing with light and its properties is called:

  • A. Optics
  • B. Acoustics
  • C. Mechanics
  • D. Thermodynamics

Answer: A. Optics

Explanation: Optics is the branch of physics that studies the behaviour and properties of light.

6. Which scientist formulated the three laws of motion and the law of universal gravitation?

  • A. Isaac Newton
  • B. Albert Einstein
  • C. Galileo Galilei
  • D. James Clerk Maxwell

Answer: A. Isaac Newton

Explanation: Isaac Newton is credited with formulating the three fundamental laws of motion and the law of universal gravitation.

7. Which scientist proposed the theory of relativity?

  • A. Albert Einstein
  • B. Isaac Newton
  • C. Niels Bohr
  • D. Max Planck

Answer: A. Albert Einstein

Explanation: Albert Einstein developed both the special and general theories of relativity, revolutionising the understanding of space, time and gravity.

8. The branch of physics dealing with phenomena at the atomic and subatomic scale is called:

  • A. Quantum mechanics
  • B. Classical mechanics
  • C. Thermodynamics
  • D. Fluid mechanics

Answer: A. Quantum mechanics

Explanation: Quantum mechanics governs the behaviour of particles at atomic and subatomic scales, differing significantly from classical physics.

9. Which four fundamental forces of nature are typically studied in physics?

  • A. Gravitational, electromagnetic, strong nuclear and weak nuclear
  • B. Only gravitational and electric
  • C. Only magnetic and thermal
  • D. Only mechanical and chemical

Answer: A. Gravitational, electromagnetic, strong nuclear and weak nuclear

Explanation: Physics recognises four fundamental forces: gravitational, electromagnetic, strong nuclear and weak nuclear forces.

10. Which of the four fundamental forces is the weakest?

  • A. Gravitational force
  • B. Electromagnetic force
  • C. Strong nuclear force
  • D. Weak nuclear force

Answer: A. Gravitational force

Explanation: Despite governing large-scale phenomena like planetary motion, the gravitational force is by far the weakest of the four fundamental forces.

11. Which of the four fundamental forces is the strongest?

  • A. Strong nuclear force
  • B. Gravitational force
  • C. Weak nuclear force
  • D. Electromagnetic force

Answer: A. Strong nuclear force

Explanation: The strong nuclear force, which binds protons and neutrons together in the nucleus, is the strongest of the fundamental forces.

12. The unification of electricity and magnetism into a single theory was achieved by:

  • A. James Clerk Maxwell
  • B. Isaac Newton
  • C. Michael Faraday
  • D. Albert Einstein

Answer: A. James Clerk Maxwell

Explanation: Maxwell formulated the equations that unified electricity, magnetism and light into a single electromagnetic theory.

13. The force that governs radioactive decay of certain atomic nuclei is the:

  • A. Weak nuclear force
  • B. Strong nuclear force
  • C. Gravitational force
  • D. Electromagnetic force

Answer: A. Weak nuclear force

Explanation: The weak nuclear force is responsible for processes such as beta decay in radioactive nuclei.

14. The scientific method typically involves:

  • A. Systematic observation, experimentation, and theoretical reasoning
  • B. Random guesses without verification
  • C. Only theoretical speculation
  • D. Only observation without analysis

Answer: A. Systematic observation, experimentation, and theoretical reasoning

Explanation: The scientific method combines careful observation, controlled experimentation, and logical/mathematical reasoning to understand phenomena.

15. Which of the following is an example of a technological application arising from the study of electromagnetism?

  • A. The electric generator
  • B. The steam engine only
  • C. The wheel
  • D. The lever

Answer: A. The electric generator

Explanation: The electric generator, which converts mechanical energy into electrical energy, is a direct technological application of electromagnetic principles.

16. Physics is often described as the most 'fundamental' science because:

  • A. Its laws underlie the principles used in other sciences like chemistry and biology
  • B. It has no relation to other sciences
  • C. It only studies living organisms
  • D. It does not use mathematics

Answer: A. Its laws underlie the principles used in other sciences like chemistry and biology

Explanation: The basic laws of physics form the foundation upon which many principles of chemistry, biology and engineering are built.

17. Which scientist is credited with discovering the laws governing planetary motion?

  • A. Johannes Kepler
  • B. Isaac Newton
  • C. Galileo Galilei
  • D. Albert Einstein

Answer: A. Johannes Kepler

Explanation: Johannes Kepler formulated three laws describing the motion of planets around the sun, based on careful astronomical observations.

18. The invention of the transistor, a key technological application of physics, revolutionised the field of:

  • A. Electronics
  • B. Mechanics only
  • C. Optics only
  • D. Thermodynamics only

Answer: A. Electronics

Explanation: The transistor, developed from principles of solid-state physics, became foundational to modern electronics.

19. Which of the following best describes 'unification' in physics?

  • A. Explaining diverse physical phenomena using a common set of fundamental laws
  • B. Separating physics into unrelated branches
  • C. Ignoring mathematical relationships
  • D. Focusing only on one type of force

Answer: A. Explaining diverse physical phenomena using a common set of fundamental laws

Explanation: Unification refers to the effort of explaining seemingly different phenomena, such as electricity and magnetism, through a single unified framework.

20. Which of the following is an example of 'reductionism' in physics?

  • A. Explaining the behaviour of bulk matter in terms of the properties of its constituent atoms and molecules
  • B. Studying only large-scale phenomena
  • C. Ignoring atomic-level details
  • D. Focusing only on biological systems

Answer: A. Explaining the behaviour of bulk matter in terms of the properties of its constituent atoms and molecules

Explanation: Reductionism involves understanding complex, large-scale behaviour by breaking it down into simpler, fundamental constituent interactions.

21. The theory of relativity primarily deals with phenomena involving:

  • A. Very high speeds and strong gravitational fields
  • B. Only small, everyday objects
  • C. Only chemical reactions
  • D. Only sound waves

Answer: A. Very high speeds and strong gravitational fields

Explanation: Einstein's theory of relativity becomes especially significant when analysing objects moving at speeds close to that of light or in strong gravitational fields.

22. Which of the following is considered a technological application of the laws of thermodynamics?

  • A. The steam engine
  • B. The optical fibre
  • C. The transistor
  • D. The radio

Answer: A. The steam engine

Explanation: The steam engine operates based on principles of heat and work transfer, directly applying the laws of thermodynamics.

23. The discovery of X-rays is attributed to:

  • A. Wilhelm Röntgen
  • B. Isaac Newton
  • C. Michael Faraday
  • D. James Clerk Maxwell

Answer: A. Wilhelm Röntgen

Explanation: Wilhelm Röntgen discovered X-rays in 1895, a discovery that had major implications for both physics and medicine.

24. The nature of physics as an experimental science means that its theories must be:

  • A. Testable and verifiable through observation or experiment
  • B. Accepted without any verification
  • C. Based purely on philosophical reasoning
  • D. Independent of mathematical description

Answer: A. Testable and verifiable through observation or experiment

Explanation: A key characteristic of physics as an experimental science is that all proposed theories must be capable of being tested and validated through experimentation.

25. Which force is responsible for holding the nucleus of an atom together despite the repulsion between protons?

  • A. Strong nuclear force
  • B. Gravitational force
  • C. Weak nuclear force
  • D. Electromagnetic force

Answer: A. Strong nuclear force

Explanation: The strong nuclear force overcomes the electromagnetic repulsion between positively charged protons, binding the nucleus together.

26. Who among the following made significant contributions to the early development of quantum theory?

  • A. Max Planck
  • B. Isaac Newton
  • C. Galileo Galilei
  • D. Johannes Kepler

Answer: A. Max Planck

Explanation: Max Planck's introduction of quantised energy in 1900 laid the foundational groundwork for the later development of quantum mechanics.

27. The scope of physics includes the study of phenomena ranging from:

  • A. The subatomic scale to the astronomical scale
  • B. Only the human-scale world
  • C. Only chemical reactions
  • D. Only biological processes

Answer: A. The subatomic scale to the astronomical scale

Explanation: Physics encompasses an extremely wide range of scales, from subatomic particles to galaxies and the universe as a whole.

28. Which of the following best represents an example of a macroscopic domain studied in classical physics?

  • A. The motion of planets and everyday objects
  • B. The behaviour of individual electrons in an atom
  • C. Nuclear decay processes
  • D. Quantum entanglement of particles

Answer: A. The motion of planets and everyday objects

Explanation: Classical physics, including Newtonian mechanics, effectively describes macroscopic phenomena like the motion of planets and large everyday objects.

29. The application of physics principles to develop devices like lasers falls under practical uses of:

  • A. Optics and quantum physics
  • B. Only classical mechanics
  • C. Only thermodynamics
  • D. Only gravitational studies

Answer: A. Optics and quantum physics

Explanation: Lasers are a direct technological application arising from principles of optics combined with quantum mechanical understanding of atomic energy levels.

30. The primary goal of physics as a discipline is to:

  • A. Discover and understand the fundamental laws governing the universe
  • B. Only classify different types of matter
  • C. Only measure physical quantities
  • D. Only develop new technologies without theoretical understanding

Answer: A. Discover and understand the fundamental laws governing the universe

Explanation: At its core, physics seeks to uncover and understand the fundamental principles and laws that govern all natural phenomena in the universe.

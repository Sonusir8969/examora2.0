Chapter 14: Decolonisation in Asia and Africa 30 Questions

1. What is 'Decolonisation'?

  • A. The expansion of empires
  • B. The process of colonies gaining independence
  • C. A type of war
  • D. The colonization of the moon

Answer: B. The process of colonies gaining independence

Explanation: Decolonisation is the historical process where colonial territories broke free from European imperial rule.

2. India gained independence from British rule in which year?

  • A. 1945
  • B. 1947
  • C. 1950
  • D. 1942

Answer: B. 1947

Explanation: India achieved independence on 15 August 1947, becoming a sovereign nation and marking the beginning of massive decolonisation in Asia.

3. Who was the first Prime Minister of independent India?

  • A. Mahatma Gandhi
  • B. Jawaharlal Nehru
  • C. Sardar Patel
  • D. Subhas Chandra Bose

Answer: B. Jawaharlal Nehru

Explanation: Jawaharlal Nehru became the first Prime Minister of India upon its independence in 1947.

4. The partition of British India into India and Pakistan was based on what line?

  • A. The Radcliffe Line
  • B. The Durand Line
  • C. The Maginot Line
  • D. The 38th Parallel

Answer: A. The Radcliffe Line

Explanation: The Radcliffe Line was drawn in 1947 to demarcate the borders between the new nations of India and Pakistan.

5. Which event in 1947 triggered a massive wave of decolonisation across Asia and Africa?

  • A. The end of WWII
  • B. The Great Depression
  • C. The Russian Revolution
  • D. The Cold War

Answer: A. The end of WWII

Explanation: WWII exhausted European powers like Britain and France, making it impossible for them to retain their vast colonial empires.

6. Who was the leader of the Indonesian independence movement against the Dutch?

  • A. Ho Chi Minh
  • B. Sukarno
  • C. Mahatma Gandhi
  • D. Jomo Kenyatta

Answer: B. Sukarno

Explanation: Sukarno declared Indonesian independence in 1945 and successfully led the struggle against Dutch colonial rule.

7. Who was the leader of the nationalist movement in Ghana against British rule?

  • A. Kwame Nkrumah
  • B. Jomo Kenyatta
  • C. Patrice Lumumba
  • D. Julius Nyerere

Answer: A. Kwame Nkrumah

Explanation: Kwame Nkrumah became the first Prime Minister of independent Ghana in 1957, a pivotal moment for African decolonisation.

8. Vietnam gained independence from which colonial power?

  • A. Britain
  • B. France
  • C. Germany
  • D. USA

Answer: B. France

Explanation: Vietnam, along with Laos and Cambodia, fought a long war for independence from French Indochina, culminating in the Dien Bien Phu victory in 1954.

9. Who led the Vietnamese independence movement against the French?

  • A. Ho Chi Minh
  • B. Vo Nguyen Giap
  • C. Ngo Dinh Diem
  • D. Pol Pot

Answer: A. Ho Chi Minh

Explanation: Ho Chi Minh was the communist revolutionary leader who declared Vietnamese independence in 1945 and fought the French and later the US.

10. What was the 'Bandung Conference' of 1955?

  • A. A meeting of colonial rulers
  • B. A conference of newly independent Asian and African nations
  • C. A US military base
  • D. A European trade meeting

Answer: B. A conference of newly independent Asian and African nations

Explanation: The Bandung Conference was a historic meeting in Indonesia where 29 Afro-Asian nations came together to oppose colonialism and push for peace.

11. The struggle against apartheid in South Africa was led by which organization?

  • A. The Indian National Congress
  • B. The African National Congress (ANC)
  • C. The Pan-African Congress
  • D. The Communist Party

Answer: B. The African National Congress (ANC)

Explanation: The ANC, led by figures like Nelson Mandela, was the primary organization fighting against the institutionalized racial segregation of apartheid.

12. Who became the first President of South Africa to be elected in a fully democratic election?

  • A. F.W. de Klerk
  • B. Nelson Mandela
  • C. Desmond Tutu
  • D. Steve Biko

Answer: B. Nelson Mandela

Explanation: Nelson Mandela was elected President in 1994, marking the end of apartheid and the final completion of decolonisation in South Africa.

13. The partition of India led to the creation of which two major nations?

  • A. India and Bangladesh
  • B. India and Pakistan
  • C. India and Myanmar
  • D. India and Sri Lanka

Answer: B. India and Pakistan

Explanation: The 1947 partition created the independent dominions of India, a predominantly Hindu nation, and Pakistan, a predominantly Muslim nation.

14. The decolonisation of Africa began in the late 1950s with the independence of which nation?

  • A. Nigeria
  • B. Ghana
  • C. Kenya
  • D. Zimbabwe

Answer: B. Ghana

Explanation: Ghana became the first black African nation south of the Sahara to achieve independence, doing so from Britain in 1957.

15. Why was the Suez Canal crisis of 1956 a significant turning point for decolonisation?

  • A. It strengthened Britain and France
  • B. It showed that European powers could no longer control colonies without US approval
  • C. It established a new canal
  • D. It united the US and USSR

Answer: B. It showed that European powers could no longer control colonies without US approval

Explanation: When Britain and France tried to seize the Suez Canal, the US and USSR forced them to retreat, proving the decline of old colonial powers.

16. What was the role of Jomo Kenyatta in the decolonisation of Kenya?

  • A. He served as the first President of independent Kenya
  • B. He led the Mau Mau rebellion
  • C. He was a major political leader
  • D. All of the above

Answer: D. All of the above

Explanation: Jomo Kenyatta was a central figure in the Kenyan independence struggle, involved in the Mau Mau uprising, and became Kenya's first President in 1964.

17. The 'Non-Aligned Movement' was heavily supported by India. What was its primary aim?

  • A. To fight in the Cold War
  • B. To stay neutral and promote peace
  • C. To join NATO
  • D. To dismantle the UN

Answer: B. To stay neutral and promote peace

Explanation: Newly decolonised nations joined the Non-Aligned Movement to avoid being drawn into the US-Soviet Cold War.

18. Which country was the first in Southeast Asia to become independent after WWII?

  • A. Myanmar
  • B. Indonesia
  • C. Philippines
  • D. Vietnam

Answer: C. Philippines

Explanation: The Philippines gained independence from the US on 4 July 1946, making it the first Southeast Asian nation to become independent after the war.

19. The end of the British Raj in India also led to the political and communal violence known as what?

  • A. The Salt March
  • B. The Partition riots
  • C. The Jallianwala Bagh massacre
  • D. The Chauri Chaura incident

Answer: B. The Partition riots

Explanation: The 1947 Partition of India was accompanied by catastrophic, deadly communal violence, resulting in the displacement of millions.

20. What was the main motivation for European countries to finally grant independence to their colonies?

  • A. They wanted to leave voluntarily
  • B. They were pressured by international opinion and local nationalist movements
  • C. They were bribed
  • D. They had no more resources left

Answer: B. They were pressured by international opinion and local nationalist movements

Explanation: The relentless pressure of strong nationalist movements, combined with international disapproval under the new UN charter, forced decolonisation.

21. Which country famously fought a revolutionary war for its independence from France, led by the FLN?

  • A. Algeria
  • B. Morocco
  • C. Senegal
  • D. Tunisia

Answer: A. Algeria

Explanation: Algeria fought a brutal, bloody war against French rule from 1954 to 1962, eventually achieving independence.

22. Who was a key leader of the Algerian struggle for independence?

  • A. Gamal Abdel Nasser
  • B. Ahmed Ben Bella
  • C. Kwame Nkrumah
  • D. Jomo Kenyatta

Answer: B. Ahmed Ben Bella

Explanation: Ahmed Ben Bella was a major leader of the Algerian National Liberation Front (FLN) and became the first President of independent Algeria.

23. How did the Cold War impact the process of decolonisation?

  • A. It stopped it completely
  • B. It turned many new nations into proxy battlegrounds between the US and USSR
  • C. It had no effect
  • D. It made it faster for Europe

Answer: B. It turned many new nations into proxy battlegrounds between the US and USSR

Explanation: Newly independent nations often became pawns in the Cold War, with the US and USSR rushing to gain their allegiance.

24. Which Southeast Asian country was not colonized by European powers, keeping its independence intact?

  • A. Thailand
  • B. Indonesia
  • C. Philippines
  • D. Singapore

Answer: A. Thailand

Explanation: Thailand (formerly Siam) successfully avoided European colonization by cleverly playing Britain and France against each other.

25. The final decolonisation of most African countries was completed by which decade?

  • A. 1950s
  • B. 1960s
  • C. 1970s
  • D. 1980s

Answer: B. 1960s

Explanation: The 1960s was the "Year of Africa", where 17 African nations achieved independence, though many others gained it later in the 1970s.

26. The African nation of the Congo (now DRC) gained independence from which colonial power?

  • A. France
  • B. Belgium
  • C. Germany
  • D. Britain

Answer: B. Belgium

Explanation: The Belgian Congo gained independence in 1960, with Patrice Lumumba becoming its first Prime Minister.

27. What was the primary challenge faced by newly decolonised nations?

  • A. Abundance of wealth
  • B. Economic dependency on former colonizers
  • C. Lack of resources
  • D. Strong domestic industrial bases

Answer: B. Economic dependency on former colonizers

Explanation: Even after independence, many African and Asian nations remained economically dependent on their former rulers for trade and aid.

28. The conflict between India and Pakistan over the region of Kashmir began right after independence. In which year did the first Indo-Pak war occur?

  • A. 1947
  • B. 1948
  • C. 1950
  • D. 1946

Answer: A. 1947

Explanation: The first war between India and Pakistan over Kashmir erupted immediately after independence in October 1947.

29. What was the name of the European colonial policy that divided Africa into arbitrary borders?

  • A. The Scramble for Africa
  • B. The Treaties of Utrecht
  • C. The Marshall Plan
  • D. The Napoleonic Code

Answer: A. The Scramble for Africa

Explanation: The Scramble for Africa (1884-85) carved arbitrary borders for the continent, which caused immense ethnic conflicts after decolonisation.

30. Which 20th-century event in the USA influenced the eventual independence of the Philippines?

  • A. The Cold War
  • B. The Great Depression
  • C. World War II
  • D. The Korean War

Answer: C. World War II

Explanation: The Japanese occupation of the Philippines during WWII weakened US control, leading the US to grant the Philippines independence in 1946.

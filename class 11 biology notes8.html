NCERT Solutions for Class 11 Biology Chapter 8 Cell: The Unit of Life

NCERT Solutions for Class 11 Biology Chapter 8 Cell: The Unit of Life provides comprehensive notes and detailed answers on cell theory, prokaryotic and eukaryotic cell structures, organelle functions, and chromosome organization.

1. What is a Cell & Cell Theory

Short Note:

• Definition: The cell is the fundamental structural and functional unit of all living organisms. Anton von Leeuwenhoek first saw and described a live cell.

• Cell Theory Formulation:

  - Matthias Schleiden (1838): Observed that all plants are composed of different kinds of cells.

  - Theodore Schwann (1839): Reported that animal cells have a thin outer layer (plasma membrane) and stated that plant cells uniquely possess a cell wall.

  - Rudolf Virchow (1855): Modified cell theory by adding "Omnis cellula-e cellula" (all cells arise from pre-existing cells).

• Modern Cell Theory Postulates:

  1. All living organisms are composed of cells and products of cells.

  2. All cells arise from pre-existing cells through cell division.

2. Prokaryotic Cells

Short Note:

• Represented by Bacteria, Blue-green algae, Mycoplasma (PPLO - Pleuro Pneumonia Like Organisms). Multiply rapidly and lack a membrane-bound nucleus or membrane-bound organelles.

• Cell Envelope: Consists of a tightly bound 3-layered structure: outermost Glycocalyx (slime layer/capsule), middle Cell Wall, and innermost Plasma Membrane.

• Mesosomes: Special membranous structures formed by extensions of plasma membrane into the cell. Function in cell wall formation, DNA replication, respiration, and secretion.

• Genetic Material: Single circular genomic DNA not enclosed by a nuclear membrane (Naked DNA/Nucleoid). Many bacteria also have small circular extrachromosomal DNA called Plasmids (confer resistance to antibiotics).

• Ribosomes & Inclusion Bodies: 70S ribosomes (50S + 30S subunits) site of protein synthesis. Reserve materials stored as non-membrane bound inclusion bodies (e.g., phosphate granules, cyanophycean granules, glycogen granules).

3. Eukaryotic Cell Structure & Organelles

Short Note:

• Characterized by extensive compartmentalization of cytoplasm through membrane-bound organelles and a well-organized nucleus.

• Plasma Membrane: Based on the Fluid Mosaic Model (Singer and Nicolson, 1972). Phospholipid bilayer with embedded proteins (integral and peripheral) and carbohydrates. Shows quasi-fluid nature allowing lateral movement of proteins.

• Endomembrane System: Coordinated organelles including:

  - Endoplasmic Reticulum (ER): RER (bearing ribosomes, protein synthesis) and SER (lipid and steroid synthesis).

  - Golgi Apparatus: Cis and trans faces; functions in packaging, modification, and secretion of proteins/lipids.

  - Lysosomes: Hydrolytic enzyme-rich vesicles ("suicide bags") active at acidic pH.

  - Vacuoles: Membrane-bound space (tonoplast) storing water, sap, and excretory products.

• Energy Transducers:

  - Mitochondria: Double-membraned "powerhouse of the cell", site of aerobic respiration producing ATP. Contains 70S ribosomes and circular DNA.

  - Plastids: Chloroplasts (photosynthesis, contains chlorophyll), Chromoplasts (fat-soluble pigments like carotene/xanthophyll), Leucoplasts (colorless storage plastids like Amyloplasts, Elaioplasts, Aleuroplasts).

• Ribosomes: 80S type (60S + 40S subunits) in eukaryotic cytoplasm, non-membrane bound, discovered by George Palade.

4. Nucleus & Chromosomes

Short Note:

• First described by Robert Brown (1831). Stained material named Chromatin by Flemming.

• Structure: Double-layered nuclear envelope with nuclear pores, nucleoplasm, nucleolus (site of active rRNA synthesis), and chromatin network.

• Chromosome Classification (based on Centromere position):

  1. Metacentric: Centromere in the middle forming two equal arms.

  2. Sub-metacentric: Centromere slightly away from middle, forming one shorter arm and one longer arm.

  3. Acrocentric: Centromere situated close to its end, forming one extremely short and one very long arm.

  4. Telocentric: Terminal centromere at the tip of chromosome.

Intext & Conceptual Questions

Q1: Which of the following is not correct?
(a) Robert Brown discovered the nucleus.
(b) Schleiden and Schwann formulated the cell theory.
(c) Virchow explained that cells arise from pre-existing cells.
(d) A unicellular organism carries out its life activities within a single cell.

Answer: All options listed above are scientifically correct statements. (If formatted as an option error question: "Schleiden and Schwann modified the cell theory" would be incorrect as Virchow gave the final shape).
Q2: What is a mesosome in a prokaryotic cell? Mention its functions.
Answer:
A mesosome is a characteristic folded structure in prokaryotic cells formed by the extensions of the plasma membrane into the cytoplasm (in the form of vesicles, tubules, and lamellae).

Functions of Mesosomes:
• Helps in cell wall formation.
• Aids in DNA replication and distribution of chromosomes to daughter cells.
• Increases the surface area of plasma membrane and enzymatic content.
• Plays an active role in cellular respiration and secretion processes.

Textbook Chapter End Exercise Questions

Q1: Differentiate between Prokaryotic and Eukaryotic Cells.
Answer:
Feature Prokaryotic Cell Eukaryotic Cell
Nuclear Envelope Absent (Genetic material in Nucleoid). Present (Well-defined nucleus).
Membrane-Bound Organelles Absent (No ER, Golgi, Mitochondria, etc.). Present (Mitochondria, ER, Golgi present).
Ribosome Type 70S type (50S + 30S). 80S type in cytoplasm (70S in organelles).
DNA Structure Circular, naked DNA without histones. Linear DNA associated with histone proteins.
Q2: Distinguish between Plant Cell and Animal Cell.
Answer:
Feature Plant Cell Animal Cell
Cell Wall Present (composed of cellulose/pectin). Absent.
Plastids/Chloroplasts Present. Absent.
Vacuole Large central permanent vacuole. Small, temporary, or absent.
Centrosome/Centrioles Absent in higher plants. Present (aids in cell division).

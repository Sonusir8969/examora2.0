Phy-5: Laws of Motion 30 Questions

1. Newton's First Law of Motion is also known as the:

  • A. Law of inertia
  • B. Law of action-reaction
  • C. Law of momentum conservation only
  • D. Law of gravitation

Answer: A. Law of inertia

Explanation: Newton's First Law describes the tendency of an object to resist changes in its state of motion, hence it is called the law of inertia.

2. According to Newton's First Law, an object at rest will remain at rest unless acted upon by:

  • A. An external unbalanced force
  • B. Its own weight
  • C. Time alone
  • D. Internal forces only

Answer: A. An external unbalanced force

Explanation: The law states that an object maintains its state of rest (or uniform motion) unless a net external force acts upon it.

3. The property of a body that resists changes in its state of motion is called:

  • A. Inertia
  • B. Momentum
  • C. Velocity
  • D. Force

Answer: A. Inertia

Explanation: Inertia is the inherent tendency of an object to resist any change in its velocity, whether at rest or in motion.

4. Newton's Second Law of Motion states that the rate of change of momentum of a body is directly proportional to the:

  • A. Applied external force
  • B. Mass of the body only
  • C. Velocity of the body only
  • D. Time period only

Answer: A. Applied external force

Explanation: Newton's Second Law establishes that force equals the rate of change of momentum, acting in the direction of the applied force.

5. The mathematical expression of Newton's Second Law is commonly written as:

  • A. F = ma
  • B. F = mv
  • C. F = m/a
  • D. F = ma²

Answer: A. F = ma

Explanation: Newton's Second Law is expressed as force equals mass multiplied by acceleration (F = ma), assuming constant mass.

6. The product of an object's mass and velocity is called its:

  • A. Momentum
  • B. Force
  • C. Inertia
  • D. Acceleration

Answer: A. Momentum

Explanation: Momentum is defined as the product of an object's mass and its velocity, and is a vector quantity.

7. Newton's Third Law of Motion states that for every action, there is:

  • A. An equal and opposite reaction
  • B. A doubled reaction
  • C. No reaction at all
  • D. A reaction in the same direction

Answer: A. An equal and opposite reaction

Explanation: Newton's Third Law describes how forces always occur in pairs: for every action force, there exists an equal and opposite reaction force.

8. According to Newton's Third Law, action and reaction forces act on:

  • A. Two different bodies
  • B. The same body
  • C. No body at all
  • D. Only stationary bodies

Answer: A. Two different bodies

Explanation: Action and reaction forces always act on two different objects, which is why they do not cancel each other out.

9. The SI unit of force is the:

  • A. Newton
  • B. Joule
  • C. Watt
  • D. Pascal

Answer: A. Newton

Explanation: The newton (N) is the SI unit of force, defined as the force needed to accelerate a 1 kg mass at 1 m/s².

10. The law of conservation of momentum states that the total momentum of an isolated system remains:

  • A. Constant, provided no external force acts on it
  • B. Always increasing
  • C. Always decreasing
  • D. Zero at all times

Answer: A. Constant, provided no external force acts on it

Explanation: In the absence of external forces, the total momentum of a closed or isolated system remains constant over time.

11. Friction is a force that acts:

  • A. Opposite to the direction of relative motion or tendency of motion
  • B. In the same direction as motion
  • C. Perpendicular to the surface only
  • D. Only in circular motion

Answer: A. Opposite to the direction of relative motion or tendency of motion

Explanation: Friction always opposes the relative sliding or tendency of sliding between two surfaces in contact.

12. The maximum value of static friction just before an object starts to move is called:

  • A. Limiting friction
  • B. Kinetic friction
  • C. Rolling friction
  • D. Zero friction

Answer: A. Limiting friction

Explanation: Limiting friction represents the maximum static frictional force that must be overcome before motion begins.

13. The friction experienced by an object while it is already in motion is called:

  • A. Kinetic friction
  • B. Static friction
  • C. Limiting friction only
  • D. Rolling friction exclusively

Answer: A. Kinetic friction

Explanation: Kinetic (or sliding) friction acts on an object that is already moving relative to another surface.

14. The type of friction that occurs when an object rolls over a surface is called:

  • A. Rolling friction
  • B. Static friction
  • C. Kinetic sliding friction
  • D. Limiting friction

Answer: A. Rolling friction

Explanation: Rolling friction specifically refers to the resistance experienced by a rolling object, generally much smaller than sliding friction.

15. Which type of friction is generally the smallest in magnitude?

  • A. Rolling friction
  • B. Static friction
  • C. Sliding (kinetic) friction
  • D. Limiting friction

Answer: A. Rolling friction

Explanation: Rolling friction is typically much smaller than both static and kinetic friction, which is why wheels are used to reduce resistance.

16. The force required to keep an object moving in a circular path is called:

  • A. Centripetal force
  • B. Centrifugal force
  • C. Gravitational force only
  • D. Frictional force only

Answer: A. Centripetal force

Explanation: Centripetal force is the net inward force that keeps an object moving along a circular path.

17. According to the impulse-momentum theorem, impulse is equal to the:

  • A. Change in momentum of the object
  • B. Force applied alone
  • C. Velocity of the object
  • D. Time of contact alone

Answer: A. Change in momentum of the object

Explanation: Impulse, defined as force multiplied by the time duration of its application, equals the resulting change in an object's momentum.

18. A system on which no external force acts is called a/an:

  • A. Isolated system
  • B. Open system
  • C. Dependent system
  • D. Accelerating system

Answer: A. Isolated system

Explanation: An isolated system experiences no net external force, allowing conservation laws like momentum conservation to apply directly.

19. The recoil of a gun when a bullet is fired is an example of:

  • A. Newton's Third Law and conservation of momentum
  • B. Newton's First Law only
  • C. Only gravitational force
  • D. Only frictional force

Answer: A. Newton's Third Law and conservation of momentum

Explanation: The equal and opposite reaction force on the gun, combined with momentum conservation, explains the recoil experienced when firing.

20. The equilibrium of a body under the action of several forces means that the net force acting on it is:

  • A. Zero
  • B. Maximum
  • C. Always equal to weight
  • D. Undefined

Answer: A. Zero

Explanation: A body is in equilibrium when the vector sum of all forces acting on it is exactly zero, resulting in no net acceleration.

21. If a body moves with constant velocity, the net force acting on it must be:

  • A. Zero
  • B. Maximum
  • C. Continuously increasing
  • D. Equal to its weight only

Answer: A. Zero

Explanation: According to Newton's First and Second Laws, constant velocity implies zero acceleration, which means the net force is zero.

22. The tendency of passengers to lean forward when a bus suddenly stops is explained by:

  • A. Inertia of motion
  • B. Inertia of rest
  • C. Newton's Third Law only
  • D. Frictional force only

Answer: A. Inertia of motion

Explanation: Since passengers were already in motion, their bodies tend to continue moving forward due to inertia, even as the bus stops.

23. In a rocket, forward motion is achieved due to:

  • A. The reaction force from expelled exhaust gases
  • B. Air resistance
  • C. Gravitational pull only
  • D. Magnetic force only

Answer: A. The reaction force from expelled exhaust gases

Explanation: As gases are expelled backward from the rocket, an equal and opposite reaction force propels the rocket forward, per Newton's Third Law.

24. The coefficient of friction between two surfaces depends primarily on:

  • A. The nature of the two surfaces in contact
  • B. The mass of the object alone
  • C. The velocity of the object
  • D. The colour of the surfaces

Answer: A. The nature of the two surfaces in contact

Explanation: The coefficient of friction is a property determined by the roughness and material characteristics of the two contacting surfaces.

25. If a net external force acts on a body, according to Newton's Second Law, the body experiences:

  • A. An acceleration in the direction of the net force
  • B. No change in motion
  • C. A constant velocity only
  • D. Only a change in mass

Answer: A. An acceleration in the direction of the net force

Explanation: A net external force produces an acceleration in the same direction as the applied force, as described by F = ma.

26. Banking of roads at curves is done primarily to:

  • A. Provide the necessary centripetal force for vehicles to turn safely
  • B. Reduce the weight of vehicles
  • C. Increase friction unnecessarily
  • D. Decrease the speed limit

Answer: A. Provide the necessary centripetal force for vehicles to turn safely

Explanation: Banking the road at an angle helps provide a component of the normal force towards the centre, assisting in safely negotiating the curve.

27. According to the law of conservation of momentum, in a collision between two isolated bodies, the total momentum before collision is:

  • A. Equal to the total momentum after collision
  • B. Always greater after collision
  • C. Always less after collision
  • D. Completely lost after collision

Answer: A. Equal to the total momentum after collision

Explanation: In an isolated system, momentum is conserved, meaning the total momentum remains unchanged before and after the collision.

28. A force of 10 N acting on a mass of 2 kg produces an acceleration of:

  • A. 5 m/s²
  • B. 20 m/s²
  • C. 0.2 m/s²
  • D. 12 m/s²

Answer: A. 5 m/s²

Explanation: Using F = ma: a = F/m = 10/2 = 5 m/s².

29. The apparent weight of a person in a lift accelerating upward is:

  • A. Greater than their actual weight
  • B. Less than their actual weight
  • C. Equal to zero
  • D. Unrelated to acceleration

Answer: A. Greater than their actual weight

Explanation: When a lift accelerates upward, the normal force (and hence apparent weight) increases beyond the person's actual weight due to the added acceleration.

30. If two objects of different masses experience the same force, the object with smaller mass will have:

  • A. Greater acceleration
  • B. Smaller acceleration
  • C. Equal acceleration
  • D. Zero acceleration

Answer: A. Greater acceleration

Explanation: Since acceleration = force/mass, for a fixed applied force, a smaller mass results in a proportionally greater acceleration.

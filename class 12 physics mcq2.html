Ch-2: Electrostatic Potential and Capacitance 25 Questions

1. What is the SI unit of Electric Potential?

  • A. Joule
  • B. Volt
  • C. Farad
  • D. Coulomb

Answer: B. Volt

Explanation: Electric potential is defined as work done per unit charge (V = W/q), which has the SI unit Joule/Coulomb, commonly known as Volt (V).

2. Electric potential is a:

  • A. Scalar quantity
  • B. Vector quantity
  • C. Tensor quantity
  • D. Dimensionless quantity

Answer: A. Scalar quantity

Explanation: Electric potential is defined as work done per unit charge. Since both work and charge are scalars, electric potential is a scalar quantity.

3. Work done in moving a test charge over an equipotential surface is:

  • A. Maximum
  • B. Minimum
  • C. Zero
  • D. Infinite

Answer: C. Zero

Explanation: On an equipotential surface, potential difference (ΔV) between any two points is zero. Since W = q × ΔV, the work done is zero.

4. The angle between an equipotential surface and the electric field lines at any point is always:

  • A. 0°
  • B. 45°
  • C. 90°
  • D. 180°

Answer: C. 90°

Explanation: Electric field lines are always perpendicular (at 90°) to equipotential surfaces because no work is done moving a charge along the surface.

5. Electric potential due to a point charge 'q' at a distance 'r' varies as:

  • A. Directly proportional to r
  • B. Inversely proportional to r
  • C. Inversely proportional to r²
  • D. Independent of r

Answer: B. Inversely proportional to r

Explanation: The potential due to a single point charge is given by V = q / (4πε₀r), showing that V ∝ 1/r.

6. What is the electric potential at any point on the equatorial plane of an electric dipole?

  • A. Maximum
  • B. Minimum
  • C. Zero
  • D. Infinite

Answer: C. Zero

Explanation: Every point on the equatorial plane is equidistant from both +q and -q charges, so the net potential V = V_+ + V_- = 0.

7. The relation between electric field 'E' and electric potential 'V' is given by:

  • A. E = -dV/dr
  • B. E = dV/dr
  • C. V = -dE/dr
  • D. E = V × r

Answer: A. E = -dV/dr

Explanation: Electric field is equal to the negative potential gradient (E = -dV/dr), indicating that field points in the direction of decreasing potential.

8. What is the electric potential inside a hollow charged spherical conductor of radius R?

  • A. Zero
  • B. Same as on its surface
  • C. Half of surface potential
  • D. Infinite

Answer: B. Same as on its surface

Explanation: Since electric field inside a hollow charged conductor is zero (E = 0), dV/dr = 0, meaning potential V remains constant and equal to its surface value everywhere inside.

9. Electrostatic potential energy of a system of two point charges q₁ and q₂ separated by distance 'r' is:

  • A. U = (1 / 4πε₀) * (q₁q₂ / r)
  • B. U = (1 / 4πε₀) * (q₁q₂ / r²)
  • C. U = (1 / 4πε₀) * (q₁ + q₂ / r)
  • D. U = zero

Answer: A. U = (1 / 4πε₀) * (q₁q₂ / r)

Explanation: Potential energy U is equal to work done in bringing charges from infinity to distance r, given by U = (q₁q₂) / (4πε₀r).

10. What is the potential energy of an electric dipole placed parallel (θ = 0°) to a uniform electric field?

  • A. U = +pE
  • B. U = -pE
  • C. U = 0
  • D. U = pE / 2

Answer: B. U = -pE

Explanation: Dipole potential energy U = -pE cosθ. For θ = 0°, cos 0° = 1, giving minimum potential energy U = -pE (stable equilibrium).

11. An electric dipole is in most unstable equilibrium in a uniform electric field when angle θ between 'p' and 'E' is:

  • A. 0°
  • B. 90°
  • C. 180°
  • D. 360°

Answer: C. 180°

Explanation: At θ = 180°, potential energy U = -pE cos(180°) = +pE, which is maximum, making it the most unstable equilibrium state.

12. What is the SI unit of Electrical Capacitance?

  • A. Volt
  • B. Farad
  • C. Ohm
  • D. Henry

Answer: B. Farad

Explanation: Capacitance C = Q/V has the SI unit Coulomb per Volt, which is defined as Farad (F).

13. The capacitance of an isolated spherical conductor of radius 'R' is given by:

  • A. C = 4πε₀ R
  • B. C = 4πε₀ / R
  • C. C = R / (4πε₀)
  • D. C = 2πε₀ R

Answer: A. C = 4πε₀ R

Explanation: Since V = Q / (4πε₀R) on a sphere, capacitance C = Q/V = 4πε₀ R, showing capacitance is directly proportional to radius.

14. The capacitance of a parallel plate capacitor with plate area 'A' and separation 'd' in vacuum is:

  • A. C = ε₀ A / d
  • B. C = ε₀ d / A
  • C. C = A d / ε₀
  • D. C = 2ε₀ A / d

Answer: A. C = ε₀ A / d

Explanation: Capacitance of a parallel plate capacitor in vacuum is C₀ = ε₀ A / d.

15. When a dielectric medium of dielectric constant 'K' is completely filled between the plates of a capacitor, its capacitance:

  • A. Decreases K times
  • B. Increases K times
  • C. Remains unchanged
  • D. Becomes zero

Answer: B. Increases K times

Explanation: Inserting a dielectric increases the capacitance to C = K × C₀, where K is the dielectric constant of the material.

16. Three capacitors each of capacitance 'C' are connected in series. Their equivalent capacitance is:

  • A. 3C
  • B. C / 3
  • C. C / 2
  • D. 9C

Answer: B. C / 3

Explanation: In series, 1/C_eq = 1/C + 1/C + 1/C = 3/C, which gives C_eq = C / 3.

17. Three capacitors each of capacitance 'C' are connected in parallel. Their equivalent capacitance is:

  • A. 3C
  • B. C / 3
  • C. C
  • D. C / 9

Answer: A. 3C

Explanation: In parallel connection, capacitances simply add up: C_eq = C₁ + C₂ + C₃ = C + C + C = 3C.

18. The energy stored in a charged capacitor of capacitance 'C' carrying charge 'Q' and potential 'V' is given by:

  • A. U = 1/2 C V²
  • B. U = 1/2 Q V
  • C. U = Q² / (2C)
  • D. All of the above

Answer: D. All of the above

Explanation: Stored energy formulas are equivalent: U = 1/2 C V² = 1/2 Q V = Q² / (2C).

19. Energy density (energy stored per unit volume) in an electric field 'E' in free space is:

  • A. u = 1/2 ε₀ E²
  • B. u = ε₀ E²
  • C. u = 1/2 ε₀ E
  • D. u = 2 ε₀ E²

Answer: A. u = 1/2 ε₀ E²

Explanation: The energy stored per unit volume in an electric field is given by u = 1/2 ε₀ E².

20. What happens to the energy stored in a capacitor when the distance between its plates is doubled keeping battery connected?

  • A. Doubles
  • B. Halves
  • C. Quadruples
  • D. Remains same

Answer: B. Halves

Explanation: Since battery is connected, V is constant. C = ε₀A/d, so doubling d halves C. Energy U = 1/2 C V² therefore becomes half.

21. The Earth is assumed to be a spherical conductor of radius 6400 km. Its capacitance is approximately:

  • A. 711 μF
  • B. 1 Farad
  • C. 9 × 10⁹ F
  • D. 6400 μF

Answer: A. 711 μF

Explanation: C = 4πε₀ R = (6.4 × 10⁶ m) / (9 × 10⁹) ≈ 7.11 × 10^-4 F = 711 μF.

22. During the sharing of charges between two charged conductors, there is always a loss of:

  • A. Charge
  • B. Electrostatic Energy
  • C. Mass
  • D. Both Charge and Energy

Answer: B. Electrostatic Energy

Explanation: Total charge remains conserved during charge sharing, but some electrostatic energy is always lost as heat and light during current flow.

23. Dielectric strength of a dielectric medium is defined as:

  • A. Maximum electric field it can withstand without breakdown
  • B. Minimum potential applied to break it
  • C. Amount of charge stored per unit area
  • D. Its capacitance value

Answer: A. Maximum electric field it can withstand without breakdown

Explanation: Dielectric strength is the maximum electric field an insulating material can tolerate before breakdown and becoming conducting.

24. What is the capacitance of a parallel plate capacitor if a thin conducting plate of same area is introduced between plates?

  • A. Decreases
  • B. Increases
  • C. Remains constant
  • D. Becomes zero

Answer: B. Increases

Explanation: Introducing a conducting slab effectively reduces the effective distance 'd' between plates, thereby increasing capacitance (C = ε₀A / (d - t)).

25. A 10 μF capacitor is charged to 50 V. The charge on the capacitor is:

  • A. 5 × 10^-4 C
  • B. 500 C
  • C. 5 C
  • D. 5 × 10^-3 C

Answer: A. 5 × 10^-4 C

Explanation: Q = C × V = (10 × 10^-6 F) × (50 V) = 500 × 10^-6 C = 5 × 10^-4 C.

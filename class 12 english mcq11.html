Ch-11: A Thing of Beauty 4 Questions

1. Who is the poet of 'A Thing of Beauty'?

  • A. John Keats
  • B. William Wordsworth
  • C. Percy Bysshe Shelley
  • D. Lord Byron

Answer: A. John Keats

Explanation: 'A Thing of Beauty' is an extract from the longer poem 'Endymion' written by the English Romantic poet John Keats.

2. According to the famous opening idea of the poem, a thing of beauty is:

  • A. A temporary pleasure that quickly fades
  • B. A joy forever, whose loveliness increases and never fades into nothingness
  • C. Only found in nature
  • D. Impossible to define

Answer: B. A joy forever, whose loveliness increases and never fades into nothingness

Explanation: The poem's celebrated opening line states that 'a thing of beauty is a joy forever', asserting that true beauty provides a lasting joy that only continues to grow rather than diminish.

3. According to Keats, what role does beauty play in human life amid suffering?

  • A. It has no impact on human suffering
  • B. It acts as a source of comfort, healing, and hope that helps humanity endure hardships and despair
  • C. It causes further suffering
  • D. It is only relevant to the wealthy

Answer: B. It acts as a source of comfort, healing, and hope that helps humanity endure hardships and despair

Explanation: Keats presents beauty—found in nature, simple pleasures, and great stories—as a vital, soothing force that helps remove the pall of despondency from the human spirit and gives strength to face life's difficulties.

4. Which of the following does the poet NOT mention as an example of a 'thing of beauty' in the poem?

  • A. The sun, the moon, and old and young trees
  • B. Simple sheep grazing in green fields
  • C. Modern machinery and technology
  • D. Daffodils and clear streams amid a forest brake

Answer: C. Modern machinery and technology

Explanation: Keats lists natural and pastoral images such as the sun, moon, trees, sheep, daffodils, and streams as sources of beauty; modern machinery is not part of his Romantic-era imagery.

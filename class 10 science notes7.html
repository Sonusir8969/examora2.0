NCERT Solutions for Class 10 Science Chapter 7 How do Organisms Reproduce?

NCERT Solutions for Class 10 Science Chapter 7 How do Organisms Reproduce? covers the cellular mechanisms of reproduction, DNA replication, asexual modes of reproduction, sexual reproduction in plants and human beings, and reproductive health awareness.

Intext Questions - Page Number: 117

Q1: What is the importance of DNA copying in reproduction?
Answer: DNA copying is fundamental to reproduction because DNA contains genetic information passed from parent to offspring. Accurate DNA replication ensures that offspring inherit structural design and traits specific to their species. Biochemical inaccuracies during copying also generate minor variations, which are essential for evolution.
Q2: Why is variation beneficial to the species but not necessarily for the individual?
Answer: Variations enable a species to survive extreme environmental changes (such as sudden temperature changes or chemical pollution). While an individual variation might not offer an immediate personal benefit or could even be harmful, genetic diversity across the population prevents total extinction of the species during ecological crises.

Intext Questions - Page Number: 123

Q1: How does binary fission differ from multiple fission?

Binary Fission: A single parent organism divides into two equal daughter cells under favorable conditions. Example: Amoeba, Paramecium.

Multiple Fission: A single parent organism divides simultaneously into numerous daughter cells within a protective cyst, usually during unfavorable conditions. Example: Plasmodium.

Q2: How will an organism be benefited if it reproduces through spores?
Answer: Spores are protected by thick outer walls that resist extreme environmental conditions like heat, drought, and lack of nutrients. Being lightweight, spores are easily dispersed by air and water across large geographic areas, germinating when they land on favorable moist surfaces.

Intext Questions - Page Number: 128

Q1: How is the tissue of placenta designed to meet the needs of the developing embryo?
Answer: The placenta is a specialized disc-like tissue embedded in the uterine wall. It features finger-like villi on the embryo’s side and blood spaces surrounding them on the mother’s side. This design creates a huge surface area for transferring glucose, oxygen, and nutrients from mother to fetus and carrying metabolic waste products from fetus back to mother for excretion.

Textbook Chapter End Exercise Questions

Q1: Asexual reproduction takes place through budding in:

(a) Amoeba
(b) Yeast
(c) Plasmodium
(d) Leishmania

Correct Answer: (b) Yeast (and also in Hydra).

Q2: Which of the following is not a part of the female reproductive system in human beings?

(a) Ovary
(b) Uterus
(c) Vas deferens
(d) Fallopian tube

Correct Answer: (c) Vas deferens (it is a part of the male reproductive system).

Q3: The anther contains:

(a) Sepals
(b) Ovules
(c) Pistil
(d) Pollen grains

Correct Answer: (d) Pollen grains.

Q4: What are the advantages of sexual reproduction over asexual reproduction?
Answer:

1. Genetic Diversity: Combines genetic material from two distinct individuals, producing variations in every generation.

2. Adaptability & Evolution: Greater variations enhance population survival rates against diseases and changing climates, accelerating evolutionary adaptation.

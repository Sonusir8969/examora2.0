Ch-3: Plant Kingdom 30 Questions

1. The plant kingdom (Kingdom Plantae in classical botanical classification) is broadly divided based on presence or absence of

  • A. A well-differentiated vascular system
  • B. Chlorophyll
  • C. A cell wall
  • D. Photosynthesis

Answer: A. A well-differentiated vascular system

Explanation: Plants are broadly classified based on the presence or absence of specialised tissues for the conduction of water and nutrients, distinguishing vascular plants (Pteridophytes and above) from non-vascular plants (Algae, Bryophytes).

2. Algae are mainly classified based on which pigment gives them their characteristic colour, as seen in the group name

  • A. Chlorophyceae (green algae), Phaeophyceae (brown algae), Rhodophyceae (red algae)
  • B. Only their habitat (freshwater or marine)
  • C. Only their reproductive structures
  • D. Only their size

Answer: A. Chlorophyceae (green algae), Phaeophyceae (brown algae), Rhodophyceae (red algae)

Explanation: Algae are classified into three main classes based on the type of dominant photosynthetic pigment: Chlorophyceae (green), Phaeophyceae (brown, due to fucoxanthin), and Rhodophyceae (red, due to phycoerythrin).

3. Chlamydomonas and Spirogyra are examples of algae belonging to class

  • A. Chlorophyceae
  • B. Phaeophyceae
  • C. Rhodophyceae
  • D. Bryophyceae

Answer: A. Chlorophyceae

Explanation: Chlamydomonas and Spirogyra are green algae, classified under Chlorophyceae, containing chlorophyll a and b as dominant pigments, giving them a grass-green colour.

4. The reserve food material typically stored in green algae (Chlorophyceae) is

  • A. Starch
  • B. Mannitol
  • C. Floridean starch
  • D. Glycogen

Answer: A. Starch

Explanation: Green algae store food mainly in the form of starch, deposited within the chloroplasts, similar to higher plants.

5. Bryophytes are often called the "amphibians of the plant kingdom" because they

  • A. Live in both land and water and require water for reproduction (fertilization)
  • B. Have gills like amphibians
  • C. Are always fully aquatic
  • D. Never require moisture at all

Answer: A. Live in both land and water and require water for reproduction (fertilization)

Explanation: Bryophytes can grow in moist land habitats but still depend on external water for the male gametes to swim to the female gametes for fertilization, similar to amphibians needing water for reproduction.

6. Which of the following is a common example of a bryophyte?

  • A. Moss (Funaria)
  • B. Fern (Pteris)
  • C. Pine (Pinus)
  • D. Mango (Mangifera)

Answer: A. Moss (Funaria)

Explanation: Moss, such as Funaria, is a classic example of a bryophyte, characterized by a thalloid or leafy structure and dependence on water for reproduction.

7. In the life cycle of bryophytes, the dominant, photosynthetic phase is the

  • A. Gametophyte (haploid) phase
  • B. Sporophyte (diploid) phase
  • C. Both phases equally
  • D. Neither phase; bryophytes have no distinct phases

Answer: A. Gametophyte (haploid) phase

Explanation: In bryophytes, the gametophyte is the dominant, free-living, photosynthetic generation, while the sporophyte remains attached to and dependent on the gametophyte for nutrition.

8. Pteridophytes are distinguished from bryophytes mainly by the presence of

  • A. Well-differentiated vascular tissue (xylem and phloem)
  • B. Chlorophyll
  • C. A haploid gametophyte generation
  • D. A need for water during fertilization

Answer: A. Well-differentiated vascular tissue (xylem and phloem)

Explanation: Unlike bryophytes, pteridophytes possess specialized tissues for the conduction of water and nutrients (xylem and phloem), classifying them as the first true vascular plants.

9. In pteridophytes (like ferns), the dominant, independent phase of the life cycle is the

  • A. Sporophyte (diploid) phase
  • B. Gametophyte (haploid) phase
  • C. Both phases equally dominant
  • D. Neither; pteridophytes reproduce only asexually

Answer: A. Sporophyte (diploid) phase

Explanation: In pteridophytes, unlike bryophytes, the sporophyte is the dominant, independent, and conspicuous phase, while the gametophyte is small and short-lived.

10. Ferns typically reproduce and disperse via

  • A. Spores produced in structures called sporangia
  • B. Seeds enclosed within fruits
  • C. Naked seeds on cone scales
  • D. Pollen grains carried by wind

Answer: A. Spores produced in structures called sporangia

Explanation: Ferns and other pteridophytes reproduce and disperse through spores, which are produced in sporangia, often found in clusters called sori on the underside of fronds (leaves).

11. Gymnosperms are characterized by seeds that are

  • A. Naked, not enclosed within a fruit
  • B. Always enclosed within a fruit
  • C. Absent altogether
  • D. Formed without fertilization

Answer: A. Naked, not enclosed within a fruit

Explanation: The term "gymnosperm" means "naked seed"; in these plants, seeds are not enclosed by any fruit wall but are usually borne exposed on the surface of cone scales.

12. Which of the following is a common example of a gymnosperm?

  • A. Pinus (Pine)
  • B. Mangifera (Mango)
  • C. Funaria (Moss)
  • D. Pteris (Fern)

Answer: A. Pinus (Pine)

Explanation: Pinus (pine) is a well-known gymnosperm, bearing naked seeds on the scales of cones, unlike flowering plants that enclose seeds in fruits.

13. Angiosperms (flowering plants) are distinguished from gymnosperms mainly by

  • A. Having seeds enclosed within a fruit, developing from a fertilized ovary
  • B. Having no vascular tissue
  • C. Lacking any reproductive structures
  • D. Reproducing only asexually via spores

Answer: A. Having seeds enclosed within a fruit, developing from a fertilized ovary

Explanation: Angiosperms are characterized by flowers as reproductive organs and seeds that develop within a fruit, formed from the fertilized ovary, unlike the naked seeds of gymnosperms.

14. Angiosperms are classified into two main classes based on the number of cotyledons in their seeds, called

  • A. Monocotyledons and Dicotyledons
  • B. Gymnosperms and Pteridophytes
  • C. Thallophytes and Bryophytes
  • D. Chlorophyceae and Rhodophyceae

Answer: A. Monocotyledons and Dicotyledons

Explanation: Angiosperms are divided into monocots (having one cotyledon in the seed, e.g., wheat, grass) and dicots (having two cotyledons, e.g., pea, mango).

15. In plants, "alternation of generations" refers to the life cycle alternating between

  • A. A haploid gametophyte phase and a diploid sporophyte phase
  • B. Two diploid phases
  • C. Two haploid phases
  • D. A vegetative phase and a reproductive phase, both diploid

Answer: A. A haploid gametophyte phase and a diploid sporophyte phase

Explanation: Plants exhibit alternation of generations, cycling between a haploid, gamete-producing gametophyte and a diploid, spore-producing sporophyte, though the dominance of each phase varies across plant groups.

16. In flowering plants (angiosperms), the dominant, conspicuous phase of the life cycle is the

  • A. Sporophyte (diploid) phase
  • B. Gametophyte (haploid) phase
  • C. Neither phase; angiosperms lack alternation of generations
  • D. Both phases equally dominant and independent

Answer: A. Sporophyte (diploid) phase

Explanation: In angiosperms, the sporophyte (the plant body we typically see, bearing leaves, stems, flowers) is the dominant, independent phase, while the gametophyte is highly reduced and dependent, existing as the pollen grain and embryo sac.

17. Double fertilization, a unique feature of angiosperms, involves the fusion events that produce

  • A. A diploid zygote and a triploid endosperm
  • B. Two separate diploid zygotes
  • C. Only a haploid gametophyte
  • D. A tetraploid zygote only

Answer: A. A diploid zygote and a triploid endosperm

Explanation: In double fertilization, one male gamete fuses with the egg to form a diploid zygote, while another male gamete fuses with the two polar nuclei to form the triploid endosperm, a defining feature of angiosperms.

18. Which group of plants, in the classical classification, is described as "seedless" and reproduces via spores?

  • A. Bryophytes and Pteridophytes
  • B. Gymnosperms and Angiosperms
  • C. Only Angiosperms
  • D. Only Gymnosperms

Answer: A. Bryophytes and Pteridophytes

Explanation: Bryophytes and pteridophytes do not produce seeds; instead, they reproduce and disperse via spores, unlike gymnosperms and angiosperms which are seed-bearing plants.

19. Which of the following correctly ranks the plant groups from evolutionarily most primitive to most advanced?

  • A. Algae → Bryophytes → Pteridophytes → Gymnosperms → Angiosperms
  • B. Angiosperms → Gymnosperms → Pteridophytes → Bryophytes → Algae
  • C. Bryophytes → Algae → Angiosperms → Gymnosperms → Pteridophytes
  • D. Gymnosperms → Algae → Bryophytes → Angiosperms → Pteridophytes

Answer: A. Algae → Bryophytes → Pteridophytes → Gymnosperms → Angiosperms

Explanation: The evolutionary progression in the plant kingdom typically moves from simple, non-vascular algae to bryophytes, then to vascular but seedless pteridophytes, followed by seed-bearing gymnosperms, and finally to the most advanced angiosperms with flowers and fruits.

20. Why are gymnosperms and angiosperms collectively considered more evolutionarily advanced than bryophytes and pteridophytes?

  • A. They produce seeds, which offer better protection and dispersal for the embryo compared to spores
  • B. They are the only plants capable of photosynthesis
  • C. They lack any vascular tissue, making them simpler
  • D. They do not require sunlight to grow

Answer: A. They produce seeds, which offer better protection and dispersal for the embryo compared to spores

Explanation: Seeds contain a protected, nourished embryo capable of surviving unfavorable conditions and dispersing efficiently, giving seed plants (gymnosperms and angiosperms) a survival advantage over spore-dependent bryophytes and pteridophytes.

21. Thallophytes (algae) are structurally simple because their body is

  • A. Not differentiated into true root, stem, and leaves (a thallus)
  • B. Highly differentiated into complex organ systems
  • C. Composed of true roots only
  • D. Always microscopic and unicellular

Answer: A. Not differentiated into true root, stem, and leaves (a thallus)

Explanation: Algae are classified as thallophytes because their plant body is a simple, undifferentiated thallus, lacking true roots, stems, and leaves found in higher plants.

22. Red algae (Rhodophyceae) obtain their characteristic colour mainly from the pigment

  • A. Phycoerythrin
  • B. Fucoxanthin
  • C. Chlorophyll b
  • D. Carotene only

Answer: A. Phycoerythrin

Explanation: Red algae get their reddish colour mainly from the pigment phycoerythrin, which masks other pigments like chlorophyll present in their cells.

23. Brown algae (Phaeophyceae) obtain their characteristic colour mainly from the pigment

  • A. Fucoxanthin
  • B. Phycoerythrin
  • C. Chlorophyll a only
  • D. Xanthophyll only

Answer: A. Fucoxanthin

Explanation: Brown algae get their brownish colour predominantly from the pigment fucoxanthin, in addition to chlorophyll a, c, and carotenoids present in their cells.

24. Why is agar, extracted from certain red algae, an economically important product?

  • A. It is widely used as a solidifying agent in microbiological culture media
  • B. It is used exclusively as a food preservative
  • C. It is used as a source of biodiesel fuel
  • D. It is used to manufacture paper only

Answer: A. It is widely used as a solidifying agent in microbiological culture media

Explanation: Agar, derived from red algae like Gelidium and Gracilaria, is extensively used in laboratories as a gelling agent for preparing solid culture media to grow microorganisms.

25. The male reproductive organ in bryophytes, which produces sperm cells, is called the

  • A. Antheridium
  • B. Archegonium
  • C. Sporangium
  • D. Rhizoid

Answer: A. Antheridium

Explanation: In bryophytes, the male sex organ, called the antheridium, produces the male gametes (antherozoids/sperm), which require water to swim to the female gametes.

26. The female reproductive organ in bryophytes and pteridophytes, which produces the egg cell, is called the

  • A. Archegonium
  • B. Antheridium
  • C. Anther
  • D. Ovary

Answer: A. Archegonium

Explanation: The archegonium is the flask-shaped female reproductive structure found in bryophytes and pteridophytes, which produces a single egg cell.

27. Simple, root-like structures found in bryophytes, which anchor the plant to the substrate and lack true vascular tissue, are called

  • A. Rhizoids
  • B. True roots
  • C. Tap roots
  • D. Prop roots

Answer: A. Rhizoids

Explanation: Rhizoids are hair-like structures in bryophytes that anchor the plant body to the substratum and help absorb water and nutrients, but they lack the vascular tissue found in true roots.

28. Why do bryophytes generally remain small in size, unlike vascular plants?

  • A. They lack a well-developed vascular system to transport water and nutrients efficiently over long distances
  • B. They lack chlorophyll and cannot photosynthesize efficiently
  • C. They grow only in extremely hot, dry deserts
  • D. They reproduce exclusively through seeds, limiting their growth

Answer: A. They lack a well-developed vascular system to transport water and nutrients efficiently over long distances

Explanation: Without specialized vascular tissue (xylem and phloem), bryophytes cannot efficiently transport water and nutrients over long distances, which limits their overall size compared to vascular plants.

29. Conifers, a common group of gymnosperms, are well adapted to cold climates mainly due to features like

  • A. Needle-like leaves with a thick cuticle that reduce water loss
  • B. Broad, flat leaves that maximize sunlight absorption
  • C. Complete absence of any leaves
  • D. Seeds enclosed within juicy fruits

Answer: A. Needle-like leaves with a thick cuticle that reduce water loss

Explanation: Conifers like pine have needle-shaped leaves with a thick waxy cuticle and sunken stomata, adaptations that help minimize water loss, making them well-suited to survive in cold, dry climates.

30. Why are angiosperms considered the most successful and dominant group of plants on Earth today?

  • A. Their flowers and fruits enable efficient pollination and seed dispersal, aiding adaptation to diverse habitats
  • B. They are the only plants capable of photosynthesis
  • C. They never require water or sunlight to survive
  • D. They are the simplest and least evolved plant group

Answer: A. Their flowers and fruits enable efficient pollination and seed dispersal, aiding adaptation to diverse habitats

Explanation: The evolution of flowers (facilitating diverse pollination strategies) and fruits (aiding effective seed dispersal) has allowed angiosperms to adapt to a vast range of habitats, making them the most diverse and dominant plant group today.

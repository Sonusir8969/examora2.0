NCERT Solutions for Class 9 Science Chapter 13 Why Do We Fall Ill

NCERT Solutions for Class 9 Science Chapter 13, "Why Do We Fall Ill," focuses on the significance of health, the difference between healthy and disease-free conditions, various types of diseases (acute, chronic, infectious, non-infectious), and the fundamental principles of prevention and treatment.

Overview of Topics & Subtopics (Click to Jump)

  • 1. Health and Its Failure
  • 2. Disease and Its Causes
  • 3. Infectious Diseases & Agents
  • 4. Principles of Treatment and Prevention

1. Health and Its Failure

Short Note:

• Health: Defined by the WHO as a state of complete physical, mental, and social well-being, and not merely the absence of disease or infirmity.

• Significance of Health: Good health allows an individual to perform their daily tasks efficiently and interact properly with their community.

• Personal and Community Issues: Human beings live in societies. Therefore, public cleanliness, good economic conditions, social equality, and harmony are essential for individual health.

• Healthy vs. Disease-Free: Being disease-free means lacking a specific sickness, but it does not automatically mean a person is healthy (which includes mental and social well-being).

2. Disease and Its Causes

Short Note:

• Acute Diseases: Diseases that last for only a very short period of time and do not cause long-term bad effects on general health (e.g., common cold, cough).

• Chronic Diseases: Diseases that last for a long time, even as much as a lifetime, causing drastic long-term effects on human health (e.g., elephantiasis, tuberculosis, diabetes).

• Causes of Diseases: Diseases can be caused by immediate factors (like pathogens) or contributory causes (like poor nourishment, genetic abnormalities, or lack of public services).

• Infectious vs. Non-Infectious:

  - Infectious: Caused by microbes/pathogens and can spread from one person to another (e.g., malaria, COVID-19).

  - Non-Infectious: Not caused by infectious agents and do not spread (e.g., cancer, high blood pressure).

3. Infectious Diseases & Agents

Short Note:

• Infectious Agents:

  - Viruses: Cause common cold, influenza, dengue fever, AIDS.

  - Bacteria: Cause typhoid, cholera, tuberculosis, anthrax.

  - Fungi: Cause many common skin infections (e.g., ringworm).

  - Protozoa: Cause malaria, kala-azar.

  - Worms: Cause intestinal infections, elephantiasis.

• Means of Spread (Transmission):

  - Air-borne: Droplets spread by coughing/sneezing (e.g., TB, pneumonia).

  - Water-borne: Contaminated water containing excreta (e.g., cholera).

  - Vectors: Animals or insects that carry pathogens (e.g., mosquitoes carrying malaria).

  - Physical Contact: Direct contact or sexual contact (e.g., syphilis, AIDS).

4. Principles of Treatment and Prevention

Short Note:

• Principles of Treatment: Involves two steps:

  1. Reduce the effects: Using medicines to bring down fever, pain, or loose motions (symptom-directed treatment) and taking bed rest.

  2. Kill the cause: Using specific drugs like antibiotics (for bacteria) or antivirals to kill the infectious microbes.

• Principles of Prevention: Prevention is always better than cure because curing a disease takes time, causes suffering, and allows the patient to become a source of further infection.

  - General ways: Preventing exposure (e.g., clean air, safe drinking water, proper sanitation) and ensuring a properly functioning immune system through a balanced diet.

  - Specific ways (Immunization): Using vaccines to introduce weakened pathogens into the body, memory cells are created by the immune system to fight off future severe infections (e.g., vaccines for polio, tetanus, measles).

Intext Questions & Textbook Exercise Answers

Q1: State any two conditions essential for good health.
Answer:

The two essential conditions for good health are:

1. Proper nutrition and balanced diet: To maintain the body's immune system and provide energy for proper functioning.

2. Better physical and social environment: Living in a clean surrounding with proper garbage disposal, clean drinking water, and living in a society with harmony and a good economic status.

Q2: Are the answers to the questions "Conditions essential for good health" and "Conditions essential for being free of disease" necessarily the same or different? Why?
Answer: They are different.

Being "free of disease" simply means not suffering from any specific physical infection or ailment. However, "good health" is a broader concept that encompasses a state of complete physical, mental, and social well-being. A person might not have a specific disease but could still be mentally stressed or socially isolated, meaning they are disease-free but not truly healthy.
Q3: Why are we normally advised to take bland and nourishing food when we are sick?
Answer: When we are sick, our normal body functions, including the digestive system, become weak. Bland food is easily digestible and does not irritate the stomach lining or require excessive energy to break down. Nourishing food provides the essential vitamins, minerals, and energy required by our immune system to fight the infection and repair damaged tissues quickly.
Q4: What is immunization?
Answer: Immunization is the process of building immunity or resistance against a particular infectious disease in an individual. This is typically done by administering a vaccine (which contains dead or weakened microbes). The immune system responds by producing specific antibodies and developing "memory cells" so that if the actual, stronger disease-causing microbe enters the body in the future, the immune system can destroy it rapidly.

Intext Questions - Page Number: 40

Q1: Give an example of a metal which:

(i) Is a liquid at room temperature: Mercury (Hg)

(ii) Can be easily cut with a knife: Sodium (Na) / Potassium (K)

(iii) Is the best conductor of heat: Silver (Ag) / Copper (Cu)

(iv) Is a poor conductor of heat: Lead (Pb) / Mercury (Hg)

Q2: Explain the meanings of malleable and ductile.

Malleable: The property of metals that allows them to be beaten or hammered into thin sheets without breaking. Example: Gold and Aluminium.

Ductile: The property of metals that allows them to be drawn or stretched into thin wires. Example: Gold and Copper.

Intext Questions - Page Number: 46

Q1: Why is sodium kept immersed in kerosene oil?
Answer: Sodium is a highly reactive alkali metal that reacts vigorously with oxygen and moisture present in air, generating extreme heat and potentially catching fire. To prevent accidental fires and unwanted oxidation, it is stored submerged in kerosene oil.
Q2: Write equations for the reactions of:

(i) Iron with steam:
3Fe (s) + 4H2O (g) → Fe3O4 (s) + 4H2 (g)

(ii) Calcium with water:
Ca (s) + 2H2O (l) → Ca(OH)2 (aq) + H2 (g)

Intext Questions - Page Number: 49

Q1: What are amphoteric oxides? Give two examples of amphoteric oxides.
Answer: Metal oxides that react with both acidic and basic substances to produce salt and water are known as amphoteric oxides.
Examples: Aluminium oxide (Al2O3) and Zinc oxide (ZnO).

Reaction with Acid: Al2O3 + 6HCl → 2AlCl3 + 3H2O
Reaction with Base: Al2O3 + 2NaOH → 2NaAlO2 + H2O

Intext Questions - Page Number: 53

Q1: Define the following terms: (i) Mineral (ii) Ore (iii) Gangue.

(i) Mineral: Naturally occurring elements or chemical compounds found in the Earth's crust.

(ii) Ore: Minerals from which a specific metal can be extracted conveniently and profitably.

(iii) Gangue: Unwanted impurities such as soil, sand, and rocky matter associated with extracted ores.

Textbook Chapter End Exercise Questions

Q1: Which of the following pairs will give displacement reaction?

(a) NaCl solution and copper metal
(b) MgCl2 solution and aluminium metal
(c) FeSO4 solution and silver metal
(d) AgNO3 solution and copper metal

Correct Answer: (d) AgNO3 solution and copper metal (Copper is more reactive than silver and displaces silver from its solution).

Q2: Which of the following methods is suitable for preventing an iron frying pan from rusting?

(a) Applying grease
(b) Applying paint
(c) Applying a coating of zinc
(d) All of the above

Correct Answer: (c) Applying a coating of zinc (Galvanisation is suitable for food-contact cooking containers where paint or grease is non-viable).

Q3: What are the general properties of ionic compounds?
Answer:

1. Physical Nature: Ionic compounds are hard, brittle solids due to strong electrostatic attraction between oppositely charged ions.

2. High Melting & Boiling Points: A high amount of heat energy is required to break the strong ionic bonds.

3. Solubility: Generally soluble in polar solvents like water, but insoluble in non-polar solvents like kerosene or petrol.

4. Electrical Conductivity: Conduct electricity in molten state or aqueous solution due to mobile ions, but do not conduct in the solid state.

Q4: Give reasons why copper is used to make hot water tanks and not steel (an alloy of iron).
Answer: Copper does not react with water or steam at any temperature, whereas iron reacts with steam to form iron oxide, causing structural corrosion and rusting over time. Furthermore, copper is a significantly better conductor of heat than steel.

Poem-02: The Laburnum Top 25 Questions

1. Who is the poet of the poem 'The Laburnum Top'?

  • A. Ted Hughes
  • B. Shirley Toulson
  • C. Walt Whitman
  • D. Markus Natten

Answer: A. Ted Hughes

Explanation: The poem 'The Laburnum Top' is written by Ted Hughes.

2. How is the Laburnum top described in the beginning of the poem?

  • A. Silent and still
  • B. Noisy and lively
  • C. Green and flowering
  • D. Covered with snow

Answer: A. Silent and still

Explanation: The opening lines state, "The Laburnum top is silent, quite still / In the afternoon yellow September sunlight."

3. What season is mentioned in the poem 'The Laburnum Top'?

  • A. Autumn (September)
  • B. Spring
  • C. Winter
  • D. Summer

Answer: A. Autumn (September)

Explanation: "September sunlight" and "yellowing leaves" indicate the autumn season.

4. What color is dominant in the description of the tree and sunlight?

  • A. Yellow
  • B. Green
  • C. Red
  • D. Brown

Answer: A. Yellow

Explanation: Yellow flowers, yellow sunlight, yellow leaves, and the yellow goldfinch create a yellow motif symbolizing warmth and life.

5. Which bird comes to the Laburnum tree?

  • A. Goldfinch
  • B. Cuckoo
  • C. Nightingale
  • D. Sparrow

Answer: A. Goldfinch

Explanation: A goldfinch arrives at the tree to feed its young chicks.

6. How does the arrival of the goldfinch affect the tree?

  • A. It transforms the tree from silent to full of noise and excitement
  • B. It makes the leaves fall down completely
  • C. It brings storm and rain
  • D. It breaks the branches of the tree

Answer: A. It transforms the tree from silent to full of noise and excitement

Explanation: The arrival of the bird brings sudden liveliness, chirping, and movement to the quiet tree.

7. What animal is the goldfinch's movement compared to?

  • A. A lizard
  • B. A snake
  • C. A squirrel
  • D. A cat

Answer: A. A lizard

Explanation: The poem says: "Sleek as a lizard, and alert, and abrupt..."

8. What figure of speech is used in "Sleek as a lizard"?

  • A. Simile
  • B. Metaphor
  • C. Personification
  • D. Hyperbole

Answer: A. Simile

Explanation: The word 'as' is used to compare the smoothness and quickness of the bird to a lizard, making it a Simile.

9. What metaphor is used for the bird's family/nest on the tree?

  • A. Machine
  • B. Engine
  • C. Factory
  • D. Orchestra

Answer: A. Machine

Explanation: The line "a machine starts up" compares the family of young birds making chirping sounds to a starting machine.

10. Who is described as "the engine of her family"?

  • A. The Goldfinch
  • B. The Laburnum tree
  • C. The poet
  • D. The September sun

Answer: A. The Goldfinch

Explanation: The goldfinch provides food and vitality to her family, acting as their engine.

11. What reveals the identity of the goldfinch when she enters the branch end?

  • A. Her barred face identity mask
  • B. Her long tail
  • C. Her red beak
  • D. Her white feathers

Answer: A. Her barred face identity mask

Explanation: The line reads: "Showing her barred face identity mask," which helps identify her amidst the yellow leaves.

12. Where does the goldfinch fly away at the end of the poem?

  • A. Towards the infinite (sky)
  • B. To another tree
  • C. Down to the ground
  • D. Into a water pond

Answer: A. Towards the infinite (sky)

Explanation: The poem states: "she launches away, towards the infinite."

13. What happens to the Laburnum tree after the bird flies away?

  • A. It subsides to empty and silent again
  • B. It catches fire
  • C. It blossoms with new flowers
  • D. It stays noisy

Answer: A. It subsides to empty and silent again

Explanation: The last line reads: "And the laburnum subsides to empty," returning to its initial state of silence.

14. What is a 'Laburnum'?

  • A. A short tree with hanging yellow flowers and poisonous seeds
  • B. A tall pine tree
  • C. A flowering rose bush
  • D. An evergreen oak tree

Answer: A. A short tree with hanging yellow flowers and poisonous seeds

Explanation: Laburnum (Amaltas) is a short tree known for its yellow flowers and toxic seeds.

15. What sound does the bird make upon arrival?

  • A. Twitching chirp / suddenness
  • B. Loud roar
  • C. Soft humming
  • D. Whistling tune

Answer: A. Twitching chirp / suddenness

Explanation: The bird arrives with a "twitching chirp" causing sudden excitement at the branch end.

16. What poetic device is used in "chitterings, and a tremor of wings, and trillings"?

  • A. Onomatopoeia
  • B. Metaphor
  • C. Simile
  • D. Oxymoron

Answer: A. Onomatopoeia

Explanation: Words like 'chitterings' and 'trillings' imitate natural bird sounds (Onomatopoeia).

17. What does "trembles and thrills" refer to in the poem?

  • A. The whole tree vibrating with activity and life
  • B. The poet shaking in fear
  • C. Strong earthquake taking place
  • D. The wind blowing violently

Answer: A. The whole tree vibrating with activity and life

Explanation: "The whole tree trembles and thrills" means the tree comes alive with energy due to the chicks' excitement.

18. What poetic device is in "tree trembles and thrills"?

  • A. Alliteration / Personification
  • B. Simile
  • C. Irony
  • D. Metaphor

Answer: A. Alliteration / Personification

Explanation: Repetition of 't' sound (Alliteration) and giving human-like feelings of thrill to tree (Personification).

19. Why are the seeds of the Laburnum tree mentioned as fallen?

  • A. Because autumn season causes leaves and seeds to fall
  • B. Because birds ate all seeds
  • C. Because children plucked them
  • D. Because of heavy rain

Answer: A. Because autumn season causes leaves and seeds to fall

Explanation: "All its seeds have fallen" marks the natural autumn cycle of the Laburnum tree.

20. What relationship is portrayed between the tree and the bird?

  • A. Mutual interdependence / Co-existence
  • B. Enmity and destruction
  • C. Temporary accidental meeting
  • D. Master and slave relationship

Answer: A. Mutual interdependence / Co-existence

Explanation: The tree gives shelter to the bird's family, and the bird gives life and vibrancy to the silent tree.

21. What does 'stokes it full' mean in the poem?

  • A. Feeds her young ones till they are full
  • B. Burns wood in the engine
  • C. Fills the tree with water
  • D. Sings loudly

Answer: A. Feeds her young ones till they are full

Explanation: Continuing the engine metaphor, 'stoking' means adding fuel (feeding food) to her young ones.

22. What is the tone of the poem at the beginning and end?

  • A. Calm, quiet, and serene
  • B. Highly chaotic and aggressive
  • C. Melancholic and depressive
  • D. Joyful and noisy

Answer: A. Calm, quiet, and serene

Explanation: The poem starts and ends with stillness and quiet solitude on the tree.

23. What contrast is highlighted in the middle of the poem?

  • A. Sudden burst of lively activity against prevailing silence
  • B. Day versus Night
  • C. Summer versus Winter
  • D. Life versus Death

Answer: A. Sudden burst of lively activity against prevailing silence

Explanation: The middle section creates a sharp contrast of loud activity against the background of silence.

24. What does 'infinite' refer to when the bird launches away?

  • A. The vast sky
  • B. The ocean
  • C. The forest
  • D. Another solar system

Answer: A. The vast sky

Explanation: 'The infinite' represents the endless expanse of the open sky where the bird flies.

25. What central theme is reflected in 'The Laburnum Top'?

  • A. The transformative power of life and vitality in nature
  • B. The danger of poisonous trees
  • C. The difficulty of hunting birds
  • D. The changing weather patterns

Answer: A. The transformative power of life and vitality in nature

Explanation: It depicts how living presence and love bring energy, purpose, and liveliness to an otherwise lifeless existence.

Phy-2: Units and Measurements 30 Questions

1. A quantity that can be measured and expressed in terms of a numerical value and a unit is called a:

  • A. Physical quantity
  • B. Fundamental quantity only
  • C. Derived quantity only
  • D. Dimensionless quantity

Answer: A. Physical quantity

Explanation: A physical quantity is any quantity that can be measured, expressed using a number along with an appropriate unit.

2. The internationally accepted system of units used in scientific measurement is called the:

  • A. SI system (International System of Units)
  • B. FPS system
  • C. CGS system only
  • D. Metric system only, unrelated to SI

Answer: A. SI system (International System of Units)

Explanation: The SI (Système International) system is the standardised, globally accepted system of units used in science and measurement.

3. The SI unit of length is the:

  • A. Metre
  • B. Centimetre
  • C. Kilometre
  • D. Foot

Answer: A. Metre

Explanation: The metre is defined as the base SI unit for measuring length.

4. The SI unit of mass is the:

  • A. Kilogram
  • B. Gram
  • C. Pound
  • D. Tonne

Answer: A. Kilogram

Explanation: The kilogram is the fundamental SI unit used for measuring mass.

5. The SI unit of time is the:

  • A. Second
  • B. Minute
  • C. Hour
  • D. Day

Answer: A. Second

Explanation: The second is defined as the base SI unit for measuring time.

6. The number of fundamental (base) quantities in the SI system is:

  • A. Seven
  • B. Five
  • C. Three
  • D. Ten

Answer: A. Seven

Explanation: The SI system defines seven fundamental base quantities: length, mass, time, electric current, temperature, amount of substance, and luminous intensity.

7. Quantities that are expressed in terms of the base quantities are called:

  • A. Derived quantities
  • B. Fundamental quantities
  • C. Scalar quantities only
  • D. Dimensionless quantities

Answer: A. Derived quantities

Explanation: Derived quantities, such as speed or force, are obtained by combining base quantities through mathematical relations.

8. The dimensional formula of velocity is:

  • A. [M0L1T-1]
  • B. [M1L1T-1]
  • C. [M0L1T-2]
  • D. [M1L0T-1]

Answer: A. [M0L1T-1]

Explanation: Velocity is displacement divided by time, giving a dimensional formula of length (L) divided by time (T), independent of mass.

9. The dimensional formula of force is:

  • A. [M1L1T-2]
  • B. [M0L1T-1]
  • C. [M1L2T-2]
  • D. [M1L1T-1]

Answer: A. [M1L1T-2]

Explanation: Since force = mass × acceleration, and acceleration has dimensions [LT-2], force has dimensions [M1L1T-2].

10. The dimensional formula of energy (or work) is:

  • A. [M1L2T-2]
  • B. [M1L1T-2]
  • C. [M0L2T-2]
  • D. [M1L2T-1]

Answer: A. [M1L2T-2]

Explanation: Since energy = force × distance, combining [M1L1T-2] with [L1] gives [M1L2T-2].

11. The principle of homogeneity of dimensions states that in a valid physical equation, both sides must have:

  • A. The same dimensions
  • B. The same numerical value only
  • C. Different units
  • D. No relation to each other

Answer: A. The same dimensions

Explanation: This principle requires that all terms in a physically meaningful equation possess identical dimensions on both sides.

12. Dimensional analysis is useful for:

  • A. Checking the correctness of physical equations and deriving relationships between quantities
  • B. Measuring exact numerical values only
  • C. Determining the colour of an object
  • D. Predicting biological outcomes

Answer: A. Checking the correctness of physical equations and deriving relationships between quantities

Explanation: Dimensional analysis helps verify equation consistency and can assist in deriving relationships between physical quantities.

13. The error that occurs due to the limitations of a measuring instrument is called:

  • A. Systematic error
  • B. Random error only
  • C. Gross error
  • D. Personal error only

Answer: A. Systematic error

Explanation: Systematic errors arise from consistent flaws in instruments or experimental methods, causing measurements to deviate in a predictable direction.

14. Errors that occur irregularly due to unpredictable fluctuations in experimental conditions are called:

  • A. Random errors
  • B. Systematic errors
  • C. Instrumental errors only
  • D. Gross errors

Answer: A. Random errors

Explanation: Random errors arise from unpredictable variations, causing measurements to fluctuate above and below the true value irregularly.

15. The number of significant figures in the measurement 0.0025 m is:

  • A. Two
  • B. Four
  • C. Five
  • D. Three

Answer: A. Two

Explanation: Leading zeros are not significant; only the digits 2 and 5 count, giving two significant figures.

16. The number of significant figures in the measurement 2.500 m is:

  • A. Four
  • B. Two
  • C. One
  • D. Three

Answer: A. Four

Explanation: Trailing zeros after a decimal point are significant, so 2.500 has four significant figures: 2, 5, 0, 0.

17. The closeness of a measured value to the true value is called:

  • A. Accuracy
  • B. Precision
  • C. Least count
  • D. Significant figures

Answer: A. Accuracy

Explanation: Accuracy refers to how close a measured value is to the actual or true value of the quantity being measured.

18. The degree to which repeated measurements under the same conditions show consistent results is called:

  • A. Precision
  • B. Accuracy
  • C. Error
  • D. Uncertainty only

Answer: A. Precision

Explanation: Precision refers to the consistency or reproducibility of measurements, regardless of how close they are to the true value.

19. The smallest value that can be measured accurately by a measuring instrument is called its:

  • A. Least count
  • B. Range
  • C. Error
  • D. Precision

Answer: A. Least count

Explanation: The least count of an instrument represents the smallest measurable increment it can accurately record.

20. The order of magnitude of a physical quantity is determined by expressing it as a power of:

  • A. 10
  • B. 2
  • C. e (Euler's number)
  • D. 100

Answer: A. 10

Explanation: Order of magnitude estimation rounds a value to the nearest power of 10, providing an approximate scale of the quantity.

21. If a physical quantity Z = A × B, then the relative error in Z is calculated by:

  • A. Adding the relative errors in A and B
  • B. Subtracting the relative errors
  • C. Multiplying the relative errors
  • D. Dividing the relative errors

Answer: A. Adding the relative errors in A and B

Explanation: For quantities combined by multiplication, the relative (percentage) errors of each individual quantity are added together.

22. The dimensions of Planck's constant are the same as those of:

  • A. Angular momentum
  • B. Force
  • C. Energy only
  • D. Velocity

Answer: A. Angular momentum

Explanation: Planck's constant has the dimensional formula [M1L2T-1], which matches the dimensions of angular momentum.

23. Which of the following pairs has the same dimensions?

  • A. Work and Energy
  • B. Force and Pressure
  • C. Velocity and Acceleration
  • D. Mass and Weight

Answer: A. Work and Energy

Explanation: Both work and energy share the identical dimensional formula [M1L2T-2], since work done is a measure of energy transfer.

24. A dimensionless quantity is one that:

  • A. Has no associated physical dimension, such as strain or refractive index
  • B. Cannot be measured at all
  • C. Always equals zero
  • D. Only applies to time measurements

Answer: A. Has no associated physical dimension, such as strain or refractive index

Explanation: Dimensionless quantities, like strain or angle in radians, are ratios of similar quantities and hence have no associated dimension.

25. The parallax method is used in physics primarily to measure:

  • A. Large distances, such as to stars or planets
  • B. Small time intervals
  • C. Electric current
  • D. Mass of subatomic particles

Answer: A. Large distances, such as to stars or planets

Explanation: The parallax method uses the apparent shift in position of a distant object to calculate large astronomical distances.

26. Which of the following instruments is used to measure very small lengths with high precision?

  • A. Vernier callipers
  • B. Metre scale
  • C. Odometer
  • D. Stopwatch

Answer: A. Vernier callipers

Explanation: Vernier callipers provide a higher degree of precision for measuring small lengths compared to a standard metre scale.

27. The screw gauge is typically used to measure:

  • A. The diameter of very thin wires or small objects
  • B. Large distances between cities
  • C. Time intervals
  • D. Temperature

Answer: A. The diameter of very thin wires or small objects

Explanation: A screw gauge, offering very fine precision, is specifically designed to measure extremely small lengths like wire diameters.

28. If the length of a rod is measured as 25.0 cm using an instrument with a least count of 0.1 cm, the reported value reflects:

  • A. The precision limit of the measuring instrument used
  • B. An exact, error-free measurement
  • C. A random guess
  • D. An average of many different instruments

Answer: A. The precision limit of the measuring instrument used

Explanation: The number of significant figures reported in a measurement reflects the least count and hence the precision of the instrument used.

29. Which of the following represents a correct example of dimensional consistency?

  • A. Comparing the dimensions of kinetic energy (1/2 mv²) with potential energy (mgh)
  • B. Comparing mass with time directly
  • C. Comparing length with temperature directly
  • D. Adding velocity and acceleration directly

Answer: A. Comparing the dimensions of kinetic energy (1/2 mv²) with potential energy (mgh)

Explanation: Both kinetic energy and potential energy share the same dimensional formula [M1L2T-2], demonstrating dimensional consistency between different forms of energy.

30. The main purpose of using significant figures in a measurement is to:

  • A. Indicate the precision and reliability of the recorded measurement
  • B. Make numbers appear larger
  • C. Simplify all calculations to whole numbers
  • D. Avoid using any units altogether

Answer: A. Indicate the precision and reliability of the recorded measurement

Explanation: Significant figures convey the degree of precision achieved during measurement, indicating how reliable a given numerical value is.

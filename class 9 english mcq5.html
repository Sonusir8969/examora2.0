Chapter 5: The Snake and the Mirror 30 Questions

1. Who is the narrator/main character in "The Snake and the Mirror"?

  • A. A young doctor
  • B. A schoolteacher
  • C. A farmer
  • D. A police officer

Answer: A. A young doctor

Explanation: The story is narrated by a struggling young homeopathic doctor who has recently set up his practice.

2. What was the financial condition of the doctor at the beginning of the story?

  • A. He was extremely wealthy
  • B. He was poor and had very few patients
  • C. He had already retired
  • D. He had inherited a fortune

Answer: B. He was poor and had very few patients

Explanation: The doctor's practice was struggling, and he had very little money, reflected in his modest and sparsely furnished room.

3. What was the doctor doing just before the snake entered his room?

  • A. He was about to go to sleep after applying oil to his body
  • B. He was eating dinner
  • C. He was treating a patient
  • D. He was reading a newspaper

Answer: A. He was about to go to sleep after applying oil to his body

Explanation: The doctor had applied oil all over his body and was preparing for bed when the snake suddenly appeared in his room.

4. How did the snake enter the doctor's room?

  • A. Through the open door
  • B. Through a hole in the wall near the cot
  • C. Through the window
  • D. It was already inside the room

Answer: B. Through a hole in the wall near the cot

Explanation: The snake entered the room through a hole in the wall close to where the doctor was resting on his cot.

5. Why couldn't the doctor immediately kill the snake or escape?

  • A. Because he was oiled all over, making his body slippery, and the snake was too close for a safe attack
  • B. Because the door was locked from outside
  • C. Because he was too weak to move at all
  • D. Because there was no weapon in the room

Answer: A. Because he was oiled all over, making his body slippery, and the snake was too close for a safe attack

Explanation: Since his body was covered in oil, the doctor feared that any sudden or risky movement could cause the snake to bite him, so he remained still and cautious.

6. What large object was present in the doctor's room that plays an important role in the story?

  • A. A wooden cupboard
  • B. A tall standing mirror
  • C. A bookshelf
  • D. A grandfather clock

Answer: B. A tall standing mirror

Explanation: A large standing mirror in the room becomes central to the plot when the snake becomes fascinated by its own reflection.

7. Why had the doctor originally purchased the expensive mirror despite his poverty?

  • A. To display his wealth to patients
  • B. He bought it on impulse and later regretted the unnecessary expense given his financial struggles
  • C. It was a gift from a patient
  • D. He needed it for his medical practice

Answer: B. He bought it on impulse and later regretted the unnecessary expense given his financial struggles

Explanation: The doctor recalls that he had bought the costly mirror somewhat rashly, an expense he found hard to justify given his poor financial situation.

8. What did the snake do when it saw its own reflection in the mirror?

  • A. It became fascinated and began interacting with its reflection, mistaking it for another snake
  • B. It immediately fled from the room
  • C. It attacked and shattered the mirror instantly
  • D. It ignored the mirror completely

Answer: A. It became fascinated and began interacting with its reflection, mistaking it for another snake

Explanation: The snake appeared captivated by its reflection, behaving as though it had encountered another snake, which distracted it from the doctor.

9. How did the snake's fascination with the mirror help the doctor?

  • A. It gave the doctor an opportunity to quietly escape or find safety while the snake was distracted
  • B. It had no effect on the situation at all
  • C. It made the snake more aggressive towards the doctor
  • D. It caused the snake to leave the house instantly

Answer: A. It gave the doctor an opportunity to quietly escape or find safety while the snake was distracted

Explanation: With the snake absorbed in its own reflection, the doctor found a chance to move away safely without provoking it.

10. What eventually happened to the snake in the story?

  • A. It left the room on its own after some time, distracted by its reflection
  • B. It was killed by the doctor
  • C. It bit the doctor
  • D. It was captured and sold

Answer: A. It left the room on its own after some time, distracted by its reflection

Explanation: After spending time captivated by its own image in the mirror, the snake eventually moved away and left the room, allowing the doctor a fortunate escape from danger.

11. What tone does the author use to narrate this potentially dangerous encounter?

  • A. A humorous and light-hearted tone despite the danger
  • B. A purely tragic and sorrowful tone
  • C. An angry and bitter tone
  • D. A completely emotionless, factual tone

Answer: A. A humorous and light-hearted tone despite the danger

Explanation: Despite the tense and dangerous situation, the story is narrated with wit and humour, characteristic of the author's storytelling style.

12. What does the doctor's initial hesitation to spend money on the mirror reveal about his character?

  • A. His careful, financially cautious nature due to his limited income
  • B. His extravagant lifestyle
  • C. His indifference to money altogether
  • D. His dishonesty in financial matters

Answer: A. His careful, financially cautious nature due to his limited income

Explanation: The doctor's regret over the mirror purchase reflects his generally cautious and frugal approach to money, given his struggling practice.

13. Which irony is central to the story's plot?

  • A. The same mirror the doctor regretted buying ends up potentially saving his life
  • B. The doctor becomes a snake charmer by the end
  • C. The snake turns out to be harmless the entire time
  • D. The doctor was never actually in any danger

Answer: A. The same mirror the doctor regretted buying ends up potentially saving his life

Explanation: The central irony is that an object the doctor once regarded as a wasteful expense turns out to play a crucial role in helping him survive a dangerous encounter.

14. What kind of practice did the doctor run?

  • A. A homeopathic medical practice
  • B. A dental clinic
  • C. A veterinary practice
  • D. An Ayurvedic pharmacy

Answer: A. A homeopathic medical practice

Explanation: The narrator of the story is a young homeopathic doctor who has recently started his own practice.

15. What time of day did this encounter with the snake take place?

  • A. Late at night
  • B. Early morning
  • C. Midday
  • D. Just after sunrise

Answer: A. Late at night

Explanation: The dramatic encounter with the snake happened late at night, just as the doctor was preparing to sleep.

16. What emotion did the doctor primarily experience upon first spotting the snake?

  • A. Fear and anxiety
  • B. Complete indifference
  • C. Amusement
  • D. Excitement and joy

Answer: A. Fear and anxiety

Explanation: The sudden appearance of the snake naturally filled the doctor with fear and anxiety about his safety.

17. What does the story suggest about the nature of fear when facing an unexpected danger?

  • A. Staying calm and thinking clearly can help one find a way out of a dangerous situation
  • B. Fear always leads to disaster with no possibility of escape
  • C. It is always better to panic and act without thinking
  • D. Fear has no impact on decision-making at all

Answer: A. Staying calm and thinking clearly can help one find a way out of a dangerous situation

Explanation: The story implies that despite his fear, the doctor's ability to remain relatively calm and observant helped him navigate the dangerous situation safely.

18. How is the snake's behaviour towards its reflection described in the story?

  • A. As curious and almost affectionate, treating the reflection like a companion
  • B. As violently aggressive towards the mirror
  • C. As entirely uninterested and dismissive
  • D. As frightened and fleeing immediately

Answer: A. As curious and almost affectionate, treating the reflection like a companion

Explanation: The snake's interaction with its mirror image is depicted as curious and gentle, almost as if it had found a companion, which is central to the story's humour and irony.

19. What can be inferred about the doctor's living conditions from the description of his room?

  • A. That he lived in modest, simple surroundings with basic furniture
  • B. That he lived in a luxurious mansion
  • C. That he had many servants attending to him
  • D. That his room was located in a hospital

Answer: A. That he lived in modest, simple surroundings with basic furniture

Explanation: The description of the doctor's sparsely furnished room reflects his modest living conditions, consistent with his struggling medical practice.

20. What literary device is prominently used in the depiction of the snake's fascination with its reflection?

  • A. Personification, by attributing human-like emotions and curiosity to the snake
  • B. Metaphor about war
  • C. Simile comparing the snake to a bird
  • D. Alliteration only

Answer: A. Personification, by attributing human-like emotions and curiosity to the snake

Explanation: The snake's seemingly curious and affectionate behaviour towards its reflection is described in a way that attributes human-like fascination to it, a form of personification.

21. Why does the author include humour in a story about a potentially life-threatening situation?

  • A. To make the narrative engaging while highlighting the unexpected, ironic twist of events
  • B. To make light of real dangers faced by people
  • C. To confuse the reader
  • D. Because the story has no serious element at all

Answer: A. To make the narrative engaging while highlighting the unexpected, ironic twist of events

Explanation: The humorous tone keeps readers engaged and emphasises the delightful irony of how an object of regret becomes the doctor's unlikely saviour.

22. What does the doctor do physically while waiting for an opportunity to escape the snake's presence?

  • A. He remains very still and quiet, careful not to provoke the snake
  • B. He shouts loudly for help
  • C. He immediately runs out of the room
  • D. He throws objects at the snake

Answer: A. He remains very still and quiet, careful not to provoke the snake

Explanation: The doctor stays motionless and cautious, aware that any hasty movement might provoke the snake into attacking him.

23. What does the story reveal about the doctor's financial struggles at the time of the incident?

  • A. He had very few patients and was finding it difficult to sustain his medical practice
  • B. He was extremely successful and wealthy
  • C. He had already given up his medical career
  • D. He had no financial concerns at all

Answer: A. He had very few patients and was finding it difficult to sustain his medical practice

Explanation: The doctor's narration reveals that his medical practice was struggling with very few patients, adding context to his regret over buying the expensive mirror.

24. What theme related to human perception does the story subtly explore through the snake's confusion with its own reflection?

  • A. How appearances and reflections can be mistaken for reality
  • B. The importance of financial planning
  • C. The dangers of keeping pets
  • D. The value of education

Answer: A. How appearances and reflections can be mistaken for reality

Explanation: The snake mistaking its own reflection for another snake subtly touches on the theme of how easily reflections or appearances can be confused with reality.

25. What is unusual or striking about how the story blends everyday realism with humour?

  • A. It presents a life-threatening event through a witty, conversational narrative style
  • B. It uses only formal, scientific language throughout
  • C. It avoids any personal narration
  • D. It is written entirely as a factual news report

Answer: A. It presents a life-threatening event through a witty, conversational narrative style

Explanation: The author's engaging, first-person, conversational storytelling style turns a potentially frightening episode into an entertaining and memorable narrative.

26. What lesson about unexpected value can be drawn from the story regarding the mirror?

  • A. Something initially seen as a wasteful or impulsive purchase can turn out to have unforeseen benefits
  • B. Money should never be spent on anything other than necessities
  • C. Mirrors have no practical use in daily life
  • D. Impulsive purchases are always regretted with no exceptions

Answer: A. Something initially seen as a wasteful or impulsive purchase can turn out to have unforeseen benefits

Explanation: The story humorously illustrates how an object initially regretted as an unnecessary expense unexpectedly proves valuable in a moment of crisis.

27. What does the doctor's narration style suggest about his personality?

  • A. That he has a good sense of humour and can look back on frightening events with wit
  • B. That he is a very serious and humourless person
  • C. That he never reflects on his own experiences
  • D. That he dislikes telling stories about himself

Answer: A. That he has a good sense of humour and can look back on frightening events with wit

Explanation: The doctor's witty, self-deprecating narration of the frightening incident suggests he possesses a good sense of humour and self-awareness.

28. In what setting does the entire story take place?

  • A. Inside the doctor's small room at night
  • B. In a busy hospital
  • C. In a forest
  • D. In a marketplace

Answer: A. Inside the doctor's small room at night

Explanation: The entire dramatic episode of the story unfolds within the confines of the doctor's modest room at night.

29. Why might the author have chosen a mirror, rather than another object, to play a key role in the snake's distraction?

  • A. Because a mirror's reflective quality naturally creates the illusion of another snake, driving the plot's irony
  • B. Because mirrors are always found in every room regardless of the story
  • C. Because the mirror had magical powers in the story
  • D. Because the snake was afraid of glass

Answer: A. Because a mirror's reflective quality naturally creates the illusion of another snake, driving the plot's irony

Explanation: The mirror's unique property of reflecting an exact image makes it perfectly suited to create the illusion that fools the snake, driving the story's central irony.

30. What is the overall theme conveyed by "The Snake and the Mirror"?

  • A. Unexpected outcomes and irony can arise from ordinary objects and situations in life
  • B. Snakes are always dangerous and cannot be outwitted
  • C. Wealth is the key to a happy life
  • D. Doctors should never keep mirrors in their homes

Answer: A. Unexpected outcomes and irony can arise from ordinary objects and situations in life

Explanation: The story humorously conveys how life can present ironic and unexpected twists, turning a source of regret into an unlikely means of safety.

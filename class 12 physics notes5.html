NCERT Solutions for Class 12 Physics Chapter 5 Magnetism and Matter

NCERT Solutions for Class 12 Physics Chapter 5 Magnetism and Matter includes quick notes, key formulas, derivations, and step-by-step solutions covering magnetic dipoles, Gauss's law for magnetism, Earth's magnetic field, and magnetic classification of materials (diamagnetic, paramagnetic, and ferromagnetic).

1. The Bar Magnet & Magnetic Dipole Moment

Short Note:

• Magnetic Dipole Moment (m): Vector quantity pointing from South pole to North pole. For a bar magnet of pole strength q_m and length 2l: m = q_m × 2l. SI unit: A·m² or J/T.

• Bar Magnet as an Equivalent Solenoid: A bar magnet creates a magnetic field identical to a finite solenoid with N turns per unit length carrying current I.

• Magnetic Field due to Bar Magnet (distance r >> l):

  - Axial Line: B_axial = (μ₀ / 4π) × (2 m / r³) (along direction of dipole moment m).

  - Equatorial Line: B_equatorial = (μ₀ / 4π) × (m / r³) (opposite to direction of dipole moment m).

• Torque & Potential Energy in Uniform B-field:

  - Torque: τ = m × B = m B sin θ.

  - Potential Energy: U = -m · B = -m B cos θ.

2. Gauss's Law in Magnetism

Short Note:

• Statement: The total magnetic flux through any closed surface is always zero: Φ_B = ∮ B · dA = 0.

• Physical Significance: Magnetic monopoles (isolated magnetic N or S poles) do not exist in nature. Magnetic field lines always form continuous closed loops.

• Comparison with Electrostatics: In electrostatics, ∮ E · dA = q_enclosed / ε₀ because isolated electric charges exist, whereas magnetic monopoles do not.

3. Earth's Magnetism & Elements of Terrestrial Magnetism

Short Note:

• Earth's Magnetic Field: Earth acts as a giant magnetic dipole tilted at an angle of roughly 11.3° to its geographic rotational axis. Magnetic South pole is located near geographic North pole.

• Elements of Earth's Magnetic Field:

  1. Magnetic Declination (D): The angle between geographic meridian and magnetic meridian at a place.

  2. Magnetic Dip or Inclination (I or δ): Angle made by total earth's magnetic field vector B with horizontal direction. Dip is 0° at equator and 90° at magnetic poles.

  3. Horizontal Component (B_H): Component of earth's total field along horizontal direction: B_H = B cos δ; Vertical component B_V = B sin δ.

• Relation: tan δ = B_V / B_H and B = √(B_H² + B_V²).

4. Magnetic Properties of Materials (Dia, Para & Ferromagnetism)

Short Note:

• Key Magnetic Quantities:

  - Magnetization (M): Magnetic moment per unit volume: M = m / V (SI unit: A/m).

  - Magnetic Intensity (H): H = B₀ / μ₀ (SI unit: A/m).

  - Magnetic Susceptibility (χ_m): Measure of response of material to applied field: χ_m = M / H.

  - Relative Permeability (μ_r): μ_r = 1 + χ_m = μ / μ₀.

• Curie's Law for Paramagnetism: Susceptibility varies inversely with absolute temperature T: χ_m = C / T.

• Curie Temperature (T_c): Critical temperature above which a ferromagnetic material transitions into a paramagnetic material.

Intext & Conceptual Questions

Q1: What is Gauss's Law for magnetism? What fundamental property of magnetic poles does it imply?
Answer:
• Statement: Gauss's law for magnetism states that the net magnetic flux through any closed Gaussian surface is always zero, i.e., ∮ B · dA = 0.
• Significance: It implies that magnetic field lines never originate or terminate on isolated sources; they form continuous closed loops. This confirms that isolated magnetic monopoles do not exist in nature. Every magnetic pole always exists as an inseparable north-south pair (dipole).
Q2: Compare the properties of Diamagnetic, Paramagnetic, and Ferromagnetic substances.
Answer:
Property Diamagnetic Paramagnetic Ferromagnetic
Behavior in External Field Feebly repelled by magnets; moves towards weaker field. Feebly attracted by magnets; moves towards stronger field. Strongly attracted by magnets; moves strongly to stronger field.
Susceptibility (χ_m) Small and negative (-1 ≤ χ < 0). Small and positive (0 < χ < ε). Very large and positive (χ >> 1000).
Relative Permeability (μ_r) Slightly less than 1 (0 ≤ μ_r < 1). Slightly greater than 1 (μ_r > 1). Much greater than 1 (μ_r >> 1).
Temperature Effect Independent of temperature. Inversely proportional (χ ∝ 1/T). Decreases above Curie temperature (T_c).
Examples Bismuth, Copper, Water, Gold, Air. Aluminum, Platinum, Chromium, Oxygen. Iron, Nickel, Cobalt, Alnico.

Textbook Chapter End Exercise Questions

Q1: A short bar magnet placed with its axis at 30° with a uniform external magnetic field of 0.25 T experiences a torque of magnitude equal to 4.5 × 10⁻² J. What is the magnitude of magnetic moment of the magnet?
Answer:
Given:
• Angle (θ) = 30°
• Uniform magnetic field (B) = 0.25 T
• Torque (τ) = 4.5 × 10⁻² J

Formula:
τ = m B sin θ

Calculation:
4.5 × 10⁻² = m × 0.25 × sin(30°)
4.5 × 10⁻² = m × 0.25 × 0.5
4.5 × 10⁻² = m × 0.125
m = (4.5 × 10⁻²) / 0.125 = 0.36 J/T (or A·m²)

The magnitude of the magnetic dipole moment of the magnet is 0.36 J/T (or 0.36 A·m²).
Q2: Distinguish between Soft Iron and Steel for selection in making Electromagnets vs Permanent Magnets.
Answer:
Property Soft Iron (Used for Electromagnets) Steel (Used for Permanent Magnets)
Retentivity High retentivity (retains strong field). High retentivity.
Coercivity Low coercivity (easily demagnetized when current stops). High coercivity (resists demagnetization).
Hysteresis Loss Small hysteresis loop area (low energy dissipation). Large hysteresis loop area.
Suitability Ideal for core of transformers, solenoids & electromagnets. Ideal for making strong, long-lasting permanent magnets.

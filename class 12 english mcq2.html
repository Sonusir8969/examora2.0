Ch-2: Lost Spring 10 Questions

1. Who is the author of 'Lost Spring'?

  • A. Anees Jung
  • B. Arundhati Roy
  • C. Kamala Das
  • D. Ruskin Bond

Answer: A. Anees Jung

Explanation: 'Lost Spring: Stories of Stolen Childhood' is written by Anees Jung, based on her observations of children trapped in poverty and exploitative labour.

2. The chapter 'Lost Spring' is divided into two parts. What is the first part titled?

  • A. The Shoes
  • B. Sometimes I Find a Rupee in the Garbage
  • C. Bangles for the Bride
  • D. Broken Promises

Answer: B. Sometimes I Find a Rupee in the Garbage

Explanation: The first part of 'Lost Spring', titled 'Sometimes I Find a Rupee in the Garbage', narrates the story of Saheb, a young ragpicker.

3. What is ironic about Saheb-e-Alam's name?

  • A. It means 'lord of the universe', yet he lives as a poor, barefoot ragpicker
  • B. It means 'poor boy', which matches his life
  • C. It has no particular meaning
  • D. It is a name given to him by his school

Answer: A. It means 'lord of the universe', yet he lives as a poor, barefoot ragpicker

Explanation: Saheb's full name, Saheb-e-Alam, literally means 'lord of the universe', creating a bitter irony given his impoverished existence as a ragpicker with no real ownership of anything.

4. What change occurs in Saheb's life when he starts working at a tea stall?

  • A. He becomes his own master and earns more freedom
  • B. He earns a fixed monthly wage but loses the carefree freedom he had as a ragpicker
  • C. He stops working altogether
  • D. He goes back to school

Answer: B. He earns a fixed monthly wage but loses the carefree freedom he had as a ragpicker

Explanation: Working at the tea stall gives Saheb a steady footing, but he is no longer his own master, having exchanged the informal freedom of ragpicking for a bound, structured job.

5. In the second part, 'The Shoes', where is Mukesh from?

  • A. Firozabad, known for its bangle-making industry
  • B. Seemapuri, known for ragpicking
  • C. Kanpur, known for leather
  • D. Agra, known for the Taj Mahal

Answer: A. Firozabad, known for its bangle-making industry

Explanation: Mukesh belongs to a family of bangle-makers in Firozabad, a town famous for its glass-blowing and bangle industry, though the work carries serious health hazards.

6. What does Mukesh dream of becoming, defying his family's traditional occupation?

  • A. A doctor
  • B. A motor mechanic
  • C. A teacher
  • D. A politician

Answer: B. A motor mechanic

Explanation: Unlike his family who have made bangles for generations, Mukesh dreams of breaking away from the family trade and becoming a motor mechanic, even though the garage is far from his home.

7. What hazardous conditions do bangle-makers of Firozabad typically work under?

  • A. Comfortable, well-lit modern factories
  • B. Poorly lit spaces with high furnace heat, leading to eye damage and other ailments
  • C. Fully automated machinery with no manual work
  • D. Air-conditioned workshops

Answer: B. Poorly lit spaces with high furnace heat, leading to eye damage and other ailments

Explanation: Bangle makers work in cramped, dingy spaces without proper ventilation or light, sitting close to hot furnaces, which often leads to loss of eyesight and other health problems.

8. According to the author, why do generations of bangle makers in Firozabad remain trapped in poverty?

  • A. They lack the desire to change their occupation
  • B. A vicious cycle involving apathetic sahukars, middlemen, the police, and government apathy keeps them bonded to the trade
  • C. They earn very high wages already
  • D. They prefer bangle-making over all other jobs

Answer: B. A vicious cycle involving apathetic sahukars, middlemen, the police, and government apathy keeps them bonded to the trade

Explanation: The author identifies a nexus of moneylenders (sahukars), middlemen, the law, and government apathy that traps generations of families in this hazardous, underpaid trade with little hope of escape.

9. What does the phrase 'Sometimes I find a rupee in the garbage' signify about the ragpickers' perspective on garbage?

  • A. Garbage is worthless to them
  • B. For the poor, garbage can represent a means of survival and even hope of unexpected fortune
  • C. It refers to actual government subsidy
  • D. It is a proverb about laziness

Answer: B. For the poor, garbage can represent a means of survival and even hope of unexpected fortune

Explanation: For Saheb and other ragpickers, garbage is not merely waste but a means of livelihood, and finding something valuable like a coin in it represents a small stroke of unexpected fortune amid their poverty.

10. What overarching theme connects both parts of 'Lost Spring'?

  • A. The joys of childhood in India
  • B. The loss of childhood among the poor due to poverty, social apathy, and exploitative traditions
  • C. The success of child labour laws in India
  • D. The migration of birds

Answer: B. The loss of childhood among the poor due to poverty, social apathy, and exploitative traditions

Explanation: Both parts of the chapter, through Saheb and Mukesh, highlight how poverty, broken promises, and societal indifference rob poor children of their rightful childhood and opportunities.

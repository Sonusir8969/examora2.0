NCERT Solutions for Class 11 Biology Chapter 13 Plant Growth and Development

NCERT Solutions for Class 11 Biology Chapter 13 Plant Growth and Development covers the phases and characteristics of plant growth, plant growth regulators (hormones), and the process of seed germination.

Overview of Topics & Subtopics (Click to Jump)

  • 1. Growth
  • 2. Plant Growth Regulators
  • 3. Development, Differentiation and Germination

1. Growth

Short Note:

• Growth is an irreversible, permanent increase in size, and is one of the most conspicuous features of living organisms, generally measured quantitatively (e.g., increase in size, mass, or cell number).

• Growth follows a characteristic sigmoid (S-shaped) curve, comprising lag, exponential (log), and stationary phases when plotted over time.

2. Plant Growth Regulators

Short Note:

• Auxins: promote cell elongation, apical dominance, and root initiation. Gibberellins: promote stem elongation, bolting, and seed germination.

• Cytokinins: promote cell division, delay senescence. Ethylene: promotes fruit ripening, senescence, and abscission. Abscisic Acid (ABA): the 'stress hormone', induces dormancy and stomatal closure, generally growth-inhibitory.

3. Development, Differentiation and Germination

Short Note:

• Development includes both growth and differentiation, integrating internal and external signals into a coordinated growth pattern.

• Seed germination is the process by which a seed develops into a seedling, usually triggered by water absorption (imbibition), followed by enzyme activation and mobilisation of stored food reserves.

NCERT Textbook Exercise Questions

What is the sigmoid growth curve? Discuss its phases.
Answer: The sigmoid (S-shaped) growth curve is a characteristic pattern of growth in living organisms/populations plotted against time, consisting of three phases: lag phase (slow initial growth as the organism adapts), exponential/log phase (rapid growth), and stationary phase (growth rate slows and levels off due to limiting factors).
Discuss the role of gibberellins in plant growth.
Answer: Gibberellins promote stem elongation (especially in genetically dwarf plants), induce bolting (rapid internode elongation before flowering) in rosette plants, promote flowering in certain long-day plants, and help break seed and bud dormancy, promoting germination by stimulating amylase production in cereal grains.
What is apical dominance? Which hormone is responsible for it?
Answer: Apical dominance is the phenomenon in which the growing apical (terminal) bud inhibits the growth of lateral (axillary) buds present below it. This effect is caused by the hormone auxin, produced by the apical bud; removal of the apical bud (e.g., by pruning) removes this inhibition and allows lateral buds to grow.
What is the function of ethylene as a plant hormone?
Answer: Ethylene is a gaseous plant hormone that promotes fruit ripening (hence used commercially to ripen fruits), induces senescence and abscission of leaves and flowers, promotes flowering in certain plants (e.g., pineapple), and can also promote or inhibit growth depending on the concentration and tissue involved.

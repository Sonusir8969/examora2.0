NCERT Solutions for Class 10 Science Chapter 10 The Human Eye and the Colourful World

1. The Human Eye and Defects of Vision

Short Note:

• Power of Accommodation: The ability of the eye lens to adjust its focal length using ciliary muscles to focus both near and distant objects clearly on the retina.

• Near Point & Far Point: The minimum distance for clear vision is 25 cm (Near Point). The maximum distance is Infinity (Far Point).

• Myopia (Near-sightedness): Person can see nearby objects clearly but cannot see distant objects. Corrected using a Concave lens.

• Hypermetropia (Far-sightedness): Person can see distant objects clearly but cannot see nearby objects. Corrected using a Convex lens.

• Presbyopia: Age-related reduction in power of accommodation due to weakening ciliary muscles. Corrected using Bifocal lenses.

2. Refraction of Light Through a Glass Prism

Short Note:

When light passes through a triangular glass prism, it undergoes refraction twice—at the entering surface and at the exiting surface. Due to the inclined refracting faces of the prism, the emergent ray bends at an angle to the direction of the incident ray. This angle is called the Angle of Deviation (δ).

3. Dispersion of White Light & Rainbow Formation

Short Note:

• Dispersion: The splitting of white light into its component seven colors (VIBGYOR) when passing through a transparent medium like a prism.

• Cause: Different wavelengths bend by different angles relative to the incident ray. Red light bends the least, while Violet light bends the most.

• Rainbow Formation: Formed by dispersion, refraction, and internal reflection of sunlight by tiny water droplets suspended in the atmosphere after rain.

4. Atmospheric Refraction Phenomena

Short Note:

Atmospheric refraction occurs due to continuous variation in air density and refractive index in different layers of Earth's atmosphere.

• Twinkling of Stars: Continuous fluctuations in atmospheric refractive index alter the apparent position and brightness of starlight.

• Advanced Sunrise & Delayed Sunset: The sun is visible to us about 2 minutes before actual sunrise and 2 minutes after actual sunset due to atmospheric bending of light.

5. Scattering of Light & Tyndall Effect

Short Note:

• Tyndall Effect: Scattering of a beam of light by colloidal particles in a fine suspension, making the path of light visible (e.g., sunlight entering a dusty canopy).

• Blue Sky: Fine air molecules scatter light of shorter wavelengths (blue end) more strongly than longer wavelengths (red end).

• Red Sunrise/Sunset: At sunrise and sunset, light travels longer atmospheric paths. Shorter blue wavelengths scatter away, while longer red wavelengths reach our eyes.

Intext Questions - Page Number: 190

Q1: What is meant by power of accommodation of the eye?
Answer: The power of accommodation is the ability of the human eye lens to adjust its focal length with the help of ciliary muscles so that sharp images of objects situated at varying distances can be formed on the retina.
Q2: A person with a myopic eye cannot see objects beyond 1.2 m distinctly. What should be the type of the corrective lens used to restore proper vision?
Answer: A myopic eye forms the image of a distant object in front of the retina. To correct this defect, a concave lens (diverging lens) of suitable power must be used so that light rays diverge and focus directly on the retina.
Q3: What is the far point and near point of the human eye with normal vision?
Answer:

• Near Point: The minimum distance at which objects can be seen clearly without strain is 25 cm.

• Far Point: The maximum distance up to which the eye can see objects clearly is Infinity (∞).

Textbook Chapter End Exercise Questions

Q1: The human eye can focus objects at different distances by adjusting the focal length of the eye lens. This is due to:

(a) presbyopia
(b) accommodation
(c) near-sightedness
(d) far-sightedness

Correct Answer: (b) accommodation.

Q2: The human eye forms the image of an object at its:

(a) cornea
(b) iris
(c) pupil
(d) retina

Correct Answer: (d) retina.

Q3: The least distance of distinct vision for a young adult with normal vision is about:

(a) 25 m
(b) 2.5 cm
(c) 25 cm
(d) 2.5 m

Correct Answer: (c) 25 cm.

Q4: Why do stars twinkle?
Answer: Stars twinkle due to atmospheric refraction of starlight. Starlight continuously passes through different layers of Earth's atmosphere with varying densities and optical refractive indices. As a result, the apparent position and intensity of starlight fluctuate continuously, causing the star to twinkle.
Q5: Why does the sky appear dark instead of blue to an astronaut in space?
Answer: Outer space lacks an atmosphere, meaning there are no air molecules or dust particles to scatter sunlight. In the absence of light scattering, sunlight passes without reflection or dispersion, making the sky appear completely dark/black to astronauts.

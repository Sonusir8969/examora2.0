Ch-9: Differential Equations 12 Questions

1. A differential equation is an equation that involves:

  • A. An unknown function and its derivatives
  • B. Only constants
  • C. Only algebraic variables without derivatives
  • D. Matrices

Answer: A. An unknown function and its derivatives

Explanation: By definition, a differential equation relates a function to one or more of its derivatives.

2. The order of a differential equation is defined as the:

  • A. Order of the highest derivative present in the equation
  • B. Power of the highest derivative
  • C. Number of terms in the equation
  • D. Number of independent variables

Answer: A. Order of the highest derivative present in the equation

Explanation: Order refers specifically to how many times the function has been differentiated in the highest derivative term.

3. The degree of a differential equation is defined only when the equation is:

  • A. Expressible as a polynomial in its derivatives
  • B. Any equation involving derivatives, regardless of form
  • C. Always assumed to be 1
  • D. Undefined for every equation

Answer: A. Expressible as a polynomial in its derivatives

Explanation: Degree is defined only for equations that can be written as a polynomial equation in derivatives, after removing radicals and fractions involving derivatives.

4. A differential equation of the form dy/dx = f(x)g(y) is called a:

  • A. Variable separable differential equation
  • B. Linear differential equation
  • C. Homogeneous differential equation
  • D. Exact differential equation

Answer: A. Variable separable differential equation

Explanation: Such equations can be solved by separating the variables x and y onto opposite sides before integrating.

5. The general solution of a differential equation contains:

  • A. A number of arbitrary constants equal to the order of the equation
  • B. No arbitrary constants at all
  • C. Always exactly one constant regardless of order
  • D. An unrelated infinite number of constants

Answer: A. A number of arbitrary constants equal to the order of the equation

Explanation: The number of independent arbitrary constants in the general solution matches the order of the differential equation.

6. A first order linear differential equation has the standard form:

  • A. dy/dx + Py = Q, where P and Q are functions of x
  • B. y'' + y = 0 only
  • C. dy/dx = a constant only
  • D. y = mx + c

Answer: A. dy/dx + Py = Q, where P and Q are functions of x

Explanation: This is the recognized standard form for a first order linear differential equation in y.

7. The integrating factor (I.F.) of the linear differential equation dy/dx + Py = Q is given by:

  • A. e^(∫P dx)
  • B. e^(∫Q dx)
  • C. ∫P dx
  • D. P · Q

Answer: A. e^(∫P dx)

Explanation: Multiplying through by this integrating factor makes the left-hand side an exact derivative, allowing direct integration.

8. A homogeneous differential equation can typically be solved by substituting:

  • A. y = vx
  • B. y = x + c
  • C. y = 1/x
  • D. y = eˣ

Answer: A. y = vx

Explanation: This substitution converts a homogeneous equation in x and y into a variable-separable equation in v and x.

9. The particular solution of a differential equation is obtained by:

  • A. Using given initial or boundary conditions to determine the arbitrary constants
  • B. Completely ignoring initial conditions
  • C. Always setting all constants to zero
  • D. Differentiating the general solution again

Answer: A. Using given initial or boundary conditions to determine the arbitrary constants

Explanation: Substituting known initial or boundary values into the general solution allows the arbitrary constants to be evaluated.

10. Differential equations are widely used to mathematically model:

  • A. Real-world problems involving rates of change, growth, and decay
  • B. Only static geometric shapes
  • C. Only fixed, unchanging quantities
  • D. Operations on matrices

Answer: A. Real-world problems involving rates of change, growth, and decay

Explanation: Because they relate a quantity to its rate of change, differential equations naturally describe dynamic real-world processes.

11. The differential equation dy/dx = ky (with k a constant) generally models:

  • A. Exponential growth or decay
  • B. Simple linear growth only
  • C. Purely oscillatory motion
  • D. No real physical phenomenon

Answer: A. Exponential growth or decay

Explanation: This equation's solution, y = Ce^(kt), is the classic form describing exponential growth (k > 0) or decay (k < 0).

12. Forming a differential equation from a given family of curves primarily involves:

  • A. Eliminating the arbitrary constants through repeated differentiation
  • B. Simply adding new constants
  • C. Directly integrating the family of curves
  • D. Using matrices to solve for constants

Answer: A. Eliminating the arbitrary constants through repeated differentiation

Explanation: By differentiating the family of curves as many times as there are arbitrary constants, those constants can be eliminated to form the differential equation.

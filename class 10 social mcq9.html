Ch-14: Political Parties 30 Questions

1. What is a group of people who come together to contest elections and hold power in the government called?

  • A. Political Party
  • B. Pressure Group
  • C. Interest Group
  • D. Trade Union

Answer: A. Political Party

Explanation: A political party is an organized group of people who share similar political views and aim to contest elections to hold government power.

2. Which of the following are the three basic components of a political party?

  • A. The leaders, active members, and followers
  • B. President, Prime Minister, and Chief Minister
  • C. Executive, Judiciary, and Legislature
  • D. Voters, candidates, and election officers

Answer: A. The leaders, active members, and followers

Explanation: Every political party comprises three main components: top leaders, active party workers/members, and mass followers.

3. A country where only one party is allowed to control and run the government is said to have a:

  • A. One-party system
  • B. Two-party system
  • C. Multi-party system
  • D. Coalition system

Answer: A. One-party system

Explanation: In a one-party system (e.g., China), only a single political party has the legal mandate to rule the state.

4. Which of the following countries is a classic example of a one-party system?

  • A. China
  • B. USA
  • C. United Kingdom
  • D. India

Answer: A. China

Explanation: In China, only the Communist Party is permitted to rule, making it a prominent example of a one-party system.

5. Which type of party system operates in countries like the United States of America and the United Kingdom?

  • A. Two-party system
  • B. One-party system
  • C. Multi-party system
  • D. Non-party system

Answer: A. Two-party system

Explanation: In a two-party system, power usually alternates between two major dominant parties, as seen in USA and UK.

6. What type of party system exists in India?

  • A. Multi-party system
  • B. Two-party system
  • C. One-party system
  • D. Authoritarian system

Answer: A. Multi-party system

Explanation: India has a multi-party system where several political parties compete in elections to form government independently or through coalitions.

7. When several parties in a multi-party system join hands for the purpose of contesting elections and winning power, it is called an:

  • A. Alliance or Front
  • B. Executive Committee
  • C. Unicameral Body
  • D. Interest Group

Answer: A. Alliance or Front

Explanation: Pre-election agreements between multiple political parties to contest combinedly and form government are called Alliances or Fronts (e.g., NDA, UPA).

8. Which independent constitutional body registers political parties in India and allocates symbols to them?

  • A. Election Commission of India
  • B. Supreme Court of India
  • C. Parliament of India
  • D. Union Home Ministry

Answer: A. Election Commission of India

Explanation: The Election Commission of India registers all political parties and assigns unique election symbols to recognized parties.

9. To be recognized as a 'National Party' in India, a party must secure at least what percentage of total votes in Lok Sabha or Assembly elections in four states?

  • A. At least 6% votes and win at least 4 Lok Sabha seats
  • B. At least 10% votes and win 2 seats
  • C. At least 50% votes
  • D. At least 15% votes across all states

Answer: A. At least 6% votes and win at least 4 Lok Sabha seats

Explanation: A party securing at least 6% of total votes in Lok Sabha or Assembly elections in 4 states and winning at least 4 Lok Sabha seats gets recognized as a National Party.

10. To be recognized as a 'State Party' (Regional Party), a political party must secure at least what percentage of total votes in a state assembly election and win how many seats?

  • A. At least 6% of total votes and win at least 2 seats
  • B. At least 10% of total votes and win 5 seats
  • C. At least 3% of total votes and win 1 seat
  • D. At least 15% of total votes

Answer: A. At least 6% of total votes and win at least 2 seats

Explanation: A party that secures at least 6 per cent of total votes in an election to the Legislative Assembly of a State and wins at least 2 seats is recognized as a State Party.

11. Which national party was formed in 1980, drawing inspiration from India's ancient culture and values, and advocates for Integral Humanism and Hindutva?

  • A. Bharatiya Janata Party (BJP)
  • B. Indian National Congress (INC)
  • C. Communist Party of India (CPI)
  • D. Bahujan Samaj Party (BSP)

Answer: A. Bharatiya Janata Party (BJP)

Explanation: Founded in 1980 (revived from Bharatiya Jana Sangh), the BJP advocates cultural nationalism (Hindutva) and strong national unity.

12. Which is one of the oldest political parties in the world, founded in 1885, that played a dominant role in Indian politics for decades?

  • A. Indian National Congress (INC)
  • B. Bharatiya Janata Party (BJP)
  • C. Communist Party of India (Marxist)
  • D. Aam Aadmi Party (AAP)

Answer: A. Indian National Congress (INC)

Explanation: The Indian National Congress (INC) was established in 1885 and led India's freedom struggle and post-independence governance for many decades.

13. Which party was formed in 1984 under the leadership of Kanshi Ram to represent and secure power for the Bahujan Samaj (Dalits, Adivasis, OBCs, and minorities)?

  • A. Bahujan Samaj Party (BSP)
  • B. Samajwadi Party (SP)
  • C. Rashtriya Janata Dal (RJD)
  • D. Communist Party of India (CPI)

Answer: A. Bahujan Samaj Party (BSP)

Explanation: Formed in 1984 by Kanshi Ram, BSP stands for securing representation and empowerment for Dalits, Adivasis, OBCs, and religious minorities.

14. Which political party adheres to Marxism-Leninism, opposes imperialism and communalism, and was formed in 1964 after a split in CPI?

  • A. CPI (Marxist) / CPI(M)
  • B. Nationalist Congress Party (NCP)
  • C. Trinamool Congress (TMC)
  • D. Janata Dal (United)

Answer: A. CPI (Marxist) / CPI(M)

Explanation: CPI(M) split from CPI in 1964 and supports Marxist philosophy, socialism, secularism, and anti-imperialism.

15. What is the role played by political parties that lose in elections and do not form the government?

  • A. Role of Opposition
  • B. Ruling Coalition
  • C. Election Observer
  • D. Neutral Mediator

Answer: A. Role of Opposition

Explanation: Parties that lose elections form the Opposition, voicing different views and criticizing government failures and policies.

16. Changing party allegiance from the party on whose ticket a person was elected to a different party is called:

  • A. Defection
  • B. Coalition
  • C. Partisanship
  • D. Infiltration

Answer: A. Defection

Explanation: Defection refers to an elected representative switching loyalty from the political party on whose symbol they were elected to another party.

17. What constitutional reform was implemented to stop elected MPs and MLAs from changing parties for cash or ministerial posts?

  • A. Anti-Defection Law
  • B. Right to Information Act
  • C. Representation of People Act
  • D. Lokpal Act

Answer: A. Anti-Defection Law

Explanation: The Anti-Defection Law (52nd Amendment, 10th Schedule) disqualifies MPs/MLAs if they switch political parties after getting elected.

18. What step was mandated by the Supreme Court to reduce the influence of money and criminals in elections?

  • A. Filing an Affidavit declaring assets and criminal cases
  • B. Compulsory military service for candidates
  • C. Banning television political advertisements
  • D. Holding elections strictly online

Answer: A. Filing an Affidavit declaring assets and criminal cases

Explanation: Every candidate contesting elections must submit a sworn legal document (affidavit) disclosing criminal cases pending and property details.

19. A signed document submitted to an officer containing a sworn statement regarding personal details is called an:

  • A. Affidavit
  • B. Ordinance
  • C. Manifesto
  • D. Article

Answer: A. Affidavit

Explanation: An affidavit is a written declaration under oath, used by election authorities to verify candidate disclosures.

20. What major challenge is faced by political parties when power is concentrated in the hands of one or a few top leaders?

  • A. Lack of internal democracy
  • B. Dynastic succession
  • C. Excessive money power
  • D. High voter turnout

Answer: A. Lack of internal democracy

Explanation: Most political parties do not hold organizational elections regularly or share internal information, leading to a lack of internal democracy.

21. The tendency of political party leaders to pass on top positions to members of their own family is known as:

  • A. Dynastic succession
  • B. Partisanship
  • C. Federalism
  • D. Bureaucracy

Answer: A. Dynastic succession

Explanation: Dynastic succession happens when leadership roles are automatically handed over to family members regardless of merit or internal democratic votes.

22. What term describes a person who is strongly committed to a political party, group, or faction and cannot take a neutral view?

  • A. Partisan
  • B. Secularist
  • C. Neutralist
  • D. Independent

Answer: A. Partisan

Explanation: A partisan is a committed member of a political party who strongly supports their party's policies and exhibits strong bias.

23. Why do voters often feel that political parties do not offer a meaningful choice in modern elections?

  • A. Because major political parties show significant decline in ideological differences
  • B. Because all parties belong to the same leader
  • C. Because elections are no longer conducted
  • D. Because all candidates are chosen by lotteries

Answer: A. Because major political parties show significant decline in ideological differences

Explanation: Differences among parties on core economic policies and programs have narrowed globally, giving voters fewer distinct choices.

24. What order did the Election Commission pass regarding the internal functioning of political parties?

  • A. Mandatory organizational elections and filing income tax returns
  • B. Banning all party membership fees
  • C. Limiting the number of party members to 1,000
  • D. Forcing parties to merge with larger ones

Answer: A. Mandatory organizational elections and filing income tax returns

Explanation: The Election Commission made it compulsory for registered political parties to conduct organizational elections and file tax returns.

25. What is a key suggestion to reform political parties regarding gender representation?

  • A. Making it mandatory to give at least one-third of ticket nominations to women candidates
  • B. Reserving all cabinet posts exclusively for women
  • C. Banning men from contesting local elections
  • D. Creating separate political parties for women only

Answer: A. Making it mandatory to give at least one-third of ticket nominations to women candidates

Explanation: Reform proposals suggest that political parties should legally be required to reserve about one-third of candidate tickets for women.

26. What proposal advocates that the government should provide funding to political parties for election expenses (in cash or in-kind like paper, petrol, phone)?

  • A. State funding of elections
  • B. Private corporate sponsorship
  • C. Election Tax Collection
  • D. Foreign election aid

Answer: A. State funding of elections

Explanation: State funding of elections implies government support for election expenditures to curb the dominance of rich donors and corporate money.

27. Which of the following is NOT a direct function of a political party?

  • A. Deliberating judicial sentences in criminal courts
  • B. Contesting elections
  • C. Forming and running governments
  • D. Shaping public opinion

Answer: A. Deliberating judicial sentences in criminal courts

Explanation: Delivering judicial verdicts is strictly the function of the independent Judiciary, not political parties.

28. Which form of government cannot exist without political parties because candidates would otherwise be independent and accountable only to their local area?

  • A. Representative Democracy
  • B. Absolute Monarchy
  • C. Military Dictatorship
  • D. Feudal Aristocracy

Answer: A. Representative Democracy

Explanation: Modern representative democracies require political parties to aggregate diverse interests and form coherent national governments.

29. The All India Trinamool Congress (AITC) was launched in 1998 under the leadership of which prominent leader?

  • A. Mamata Banerjee
  • B. Jayalalithaa
  • C. Mayawati
  • D. Sushma Swaraj

Answer: A. Mamata Banerjee

Explanation: All India Trinamool Congress (AITC) was established on January 1, 1998 under the leadership of Mamata Banerjee.

30. How can ordinary citizens effectively pressurize political parties to reform?

  • A. Through petitions, publicity, movements, and active participation
  • B. By boycotting all elections permanently
  • C. By refusing to pay municipal taxes
  • D. By seeking foreign intervention

Answer: A. Through petitions, publicity, movements, and active participation

Explanation: Citizens, pressure groups, movements, and media can pressure parties to reform, and citizens can join parties to improve them from within.

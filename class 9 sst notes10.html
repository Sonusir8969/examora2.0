NCERT Solutions for Class 9 Geography Chapter 5 Natural Vegetation and Wildlife

NCERT Solutions for Class 9 Geography Chapter 5 covers Factors Affecting Vegetation, Types of Vegetation in India, Wildlife Diversity, and Conservation Measures.

1. Factors Affecting Vegetation (Relief & Climate)

Short Note:

• Virgin Vegetation: Plant community that has grown naturally without human aid and left undisturbed for a long time (also called Flora).

• Relief Factors: Land (determines agriculture vs forests) and Soil (different soil types support different vegetation, e.g., sandy soil supports cacti, mangrove swamps in deltaic soil).

• Climate Factors: Temperature, Photoperiod (Sunlight), and Precipitation (Rainfall) directly dictate tree height, density, and species distribution.

2. Major Vegetation Types in India

Short Note:

• Tropical Evergreen Forests: Heavy rainfall (>200 cm); dense canopy; evergreen trees like Rosewood, Ebony, Mahogany.

• Tropical Deciduous Forests: Most widespread in India (70-200 cm rainfall); shed leaves in dry summer; divided into Moist (Teak, Sal) and Dry Deciduous.

• Tropical Thorn Forests & Scrubs: Low rainfall (<70 cm); thorny bushes and cacti (Acacia, Euphorbia); long roots and thick leaves.

• Montane Forests: Successive vegetation belts with altitude in Himalayas (Wet temperate, Temperate, Alpine vegetation like Pine, Deodar, Silver fir).

• Mangrove Forests: Found in coastal delta regions influenced by tides; famous tree is Sundari in the Ganga-Brahmaputra delta (Sundarbans).

3. Wildlife Diversity & Conservation Initiatives

Short Note:

• Rich Fauna: India hosts over 90,000 animal species, 2,000+ bird species, and thousands of fish and reptile varieties.

• Key Habitats: Elephants in hot wet forests of Assam & Kerala; One-horned Rhinos in Assam & West Bengal; Asiatic Lions in Gir Forest (Gujarat); Tigers in Sundarbans and Madhya Pradesh.

• Conservation Efforts: Setting up 18 Biosphere Reserves (e.g., Nilgiri, Sundarbans), Project Tiger, Project Rhino, and establishment of National Parks and Wildlife Sanctuaries.

Intext & Conceptual Questions

Q1: Distinguish between Tropical Evergreen and Tropical Deciduous Forests.
Tropical Evergreen Forests Tropical Deciduous Forests
Found in regions with over 200 cm annual rainfall. Found in regions receiving between 70 cm and 200 cm rainfall.
Trees do not shed leaves at the same time; look green year-round. Trees shed their leaves for 6 to 8 weeks in dry summer.
Important trees: Ebony, Mahogany, Rosewood, Rubber. Important trees: Teak, Sal, Sheesham, Sandalwood.
Q2: Name the major vegetation types found at different altitudes in the Himalayas.
Answer:
1. 1,000 to 2,000m: Wet temperate forests with broad-leafed trees like Oak and Chestnut.
2. 1,500 to 3,000m: Temperate coniferous forests with Pine, Deodar, Silver Fir, and Cedar.
3. Above 3,600m: Alpine vegetation consisting of Silver fir, Junipers, Pines, and Birches giving way to Alpine grasslands.

Q1: Choose the correct answer from the options given below:
(i) To which one of the following types of vegetation does rubber belong?
(a) Tundra   (b) Tidal   (c) Himalayan   (d) Tropical Evergreen
Answer: (d) Tropical Evergreen

(ii) Cinchona trees are found in the areas of rainfall more than:
(a) 100 cm   (b) 50 cm   (c) 70 cm   (d) 20 cm
Answer: (a) 100 cm

(iii) In which of the following states is the Simlipal bio-reserve located?
(a) Punjab   (b) Odisha   (c) Delhi   (d) West Bengal
Answer: (b) Odisha

(iv) Which one of the following bio-reserves of India is not included in the world network of bio-reserves?
(a) Manas   (b) Gulf of Mannar   (c) Nilgiri   (d) Nanda Devi
Answer: (a) Manas
Q2: Why are animals and plants endangered in India?
Answer:
1. Poaching & Hunting: Illegal hunting for commercial purposes (fur, ivory, horns, bones).
2. Habitat Destruction: Deforestation for agricultural expansion, urbanization, and industrialization.
3. Environmental Pollution: Chemical pollutants, industrial waste, and acid rain disrupting ecosystems.
4. Alien Species Introduction: Encroachment by non-native invasive plant and animal species.
Q3: What steps have been taken by the government to protect the flora and fauna of the country?
Answer:
1. Biosphere Reserves: Set up 18 Biosphere Reserves across the country to protect natural habitats.
2. Conservation Projects: Launched targeted species projects like Project Tiger, Project Rhino, and Project Great Indian Bustard.
3. Protected Areas Network: Established 103+ National Parks, 535+ Wildlife Sanctuaries, and Zoological gardens.
4. Legal Protection: Enacted the Wildlife Protection Act, 1972, making poaching and illegal wildlife trade strictly punishable.

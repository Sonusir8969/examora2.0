Ch-14: Probability 30 Questions

1. The set of all possible outcomes of a random experiment is called:

  • A. Sample Space
  • B. Event Space
  • C. Outcome Set
  • D. Probability Field

Answer: A. Sample Space

Explanation: The collection of all possible outcomes of a random experiment is defined as its sample space (S).

2. If a coin is tossed 3 times, the number of elements in the sample space is:

  • A. 8
  • B. 6
  • C. 4
  • D. 16

Answer: A. 8

Explanation: Total outcomes = 2³ = 8.

3. An event having only one sample point is called a/an:

  • A. Simple Event
  • B. Compound Event
  • C. Impossible Event
  • D. Certain Event

Answer: A. Simple Event

Explanation: An event containing a single outcome of the sample space is called an elementary or simple event.

4. Two events A and B are mutually exclusive if:

  • A. A ∩ B = ∅
  • B. A ∪ B = S
  • C. A ∩ B = S
  • D. P(A) = P(B)

Answer: A. A ∩ B = ∅

Explanation: Mutually exclusive events cannot occur simultaneously, meaning their intersection is the empty set (∅).

5. Events A₁, A₂, ..., Aₙ are called exhaustive if:

  • A. A₁ ∪ A₂ ∪ ... ∪ Aₙ = S
  • B. A₁ ∩ A₂ ∩ ... ∩ Aₙ = ∅
  • C. P(A₁) + P(A₂) + ... + P(Aₙ) = 0
  • D. A₁ ∩ A₂ = ∅

Answer: A. A₁ ∪ A₂ ∪ ... ∪ Aₙ = S

Explanation: Events are exhaustive if their union equals the entire sample space S.

6. For any event A, the probability P(A) always satisfies:

  • A. 0 ≤ P(A) ≤ 1
  • B. -1 ≤ P(A) ≤ 1
  • C. 0 < P(A) < 1
  • D. P(A) ≥ 1

Answer: A. 0 ≤ P(A) ≤ 1

Explanation: Probability of any event lies between 0 and 1 inclusive.

7. The probability of an impossible event is:

  • A. 0
  • B. 1
  • C. 1/2
  • D. -1

Answer: A. 0

Explanation: An impossible event contains no sample points (∅), so its probability is 0.

8. The probability of a certain (sure) event is:

  • A. 1
  • B. 0
  • C. 1/2
  • D. Infinity

Answer: A. 1

Explanation: A sure event encompasses the complete sample space S, so P(S) = 1.

9. If P(A) is the probability of event A, then P(not A) or P(A') is:

  • A. 1 - P(A)
  • B. P(A) - 1
  • C. 1 / P(A)
  • D. P(A)

Answer: A. 1 - P(A)

Explanation: Since P(A) + P(A') = 1, P(A') = 1 - P(A).

10. For any two events A and B, P(A ∪ B) is given by:

  • A. P(A) + P(B) - P(A ∩ B)
  • B. P(A) + P(B) + P(A ∩ B)
  • C. P(A) × P(B)
  • D. P(A) - P(B)

Answer: A. P(A) + P(B) - P(A ∩ B)

Explanation: General addition theorem of probability: P(A ∪ B) = P(A) + P(B) - P(A ∩ B).

11. If A and B are mutually exclusive events, then P(A ∪ B) is equal to:

  • A. P(A) + P(B)
  • B. P(A) × P(B)
  • C. P(A) - P(B)
  • D. 0

Answer: A. P(A) + P(B)

Explanation: For mutually exclusive events, P(A ∩ B) = 0, so P(A ∪ B) = P(A) + P(B).

12. A die is thrown once. What is the probability of getting a prime number?

  • A. 1/2
  • B. 1/3
  • C. 2/3
  • D. 1/6

Answer: A. 1/2

Explanation: S = {1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6}. Prime numbers = {2, 3, 5} (3 outcomes). P = 3/6 = 1/2.

13. Two dice are thrown simultaneously. Number of elements in the sample space is:

  • A. 36
  • B. 12
  • C. 24
  • D. 6

Answer: A. 36

Explanation: Total outcomes = 6 × 6 = 36.

14. Probability of drawing a king from a well-shuffled deck of 52 cards is:

  • A. 1/13
  • B. 1/52
  • C. 4/13
  • D. 1/26

Answer: A. 1/13

Explanation: Total kings = 4. P(King) = 4/52 = 1/13.

15. If P(A) = 0.4, P(B) = 0.7, and P(A ∩ B) = 0.3, then P(A ∪ B) is:

  • A. 0.8
  • B. 0.9
  • C. 1.1
  • D. 0.5

Answer: A. 0.8

Explanation: P(A ∪ B) = P(A) + P(B) - P(A ∩ B) = 0.4 + 0.7 - 0.3 = 0.8.

16. If A and B are mutually exclusive events with P(A) = 0.35 and P(B) = 0.45, then P(A ∪ B) is:

  • A. 0.80
  • B. 0.10
  • C. 0.1575
  • D. 0.70

Answer: A. 0.80

Explanation: Mutually exclusive means P(A ∩ B) = 0. So, P(A ∪ B) = 0.35 + 0.45 = 0.80.

17. What is the probability of getting a sum of 8 when two dice are thrown?

  • A. 5/36
  • B. 1/6
  • C. 4/36
  • D. 7/36

Answer: A. 5/36

Explanation: Pairs giving sum 8: (2,6), (3,5), (4,4), (5,3), (6,2) = 5 outcomes. P = 5/36.

18. Three coins are tossed. Probability of getting at least two heads is:

  • A. 1/2
  • B. 3/8
  • C. 5/8
  • D. 1/4

Answer: A. 1/2

Explanation: Outcomes with ≥ 2 heads: {HHH, HHT, HTH, THH} = 4 outcomes. Total = 8. P = 4/8 = 1/2.

19. In a single throw of a die, probability of getting a number greater than 4 is:

  • A. 1/3
  • B. 1/2
  • C. 2/3
  • D. 1/6

Answer: A. 1/3

Explanation: Numbers greater than 4 are {5, 6} (2 outcomes). P = 2/6 = 1/3.

20. Probability of getting 53 Sundays in a non-leap year is:

  • A. 1/7
  • B. 2/7
  • C. 53/365
  • D. 0

Answer: A. 1/7

Explanation: Non-leap year = 365 days = 52 weeks + 1 extra day. Probability that extra day is Sunday = 1/7.

21. Probability of getting 53 Sundays in a leap year is:

  • A. 2/7
  • B. 1/7
  • C. 53/366
  • D. 3/7

Answer: A. 2/7

Explanation: Leap year = 366 days = 52 weeks + 2 extra days. Pairs containing Sunday are (Sat, Sun) and (Sun, Mon) = 2/7.

22. Event "A but not B" in set notation is represented by:

  • A. A ∩ B'
  • B. A' ∩ B
  • C. A ∪ B'
  • D. (A ∪ B)'

Answer: A. A ∩ B'

Explanation: "A but not B" means elements belonging to A and not to B, denoted as A - B or A ∩ B'.

23. If P(A) = 0.6 and P(A ∩ B) = 0.2, then P(A ∩ B') is:

  • A. 0.4
  • B. 0.8
  • C. 0.3
  • D. 0.12

Answer: A. 0.4

Explanation: P(A ∩ B') = P(A) - P(A ∩ B) = 0.6 - 0.2 = 0.4.

24. If a card is drawn from a deck of 52 cards, probability that it is a red card or a king is:

  • A. 7/13
  • B. 1/2
  • C. 15/26
  • D. 6/13

Answer: A. 7/13

Explanation: P(Red) = 26/52, P(King) = 4/52, P(Red King) = 2/52. P = (26 + 4 - 2)/52 = 28/52 = 7/13.

25. If P(A) = 2/11, probability of event 'not A' is:

  • A. 9/11
  • B. 2/11
  • C. 11/2
  • D. 1/11

Answer: A. 9/11

Explanation: P(not A) = 1 - P(A) = 1 - 2/11 = 9/11.

26. Which of the following CANNOT be the probability of an event?

  • A. -1.5
  • B. 0.6
  • C. 2/3
  • D. 15%

Answer: A. -1.5

Explanation: Probability cannot be negative (0 ≤ P(E) ≤ 1).

27. A box contains 3 red and 5 black balls. One ball is drawn at random. Probability that it is red is:

  • A. 3/8
  • B. 5/8
  • C. 3/5
  • D. 1/8

Answer: A. 3/8

Explanation: Total balls = 8, Red balls = 3. P(Red) = 3/8.

28. Two events A and B are mutually exclusive and exhaustive. If P(A) = 3/5, then P(B) is:

  • A. 2/5
  • B. 3/5
  • C. 1/5
  • D. 0

Answer: A. 2/5

Explanation: Mutually exclusive & exhaustive means P(A) + P(B) = 1 ⇒ P(B) = 1 - 3/5 = 2/5.

29. In a simultaneous throw of two coins, probability of getting at most one head is:

  • A. 3/4
  • B. 1/2
  • C. 1/4
  • D. 2/3

Answer: A. 3/4

Explanation: Outcomes: {HH, HT, TH, TT}. At most one head = {HT, TH, TT} = 3 outcomes. P = 3/4.

30. If A and B are two events such that P(A ∪ B) = 1, then A and B are:

  • A. Exhaustive events
  • B. Mutually exclusive events
  • C. Independent events
  • D. Impossible events

Answer: A. Exhaustive events

Explanation: By definition, events whose union equals the sample space (P(A ∪ B) = P(S) = 1) are exhaustive events.

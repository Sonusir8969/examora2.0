Ch-15: Probability 30 Questions

1. The numerical measure of uncertainty is called:

  • A. Statistics
  • B. Probability
  • C. Frequency
  • D. Variance

Answer: B. Probability

Explanation: Probability quantifies how likely an event is to occur.

2. The probability of an event lies between:

  • A. -1 and 1
  • B. 0 and 1 (inclusive)
  • C. 0 and 100
  • D. 1 and 2

Answer: B. 0 and 1 (inclusive)

Explanation: For any event E, 0 ≤ P(E) ≤ 1.

3. The probability of a sure (certain) event is:

  • A. 0
  • B. 0.5
  • C. 1
  • D. Infinity

Answer: C. 1

Explanation: An event that is certain to occur has a probability of 1.

4. The probability of an impossible event is:

  • A. 0
  • B. 1
  • C. -1
  • D. 0.5

Answer: A. 0

Explanation: An event that can never occur has a probability equal to 0.

5. Which of the following CANNOT be the probability of an event?

  • A. ⅓
  • B. 1.5
  • C. 15%
  • D. 0.7

Answer: B. 1.5

Explanation: Probability can never exceed 1 or be less than 0. Thus, 1.5 is impossible.

6. If P(E) is the probability of an event E, then P(not E) or P(E') is given by:

  • A. P(E) - 1
  • B. 1 - P(E)
  • C. 1 / P(E)
  • D. P(E) + 1

Answer: B. 1 - P(E)

Explanation: Sum of complementary events P(E) + P(not E) = 1 ⇒ P(not E) = 1 - P(E).

7. A coin is tossed 100 times and Head obtained is 60 times. The empirical probability of getting a Head is:

  • A. ⅗ (0.6)
  • B. ⅖ (0.4)
  • C. ½ (0.5)
  • D. 1

Answer: A. ⅗ (0.6)

Explanation: Empirical probability = Favourable outcomes / Total trials = 60 / 100 = 0.6 = ⅗.

8. A fair die is rolled once. The probability of getting an even number (2, 4, 6) is:

  • A. ⅙
  • B. ½
  • C. ⅓
  • D. ⅔

Answer: B. ½

Explanation: Total outcomes = 6 {1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6}. Favourable outcomes = 3 {2, 4, 6}. P = 3/6 = 1/2.

9. In a single roll of a die, what is the probability of getting a number greater than 4?

  • A. ½
  • B. ⅓
  • C. ⅙
  • D. ⅔

Answer: B. ⅓

Explanation: Numbers greater than 4 are 5 and 6 (2 outcomes). P = 2/6 = 1/3.

10. What is the probability of getting a number 7 in a single throw of a standard six-faced die?

  • A. 1
  • B. 0
  • C. ⅙
  • D. ⅐

Answer: B. 0

Explanation: Since a die faces are numbered 1 to 6, getting a 7 is an impossible event (P = 0).

11. If P(E) = 0.37, then P(not E) is equal to:

  • A. 0.63
  • B. 0.37
  • C. 0.73
  • D. -0.37

Answer: A. 0.63

Explanation: P(not E) = 1 - P(E) = 1 - 0.37 = 0.63.

12. A bag contains 5 red balls and 3 blue balls. The probability of drawing a red ball at random is:

  • A. ⅜
  • B. ⅝
  • C. ⅗
  • D. ⅖

Answer: B. ⅝

Explanation: Total balls = 5 + 3 = 8. Red balls = 5. Probability = 5/8.

13. In a cricket match, a batswoman hits a boundary 6 times out of 30 balls played. The probability that she did NOT hit a boundary is:

  • A. ⅕ (0.2)
  • B. ⅘ (0.8)
  • C. ½ (0.5)
  • D. ⅙ (0.16)

Answer: B. ⅘ (0.8)

Explanation: Balls without boundary = 30 - 6 = 24. P = 24/30 = 4/5 = 0.8.

14. Two coins are tossed simultaneously 500 times. Getting 2 Heads = 105 times. The empirical probability of 2 Heads is:

  • A. 0.21
  • B. 0.42
  • C. 0.50
  • D. 0.105

Answer: A. 0.21

Explanation: P(2 Heads) = 105 / 500 = 21 / 100 = 0.21.

15. Sum of probabilities of all primary/elementary events of an experiment is always:

  • A. 0
  • B. 0.5
  • C. 1
  • D. Variable

Answer: C. 1

Explanation: The sum of probabilities of all elementary events of an experiment is 1.

16. Out of 1000 families with 2 children, 475 families have 2 girls. The probability of a randomly chosen family having 2 girls is:

  • A. 0.475
  • B. 0.525
  • C. 0.250
  • D. 0.800

Answer: A. 0.475

Explanation: P(2 girls) = 475 / 1000 = 0.475.

17. A deck of playing cards contains how many total cards?

  • A. 48
  • B. 52
  • C. 54
  • D. 36

Answer: B. 52

Explanation: A standard deck of playing cards has 52 cards.

18. Probability based on actual experiments and observations is called:

  • A. Theoretical probability
  • B. Empirical (experimental) probability
  • C. Subjective probability
  • D. Classical probability

Answer: B. Empirical (experimental) probability

Explanation: Empirical probability relies on real trial data and observed outcomes.

19. In a sample of 200 light bulbs, 10 bulbs are found defective. The probability of selecting a non-defective bulb is:

  • A. 0.05
  • B. 0.95
  • C. 0.10
  • D. 0.90

Answer: B. 0.95

Explanation: Non-defective bulbs = 200 - 10 = 190. P = 190 / 200 = 0.95.

20. What is the probability of a leap year having 53 Sundays?

  • A. ⅐
  • B. ⅔
  • C. ⅖
  • D. ⅖ (2/7)

Answer: D. ⅖ (2/7)

Explanation: A leap year has 366 days = 52 weeks + 2 extra days. Total possibilities for 2 extra days = 7. Favourable for Sunday = 2 (Sat-Sun, Sun-Mon). P = 2/7.

21. What is the probability of a non-leap year having 53 Sundays?

  • A. ⅐
  • B. ⅖
  • C. ⅓
  • D. 0

Answer: A. ⅐

Explanation: Non-leap year has 365 days = 52 weeks + 1 extra day. Out of 7 days in a week, 1 can be Sunday. P = 1/7.

22. The probability of choosing a vowel from the English alphabet is:

  • A. ⁵/₂₆
  • B. ²¹/₂₆
  • C. ⅙
  • D. ½

Answer: A. ⁵/₂₆

Explanation: English alphabets = 26. Vowels = 5 {a, e, i, o, u}. Probability = 5/26.

23. If an event cannot happen, its probability is 0. Such an event is called an:

  • A. Sure event
  • B. Elementary event
  • C. Impossible event
  • D. Independent event

Answer: C. Impossible event

Explanation: Events with zero probability are defined as impossible events.

24. Three coins are tossed simultaneously 200 times. 3 Heads appear 23 times. The probability of 3 Heads is:

  • A. 0.115
  • B. 0.230
  • C. 0.100
  • D. 0.050

Answer: A. 0.115

Explanation: P(3 Heads) = 23 / 200 = 0.115.

25. A number x is chosen at random from numbers -3, -2, -1, 0, 1, 2, 3. The probability that |x| < 2 is:

  • A. ⅗
  • B. ⅐
  • C. ⅜
  • D. ⅗ (3/7)

Answer: D. ⅗ (3/7)

Explanation: Total numbers = 7. Numbers with |x| < 2 are -1, 0, 1 (3 numbers). P = 3/7.

26. In 50 throws of a die, '4' was obtained 12 times. The empirical probability of getting 4 is:

  • A. 0.24
  • B. 0.12
  • C. 0.50
  • D. 0.36

Answer: A. 0.24

Explanation: Empirical probability = 12 / 50 = 24 / 100 = 0.24.

27. Which of the following CANNOT be a probability value?

  • A. 0.001
  • B. -0.5
  • C. ⅗
  • D. 85%

Answer: B. -0.5

Explanation: Probability values cannot be negative.

28. Probability of an event E + Probability of event 'not E' is equal to:

  • A. 0
  • B. 1
  • C. -1
  • D. 2

Answer: B. 1

Explanation: P(E) + P(not E) = 1 for any event E.

29. A card is selected from a pack of 52 cards. The probability of getting a red face card is:

  • A. ³/₂₆
  • B. ⁶/₅₂
  • C. Both A and B
  • D. ¹/₂₆

Answer: C. Both A and B

Explanation: Total red face cards = 6 (3 Heart + 3 Diamond). P = 6/52 = 3/26.

30. A box contains 90 discs numbered 1 to 90. A disc is drawn at random. The probability that it bears a 2-digit number is:

  • A. ⁸¹/₉₀ (9/10)
  • B. ⁹/₉₀ (1/10)
  • C. ⁸⁰/₉₀
  • D. 1

Answer: A. ⁸¹/₉₀ (9/10)

Explanation: 1-digit numbers (1-9) = 9. Two-digit numbers (10-90) = 81. P = 81/90 = 9/10.

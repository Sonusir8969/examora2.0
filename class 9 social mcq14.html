Chapter 14: Electoral Politics 30 Questions

1. Why are elections considered necessary in a democracy?

  • A. To allow citizens to choose their representatives periodically
  • B. To let the army select leaders
  • C. To avoid any change in government
  • D. To eliminate political parties

Answer: A. To allow citizens to choose their representatives periodically

Explanation: Elections give citizens the opportunity to choose their representatives at regular intervals, forming the basis of representative democracy.

2. What is a 'constituency' in the context of elections?

  • A. A political party
  • B. A geographical area from which voters elect a representative
  • C. A polling booth
  • D. A candidate's home

Answer: B. A geographical area from which voters elect a representative

Explanation: A constituency is a specific area whose voters elect one or more representatives to a legislative body.

3. What is 'universal adult franchise'?

  • A. The right to vote given only to men above 21
  • B. The right to vote given to all adult citizens without discrimination
  • C. The right to vote given only to property owners
  • D. The right to vote given only to government employees

Answer: B. The right to vote given to all adult citizens without discrimination

Explanation: Universal adult franchise means every adult citizen has the right to vote, regardless of caste, religion, gender, or economic status.

4. What is the minimum voting age in India?

  • A. 16 years
  • B. 18 years
  • C. 21 years
  • D. 25 years

Answer: B. 18 years

Explanation: As per Indian law, every citizen who has attained 18 years of age is eligible to be registered as a voter.

5. What is a 'reserved constituency'?

  • A. A constituency where only certain groups such as SC/ST can be candidates
  • B. A constituency where no elections are held
  • C. A constituency reserved for foreign observers
  • D. A constituency with no voters

Answer: A. A constituency where only certain groups such as SC/ST can be candidates

Explanation: Reserved constituencies are those where only candidates belonging to Scheduled Castes, Scheduled Tribes or other specified groups can contest, ensuring fair representation.

6. What is the official list of eligible voters called?

  • A. Nomination paper
  • B. Electoral roll or voters' list
  • C. Manifesto
  • D. Ballot paper

Answer: B. Electoral roll or voters' list

Explanation: The electoral roll, commonly called the voters' list, contains the names of all citizens who are registered and eligible to vote.

7. What is an EVM used for in Indian elections?

  • A. Counting the population
  • B. Casting and recording votes electronically
  • C. Printing newspapers
  • D. Registering candidates online

Answer: B. Casting and recording votes electronically

Explanation: The Electronic Voting Machine (EVM) is used in Indian elections to record and count votes electronically instead of using paper ballots.

8. Which independent body is responsible for conducting free and fair elections in India?

  • A. Supreme Court
  • B. Election Commission of India
  • C. Ministry of Home Affairs
  • D. Parliament

Answer: B. Election Commission of India

Explanation: The Election Commission of India is an independent constitutional body responsible for administering all electoral processes in the country.

9. Who heads the Election Commission of India?

  • A. The President of India
  • B. The Chief Election Commissioner
  • C. The Prime Minister
  • D. The Chief Justice of India

Answer: B. The Chief Election Commissioner

Explanation: The Election Commission of India is headed by the Chief Election Commissioner, who along with other Election Commissioners oversees elections.

10. What document must a candidate file to formally enter an election contest?

  • A. Manifesto
  • B. Nomination paper
  • C. Voter ID
  • D. Affidavit of income tax only

Answer: B. Nomination paper

Explanation: A prospective candidate must file a nomination paper along with the required security deposit to be able to contest an election.

11. Candidates contesting elections in India are legally required to declare what in an affidavit?

  • A. Their favourite hobbies
  • B. Details of their criminal cases, assets and educational qualifications
  • C. Their family recipes
  • D. Their travel history

Answer: B. Details of their criminal cases, assets and educational qualifications

Explanation: Election rules require candidates to file a sworn affidavit declaring serious pending criminal cases, financial assets and liabilities, and educational qualifications.

12. What is the minimum period allowed for public campaigning between the final list of candidates and polling day?

  • A. One day
  • B. Two weeks
  • C. Six months
  • D. One year

Answer: B. Two weeks

Explanation: In India, parties and candidates are given a minimum of two weeks for public campaigning before polling takes place.

13. What is the 'Model Code of Conduct' enforced by the Election Commission?

  • A. A set of rules that all candidates and parties must follow during elections
  • B. A code that applies only to voters
  • C. A law that bans all campaigning
  • D. A rule that applies only after elections end

Answer: A. A set of rules that all candidates and parties must follow during elections

Explanation: The Model Code of Conduct is a set of guidelines issued by the Election Commission that all parties and candidates must observe during the election period.

14. Which of these powers does the Election Commission of India NOT have?

  • A. Implementing the code of conduct
  • B. Cancelling an election where malpractice is proven
  • C. Amending the Constitution
  • D. Controlling government officers on election duty

Answer: C. Amending the Constitution

Explanation: While the Election Commission has extensive powers over conducting elections, amending the Constitution is not among its functions; that power lies with Parliament.

15. What term is used for the stage when votes are counted after polling ends?

  • A. Nomination
  • B. Counting of votes
  • C. Campaigning
  • D. Filing

Answer: B. Counting of votes

Explanation: After polling closes, votes recorded in the EVMs are counted at designated centres, and results are declared based on the tally.

16. What is a key criterion for elections to be considered free and fair?

  • A. Only the ruling party can put up candidates
  • B. All parties and candidates get a fair chance to compete
  • C. Voters are forced to vote for a specific party
  • D. There is no independent body overseeing the process

Answer: B. All parties and candidates get a fair chance to compete

Explanation: Free and fair elections require that every eligible party and candidate has an equal opportunity to contest and campaign without unfair restrictions.

17. What does the term 'incumbent' refer to in the context of elections?

  • A. A new voter
  • B. The person currently holding the office or seat that is being contested
  • C. An election officer
  • D. A losing candidate from the last election

Answer: B. The person currently holding the office or seat that is being contested

Explanation: The incumbent is the current officeholder in a constituency or position, who may or may not choose to recontest that seat.

18. Under what circumstance can the Election Commission order re-polling in a booth?

  • A. If the weather is bad
  • B. If it finds evidence of malpractice or rigging
  • C. If too many candidates contest
  • D. If the winning margin is large

Answer: B. If it finds evidence of malpractice or rigging

Explanation: The Election Commission has the power to order fresh polling in a booth or constituency if there is clear evidence of electoral malpractice.

19. In the Indian electoral system, most elections use which method to decide the winner?

  • A. Proportional representation only
  • B. First-Past-The-Post System
  • C. Direct appointment by the government
  • D. Lottery system

Answer: B. First-Past-The-Post System

Explanation: In India's First-Past-The-Post system, the candidate who secures the largest number of votes in a constituency is declared the winner, even without an absolute majority.

20. Why is it important for elections to allow genuine choice between different candidates and parties?

  • A. It is not important
  • B. It ensures voters can express different preferences and hold representatives accountable
  • C. It only benefits the ruling party
  • D. It makes elections unnecessary

Answer: B. It ensures voters can express different preferences and hold representatives accountable

Explanation: Real choice between parties and candidates allows voters to express diverse opinions and to reward or punish representatives based on their performance.

21. What role does the judiciary play if there is a dispute about the conduct of an election?

  • A. It has no role at all
  • B. Courts can hear election petitions and give judgments on disputed elections
  • C. Only the police can resolve disputes
  • D. Election disputes are handled by political parties themselves

Answer: B. Courts can hear election petitions and give judgments on disputed elections

Explanation: Courts in India can hear election petitions challenging the outcome of an election on legal grounds and can order fresh elections if malpractice is proven.

22. What is the significance of the secret ballot in elections?

  • A. It allows others to see how a person votes
  • B. It protects the voter's choice from being known to others, ensuring free voting
  • C. It makes voting compulsory
  • D. It eliminates the need for polling booths

Answer: B. It protects the voter's choice from being known to others, ensuring free voting

Explanation: The secret ballot ensures no one can know how an individual voted, protecting voters from pressure or retaliation and enabling free choice.

23. What is meant by a 'by-election'?

  • A. An election held to fill a vacancy that arises before the term ends
  • B. The first election ever held in a country
  • C. An election with no candidates
  • D. An election held only for the President

Answer: A. An election held to fill a vacancy that arises before the term ends

Explanation: A by-election is conducted to fill a seat that has fallen vacant due to death, resignation, or disqualification of the sitting member, before the next general election.

24. Which of the following is one of the minimum conditions for an election to be democratic, as per the chapter?

  • A. Everyone must vote for the ruling party
  • B. Almost everyone should be able to choose their own representative
  • C. Only the wealthy should be allowed to vote
  • D. The same person should always win

Answer: B. Almost everyone should be able to choose their own representative

Explanation: A democratic election must offer voters a genuine choice and the ability to elect their own representative freely.

25. What is the role of political parties in the electoral process?

  • A. They have no role in elections
  • B. They put up candidates, prepare manifestos and campaign for votes
  • C. They only count votes after elections
  • D. They replace the Election Commission

Answer: B. They put up candidates, prepare manifestos and campaign for votes

Explanation: Political parties select and field candidates, prepare election manifestos outlining their promises, and campaign to win public support.

26. What is an election 'manifesto'?

  • A. A legal document proving citizenship
  • B. A document in which a party outlines its policies and promises for the election
  • C. A list of polling booths
  • D. A record of election expenses

Answer: B. A document in which a party outlines its policies and promises for the election

Explanation: A manifesto is issued by a political party stating its policy positions, goals and promises to voters ahead of an election.

27. Why does India follow a system of periodic elections rather than a single lifetime term for representatives?

  • A. To allow citizens to regularly evaluate and change their representatives
  • B. Because the Constitution forbids repeated voting
  • C. Because periodic elections are cheaper to skip
  • D. Because there is no fixed term for representatives

Answer: A. To allow citizens to regularly evaluate and change their representatives

Explanation: Regular elections allow citizens to assess the performance of their representatives and decide whether to re-elect or replace them.

28. Which of the following functions does the Election Commission perform before elections are held?

  • A. Preparation of voter lists and announcement of the election schedule
  • B. Selecting who will win the election
  • C. Writing party manifestos
  • D. Deciding government policy

Answer: A. Preparation of voter lists and announcement of the election schedule

Explanation: Before an election, the Election Commission prepares and updates voter lists, announces the schedule, and oversees the entire process from nomination to counting.

29. What happens if a candidate is found to have made a false declaration in the election affidavit?

  • A. Nothing happens to the candidate
  • B. It can be treated as an offence with legal consequences, including disqualification
  • C. The candidate automatically wins
  • D. The election is cancelled for all candidates

Answer: B. It can be treated as an offence with legal consequences, including disqualification

Explanation: Making a false declaration in the mandatory election affidavit can lead to legal action against the candidate, including possible disqualification.

30. What is the overall purpose of holding elections regularly in a democracy?

  • A. To keep only one party in permanent power
  • B. To give the electorate a periodic opportunity to assess and change their government
  • C. To avoid any accountability of leaders
  • D. To make governance impossible

Answer: B. To give the electorate a periodic opportunity to assess and change their government

Explanation: Regular elections are the mechanism through which citizens exercise control over their representatives, ensuring accountability and the possibility of change.

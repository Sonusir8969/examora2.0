NCERT Solutions for Class 12 English Chapter 16 Journey to the End of the Earth

NCERT Solutions for Class 12 English Vistas Chapter 16 Journey to the End of the Earth covers Tishani Doshi's account of her expedition to Antarctica, the theme of climate change and geological history, and important questions and answers.

Overview of Topics & Subtopics (Click to Jump)

  • 1. Chapter Summary
  • 2. Key Observations
  • 3. Theme & Message

1. Chapter Summary

Short Note:

• The author describes her journey to Antarctica as part of an educational expedition, reflecting on the vast, extreme geographical isolation and history of the continent, once part of the ancient supercontinent Gondwana.

• She provides insight into Antarctica's unique environmental significance, particularly its ice cores, which hold valuable historical climate data, allowing scientists to study long-term patterns of global climate change.

• The chapter emphasises the urgent threat posed by global warming and rising sea levels, illustrating how the melting of Antarctica's icy landscape could dramatically impact global ecosystems and human civilisation.

2. Key Observations

Short Note:

• The author highlights the extreme conditions faced by researchers living and working temporarily in Antarctica, describing scientific research stations and the challenges of conducting meaningful research in such isolated and harsh conditions.

• She reflects on humanity's brief history compared to the vast geological timescale represented by Antarctica's ice, prompting reflection about humanity's responsibility towards environmental preservation.

• The chapter also touches upon interesting facts about Antarctica's unique wildlife, such as penguins and various microscopic organisms, emphasising the delicate balance of this fragile ecosystem.

3. Theme & Message

Short Note:

• The chapter's central theme is raising awareness about climate change, emphasising humanity's responsibility to understand, protect and preserve fragile ecosystems like Antarctica.

• It also encourages reflection on humanity's insignificant timescale relative to Earth's geological history, urging greater humility, awareness, and proactive environmental stewardship.

Reading With Insight (Textbook Questions)

Q1: Why does the author consider Antarctica important for studying climate change?
Answer: The author considers Antarctica crucial for studying climate change because its ice cores contain valuable historical data about the Earth's climate over hundreds of thousands of years, allowing scientists to analyse long-term patterns and better understand the causes and impacts of contemporary global warming.
Q2: What does the author mean by describing humans as 'the biggest gluttons of them all'?
Answer: The author uses this phrase to criticise humanity's excessive consumption of natural resources and significant contribution to environmental degradation and climate change, highlighting the disproportionate impact humans have had on the planet's ecosystems compared to other species.
Q3: How does the author's reflection on Gondwana relate to broader environmental themes in the chapter?
Answer: The author's reflection on Gondwana, the ancient supercontinent from which Antarctica separated millions of years ago, emphasises the vast geological timescale of Earth's history, encouraging readers to consider humanity's relatively brief presence and significant recent environmental impact within this much larger, ancient context.

Extract-Based / Short Answer Questions

Q4: What environmental challenges and threats to Antarctica does the author highlight in the chapter?
Answer: The author highlights significant threats posed by global warming, particularly the alarming rate of ice melting, and its potential consequences, including drastic rises in global sea levels, disruption to unique polar ecosystems, and broader worldwide climatic instability.
Q5: What overall message does the chapter convey about humanity's responsibility towards the environment?
Answer: The chapter conveys a strong message about humanity's urgent responsibility to actively address climate change and protect fragile, ecologically significant regions like Antarctica, emphasising the importance of scientific research, global cooperation, and individual awareness in preserving the planet's future.

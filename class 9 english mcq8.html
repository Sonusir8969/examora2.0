Chapter 8: Kathmandu 30 Questions

1. Who is the author of "Kathmandu"?

  • A. Vikram Seth
  • B. Ruskin Bond
  • C. R.K. Narayan
  • D. Anita Desai

Answer: A. Vikram Seth

Explanation: "Kathmandu" is an excerpt from Vikram Seth's travelogue "From Heaven Lake", describing his journey through Nepal.

2. What type of literary work does this chapter belong to?

  • A. A travelogue
  • B. A short story
  • C. A poem
  • D. A biography

Answer: A. A travelogue

Explanation: The chapter is a travel narrative, part of Vikram Seth's account of his overland journey from China to India, passing through Nepal.

3. Which famous temple does the author visit and describe in detail in Kathmandu?

  • A. The Pashupatinath Temple
  • B. The Golden Temple
  • C. The Meenakshi Temple
  • D. The Somnath Temple

Answer: A. The Pashupatinath Temple

Explanation: The author visits the famous Pashupatinath Temple, a major Hindu shrine dedicated to Lord Shiva, located in Kathmandu.

4. What activity does the author observe taking place near the Pashupatinath Temple, along the river?

  • A. Cremation rituals being performed on the banks of the Bagmati River
  • B. A grand wedding ceremony
  • C. A local sports event
  • D. A farmers' market

Answer: A. Cremation rituals being performed on the banks of the Bagmati River

Explanation: The author witnesses funeral pyres and cremation rites being conducted along the sacred Bagmati River near the temple.

5. What restriction did the author, as a non-Hindu, encounter at the Pashupatinath Temple?

  • A. He was not allowed to enter the inner sanctum of the temple
  • B. He was completely barred from the temple complex
  • C. He had to pay an enormous entry fee
  • D. He was forced to convert before entering

Answer: A. He was not allowed to enter the inner sanctum of the temple

Explanation: As a non-Hindu, the author was restricted from entering the innermost sanctuary of the Pashupatinath Temple, a common rule at such shrines.

6. Which famous stupa, an important Buddhist site, does the author describe visiting?

  • A. Bodhnath Stupa
  • B. Sanchi Stupa
  • C. Amaravati Stupa
  • D. Shwedagon Pagoda

Answer: A. Bodhnath Stupa

Explanation: The author visits the Bodhnath Stupa, one of the largest and most significant Buddhist stupas in Kathmandu.

7. What does the author observe about the coexistence of Hinduism and Buddhism in Kathmandu?

  • A. Both religions coexist closely, sharing spaces and even certain traditions
  • B. The two religions are in constant conflict
  • C. Only Hinduism is practised in Kathmandu
  • D. Only Buddhism is practised in Kathmandu

Answer: A. Both religions coexist closely, sharing spaces and even certain traditions

Explanation: The chapter notes the harmonious coexistence of Hinduism and Buddhism in Kathmandu, with shared religious sites and cultural practices.

8. What impression does the author give of the city's markets and streets?

  • A. Vibrant, bustling and full of colourful stalls and diverse crowds
  • B. Empty and deserted
  • C. Strictly organised with no informal trading
  • D. Entirely modern with no traditional elements

Answer: A. Vibrant, bustling and full of colourful stalls and diverse crowds

Explanation: The author paints a lively picture of Kathmandu's markets, filled with vibrant activity, diverse goods, and bustling crowds.

9. What incident occurs when the author visits a bookshop in Kathmandu?

  • A. He has a memorable conversation with the shop's owner about various topics
  • B. He is refused entry into the shop
  • C. He buys nothing at all
  • D. The shop is closed when he arrives

Answer: A. He has a memorable conversation with the shop's owner about various topics

Explanation: The author recounts an engaging and thought-provoking conversation with a bookshop owner during his visit, touching on culture and ideas.

10. What is one significant historical or architectural detail the author mentions about Kathmandu's temples?

  • A. Many temples feature intricately carved wooden and stone architecture reflecting rich craftsmanship
  • B. The temples are entirely modern with glass and steel structures
  • C. The temples have no artistic decoration at all
  • D. The temples were built very recently, within the last decade

Answer: A. Many temples feature intricately carved wooden and stone architecture reflecting rich craftsmanship

Explanation: The author admires the elaborate wood and stone carvings that adorn many of Kathmandu's ancient temples, showcasing exceptional traditional craftsmanship.

11. What is the author's overall tone while describing his experiences in Kathmandu?

  • A. Observant, reflective, and appreciative of the city's culture and history
  • B. Dismissive and uninterested
  • C. Angry and critical throughout
  • D. Entirely humorous with no serious observations

Answer: A. Observant, reflective, and appreciative of the city's culture and history

Explanation: Throughout the chapter, the author maintains a thoughtful, observant tone, showing genuine interest and appreciation for Kathmandu's culture, history and people.

12. What does the author notice about the presence of monkeys near certain temples?

  • A. Monkeys freely roam around temple areas, adding to the lively atmosphere
  • B. Monkeys are strictly kept away from all temples
  • C. There are no animals mentioned near the temples
  • D. Only domesticated animals are seen near temples

Answer: A. Monkeys freely roam around temple areas, adding to the lively atmosphere

Explanation: The author observes monkeys moving about freely near temple premises, a common and characteristic sight at many South Asian religious sites.

13. How does the author describe the religious devotion he witnesses among the people at Pashupatinath?

  • A. As deep, sincere, and an integral part of everyday life
  • B. As superficial and insincere
  • C. As completely absent
  • D. As practised only by a small handful of people

Answer: A. As deep, sincere, and an integral part of everyday life

Explanation: The author is struck by the genuine and deep religious devotion displayed by the people at the temple, reflecting the central role of faith in their daily lives.

14. What subject does the author discuss with the bookshop owner regarding world events or philosophy?

  • A. A range of topics including politics, religion, and general worldviews
  • B. Only the weather
  • C. Only sports news
  • D. Only local gossip

Answer: A. A range of topics including politics, religion, and general worldviews

Explanation: The conversation with the bookshop owner covers a variety of substantial topics, including politics and philosophical perspectives, reflecting an intellectually engaging exchange.

15. What geographical description does the author provide about Kathmandu's location?

  • A. It lies in a valley surrounded by hills, in the country of Nepal
  • B. It is located on a coastal plain
  • C. It is a desert city
  • D. It lies underground

Answer: A. It lies in a valley surrounded by hills, in the country of Nepal

Explanation: Kathmandu is described as being situated in a valley amidst hills, characteristic of its location within Nepal's mountainous geography.

16. What does the author's account suggest about the blending of the sacred and the everyday in Kathmandu?

  • A. Religious practices are deeply woven into the daily life and public spaces of the city
  • B. Religion is kept strictly separate from public life
  • C. There is no visible religious practice in the city at all
  • D. Religious sites are located far outside the city

Answer: A. Religious practices are deeply woven into the daily life and public spaces of the city

Explanation: The chapter illustrates how religious life in Kathmandu is intricately blended with everyday activities, evident in its temples, rituals and public spaces.

17. What sensory details does the author use to bring the scene at the cremation grounds to life?

  • A. Descriptions of smoke, chanting, and the solemn atmosphere along the riverbank
  • B. Descriptions of bright colours and festive music only
  • C. Complete silence with no sensory detail
  • D. Descriptions of a clean and empty riverbank

Answer: A. Descriptions of smoke, chanting, and the solemn atmosphere along the riverbank

Explanation: The author vividly captures the solemn, sensory atmosphere of the cremation grounds, including the sight of smoke and sounds of ritual chanting.

18. What does the author's visit to Kathmandu reveal about his interest as a traveller?

  • A. A deep curiosity about different cultures, religions, and everyday human life
  • B. An interest only in modern architecture
  • C. A complete disinterest in local customs
  • D. An interest solely in shopping and commerce

Answer: A. A deep curiosity about different cultures, religions, and everyday human life

Explanation: Throughout his travelogue, Vikram Seth demonstrates a genuine curiosity about the cultural, religious and everyday aspects of the places he visits, including Kathmandu.

19. How does the chapter portray the relationship between Nepal's culture and its neighboring Indian culture?

  • A. As closely intertwined, sharing many religious and cultural traditions
  • B. As entirely separate with no shared elements
  • C. As openly hostile towards one another
  • D. As unrelated and unknown to each other

Answer: A. As closely intertwined, sharing many religious and cultural traditions

Explanation: The chapter highlights the close cultural and religious ties between Nepal and India, evident in shared Hindu traditions and temple practices.

20. What does the presence of both Hindu and Buddhist pilgrims in Kathmandu suggest about the city?

  • A. That it serves as an important spiritual centre for multiple faiths
  • B. That it is exclusively significant to one religion only
  • C. That religious tourism does not exist there
  • D. That pilgrims avoid the city entirely

Answer: A. That it serves as an important spiritual centre for multiple faiths

Explanation: The presence of significant Hindu and Buddhist sites, both drawing pilgrims, underscores Kathmandu's importance as a multi-faith spiritual hub.

21. What kind of shops or goods does the author mention seeing in Kathmandu's markets?

  • A. A variety including religious items, crafts, books, and everyday goods
  • B. Only electronic gadgets
  • C. Only luxury imported items
  • D. No shops are mentioned in the chapter

Answer: A. A variety including religious items, crafts, books, and everyday goods

Explanation: The author describes a diverse array of goods available in Kathmandu's markets, ranging from religious artefacts to books and daily necessities.

22. What can be inferred about the author's mode of travel from the broader context of the book "From Heaven Lake"?

  • A. He was undertaking an extensive overland journey through Tibet, Nepal, and back to India
  • B. He travelled exclusively by air throughout his journey
  • C. He never left his home country during this trip
  • D. He travelled purely for business purposes

Answer: A. He was undertaking an extensive overland journey through Tibet, Nepal, and back to India

Explanation: The chapter is part of a larger travelogue documenting the author's overland journey from China through Tibet and Nepal, eventually returning to India.

23. What emotion does the author seem to feel while observing the funeral rites at the temple?

  • A. A mix of solemnity and reflective contemplation on life and death
  • B. Complete indifference
  • C. Fear and panic
  • D. Amusement and laughter

Answer: A. A mix of solemnity and reflective contemplation on life and death

Explanation: Witnessing the cremation rituals prompts a reflective and solemn response from the author, as he contemplates themes of mortality and tradition.

24. What does the chapter suggest about the pace and rhythm of daily life in Kathmandu?

  • A. A blend of traditional rhythms with the bustle of everyday commerce and religious practice
  • B. An entirely fast-paced, modern city life with no traditional elements
  • C. A completely static, unchanging environment
  • D. A city where daily life has come to a standstill

Answer: A. A blend of traditional rhythms with the bustle of everyday commerce and religious practice

Explanation: The chapter presents Kathmandu as a city where traditional rhythms of religious life coexist with the everyday bustle of markets and commerce.

25. What overall impression of Nepal's cultural richness does the chapter convey to readers?

  • A. A vivid picture of a culturally rich and spiritually significant destination
  • B. A depiction of a culturally barren location
  • C. A portrayal of Nepal as entirely modernised with no traditions
  • D. A neutral, factual account with no cultural observations

Answer: A. A vivid picture of a culturally rich and spiritually significant destination

Explanation: Through detailed observations of temples, rituals and daily life, the chapter presents Kathmandu as a vibrant centre of cultural and spiritual heritage.

26. What literary technique does the author primarily use to convey his experiences in Kathmandu?

  • A. Detailed descriptive narration based on personal observation
  • B. Dialogue-heavy fictional storytelling
  • C. Purely statistical reporting
  • D. Poetic verse throughout

Answer: A. Detailed descriptive narration based on personal observation

Explanation: As a travelogue, the chapter relies on vivid, descriptive prose drawn from the author's own first-hand observations and experiences in Kathmandu.

27. What does the interaction with the bookshop owner reveal about the people the author meets during his travels?

  • A. That locals often engage thoughtfully with travellers, sharing diverse perspectives
  • B. That locals are uninterested in engaging with visitors
  • C. That conversations with locals are always superficial
  • D. That the bookshop owner refused to speak with him

Answer: A. That locals often engage thoughtfully with travellers, sharing diverse perspectives

Explanation: The engaging conversation with the bookshop owner reflects the author's experience of meaningful, thoughtful interactions with local people during his travels.

28. Why might the chapter be considered valuable for readers interested in cultural and religious diversity?

  • A. It offers a first-hand, respectful glimpse into the coexistence of different faiths and traditions in one city
  • B. It dismisses the importance of cultural diversity
  • C. It focuses solely on one religion, ignoring others
  • D. It avoids any discussion of religion or culture

Answer: A. It offers a first-hand, respectful glimpse into the coexistence of different faiths and traditions in one city

Explanation: The chapter provides valuable insight into how multiple religious traditions can coexist harmoniously within a single city, enriching readers' understanding of cultural diversity.

29. What does the author's careful attention to architectural and ritual detail suggest about his approach as a travel writer?

  • A. He values thorough observation and thoughtful reflection over superficial description
  • B. He is interested only in brief, general impressions
  • C. He avoids all specific details in his writing
  • D. He focuses exclusively on personal opinions with no factual detail

Answer: A. He values thorough observation and thoughtful reflection over superficial description

Explanation: The detailed and reflective nature of the author's descriptions demonstrates his careful, observant approach as a travel writer, prioritising depth over superficiality.

30. What is the overall theme conveyed by the chapter "Kathmandu"?

  • A. The rich blending of spirituality, culture, and everyday life in a historic city
  • B. The dangers of international travel
  • C. The decline of traditional culture in modern cities
  • D. The economic development of Nepal

Answer: A. The rich blending of spirituality, culture, and everyday life in a historic city

Explanation: The chapter's central theme captures how spirituality, cultural heritage, and everyday life are deeply intertwined in the historic city of Kathmandu.

NCERT Solutions for Class 12 English Chapter 12 A Roadside Stand

NCERT Solutions for Class 12 English Flamingo Chapter 12 A Roadside Stand by Robert Frost covers the theme of rural poverty and the indifference of wealthy urban society, poetic devices, and important questions and answers.

1. About the Poet and Poem

Short Note:

• Robert Frost's poem depicts a small roadside stand set up by poor rural people alongside a busy highway, hoping to earn a modest income by selling local produce and goods to passing city travellers.

• Despite their hopeful efforts, the wealthy travellers speeding by in their cars largely ignore the roadside stand, showing little interest or sympathy for the impoverished rural sellers, highlighting the vast gap between rural poverty and urban indifference.

• The poem also critiques government and societal promises of rural development and progress, suggesting these promises are often hollow, leaving rural communities further marginalised and disillusioned.

2. Theme & Central Idea

Short Note:

• The poem's central theme is the stark socio-economic divide between rural and urban communities, highlighting how rural poverty often goes unnoticed or is dismissed by wealthier, urban populations absorbed in their own concerns.

• It criticises the false hopes and promises made by governments regarding rural development and progress, showing how these promises frequently fail to translate into meaningful, tangible improvements for struggling rural communities.

• The poem also touches upon the theme of human dignity and desperation, revealing the frustration and hopelessness experienced by rural people who are largely overlooked and undervalued by mainstream society.

3. Poetic Devices

Short Note:

• Irony: The poem employs irony, particularly regarding government promises of progress and development that fail to materialise, thus deepening rural despair rather than alleviating it.

• Imagery: Vivid imagery of speeding cars and reluctant, occasional customers stopping briefly at the roadside stand starkly illustrates the indifference of urban travellers towards rural poverty.

• Tone: The poem's tone shifts between gentle sympathy for the rural poor and sharp criticism of societal neglect and false promises, effectively conveying the poet's social commentary.

Reading With Insight (Textbook Questions)

Q1: Why do the rural people set up a roadside stand, and what do they hope to achieve?
Answer: The rural people set up the roadside stand hoping to earn some additional income by selling local produce and handmade goods to travellers passing by on the highway, seeking a small share of the economic prosperity enjoyed by wealthier city dwellers who frequently pass their homes.
Q2: How do the city travellers react to the roadside stand, and what does this reveal about societal attitudes?
Answer: Most city travellers largely ignore the roadside stand, speeding past without stopping, and those who do stop often show little genuine interest, sometimes only stopping to turn their vehicles around; this reaction reveals a broader societal indifference and lack of empathy from wealthier urban populations towards the economic struggles of rural communities.
Q3: What criticism does the poem offer regarding governmental promises of rural development?
Answer: The poem criticises governmental promises of rural development and progress as largely empty and ineffective, suggesting that such promises often fail to materialise into real, tangible benefits for rural communities, leaving them further disillusioned, marginalised, and neglected despite official assurances of improvement.

Extract-Based / Short Answer Questions

Q4: What does the poet's deep sympathy for the rural poor reveal about his own perspective in the poem?
Answer: The poet's deep, evident sympathy for the struggles and disappointments of the rural poor reflects his critical perspective on the social and economic inequalities of his time, revealing his concern about the neglect and marginalisation experienced by rural communities amid rapid urban development and prosperity.
Q5: What is the significance of the poem's closing lines, expressing a wish to end rural suffering?
Answer: The closing lines, expressing a poignant desire to relieve rural suffering, even suggesting that death might be preferable to prolonged suffering, highlight the poet's profound empathy and frustration regarding the systemic neglect of rural poverty, emphasising the urgent need for genuine societal and governmental attention to address these deep-rooted inequalities.

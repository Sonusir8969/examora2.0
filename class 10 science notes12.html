NCERT Solutions for Class 10 Science Chapter 12 Magnetic Effects of Electric Current

NCERT Solutions for Class 10 Science Chapter 12 Magnetic Effects of Electric Current covers magnetic field lines, magnetic field due to current-carrying conductors, solenoid properties, Fleming's Left-Hand Rule, force on current conductors, and domestic circuit safety devices.

1. Magnetic Field and Field Lines

Short Note:

• Magnetic Field: The region surrounding a magnet where its magnetic force can be experienced by other magnets or magnetic substances.

• Properties of Field Lines:

  1. Emerge from the North pole and enter at the South pole outside a magnet; travel South to North inside.

  2. Field lines form continuous, closed loops.

  3. Degree of closeness of lines indicates strength of the magnetic field.

  4. Two magnetic field lines never intersect because a compass needle cannot point in two different directions at one point.

2. Field due to a Current-Carrying Conductor & Solenoid

Short Note:

• Right-Hand Thumb Rule: Imagine holding a current-carrying conductor in your right hand with the thumb pointing in the direction of current; your curled fingers point in the direction of the magnetic field lines.

• Straight Wire: Magnetic field lines are concentric circles around the wire.

• Solenoid: A long coil of many circular turns of insulated copper wire. The magnetic field inside a uniform solenoid is uniform and resembles that of a bar magnet.

• Electromagnet: Formed when a soft iron core is placed inside a current-carrying solenoid.

3. Force on a Conductor in a Magnetic Field & Fleming's Left-Hand Rule

Short Note:

• A current-carrying conductor experiences a mechanical force when placed in a magnetic field.

• The force is maximum when the direction of current is perpendicular (at 90 degrees) to the magnetic field.

• Fleming's Left-Hand Rule: Stretch the thumb, forefinger, and middle finger of your left hand mutually perpendicular to each other:

  - Forefinger: Magnetic Field

  - Middle Finger: Electric Current

  - Thumb: Direction of Motion / Force on the conductor.

4. Domestic Electric Circuits & Safety Features

Short Note:

• Wires in Supply: Live Wire (Red insulation, 220 V), Neutral Wire (Black insulation, 0 V), Earth Wire (Green insulation, safety grounding).

• Earth Wire: Provides a low-resistance conducting path for leakage currents, protecting users from severe electric shocks.

• Short Circuiting: Occurs when live and neutral wires come in direct contact due to damaged insulation, causing an abrupt increase in current.

• Overloading: Occurs when too many electrical appliances are connected to a single socket simultaneously.

• Electric Fuse: A safety device made of a low melting point alloy that melts and breaks the circuit during high current surges.

Intext Questions - Page Number: 224

Q1: Why does a compass needle get deflected when brought near a bar magnet?
Answer: A compass needle is a small bar magnet. When brought near another bar magnet, the magnetic field of the bar magnet exerts a magnetic force on the poles of the compass needle, causing it to deflect.

Intext Questions - Page Number: 228

Q1: Draw magnetic field lines around a bar magnet.
Answer: Continuous closed loops emerging from the North pole (N) and entering the South pole (S) externally, while moving from South (S) to North (N) internally. The field lines are crowded near the poles where the magnetic field is strongest.
Q2: List the properties of magnetic lines of force.
Answer:

1. They originate at the North pole and terminate at the South pole outside the magnet.

2. They are continuous closed curves.

3. Closer field lines indicate a stronger magnetic field.

4. No two magnetic field lines ever cross or intersect each other.

Q3: Why don't two magnetic lines of force intersect each other?
Answer: If two field lines crossed each other, it would mean that at the point of intersection, a magnetic compass needle would point in two different directions simultaneously, which is physically impossible.

Intext Questions - Page Number: 231

Q1: State Fleming’s left-hand rule.
Answer: According to Fleming’s Left-Hand Rule, stretch the thumb, forefinger, and middle finger of your left hand mutually perpendicular to each other. If the forefinger points in the direction of the magnetic field and the middle finger in the direction of current, then the thumb points in the direction of motion or force acting on the conductor.

Textbook Chapter End Exercise Questions

Q1: Which of the following correctly describes the magnetic field near a long straight wire?

(a) The field consists of straight lines perpendicular to the wire.
(b) The field consists of straight lines parallel to the wire.
(c) The field consists of radial lines originating from the wire.
(d) The field consists of concentric circles centred on the wire.

Correct Answer: (d) The field consists of concentric circles centred on the wire.

Q2: The phenomenon of electromagnetic induction is:

(a) the process of charging a body.
(b) the process of generating magnetic field due to a current passing through a coil.
(c) producing induced current in a coil due to relative motion between a magnet and the coil.
(d) the process of rotating a coil of an electric motor.

Correct Answer: (c) producing induced current in a coil due to relative motion between a magnet and the coil.

Q3: What is the function of an earth wire? Why is it necessary to earth metallic appliances?
Answer: The earth wire connects the metallic casing of an appliance to a metal plate buried deep inside the Earth. If electric current leaks to the metallic body due to faulty insulation, the earth wire provides a zero-resistance path for the current to flow into the earth. This prevents the potential of the appliance body from rising and protects the user from severe electric shocks.

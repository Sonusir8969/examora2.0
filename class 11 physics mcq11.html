Ch-11: Thermal Properties of Matter 30 Questions

1. The SI unit of temperature is

  • A. Kelvin
  • B. Celsius
  • C. Fahrenheit
  • D. Joule

Answer: A. Kelvin

Explanation: Kelvin is the SI base unit of thermodynamic temperature, with 0 K representing absolute zero.

2. The relationship between Celsius and Kelvin scale is

  • A. T(K) = t(°C) + 273.15
  • B. T(K) = t(°C) - 273.15
  • C. T(K) = t(°C) × 273.15
  • D. T(K) = t(°C)/273.15

Answer: A. T(K) = t(°C) + 273.15

Explanation: The Kelvin scale is obtained by adding 273.15 to the Celsius temperature, since 0°C corresponds to 273.15 K.

3. The increase in length per unit original length per degree rise in temperature is called

  • A. Coefficient of linear expansion
  • B. Coefficient of volume expansion
  • C. Coefficient of superficial expansion
  • D. Thermal conductivity

Answer: A. Coefficient of linear expansion

Explanation: The coefficient of linear expansion (α) is defined as the fractional change in length per unit rise in temperature.

4. For an ideal gas, the relation between coefficients of linear (α), superficial (β), and cubical (γ) expansion is approximately

  • A. α : β : γ = 1 : 2 : 3
  • B. α : β : γ = 1 : 3 : 2
  • C. α : β : γ = 3 : 2 : 1
  • D. α : β : γ = 2 : 1 : 3

Answer: A. α : β : γ = 1 : 2 : 3

Explanation: The three coefficients of expansion are related by α : β : γ = 1 : 2 : 3, i.e., β = 2α and γ = 3α.

5. Water shows anomalous expansion between

  • A. 0°C and 4°C
  • B. 4°C and 100°C
  • C. -4°C and 0°C
  • D. 4°C and 10°C

Answer: A. 0°C and 4°C

Explanation: Water shows anomalous behavior between 0°C and 4°C, contracting instead of expanding as temperature rises, with maximum density at 4°C.

6. The amount of heat required to raise the temperature of a unit mass of a substance by one degree is called

  • A. Specific heat capacity
  • B. Latent heat
  • C. Thermal capacity
  • D. Molar heat capacity

Answer: A. Specific heat capacity

Explanation: Specific heat capacity is defined as the heat required to raise the temperature of a unit mass of a substance by one degree Celsius (or Kelvin).

7. The heat required to change the state of a unit mass of a substance without changing its temperature is called

  • A. Latent heat
  • B. Specific heat
  • C. Sensible heat
  • D. Radiant heat

Answer: A. Latent heat

Explanation: Latent heat is the heat absorbed or released during a change of state (like melting or boiling) at constant temperature.

8. The specific heat capacity of water is

  • A. 4186 J/kg·K
  • B. 2100 J/kg·K
  • C. 1000 J/kg·K
  • D. 500 J/kg·K

Answer: A. 4186 J/kg·K

Explanation: Water has a high specific heat capacity of about 4186 J/kg·K, which is why it heats up and cools down slowly compared to many other substances.

9. Heat transfer through a solid medium without actual movement of particles is called

  • A. Conduction
  • B. Convection
  • C. Radiation
  • D. Diffusion

Answer: A. Conduction

Explanation: Conduction is the mode of heat transfer through a medium in which heat travels from particle to particle without any actual movement of the particles.

10. Heat transfer through fluids by actual movement of particles is called

  • A. Convection
  • B. Conduction
  • C. Radiation
  • D. Insulation

Answer: A. Convection

Explanation: In convection, heat is transferred through a fluid by the actual bulk movement of heated particles from one place to another.

11. Heat transfer that does not require a medium is called

  • A. Radiation
  • B. Conduction
  • C. Convection
  • D. Conduction and convection both

Answer: A. Radiation

Explanation: Radiation is the transfer of heat through electromagnetic waves and can occur even through vacuum, unlike conduction and convection.

12. According to Stefan-Boltzmann law, the energy radiated per unit time by a black body is proportional to

  • A. The fourth power of its absolute temperature
  • B. The square of its absolute temperature
  • C. Its absolute temperature directly
  • D. The square root of its absolute temperature

Answer: A. The fourth power of its absolute temperature

Explanation: The Stefan-Boltzmann law states that the total radiant energy emitted per unit time by a black body is proportional to T⁴, where T is the absolute temperature.

13. Wien's displacement law relates the wavelength of maximum emission of a black body to its

  • A. Absolute temperature (inversely)
  • B. Absolute temperature (directly)
  • C. Surface area
  • D. Emissivity only

Answer: A. Absolute temperature (inversely)

Explanation: Wien's displacement law states that the wavelength at which a black body's emission is maximum is inversely proportional to its absolute temperature (λ_m T = constant).

14. A perfect black body is one that

  • A. Absorbs all radiation incident on it
  • B. Reflects all radiation incident on it
  • C. Transmits all radiation incident on it
  • D. Neither absorbs nor emits radiation

Answer: A. Absorbs all radiation incident on it

Explanation: A perfect black body absorbs all the radiant energy falling on it, regardless of wavelength, and is also the best possible emitter of radiation.

15. Newton's law of cooling states that the rate of loss of heat of a body is proportional to

  • A. The temperature difference between the body and its surroundings
  • B. The square of the temperature difference
  • C. The absolute temperature of the body only
  • D. The mass of the body

Answer: A. The temperature difference between the body and its surroundings

Explanation: Newton's law of cooling states that the rate of loss of heat is directly proportional to the temperature difference between the body and its surroundings, valid for small temperature differences.

16. Which mode of heat transfer allows the Sun's energy to reach the Earth?

  • A. Radiation
  • B. Conduction
  • C. Convection
  • D. Diffusion

Answer: A. Radiation

Explanation: Since space between the Sun and Earth is largely a vacuum, energy travels only by radiation, which does not need a material medium.

17. Land and sea breezes are examples of heat transfer by

  • A. Convection
  • B. Conduction
  • C. Radiation
  • D. Evaporation only

Answer: A. Convection

Explanation: Land and sea breezes arise from convection currents caused by unequal heating of land and sea, which changes air density and drives circulation.

18. The triple point of water on the Kelvin scale is

  • A. 273.16 K
  • B. 273.15 K
  • C. 373.16 K
  • D. 0 K

Answer: A. 273.16 K

Explanation: The triple point of water, where ice, water, and vapour coexist in equilibrium, is defined as exactly 273.16 K.

19. A bimetallic strip bends on heating because the two metals have

  • A. Different coefficients of linear expansion
  • B. The same coefficient of linear expansion
  • C. Different specific heat capacities only
  • D. Different melting points only

Answer: A. Different coefficients of linear expansion

Explanation: A bimetallic strip bends when heated because the two bonded metals expand by different amounts due to differing coefficients of linear expansion, used in thermostats.

20. Which of these correctly ranks thermal conductivity from highest to lowest?

  • A. Metals > liquids > gases
  • B. Gases > liquids > metals
  • C. Liquids > metals > gases
  • D. Gases > metals > liquids

Answer: A. Metals > liquids > gases

Explanation: Metals are the best conductors of heat due to free electrons, followed by liquids, while gases are generally the poorest conductors.

21. The process of change from liquid to vapour state at any temperature below the boiling point is called

  • A. Evaporation
  • B. Boiling
  • C. Condensation
  • D. Sublimation

Answer: A. Evaporation

Explanation: Evaporation is a surface phenomenon in which molecules escape from a liquid's surface at temperatures below its boiling point.

22. Evaporation causes cooling because

  • A. The escaping molecules carry away kinetic energy, lowering the average energy of the remaining liquid
  • B. It increases the pressure of the surroundings
  • C. It decreases the surface tension of the liquid
  • D. It increases the density of the liquid

Answer: A. The escaping molecules carry away kinetic energy, lowering the average energy of the remaining liquid

Explanation: The fastest-moving, high-energy molecules escape during evaporation, reducing the average kinetic energy (and hence temperature) of the liquid left behind.

23. Direct conversion of a solid into vapour without passing through the liquid state is called

  • A. Sublimation
  • B. Fusion
  • C. Vaporization
  • D. Condensation

Answer: A. Sublimation

Explanation: Sublimation is the direct transition of a substance from the solid to the gaseous state, as seen in substances like camphor or dry ice.

24. During melting of ice at 0°C, the temperature of the ice-water mixture

  • A. Remains constant until all ice melts
  • B. Rises continuously
  • C. Falls continuously
  • D. Rises then falls

Answer: A. Remains constant until all ice melts

Explanation: During a change of state like melting, all the heat supplied goes into breaking intermolecular bonds (latent heat), so temperature stays constant until the process is complete.

25. Calorimetry is the technique used to measure

  • A. Heat exchanged between bodies
  • B. Pressure of a gas
  • C. Coefficient of expansion
  • D. Density of a liquid

Answer: A. Heat exchanged between bodies

Explanation: Calorimetry is the measurement of heat transfer, based on the principle that heat lost by a hotter body equals heat gained by a colder body in an isolated system.

26. Why is a car radiator often painted black?

  • A. Black surfaces are better emitters and absorbers of radiant heat
  • B. Black paint is cheaper
  • C. It reduces conduction of heat
  • D. It has no relation to heat transfer

Answer: A. Black surfaces are better emitters and absorbers of radiant heat

Explanation: Black surfaces have high emissivity, so painting parts like radiators black helps them radiate heat away more efficiently.

27. Woollen clothes keep us warm in winter mainly because

  • A. Air trapped in wool is a poor conductor of heat
  • B. Wool generates heat by itself
  • C. Wool has very high thermal conductivity
  • D. Wool absorbs cold from the body

Answer: A. Air trapped in wool is a poor conductor of heat

Explanation: Wool traps air in its fibers, and since air is a poor conductor, it slows the loss of body heat, keeping us warm.

28. Two bodies at different temperatures placed in contact will eventually reach

  • A. Thermal equilibrium at a common temperature
  • B. The same specific heat
  • C. The same mass
  • D. Zero temperature

Answer: A. Thermal equilibrium at a common temperature

Explanation: When two bodies at different temperatures are in contact, heat flows from the hotter to the colder body until both reach the same temperature, called thermal equilibrium.

29. Why do railway tracks have small gaps left between rail segments?

  • A. To allow for thermal expansion of the rails in summer
  • B. To reduce the weight of the tracks
  • C. To allow water drainage
  • D. To make the tracks cheaper to manufacture

Answer: A. To allow for thermal expansion of the rails in summer

Explanation: Small gaps are left between rail segments to accommodate linear expansion of the metal in hot weather, preventing the tracks from buckling.

30. On a cold morning, a metal door handle feels colder to touch than a wooden door, though both are at the same temperature, because

  • A. Metal has higher thermal conductivity and conducts heat away from the hand faster
  • B. Metal is actually at a lower temperature than wood
  • C. Wood absorbs heat from the surroundings
  • D. Metal has a higher specific heat capacity than wood

Answer: A. Metal has higher thermal conductivity and conducts heat away from the hand faster

Explanation: Even at the same temperature, metal feels colder because its high thermal conductivity draws heat rapidly away from the warmer hand, while wood, a poor conductor, does not.

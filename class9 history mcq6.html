Chapter 6: Peasants and Farmers 30 Questions

1. What was the 'Enclosure Movement' in England?

  • A. Fencing of public lands for private use
  • B. Opening up forests
  • C. Building farmhouses
  • D. Forming farmer unions

Answer: A. Fencing of public lands for private use

Explanation: The Enclosure Movement involved fencing off common village lands to turn them into large private farms for wealthy landowners.

2. Who invented the 'Threshing Machine' in England?

  • A. Jethro Tull
  • B. Andrew Meikle
  • C. James Watt
  • D. Thomas Newcomen

Answer: B. Andrew Meikle

Explanation: Scottish engineer Andrew Meikle successfully developed the steam-powered threshing machine in the late 18th century.

3. How did the Enclosure Movement impact the small British farmers?

  • A. They became richer
  • B. They were displaced and became landless laborers
  • C. They got bigger lands
  • D. They started renting tractors

Answer: B. They were displaced and became landless laborers

Explanation: The common lands that small farmers depended on for grazing were fenced off, forcing them to sell their land and become laborers.

4. Which crop became the backbone of the Irish economy before the famine?

  • A. Wheat
  • B. Rice
  • C. Potato
  • D. Barley

Answer: C. Potato

Explanation: The Irish peasantry heavily depended on the potato as their primary food source due to its high yield and nutritional value.

5. When did the Great Irish Potato Famine occur?

  • A. 1840s
  • B. 1860s
  • C. 1880s
  • D. 1900s

Answer: A. 1840s

Explanation: The Great Irish Potato Famine, caused by a widespread potato blight, devastated Ireland between 1845 and 1849.

6. What caused the Irish Potato Famine?

  • A. Drought
  • B. Potato Blight (fungus)
  • C. Floods
  • D. Locusts

Answer: B. Potato Blight (fungus)

Explanation: A fungal disease called potato blight destroyed the potato crops across Europe, causing catastrophic famine in Ireland.

7. What was the result of the Irish Potato Famine?

  • A. Mass migration and death
  • B. Economic boom
  • C. Industrial revolution
  • D. Higher production

Answer: A. Mass migration and death

Explanation: The famine resulted in the deaths of over a million people and triggered a massive wave of Irish emigration to the USA.

8. Which invention transformed wheat cultivation in the USA in the 19th century?

  • A. Cotton gin
  • B. Mechanical reaper
  • C. Spinning jenny
  • D. Steam engine

Answer: B. Mechanical reaper

Explanation: The mechanical reaper, invented by Cyrus McCormick, greatly increased the efficiency and scale of wheat harvesting in America.

9. The 'Homestead Act' of 1862 was passed in which country?

  • A. England
  • B. France
  • C. USA
  • D. Germany

Answer: C. USA

Explanation: The Homestead Act was passed in the USA to encourage westward expansion by granting free land to settlers who agreed to farm it.

10. How did the expansion of railways help the farmers in the USA?

  • A. By providing luxury travel
  • B. By transporting agricultural produce to markets
  • C. By building factories
  • D. By creating jobs in cities

Answer: B. By transporting agricultural produce to markets

Explanation: The expansion of the railroad network allowed US farmers to easily transport their surplus wheat and corn to distant urban markets.

11. Why did the British farmers resist the threshing machine in England?

  • A. It was too slow
  • B. It took away their manual jobs
  • C. It was too expensive
  • D. It damaged the crops

Answer: B. It took away their manual jobs

Explanation: Agricultural laborers feared the threshing machine would replace manual labor, leading to widespread job losses and protests.

12. What was the role of the 'Lord' in the English agricultural system?

  • A. A humble farmer
  • B. An aristocratic landowner
  • C. A village priest
  • D. A factory owner

Answer: B. An aristocratic landowner

Explanation: The Lords were the wealthy aristocratic landowners who controlled the vast estates and common lands in England.

13. What were the 'Open Fields' in the traditional English system?

  • A. Privately owned commercial farms
  • B. Community-shared farming lands
  • C. Barren lands
  • D. State-owned forests

Answer: B. Community-shared farming lands

Explanation: Open fields were large communal plots where villagers farmed strips of land collectively, a common practice before the Enclosure Movement.

14. Who passed the Enclosure Acts in the 18th century England?

  • A. The British Parliament
  • B. The King
  • C. The Church
  • D. The Farmers

Answer: A. The British Parliament

Explanation: The British Parliament passed numerous private Enclosure Acts in the 18th century, authorizing wealthy landowners to fence off common lands.

15. What economic system replaced feudalism in the British countryside?

  • A. Socialism
  • B. Capitalist agriculture
  • C. Communism
  • D. Mercantilism

Answer: B. Capitalist agriculture

Explanation: The introduction of enclosures and machines transformed England into a system of capitalist agriculture based on private ownership and profit.

16. Which country was the largest wheat exporter by the late 19th century?

  • A. England
  • B. France
  • C. USA
  • D. India

Answer: C. USA

Explanation: By the late 1800s, the USA had become the world's largest exporter of wheat, fueled by its vast plains and agricultural machinery.

17. What was the 'Crop Rotation' method introduced in England?

  • A. Rotating crops yearly to maintain fertility
  • B. Planting the same crop everywhere
  • C. Abandoning crops entirely
  • D. Using chemical fertilizers

Answer: A. Rotating crops yearly to maintain fertility

Explanation: Crop rotation was the practice of alternating different crops (like wheat, turnips, and clover) to replenish soil nutrients naturally.

18. How did the Enclosure Movement affect the poor peasant children?

  • A. They attended more schools
  • B. They were forced into factory work
  • C. They became landowners
  • D. They moved to the countryside

Answer: B. They were forced into factory work

Explanation: Displaced by enclosures, poor peasant families moved to cities, and their children were forced into grueling labor in factories.

19. Who led the Swing Riots in England against the threshing machines?

  • A. Rural farm laborers
  • B. Wealthy landlords
  • C. Industrial workers
  • D. Soldiers

Answer: A. Rural farm laborers

Explanation: Disgruntled agricultural laborers, known as 'Swing' rioters, smashed threshing machines and set fire to farm buildings in 1830s England.

20. What was the 'British Agricultural Revolution' characterized by?

  • A. Increased use of machines and new techniques
  • B. Abandoning farming
  • C. Importing all food
  • D. Moving to cities

Answer: A. Increased use of machines and new techniques

Explanation: The revolution saw massive improvements in agriculture through the adoption of new machinery, crop rotation, and selective breeding.

21. The farmers of USA were known for cultivating huge tracts of what?

  • A. Rice
  • B. Wheat
  • C. Coffee
  • D. Tobacco

Answer: B. Wheat

Explanation: US farmers (homesteaders) cultivated massive fields of wheat on the Great Plains to meet global and domestic demand.

22. What technological innovation allowed US farmers to cultivate plains previously thought barren?

  • A. The plow
  • B. Steel ploughs and barbed wire
  • C. Tractors
  • D. Combines

Answer: B. Steel ploughs and barbed wire

Explanation: The heavy steel ploughs broke the tough prairie soil, while barbed wire allowed farmers to fence their lands efficiently.

23. Who were the main victims of the Enclosure Movement?

  • A. Tenant farmers and poor peasants
  • B. The church
  • C. The nobility
  • D. The British monarch

Answer: A. Tenant farmers and poor peasants

Explanation: The poor tenant farmers and peasants lost their grazing and farming rights, forcing them into poverty or urban labor.

24. The seed drill was invented by whom?

  • A. Jethro Tull
  • B. James Watt
  • C. Andrew Meikle
  • D. George Stephenson

Answer: A. Jethro Tull

Explanation: Jethro Tull, an English agricultural pioneer, invented the seed drill in the early 1700s to sow seeds in neat rows.

25. The movement of English farmers to urban areas was primarily driven by what?

  • A. Better wages in factories
  • B. Loss of agricultural land
  • C. New universities
  • D. Coastal living

Answer: B. Loss of agricultural land

Explanation: The enclosure movement made countless farmers landless, driving them to abandon rural life and seek employment in industrial cities.

26. Which law provided land to Americans moving west in 1862?

  • A. The Land Grant Act
  • B. The Homestead Act
  • C. The Westward Act
  • D. The Settler Act

Answer: B. The Homestead Act

Explanation: The Homestead Act of 1862 provided settlers with 160 acres of public land if they lived on and improved it for five years.

27. What did the Irish migrate to the USA for?

  • A. To escape the Potato Famine
  • B. To find gold
  • C. To join the military
  • D. To start plantations

Answer: A. To escape the Potato Famine

Explanation: Millions of Irish people fled to America during the 1840s to escape starvation and poverty caused by the devastating potato blight.

28. What is the name of the movement that revolted against the Threshing Machine in England?

  • A. The Luddite Movement
  • B. The Swing Riots
  • C. The Chartist Movement
  • D. The Reform Movement

Answer: B. The Swing Riots

Explanation: The Swing Riots of 1830 were a series of violent protests by rural workers in southern England against agricultural mechanization.

29. What was the impact of agricultural machinery on food prices?

  • A. Increased food prices
  • B. Decreased food prices
  • C. Food became available only to the rich
  • D. Prices remained unchanged

Answer: B. Decreased food prices

Explanation: Large-scale production using machinery led to a massive surplus of crops, which significantly reduced the market prices of food.

30. Which country's agricultural output was most deeply affected by the introduction of machinery in the 19th century?

  • A. Russia
  • B. USA and England
  • C. Japan
  • D. Italy

Answer: B. USA and England

Explanation: The USA and England led the 19th-century Agricultural Revolution, greatly boosting their outputs through mechanization and new farming methods.

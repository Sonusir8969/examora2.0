NCERT Solutions for Class 11 Chemistry Chapter 3 Classification of Elements & Periodicity in Properties

NCERT Solutions for Class 11 Chemistry Chapter 3 Classification of Elements and Periodicity in Properties covers historical development of periodic tables, Modern Periodic Law, division of elements into s, p, d, and f blocks, IUPAC nomenclature for elements with Z > 100, and comprehensive periodic trends in physical and chemical properties including atomic radius, ionic radius, ionization enthalpy, electron gain enthalpy, electronegativity, and chemical reactivity.

1. Historical Development & Modern Periodic Law

Short Note:

• Dobereiner's Triads & Newlands' Law of Octaves: Early attempts to classify elements based on atomic masses.

• Mendeleev's Periodic Law: Properties of elements are a periodic function of their atomic masses. Arranged elements in periods and groups based on similarities in formula of hydrides and oxides.

• Modern Periodic Law (Henry Moseley): Physical and chemical properties of the elements are periodic functions of their atomic numbers (Z).

• Long Form of Periodic Table: Consists of 7 horizontal rows called Periods and 18 vertical columns called Groups.

2. IUPAC Nomenclature (Z > 100) & Electronic Configuration in Blocks

Short Note:

• IUPAC Nomenclature for Z > 100: Roots used based on digits: 0=nil, 1=un, 2=bi, 3=tri, 4=quad, 5=pent, 6=hex, 7=sept, 8=oct, 9=enn. Suffix -ium is added (e.g., Z = 101 → Unnilunium, Symbol: Unu; Z = 120 → Unbinilium, Symbol: Ubn).

• Classification into Blocks:

  - s-Block (Groups 1 & 2): Outer configuration ns1-2. Reactive metals, low ionization energy.

  - p-Block (Groups 13 to 18): Outer configuration ns2 np1-6. Includes metals, non-metals, and metalloids.

  - d-Block (Groups 3 to 12): Transition elements, configuration (n-1)d1-10 ns1-2. Form colored ions and exhibit variable oxidation states.

  - f-Block (Lanthanoids & Actinoids): Inner transition elements, configuration (n-2)f1-14 (n-1)d0-1 ns2.

3. Periodic Trends in Physical Properties

Short Note:

• Atomic & Ionic Radius:

  - Across a Period: Decreases left to right due to increase in effective nuclear charge (Zeff).

  - Down a Group: Increases top to bottom due to addition of new energy shells.

  - Isoelectronic Species: Cations are smaller than neutral atoms; anions are larger. Higher positive charge = smaller size; higher negative charge = larger size.

• Ionization Enthalpy (ΔiH): Energy required to remove the most loosely bound electron from an isolated gaseous atom.

  - Increases across a period, decreases down a group.

  - Anomalies: N > O (due to half-filled stable 2p3 shell) and Be > B (due to fully-filled stable 2s2 shell).

• Electron Gain Enthalpy (ΔegH): Enthalpy change when an electron is added to a neutral gaseous atom.

  - Becomes more negative across a period, less negative down a group.

  - Anomaly: Chlorine has a more negative ΔegH than Fluorine due to less inter-electronic repulsion in Chlorine's larger 3p shell.

• Electronegativity: Ability of an atom in a compound to attract shared pair of electrons towards itself (Pauling scale: F = 4.0).

4. Periodic Trends in Chemical Properties & Anomalous Behaviors

Short Note:

• Valence / Oxidation States: Valence increases from 1 to 4 and then decreases to 0 across a period for main group elements.

• Anomalous Properties of Second Period Elements: Li, Be, B, C, N, O, F differ significantly from rest of their group members due to small size, high electronegativity, high charge/radius ratio, and absence of d-orbitals.

• Diagonal Relationship: Similarity in properties between certain elements of period 2 and period 3 placed diagonally (e.g., Li ~ Mg, Be ~ Al, B ~ Si).

Intext & Conceptual Questions

Q1: Why is the first ionization enthalpy of nitrogen greater than that of oxygen?
Answer:

Explanation:

The electronic configurations of Nitrogen (Z = 7) and Oxygen (Z = 8) are:

• Nitrogen (N): 1s2 2s2 2px1 2py1 2pz1 (Half-filled 2p subshell)

• Oxygen (O): 1s2 2s2 2px2 2py1 2pz1

Nitrogen has an extra stable half-filled 2p subshell configuration, which requires more energy to remove an electron compared to Oxygen, where removing one electron yields a stable half-filled configuration. Therefore, Nitrogen has a higher first ionization enthalpy than Oxygen.

Textbook Chapter End Exercise Questions

Q1: Consider the following species: N3-, O2-, F-, Na+, Mg2+, and Al3+.
(a) What is common in them?
(b) Arrange them in order of increasing ionic radii.
Answer:
(a) Common Feature:
All these species contain 10 electrons each. Therefore, they are isoelectronic species.

(b) Order of Increasing Ionic Radii:
For isoelectronic species, as the nuclear charge (atomic number) increases, the attractive force on the valence electrons increases, causing a decrease in ionic radius.
Nuclear charges (Z): Al3+ (13), Mg2+ (12), Na+ (11), F- (9), O2- (8), N3- (7).

Increasing order of ionic radii:
Al3+ < Mg2+ < Na+ < F- < O2- < N3-
Q2: What would be the IUPAC name and symbol for the element with atomic number 120?
Answer:
For atomic number 120:
• Digit 1 = un
• Digit 2 = bi
• Digit 0 = nil

Combining the roots with the suffix '-ium':
IUPAC Name = Unbinilium
IUPAC Symbol = Ubn

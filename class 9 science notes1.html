NCERT Solutions for Class 9 Science Chapter 1 Matter in Our Surroundings

NCERT Solutions for Class 9 Science Chapter 1 Matter in Our Surroundings covers physical nature of matter, characteristics of particles, states of matter, interconversion of states, latent heat, and factors affecting evaporation.

1. Physical Nature & Characteristics of Particles of Matter

Short Note:

• Matter: Anything that occupies space and has mass is called matter.

• Characteristics of Particles of Matter:

  1. Particles of matter have spaces between them.

  2. Particles of matter are continuously moving (possess kinetic energy).

  3. Particles of matter attract each other due to intermolecular forces.

• Diffusion: The intermixing of particles of two different types of matter on their own.

2. States of Matter (Solid, Liquid, Gas)

Short Note:

• Solids: Definite shape, distinct boundaries, fixed volume, high density, negligibly compressible, rigid.

• Liquids: No fixed shape, fixed volume, lower density than solids, fluid, moderate compressibility.

• Gases: No fixed shape or volume, highly compressible, high fluidity, low density, expand to fill containers.

3. Change of State of Matter & Latent Heat

Short Note:

• Melting Point: Temperature at which a solid changes into a liquid at atmospheric pressure.

• Boiling Point: Temperature at which a liquid changes into a gas at atmospheric pressure.

• Latent Heat of Fusion: Amount of heat energy required to change 1 kg of solid into liquid at its melting point.

• Latent Heat of Vaporisation: Amount of heat energy required to change 1 kg of liquid into gas at its boiling point.

• Sublimation: Change of state directly from solid to gas without changing into liquid (e.g., Camphor, Ammonium Chloride).

4. Evaporation & Factors Affecting It

Short Note:

• Evaporation: Surface phenomenon where a liquid changes into vapor at any temperature below its boiling point.

• Factors Increasing Evaporation:

  1. Increase in surface area.

  2. Increase in temperature.

  3. Increase in wind speed.

  4. Decrease in humidity.

• Cooling Effect: Evaporation causes cooling because high-energy particles escape, absorbing energy from surroundings.

Intext Questions - Page Number: 3

Q1: Which of the following are matter? Chair, air, love, smell, hate, almonds, thought, cold, cold-drink, smell of perfume.
Answer: Matter occupies space and has mass.
Matter: Chair, air, almonds, cold-drink, and smell of perfume (due to presence of gaseous particles).
Non-matter: Love, hate, thought, cold (these are feelings or sensory perceptions).

Intext Questions - Page Number: 6

Q1: The mass per unit volume of a substance is called density (density = mass/volume). Arrange the following in order of increasing density: air, exhaust from chimneys, honey, water, chalk, cotton and iron.
Answer: Air < Exhaust from chimneys < Cotton < Water < Honey < Chalk < Iron.

Textbook Chapter End Exercise Questions

Q1: Convert the following temperatures to the Celsius scale: (a) 300 K (b) 573 K
Answer: Temperature in °C = Temperature in K − 273

(a) 300 − 273 = 27 °C

(b) 573 − 273 = 300 °C

Q2: Why does a desert cooler cool better on a hot dry day?
Answer: On a hot dry day, the temperature is high and humidity is very low. Low humidity and high temperature increase the rate of evaporation of water in the cooler. As water evaporates rapidly, it absorbs heat from the surrounding air, creating a strong cooling effect.
Q3: Why are we able to sip hot tea or milk faster from a saucer rather than a cup?
Answer: A saucer has a larger surface area than a cup. Increased surface area accelerates the rate of evaporation, which cools the hot tea or milk much faster, allowing us to sip it comfortably.

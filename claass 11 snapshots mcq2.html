Chapter-02: The Address 25 Questions

1. Who is the author of the story 'The Address'?

  • A. Marga Minco
  • B. William Saroyan
  • C. Gordon Cook
  • D. Shirley Toulson

Answer: A. Marga Minco

Explanation: 'The Address' is an autobiographical account written by Dutch author Marga Minco.

2. What was the address where Mrs. Dorling lived?

  • A. Number 46, Marconi Street
  • B. Number 50, Baker Street
  • C. Number 12, Oxford Street
  • D. Number 26, Marconi Street

Answer: A. Number 46, Marconi Street

Explanation: The narrator's mother gave her the address "Number 46, Marconi Street" to remember.

3. Why did the narrator go to Number 46, Marconi Street?

  • A. To see and touch her mother's former belongings stored there
  • B. To buy the house from Mrs. Dorling
  • C. To attend a social gathering
  • D. To demand money from Mrs. Dorling

Answer: A. To see and touch her mother's former belongings stored there

Explanation: The narrator wanted to reconnect with her past and see her mother's possessions kept safely by Mrs. Dorling during the war.

4. How did Mrs. Dorling react when the narrator visited her house for the first time?

  • A. She pretended not to recognize her and showed cold reluctance
  • B. She welcomed her warmly with tea
  • C. She immediately returned all the silver cutlery
  • D. She started crying with happiness

Answer: A. She pretended not to recognize her and showed cold reluctance

Explanation: Mrs. Dorling barely opened the door, kept staring at her silently, and claimed it was not convenient to talk.

5. What was Mrs. Dorling wearing when she opened the door during the first visit?

  • A. The narrator's mother's green knitted cardigan
  • B. A red woolen shawl
  • C. A black silk dress
  • D. An apron with flower prints

Answer: A. The narrator's mother's green knitted cardigan

Explanation: The narrator recognized her mother's green cardigan with wooden buttons worn by Mrs. Dorling.

6. Who opened the door when the narrator visited the address for the second time?

  • A. A fifteen-year-old girl (Mrs. Dorling's daughter)
  • B. Mrs. Dorling herself
  • C. A maid servant
  • D. An old man

Answer: A. A fifteen-year-old girl (Mrs. Dorling's daughter)

Explanation: During her second visit, Mrs. Dorling was out, and her 15-year-old daughter opened the door.

7. What historic background serves as the backdrop of the story 'The Address'?

  • A. World War II in Holland
  • B. World War I in France
  • C. The American Civil War
  • D. The Russian Revolution

Answer: A. World War II in Holland

Explanation: The story portrays the painful aftermath of World War II in Holland for Jewish families.

8. Why had Mrs. Dorling offered to take away all nice things from the narrator's house?

  • A. To save them from being lost if the family had to leave during the war
  • B. To sell them in the market
  • C. To decorate her own house permanently
  • D. Because the narrator's mother gifted them to her

Answer: A. To save them from being lost if the family had to leave during the war

Explanation: Mrs. Dorling claimed she wanted to preserve their antique silver and china from damage or loss in war.

9. What object did the narrator notice burning mark on during her second visit inside the house?

  • A. The woolen tablecloth
  • B. The wooden table
  • C. The green cardigan
  • D. The curtains

Answer: A. The woolen tablecloth

Explanation: Narrator touched the woolen tablecloth and traced the burn mark that had never been repaired.

10. What special cutlery items did the narrator recall using for eating everyday without realizing their value?

  • A. Silver spoons, forks, and knives
  • B. Gold-plated bowls
  • C. Brass plates
  • D. Crystal glasses

Answer: A. Silver spoons, forks, and knives

Explanation: The daughter mentioned using silver spoons daily, reminding the narrator of how her mother told her they ate with silver cutlery.

11. What was the name of the narrator's mother?

  • A. Mrs. S.
  • B. Mrs. Dorling
  • C. Mrs. Minco
  • D. Mrs. Anne

Answer: A. Mrs. S.

Explanation: In the text, the narrator's mother is referred to as "Mrs. S."

12. How did the narrator feel when she was surrounded by her mother's belongings in Mrs. Dorling's house?

  • A. Oppressed and pained by the tasteless arrangement and strange surroundings
  • B. Extremely joyful and happy
  • C. Indifferent and bored
  • D. Proud and boastful

Answer: A. Oppressed and pained by the tasteless arrangement and strange surroundings

Explanation: Seeing her mother's cherished items kept in a ugly, tasteless, and strange setting caused her immense pain.

13. Why did the narrator decide NOT to claim or take back her mother's possessions?

  • A. They lost their emotional value when detached from her original home and loved ones
  • B. They were damaged beyond repair
  • C. She had no money to pay transport charges
  • D. Mrs. Dorling refused to give them back

Answer: A. They lost their emotional value when detached from her original home and loved ones

Explanation: The things had lost their charm because those associated with them (her family) were dead, and her current small room had no space.

14. What resolution did the narrator make at the end of the story?

  • A. To forget the address (Number 46, Marconi Street)
  • B. To sue Mrs. Dorling in court
  • C. To visit the house every month
  • D. To buy a big house to store everything

Answer: A. To forget the address (Number 46, Marconi Street)

Explanation: She resolved to forget the address as it brought back tragic memories of loss and war.

15. How was Mrs. Dorling described by Mrs. S. in terms of relationship?

  • A. An old acquaintance who renewed contact suddenly
  • B. A close sister
  • C. A maid working in their house
  • D. A school friend from childhood

Answer: A. An old acquaintance who renewed contact suddenly

Explanation: Mrs. S. mentioned Mrs. Dorling was an old acquaintance who resurfaced right before the war crisis.

16. What antique item was hanging above the table in Mrs. Dorling's living room?

  • A. The Hanukkah candle-holder
  • B. A golden clock
  • C. A painting of sunflowers
  • D. A crystal chandelier

Answer: A. The Hanukkah candle-holder

Explanation: The narrator noticed the brass Hanukkah candle-holder hanging next to a mirror.

17. What kind of room was the narrator living in after the war?

  • A. A small rented room with black-out paper strips on windows
  • B. A spacious luxury apartment
  • C. Her old childhood house
  • D. A hotel suite

Answer: A. A small rented room with black-out paper strips on windows

Explanation: After the war, she lived in a small rented space where all those massive antique objects would not fit.

18. What was Mrs. Dorling's main motive in taking all items from Mrs. S.'s house?

  • A. Greed and self-interest under the guise of sympathy
  • B. Pure friendship and kindness
  • C. Social service duty
  • D. Orders from the government

Answer: A. Greed and self-interest under the guise of sympathy

Explanation: Mrs. Dorling exploited their vulnerable situation to take possession of valuable household antiques.

19. How did Mrs. Dorling carry away the heavy items like vases and silver?

  • A. In heavy suitcases and luggage during each visit
  • B. In a truck sent by government
  • C. With the help of porters
  • D. In a horse cart

Answer: A. In heavy suitcases and luggage during each visit

Explanation: Mrs. S. noted how Mrs. Dorling walked away with heavy suitcases full of silver and antiques every time she visited.

20. What tea set feature did the narrator recognize when Mrs. Dorling's daughter served tea?

  • A. White cups with a gold border on the lid
  • B. Red porcelain cups with dragons
  • C. Plain glass mugs
  • D. Silver teapot with wooden handles

Answer: A. White cups with a gold border on the lid

Explanation: The narrator distinctly remembered the white teapot with a delicate gold border on its lid.

21. What tone best describes the end of the story 'The Address'?

  • A. Melancholic yet resolute acceptance
  • B. Aggressive revenge
  • C. Overwhelming celebration
  • D. Hopeless despair

Answer: A. Melancholic yet resolute acceptance

Explanation: The story ends on a quiet note of moving forward in life by leaving behind painful attachments.

22. Why did the narrator leave the house abruptly during her second visit?

  • A. The sight of her dead mother's items in an alien setup triggered unbearable psychological pain
  • B. Mrs. Dorling returned and asked her to leave
  • C. She had to catch her train back
  • D. She felt sick physically

Answer: A. The sight of her dead mother's items in an alien setup triggered unbearable psychological pain

Explanation: Looking at those lifeless symbols of her lost family deeply grieved her, prompting her to rush out.

23. What primary loss does the story 'The Address' highlight?

  • A. Emotional, human, and material losses caused by war
  • B. Loss of physical property value
  • C. Loss of business opportunity
  • D. Loss of memory in old age

Answer: A. Emotional, human, and material losses caused by war

Explanation: It highlights how war destroys families, human bonds, and renders physical treasures meaningless.

24. What makes the title 'The Address' fitting and symbolic?

  • A. It represents both a physical place and the memory the narrator chooses to erase
  • B. It is the post office address of the narrator
  • C. It refers to a speech delivered by a politician
  • D. It is where the narrator currently resides

Answer: A. It represents both a physical place and the memory the narrator chooses to erase

Explanation: The address was crucial to find her past, but forgetting it became essential to move forward in life.

25. Which thing out of all memories was easiest for the narrator to forget?

  • A. The street address (Number 46, Marconi Street)
  • B. The memory of her mother
  • C. The horrors of the war
  • D. Her childhood friends

Answer: A. The street address (Number 46, Marconi Street)

Explanation: The narrator concludes: "Of all the things I had to forget, that would be the easiest."

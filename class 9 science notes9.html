NCERT Solutions for Class 9 Science Chapter 9 Gravitation

NCERT Solutions for Class 9 Science Chapter 9 Gravitation covers the universal law of gravitation, free fall, acceleration due to gravity (g), mass, weight, weight of an object on the Moon, thrust, pressure, Archimedes' principle, buoyancy, and relative density.

1. Universal Law of Gravitation & Free Fall

Short Note:

• Universal Law of Gravitation: Every object in the universe attracts every other object with a force proportional to the product of their masses and inversely proportional to the square of the distance between them.

  - Formula: F = G × (M × m) / d²

  - Universal Gravitational Constant (G): G = 6.673 × 10⁻¹¹ N·m²/kg²

• Free Fall: Whenever an object falls towards Earth under the influence of gravitational force alone, it is said to be in free fall.

• Acceleration due to Gravity (g): Acceleration experienced by a freely falling body due to Earth's gravitational pull. Average value near Earth's surface: g = 9.8 m/s².

  - Relation between g and G: g = (G × M) / R²

2. Mass, Weight & Weight on the Moon

Short Note:

• Mass (m): Quantity of matter contained in a body. It remains constant everywhere in the universe. SI Unit: kilogram (kg). Scalar quantity.

• Weight (W): Force with which Earth attracts an object towards its center. Formula: W = m × g. SI Unit: Newton (N). Vector quantity, changes with position/gravity.

• Weight of an Object on the Moon: Since the mass and radius of the Moon are smaller than Earth's, the acceleration due to gravity on the Moon is about 1/6th of that on Earth.

  - Weight on Moon = (1/6) × Weight on Earth

3. Thrust, Pressure & Archimedes' Principle

Short Note:

• Thrust: Total net force acting perpendicular to a surface. SI Unit: Newton (N).

• Pressure (P): Thrust per unit area. Formula: Pressure = Thrust / Area. SI Unit: Pascal (Pa) or N/m².

  - Smaller area produces greater pressure for the same thrust (e.g., sharp nails, thin straps on bags).

• Archimedes' Principle: When a body is immersed fully or partially in a fluid, it experiences an upward force (buoyant force) equal to the weight of the fluid displaced by it.

  - Applications: Designing ships, submarines, hydrometers, lactometers.

4. Buoyancy & Relative Density

Short Note:

• Buoyancy: The upward force exerted by a fluid on an object immersed in it is called buoyant force or upthrust.

  - An object floats if its density is less than the density of the fluid.

  - An object sinks if its density is greater than the density of the fluid.

• Relative Density: Ratio of the density of a substance to the density of water at 4°C.

  - Formula: Relative Density = Density of substance / Density of water

  - Since it is a ratio of identical quantities, Relative Density has no unit.

Intext Questions - Page Number: 102

Q1: State the universal law of gravitation.
Answer: The Universal Law of Gravitation states that every particle in the universe attracts every other particle with a force that is directly proportional to the product of their masses and inversely proportional to the square of the distance between their centers. The direction of the force is along the line joining the centers of the two particles.
Q2: Write the formula to find the magnitude of the gravitational force between the earth and an object on the surface of the earth.
Answer: F = G × (M × m) / R²

Where:

• F: Gravitational force

• G: Universal gravitational constant

• M: Mass of the Earth

• m: Mass of the object

• R: Radius of the Earth

Intext Questions - Page Number: 104

Q1: What do you mean by free fall?
Answer: When an object falls towards the Earth under the sole influence of the Earth's gravitational pull without any other forces (like air resistance) opposing it, the object is said to be in free fall.
Q2: What do you mean by acceleration due to gravity?
Answer: Acceleration due to gravity is the uniform acceleration produced in a freely falling body due to the gravitational attraction of the Earth. It is denoted by 'g' and has a standard value of approximately 9.8 m/s² near Earth's surface.

Textbook Chapter End Exercise Questions

Q1: What is the magnitude of the gravitational force between the earth and a 1 kg object on its surface? (Mass of Earth = 6 × 10²⁴ kg, Radius of Earth = 6.4 × 10⁶ m, G = 6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ N·m²/kg²)
Answer:

• Mass of Earth (M) = 6 × 10²⁴ kg

• Mass of object (m) = 1 kg

• Radius of Earth (R) = 6.4 × 10⁶ m

• F = G × (M × m) / R²

• F = (6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ × 6 × 10²⁴ × 1) / (6.4 × 10⁶)²

• F = (40.02 × 10¹³) / (40.96 × 10¹²)

• F ≈ 9.77 N ≈ 9.8 N

Q2: Why is it difficult to hold a school bag having a strap made of a thin and strong string?
Answer: Pressure is inversely proportional to the contact surface area (Pressure = Force / Area). A thin string has a very small contact area, which exerts a large amount of pressure on the shoulder, making it painful and difficult to carry the school bag.
Q3: Why does an object float or sink when placed on the surface of water?
Answer:

• An object floats if its density is less than the density of water (the buoyant force acting upwards is greater than or equal to the weight of the object).

• An object sinks if its density is greater than the density of water (the buoyant force is less than the weight of the object).

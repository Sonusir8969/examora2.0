Ch-11: Electricity 30 Questions

1. The SI unit of electric current is:

  • A. Volt
  • B. Joule
  • C. Ampere
  • D. Coulomb

Answer: C. Ampere

Explanation: Electric current is the rate of flow of electric charge, defined as 1 Ampere = 1 Coulomb per second (A = C/s).

2. The SI unit of electric charge is:

  • A. Ampere
  • B. Coulomb
  • C. Ohm
  • D. Watt

Answer: B. Coulomb

Explanation: Electric charge is measured in Coulombs (C). One electron carries an elementary charge of 1.6 × 10^-19 C.

3. How many electrons constitute a charge of 1 Coulomb?

  • A. 6 × 10^18
  • B. 1.6 × 10^-19
  • C. 6 × 10^23
  • D. 1.6 × 10^19

Answer: A. 6 × 10^18

Explanation: Number of electrons n = Q / e = 1 / (1.6 × 10^-19) ≈ 6.25 × 10^18 (approx 6 × 10^18).

4. Electric current is measured using which instrument connected in series?

  • A. Voltmeter
  • B. Galvanometer
  • C. Ammeter
  • D. Rheostat

Answer: C. Ammeter

Explanation: An ammeter has low resistance and is always connected in series to measure total current passing through a circuit.

5. The potential difference between two points is measured using which instrument connected in parallel?

  • A. Ammeter
  • B. Voltmeter
  • C. Rheostat
  • D. Multimeter

Answer: B. Voltmeter

Explanation: A voltmeter has high resistance and is connected in parallel across the two points to measure potential difference.

6. The work done in moving a unit charge from one point to another in an electric circuit is defined as:

  • A. Electric Current
  • B. Electric Power
  • C. Potential Difference
  • D. Resistance

Answer: C. Potential Difference

Explanation: Potential Difference (V) = Work done (W) / Charge (Q), measured in Volts (V = J/C).

7. According to Ohm's Law, at constant temperature, current flowing through a metallic wire is directly proportional to:

  • A. Its resistance
  • B. Its length
  • C. Potential difference across its ends
  • D. Area of cross-section

Answer: C. Potential difference across its ends

Explanation: Ohm's Law states V ∝ I or V = I × R, provided physical conditions like temperature remain constant.

8. The SI unit of electrical resistance is:

  • A. Volt
  • B. Ohm (Ω)
  • C. Ampere
  • D. Ohm-meter

Answer: B. Ohm (Ω)

Explanation: Resistance R = V / I. Its SI unit is Ohm, denoted by the Greek letter Ω.

9. The resistance of a conductor is directly proportional to its:

  • A. Area of cross-section
  • B. Length (l)
  • C. Shape
  • D. Volume

Answer: B. Length (l)

Explanation: R ∝ l. Doubling the length of a conductor doubles its resistance.

10. If the area of cross-section of a wire is doubled, its resistance becomes:

  • A. Doubled
  • B. Halved
  • C. Four times
  • D. Unchanged

Answer: B. Halved

Explanation: Resistance is inversely proportional to area of cross-section (R ∝ 1/A). Doubling A halves R.

11. The SI unit of electrical resistivity (ρ) is:

  • A. Ohm
  • B. Ohm / meter
  • C. Ohm-meter (Ω·m)
  • D. Volt / meter

Answer: C. Ohm-meter (Ω·m)

Explanation: From R = ρ(l/A), ρ = (R × A) / l. The unit calculates as (Ω × m^2) / m = Ω·m.

12. Which of the following material has the lowest electrical resistivity and is the best conductor?

  • A. Copper
  • B. Silver
  • C. Aluminum
  • D. Iron

Answer: B. Silver

Explanation: Silver has the lowest electrical resistivity (~1.60 × 10^-8 Ω·m) among all metals, making it the best conductor.

13. Nichrome is an alloy commonly used in heating elements because:

  • A. It has low resistance and low melting point
  • B. It has high resistivity and does not oxidize (burn) easily at high temperatures
  • C. It is very cheap and lightweight
  • D. It exhibits zero resistance at room temperature

Answer: B. It has high resistivity and does not oxidize (burn) easily at high temperatures

Explanation: Nichrome (Ni-Cr alloy) resists oxidation at glowing temperatures and possesses high resistivity ideal for heating coils.

14. When three resistors of resistances R1, R2, and R3 are connected in series, the equivalent resistance (Rs) is:

  • A. 1/R1 + 1/R2 + 1/R3
  • B. R1 + R2 + R3
  • C. (R1 × R2 × R3) / (R1 + R2 + R3)
  • D. R1 - R2 - R3

Answer: B. R1 + R2 + R3

Explanation: In a series connection, total equivalent resistance is simply the sum of individual resistances (Rs = R1 + R2 + R3).

15. When resistors are connected in parallel, which quantity remains the same across each resistor?

  • A. Current
  • B. Potential Difference
  • C. Resistance
  • D. Heat generated

Answer: B. Potential Difference

Explanation: In parallel combinations, potential difference across every branch is equal to the supply voltage, while current divides.

16. Three resistors of 2 Ω, 3 Ω, and 6 Ω are connected in parallel. Their total equivalent resistance (Rp) is:

  • A. 11 Ω
  • B. 1 Ω
  • C. 2 Ω
  • D. 0.5 Ω

Answer: B. 1 Ω

Explanation: 1/Rp = 1/2 + 1/3 + 1/6 = (3 + 2 + 1) / 6 = 6/6 = 1. Therefore, Rp = 1 Ω.

17. Domestic electric circuits are always wired in parallel rather than series because:

  • A. In parallel, if one appliance fails, others continue working independently
  • B. Series wiring requires thicker copper wires
  • C. Parallel wiring increases total resistance
  • D. In series, voltage across each appliance increases

Answer: A. In parallel, if one appliance fails, others continue working independently

Explanation: Parallel circuits give each appliance full line voltage (220V) and independent switches so one faulty device doesn't open the whole circuit.

18. According to Joule's Law of Heating, the heat (H) generated in a resistor is given by the formula:

  • A. H = I × R × t
  • B. H = I^2 × R × t
  • C. H = V × I^2 × t
  • D. H = I × R^2 × t

Answer: B. H = I^2 × R × t

Explanation: Joule's Law states H = I^2 R t, where I is current, R is resistance, and t is time.

19. Which metal is almost exclusively used for filaments of electric bulbs due to its very high melting point (~3380 °C)?

  • A. Copper
  • B. Tungsten
  • C. Aluminum
  • D. Iron

Answer: B. Tungsten

Explanation: Tungsten has an extremely high melting point and high resistivity, enabling it to glow white-hot without melting.

20. Electric bulbs are usually filled with chemically inactive gases like:

  • A. Oxygen and Hydrogen
  • B. Nitrogen and Argon
  • C. Carbon dioxide and Helium
  • D. Chlorine and Fluorine

Answer: B. Nitrogen and Argon

Explanation: Inactive gases like argon and nitrogen prevent oxidation and prolong filament life.

21. An electric fuse is a safety device connected in:

  • A. Parallel with live wire
  • B. Series with live wire
  • C. Series with neutral wire
  • D. Parallel with earth wire

Answer: B. Series with live wire

Explanation: Fuse wire is connected in series with the live wire so that excess current melts it, breaking the circuit instantly.

22. The rate at which electrical energy is consumed in an electric circuit is called:

  • A. Electric Current
  • B. Potential Difference
  • C. Electric Power
  • D. Resistance

Answer: C. Electric Power

Explanation: Power P = Energy / time = V × I, representing rate of electrical work done.

23. The SI unit of electric power is:

  • A. Joule
  • B. Watt (W)
  • C. Kilowatt-hour
  • D. Ampere

Answer: B. Watt (W)

Explanation: 1 Watt is the power consumed by a device carrying 1 A current at 1 V potential difference (1 W = 1 V × 1 A = 1 J/s).

24. Commercial unit of electrical energy is kilowatt-hour (kWh), commonly called 'unit'. 1 kWh is equal to:

  • A. 3.6 × 10^5 J
  • B. 3.6 × 10^6 J
  • C. 1.6 × 10^6 J
  • D. 36 × 10^6 J

Answer: B. 3.6 × 10^6 J

Explanation: 1 kWh = 1000 W × 3600 s = 3,600,000 J = 3.6 × 10^6 Joules.

25. An electric heater rated 1000 W operates on a 220 V line. The current drawn by the heater is approximately:

  • A. 4.55 A
  • B. 2.2 A
  • C. 10 A
  • D. 0.22 A

Answer: A. 4.55 A

Explanation: Current I = P / V = 1000 W / 220 V = 4.545 A ≈ 4.55 A.

26. If the length of a wire is stretched to double its original length keeping volume constant, its new resistance becomes:

  • A. 2 times
  • B. 4 times
  • C. Half
  • D. 8 times

Answer: B. 4 times

Explanation: Stretching to double length halves area A. Since R = ρ(l/A), new resistance R' = ρ(2l/(A/2)) = 4 R.

27. A variable resistor used in circuits to change current without changing voltage source is called a:

  • A. Galvanometer
  • B. Rheostat
  • C. Voltmeter
  • D. Transformer

Answer: B. Rheostat

Explanation: A rheostat allows manual variation of circuit resistance to regulate electrical current flow.

28. Two electric bulbs rated (220 V, 100 W) and (220 V, 60 W) are connected in parallel to a 220 V supply. Which bulb draws more current?

  • A. 60 W bulb
  • B. 100 W bulb
  • C. Both draw equal current
  • D. Neither draws current

Answer: B. 100 W bulb

Explanation: Since I = P / V and V is constant in parallel, the higher power bulb (100 W) draws more current.

29. Which mathematical formula correctly represents electric power?

  • A. P = V × I
  • B. P = I^2 × R
  • C. P = V^2 / R
  • D. All of the above

Answer: D. All of the above

Explanation: Using Ohm's law V = IR, electric power can be expressed as P = VI = I^2 R = V^2 / R.

30. When a wire of resistance R is cut into 5 equal parts and connected in parallel, the equivalent resistance R' gives a ratio R/R' equal to:

  • A. 1/25
  • B. 1/5
  • C. 5
  • D. 25

Answer: D. 25

Explanation: Each piece has resistance R/5. In parallel, 1/R' = 5/(R/5) = 25/R. Thus R' = R/25, making R/R' = 25.

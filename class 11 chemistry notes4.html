NCERT Solutions for Class 11 Chemistry Chapter 4 Chemical Bonding and Molecular Structure

NCERT Solutions for Class 11 Chemistry Chapter 4 Chemical Bonding and Molecular Structure covers the Kossel-Lewis approach to bonding, ionic and covalent bonds, VSEPR theory, valence bond theory, hybridisation, and molecular orbital theory.

1. Ionic and Covalent Bonding

Short Note:

• Ionic (Electrovalent) Bond: formed by the complete transfer of electrons from one atom to another, resulting in oppositely charged ions held together by electrostatic attraction.

• Covalent Bond: formed by the mutual sharing of electron pairs between two atoms, as described by the Lewis dot structure and the octet rule.

2. VSEPR Theory & Molecular Shapes

Short Note:

• Valence Shell Electron Pair Repulsion (VSEPR) Theory predicts molecular shape based on the principle that electron pairs (bonding and lone) around a central atom arrange themselves to minimise repulsion.

• Lone pair – lone pair repulsion > lone pair – bond pair repulsion > bond pair – bond pair repulsion.

3. Valence Bond Theory and Hybridisation

Short Note:

• Hybridisation is the intermixing of atomic orbitals of slightly different energies to form new equivalent orbitals (hybrid orbitals) of same energy and shape.

• Common types: sp (linear, 180°), sp² (trigonal planar, 120°), sp³ (tetrahedral, 109.5°).

4. Molecular Orbital Theory

Short Note:

• Atomic orbitals combine to form bonding molecular orbitals (lower energy, stabilising) and antibonding molecular orbitals (higher energy, destabilising).

• Bond Order = ½ (number of electrons in bonding MOs − number of electrons in antibonding MOs). A higher bond order generally means a more stable, shorter bond.

NCERT Textbook Exercise Questions

Draw the Lewis structure of CO2 and identify the type of bonds present.
Answer: CO2 has the structure O=C=O, with each carbon-oxygen bond being a double covalent bond, consisting of one sigma (σ) and one pi (π) bond, giving two sigma and two pi bonds in total.
Explain the shape of NH3 molecule using VSEPR theory.
Answer: Nitrogen in NH3 has 3 bond pairs and 1 lone pair (sp³ hybridisation), giving a tetrahedral electron geometry, but due to lone pair-bond pair repulsion pushing the bond pairs closer together, the actual molecular shape is trigonal pyramidal with bond angle slightly less than 109.5° (about 107°).
What is the state of hybridisation of carbon in ethene (C2H4)?
Answer: Each carbon atom in ethene is sp² hybridised, forming three sigma bonds (2 with H, 1 with the other C) in a trigonal planar arrangement, with the unhybridised p-orbital forming a pi bond between the two carbons.
Using molecular orbital theory, calculate the bond order of the nitrogen molecule (N2) and comment on its stability.
Answer: N2 has 14 electrons total in its molecular orbitals: configuration gives 10 electrons in bonding MOs and 4 in antibonding MOs. Bond order = ½(10−4) = 3. A bond order of 3 (triple bond) indicates N2 is very stable, consistent with its high bond dissociation energy.
Why does the H2 molecule not exist in the form He2, based on molecular orbital theory?
Answer: He2 would have 4 electrons: 2 in the bonding σ1s and 2 in the antibonding σ*1s orbital, giving a bond order of ½(2−2) = 0. A bond order of zero means no net bonding, so He2 does not exist as a stable molecule.

Ch-6: Application of Derivatives 12 Questions

1. The derivative dy/dx represents the:

  • A. Rate of change of y with respect to x
  • B. Area under the curve
  • C. Value of the function at a point
  • D. The integral of the function

Answer: A. Rate of change of y with respect to x

Explanation: Derivatives fundamentally measure the instantaneous rate of change of one quantity with respect to another.

2. A function is said to be increasing on an interval if its derivative there is:

  • A. Positive (≥ 0) throughout the interval
  • B. Negative throughout the interval
  • C. Zero throughout the interval
  • D. Undefined throughout

Answer: A. Positive (≥ 0) throughout the interval

Explanation: A non-negative derivative throughout an interval indicates the function's values are non-decreasing there.

3. A point where f'(x) = 0 is called a:

  • A. Critical point
  • B. Point of inflection, always
  • C. Point of discontinuity
  • D. Asymptote

Answer: A. Critical point

Explanation: Critical points occur where the derivative is zero (or undefined) and are candidates for local maxima or minima.

4. The second derivative test uses f''(x) mainly to determine:

  • A. Whether a critical point is a local maximum or minimum
  • B. Whether the function is continuous
  • C. The domain of the function
  • D. The range of the function

Answer: A. Whether a critical point is a local maximum or minimum

Explanation: A positive f''(x) at a critical point indicates a local minimum, while a negative value indicates a local maximum.

5. If f''(x) > 0 at a critical point, that point is a:

  • A. Local minimum
  • B. Local maximum
  • C. Point of inflection
  • D. Point of discontinuity

Answer: A. Local minimum

Explanation: A positive second derivative indicates the curve is concave upward at that point, corresponding to a local minimum.

6. The equation of the tangent to the curve y = f(x) at the point (x1, y1) is:

  • A. y - y1 = f'(x1)(x - x1)
  • B. y = f'(x1)
  • C. x - x1 = f(x1)
  • D. y + y1 = f'(x1)

Answer: A. y - y1 = f'(x1)(x - x1)

Explanation: This is the standard point-slope form of the tangent line, where f'(x1) is the slope of the curve at that point.

7. Rate of change problems (such as related rates) are typically solved using:

  • A. Derivatives with respect to time
  • B. Integration with respect to time
  • C. Purely algebraic equations
  • D. Matrices

Answer: A. Derivatives with respect to time

Explanation: Since rate of change refers to how a quantity varies with time, such problems are naturally modeled using derivatives with respect to t.

8. The normal to a curve at a given point is:

  • A. Perpendicular to the tangent at that point
  • B. Parallel to the tangent at that point
  • C. The same line as the tangent
  • D. Always horizontal

Answer: A. Perpendicular to the tangent at that point

Explanation: By definition, the normal line is drawn perpendicular to the tangent line at the point of contact.

9. Maxima and minima problems in real-world optimization are generally solved using the:

  • A. First and second derivative tests
  • B. Method of integration only
  • C. Concept of limits only
  • D. Matrices and determinants

Answer: A. First and second derivative tests

Explanation: Optimization problems are solved by finding critical points using the first derivative and classifying them using the second derivative test.

10. A function is said to be decreasing on an interval if its derivative there is:

  • A. Negative (≤ 0) throughout the interval
  • B. Positive throughout the interval
  • C. Zero throughout the interval
  • D. Undefined throughout

Answer: A. Negative (≤ 0) throughout the interval

Explanation: A non-positive derivative throughout an interval means the function's values are non-increasing there.

11. The approximate value of a function near a known point can be estimated using:

  • A. Differentials
  • B. Definite integrals
  • C. Determinants
  • D. Matrices

Answer: A. Differentials

Explanation: The concept of differentials, dy = f'(x)dx, provides a linear approximation for small changes in a function.

12. The absolute maximum of a continuous function on a closed interval occurs at:

  • A. A critical point or one of the endpoints of the interval
  • B. Only at critical points inside the interval
  • C. Only at the endpoints
  • D. Nowhere on a closed interval

Answer: A. A critical point or one of the endpoints of the interval

Explanation: The Extreme Value Theorem guarantees that the absolute maximum on a closed interval is attained either at a critical point or at an endpoint.

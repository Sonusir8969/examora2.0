NCERT Solutions for Class 11 Biology Chapter 10 Cell Cycle and Cell Division

NCERT Solutions for Class 11 Biology Chapter 10 Cell Cycle and Cell Division provides complete notes and detailed answers on the phases of the cell cycle (Interphase and M Phase), Mitosis (equational division), Meiosis (reductional division), and their biological significance.

1. Cell Cycle & Interphase

Short Note:

• Cell Cycle: The sequence of events by which a cell duplicates its genome, synthesizes other cell constituents, and eventually divides into two daughter cells.

• Phases of Cell Cycle: Divided into two basic phases:

  1. Interphase (Resting Phase): Occupies over 95% of cell cycle duration. Cell prepares for division through cell growth and DNA replication.

    - G1 Phase (Gap 1): Interval between mitosis and initiation of DNA replication. Cell is metabolically active and continuously grows without DNA replication.

    - S Phase (Synthesis): DNA replication occurs (DNA amount doubles from 2C to 4C, but chromosome number remains 2n). Centriole duplicates in cytoplasm of animal cells.

    - G2 Phase (Gap 2): Proteins required for mitosis are synthesized while cell growth continues.

  2. G0 Phase (Quiescent Stage): Inactive metabolic stage where cells exit G1 phase and cease division unless called upon to divide (e.g., heart cells, neurons).

2. M Phase: Mitosis

Short Note:

• Equational Division: Number of chromosomes in parent and daughter cells remains equal (2n → 2n).

• Karyokinesis (Nuclear Division Stages):

  - Prophase: Chromatin condenses into distinct chromosomes; nucleolus, nuclear envelope, and Golgi complex disappear; mitotic spindle begins forming.

  - Metaphase: Chromosomes align along the metaphase plate (equator); spindle fibers attach to kinetochores of sister chromatids.

  - Anaphase: Centromeres split, sister chromatids separate and move toward opposite poles driven by spindle fiber shortening.

  - Telophase: Chromosomes reach poles and decondense; nuclear membrane, nucleolus, and organelles reappear around each set of chromosomes.

• Cytokinesis (Cytoplasmic Division):

  - Animal Cells: Forms a cell furrow in the plasma membrane that deepens centripetally.

  - Plant Cells: Formation of a cell plate (precursor of middle lamella) growing centrifugally from the center outwards.

3. Meiosis

Short Note:

• Reductional Division: Special division in germ cells that reduces chromosome number by half (2n → n), producing four haploid daughter cells.

• Meiosis I:

  - Prophase I (Subdivided into 5 substages):

    1. Leptotene: Chromosomes become visible and compact.

    2. Zygotene: Homologous chromosomes pair up (synapsis) to form synaptonemal complexes and bivalents/tetrads.

    3. Pachytene: Crossing over occurs between non-sister chromatids of homologous chromosomes mediated by the enzyme recombinase.

    4. Diplotene: Dissolution of synaptonemal complex; homologous chromosomes separate except at crossover sites forming X-shaped structures called Chiasmata.

    5. Diakinesis: Terminalisation of chiasmata; nuclear membrane breaks down.

  - Metaphase I, Anaphase I (homologous chromosomes separate, sister chromatids remain associated), Telophase I.

• Interkinesis: Short-lived stage between Meiosis I and Meiosis II (no DNA replication occurs).

• Meiosis II: Similar to a normal mitosis, separating sister chromatids to produce 4 haploid cells.

4. Significance of Mitosis & Meiosis

Short Note:

• Significance of Mitosis:

  - Ensures genetic stability by producing identical daughter cells.

  - Essential for growth of multicellular organisms and continuous cell repair/replacement (e.g., blood cells, gut lining, epidermis).

• Significance of Meiosis:

  - Conserves specific chromosome number of sexually reproducing species across generations.

  - Introduces genetic variations through crossing over and independent assortment, driving evolution.

Intext & Conceptual Questions

Q1: What is the Quiescent Stage (G0) of the cell cycle?
Answer:
The G0 phase (Quiescent Stage) is an inactive phase of the cell cycle. Cells in adult organisms that do not divide further (such as heart cells and mature neurons) exit the G1 phase and enter G0. These cells remain metabolically active but do not proliferate unless stimulated by specific physiological cues or requirements for tissue repair.
Q2: Name the substage of Prophase I where crossing over occurs, and name the enzyme involved.
Answer:
• Substage: Pachytene stage of Prophase I (Meiosis I).
• Enzyme: Recombinase.

Textbook Chapter End Exercise Questions

Q1: Distinguish between Mitosis and Meiosis.
Answer:
Feature Mitosis Meiosis
Type of Cells Occurs in somatic cells. Occurs in reproductive/germ cells.
Number of Divisions Single division (1 nuclear division). Two sequential divisions (Meiosis I & II).
Daughter Cells Produced 2 diploid (2n) identical daughter cells. 4 haploid (n) non-identical daughter cells.
Synapsis & Crossing Over Absent. Present during Prophase I.
Biological Function Growth, repair, and asexual reproduction. Gamete formation and genetic variation.
Q2: Distinguish between Cytokinesis in Plant Cells and Animal Cells.
Answer:
Feature Plant Cell Cytokinesis Animal Cell Cytokinesis
Mechanism Occurs by Cell Plate formation. Occurs by Cleavage Furrow formation.
Direction Centrifugal (grows from center outward). Centripetal (deepens from periphery inward).
Middle Lamella Formation Cell plate forms middle lamella between walls. No middle lamella involved.

1. General Properties & Trends of Transition Elements (d-Block)

Short Note:

• Electronic Configuration: General valence shell electronic configuration: $(n-1)d^{1-10} ns^{1-2}$. Transition metals are defined as elements having partially filled $d$-orbitals in their ground state or in any common oxidation state (Note: $\text{Zn, Cd, Hg}$ are not regarded as transition elements due to completely filled $d$-orbitals).

• Variable Oxidation States: Due to small energy difference between $(n-1)d$ and $ns$ orbitals, electrons from both subshells participate in bond formation.

• Magnetic Properties: Paramagnetism arises due to unpaired electrons. Magnetic moment ($\mu$) is calculated using the spin-only formula:

  $\mu = \sqrt{n(n+2)}\text{ BM}$ (where $n$ is number of unpaired electrons and BM is Bohr Magneton).

• Formation of Coloured Ions: Caused by $d$-$d$ electronic transitions of unpaired electrons upon absorption of visible light energy.

• Catalytic Activity: Ability to show variable oxidation states and form reaction intermediates (e.g., $\text{V}_2\text{O}_5$ in Contact process, Fine $\text{Fe}$ in Haber process).

2. Potassium Dichromate ($\text{K}_2\text{Cr}_2\text{O}_7$) & Potassium Permanganate ($\text{KMnO}_4$)

Short Note:

• Potassium Dichromate ($\text{K}_2\text{Cr}_2\text{O}_7$):

  - Prepared from chromite ore ($\text{FeCr}_2\text{O}_4$).

  - pH Dependent Equilibrium: Chromate ($\text{CrO}_4^{2-}$, yellow) and dichromate ($\text{Cr}_2\text{O}_7^{2-}$, orange) interconvert depending on pH:

    $2\text{CrO}_4^{2-} + 2\text{H}^+ \rightleftharpoons \text{Cr}_2\text{O}_7^{2-} + \text{H}_2\text{O}$

  - Powerful oxidizing agent in acidic medium: $\text{Cr}_2\text{O}_7^{2-} + 14\text{H}^+ + 6e^- \rightarrow 2\text{Cr}^{3+} + 7\text{H}_2\text{O}$

• Potassium Permanganate ($\text{KMnO}_4$):

  - Prepared from pyrolusite ore ($\text{MnO}_2$).

  - Oxidizing Action in Acidic Medium ($E^\circ = +1.51\text{ V}$):

    $\text{MnO}_4^- + 8\text{H}^+ + 5e^- \rightarrow \text{Mn}^{2+} + 4\text{H}_2\text{O}$

3. Inner Transition Elements (f-Block): Lanthanoids & Actinoids

Short Note:

• Lanthanoids ($4f$-series, $\text{Ce}_{58}$ to $\text{Lu}_{71}$):

  - Common oxidation state is $+3$. $+2$ and $+4$ states exist if they yield extra stable $4f^0, 4f^7, 4f^{14}$ configurations (e.g., $\text{Ce}^{4+}, \text{Eu}^{2+}$).

  - Lanthanoid Contraction: Steady decrease in atomic and ionic radii ($\text{M}^{3+}$) from $\text{La}$ to $\text{Lu}$ due to poor shielding effect of $4f$-electrons.

  - Consequences: Similar chemical properties, difficult separation, equal atomic radii of $4d$ and $5d$ series elements (e.g., $\text{Zr} \approx \text{Hf}$).

• Actinoids ($5f$-series, $\text{Th}_{90}$ to $\text{Lr}_{103}$):

  - All elements are radioactive. Show greater variety of oxidation states ($+3, +4, +5, +6, +7$) due to comparable energies of $5f, 6d$, and $7s$ subshells.

Intext & Conceptual Questions

Q1: What is Lanthanoid Contraction? Mention two of its major consequences.
Answer:
• Definition: The steady and continuous decrease in the atomic and ionic radii of lanthanoid elements with increasing atomic number from Lanthanum ($\text{La}$) to Lutetium ($\text{Lu}$) is known as Lanthanoid Contraction. It occurs because $4f$ electrons have poor nuclear shielding efficacy.

• Consequences:
1. Similarity in Radii of $4d$ and $5d$ Transition Series: Elements belonging to the 2nd and 3rd transition series of the same group exhibit almost identical atomic radii (e.g., $\text{Zr} = 160\text{ pm}$ and $\text{Hf} = 159\text{ pm}$), making their chemical properties virtually identical.
2. Difficulty in Separation: Due to nearly identical ionic radii and chemical properties, separating individual lanthanoid elements in pure form is extremely difficult.
3. Trend in Basic Strength: Basic strength of hydroxides decreases from $\text{La(OH)}_3$ (most basic) to $\text{Lu(OH)}_3$ (least basic) as covalent character increases.
Q2: Why do transition metals exhibit variable oxidation states and form a large number of complex compounds?
Answer:
Property Scientific Reason
Variable Oxidation States Energy levels of $(n-1)d$ and $ns$ subshells are very close. Therefore, electrons from both $ns$ and $(n-1)d$ subshells can participate in chemical bonding.
Complex Formation Driven by: (i) Small ionic size of metal cations, (ii) High ionic charge density, and (iii) Availability of vacant $d$-orbitals to accept electron pairs donated by ligands.
Catalytic Activity Ability to adopt multiple oxidation states, form variable intermediates, and provide large active surface areas for reactant adsorption.

Textbook Chapter End Exercise Questions

Q1: Calculate the spin-only magnetic moment of a divalent ion of an element with atomic number 25.
Answer:
Given:
• Atomic Number ($Z$) = 25 (Manganese, $\text{Mn}$)
• Divalent ion = $\text{M}^{2+}$

Step 1: Write Electronic Configuration
• Neutral atom ($Z=25$): $[\text{Ar}] 3d^5 4s^2$
• Divalent cation ($\text{M}^{2+}$): $[\text{Ar}] 3d^5 4s^0$

Step 2: Determine Number of Unpaired Electrons ($n$)
• The $3d^5$ subshell contains 5 unpaired electrons ($n = 5$).

Step 3: Calculate Spin-Only Magnetic Moment ($\mu$)
$\mu = \sqrt{n(n+2)}\text{ BM}$
$\mu = \sqrt{5(5+2)} = \sqrt{35} \approx \mathbf{5.92\text{ BM}}$

• The magnetic moment of the divalent ion is 5.92 BM (Bohr Magnetons).
Q2: Complete and balance the following ionic chemical equations: (i) Cr₂O₇²⁻(aq) + 6 Fe²⁺(aq) + 14 H⁺(aq) → ... (ii) 2 MnO₄⁻(aq) + 5 C₂O₄²⁻(aq) + 16 H⁺(aq) → ...
Answer:

(i) Reaction of Dichromate ion with Ferrous ion in Acidic Medium:
• Dichromate oxidizes $\text{Fe}^{2+}$ to $\text{Fe}^{3+}$ while getting reduced to $\text{Cr}^{3+}$.
$$\text{Cr}_2\text{O}_7^{2-}\text{(aq)} + 6\text{Fe}^{2+}\text{(aq)} + 14\text{H}^+\text{(aq)} \rightarrow 2\text{Cr}^{3+}\text{(aq)} + 6\text{Fe}^{3+}\text{(aq)} + 7\text{H}_2\text{O(l)}$$

(ii) Reaction of Permanganate ion with Oxalate ion in Acidic Medium:
• Permanganate oxidizes oxalate ion ($\text{C}_2\text{O}_4^{2-}$) to carbon dioxide ($\text{CO}_2$) while getting reduced to $\text{Mn}^{2+}$.
$$2\text{MnO}_4^-\text{(aq)} + 5\text{C}_2\text{O}_4^{2-}\text{(aq)} + 16\text{H}^+\text{(aq)} \rightarrow 2\text{Mn}^{2+}\text{(aq)} + 10\text{CO}_2\text{(g)} + 8\text{H}_2\text{O(l)}$$

Ch-11: Organisms and Populations 12 Questions

1. The branch of ecology that deals with the relationship of an individual organism with its environment is called:

  • A. Autecology
  • B. Synecology
  • C. Population ecology
  • D. Community ecology

Answer: A. Autecology

Explanation: Autecology focuses specifically on how individual species or organisms interact with and adapt to their surrounding environment.

2. An organism's ecological niche refers to its:

  • A. Functional role and specific position within an ecosystem
  • B. Physical location alone
  • C. Population size alone
  • D. Genetic makeup alone

Answer: A. Functional role and specific position within an ecosystem

Explanation: A niche encompasses all aspects of an organism's way of life, including its habitat, feeding habits, and interactions with other species.

3. Organisms capable of maintaining a constant internal environment despite changes in their external surroundings are called:

  • A. Regulators
  • B. Conformers
  • C. Migrants
  • D. Suspended organisms

Answer: A. Regulators

Explanation: Regulators, such as mammals maintaining a constant body temperature, use physiological mechanisms to keep their internal environment stable.

4. Population density is defined as the:

  • A. Number of individuals of a species present per unit area or volume
  • B. Total number of different species found in an area
  • C. Overall rate of births occurring in a population
  • D. Overall rate of deaths occurring in a population

Answer: A. Number of individuals of a species present per unit area or volume

Explanation: Population density gives a measure of how crowded or sparse a particular species is within a defined space.

5. The exponential growth model of population growth assumes that:

  • A. Resources available to the population are unlimited
  • B. Resources available to the population are always limited
  • C. The population maintains a zero birth rate
  • D. The population size remains constant over time

Answer: A. Resources available to the population are unlimited

Explanation: Under this idealized model, with unlimited resources, a population can grow continuously without any check on its growth rate.

6. The logistic growth model differs from the exponential model by incorporating the concept of:

  • A. The carrying capacity of the environment
  • B. Completely unlimited resource availability
  • C. A permanently zero mortality rate
  • D. A constant, unchanging rate of immigration

Answer: A. The carrying capacity of the environment

Explanation: The logistic model accounts for environmental limitations, causing population growth to slow as it approaches the environment's carrying capacity.

7. Age pyramids, used in the study of population ecology, are primarily used to represent:

  • A. The distribution of individuals across different age groups within a population
  • B. The physical height of individual organisms
  • C. The overall geographic spread of a population
  • D. The genetic diversity present within a population

Answer: A. The distribution of individuals across different age groups within a population

Explanation: Age pyramids visually depict the proportion of pre-reproductive, reproductive, and post-reproductive individuals, helping predict future population trends.

8. Commensalism is a type of interspecific interaction in which:

  • A. One species benefits, while the other is neither significantly helped nor harmed
  • B. Both interacting species benefit equally
  • C. Both interacting species are harmed
  • D. One species benefits directly at the expense of harming the other

Answer: A. One species benefits, while the other is neither significantly helped nor harmed

Explanation: In commensalism, only one partner gains an advantage from the relationship, while the other experiences no notable effect.

9. Mutualism is an ecological interaction in which:

  • A. Both of the interacting species benefit from their association
  • B. Only one of the two species benefits
  • C. Both interacting species are harmed
  • D. One species benefits while directly harming the other

Answer: A. Both of the interacting species benefit from their association

Explanation: Mutualistic relationships, such as that between pollinators and flowering plants, provide reciprocal benefits to both participating species.

10. Parasitism is an interaction characterized by:

  • A. One species, the parasite, benefiting at the direct expense of the other, the host
  • B. Both interacting species benefiting equally from the association
  • C. Neither of the two interacting species being affected
  • D. Both interacting species being harmed to an equal extent

Answer: A. One species, the parasite, benefiting at the direct expense of the other, the host

Explanation: Parasites derive nutrition and shelter from their host, often causing harm to the host in the process.

11. Adaptations commonly shown by organisms living in extremely cold climates include having:

  • A. Thicker fur along with a layer of insulating body fat
  • B. Thinner fur and a complete absence of any fat layer
  • C. A relatively larger surface area in proportion to body volume
  • D. A complete absence of any physiological or structural adaptation

Answer: A. Thicker fur along with a layer of insulating body fat

Explanation: Thick fur and fat layers help reduce heat loss, allowing organisms to maintain their body temperature in extremely cold environments.

12. The overall growth rate of a population is influenced mainly by the combined effects of:

  • A. Natality, mortality, immigration and emigration
  • B. The birth rate alone, without any other factor
  • C. The death rate alone, without any other factor
  • D. Immigration alone, without any other factor

Answer: A. Natality, mortality, immigration and emigration

Explanation: Population size changes over time as a net result of these four fundamental demographic processes acting together.

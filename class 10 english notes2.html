NCERT Solutions for Class 10 English Chapter 2 Nelson Mandela Long Walk to Freedom

NCERT Solutions for Class 10 English First Flight Chapter 2 Nelson Mandela Long Walk to Freedom covers Mandela's inauguration speech, his reflections on apartheid and freedom, and important questions and answers.

1. About the Chapter and Setting

Short Note:

• This chapter is an excerpt from Nelson Mandela's autobiography, describing the historic day of his inauguration as the first democratically elected President of South Africa in 1994.

• Mandela reflects on the significance of this moment, describing the presence of world leaders and the pride of South Africans finally living in a free, democratic nation after decades of apartheid.

• He recalls the painful history of apartheid, a system of institutionalised racial segregation that denied basic rights and dignity to the black population of South Africa.

2. Key Ideas and Reflections

Short Note:

• Mandela describes the twin obligations every individual carries: one to family and community, and one to the country and its people, emphasising service to the nation as the highest calling.

• He explains that true freedom is not just for the oppressed but must also liberate the oppressor, since both are diminished by injustice, and hatred only imprisons the hater as well as the hated.

• He describes his long walk to freedom as an ongoing journey, noting that gaining freedom only reveals further responsibilities and that there are still many more hills to climb.

3. Theme & Message

Short Note:

• The chapter's central theme is the struggle against injustice and the pursuit of freedom, dignity and equality for all people regardless of race.

• It emphasises courage, sacrifice, and the idea that true liberation must free both the oppressed and the oppressor from the chains of hatred and prejudice.

• Mandela's message inspires readers to see freedom not as a final destination but as a continuous journey of responsibility and progress.

Reading With Insight (Textbook Questions)

Q1: What were the twin obligations Mandela speaks of?
Answer: Mandela speaks of an obligation to one's family, parents, wife and children, and a second obligation to one's people, community and country, explaining that in a country like South Africa under apartheid, fulfilling obligations to one's people often made it impossible to fulfil obligations to one's immediate family.
Q2: According to Mandela, why is a man who takes away another's freedom also a prisoner?
Answer: According to Mandela, an oppressor is also robbed of his own humanity and freedom because hatred, prejudice and narrow-mindedness imprison him behind the bars of his own bigotry, making him as much a victim of the unjust system as the person he oppresses.
Q3: What does Mandela mean when he says the 'true test of our devotion to freedom is just beginning'?
Answer: Mandela means that gaining political freedom and the right to vote was only the first step, and the harder task of building genuine equality, opportunity and dignity for all South Africans, especially addressing poverty and deprivation, still lay ahead.

Extract-Based / Short Answer Questions

Q4: How does Mandela describe the significance of his inauguration ceremony?
Answer: Mandela describes the ceremony as historic and deeply moving, symbolising the end of centuries of racial oppression and the birth of a new, inclusive nation, made especially significant by the presence of leaders and dignitaries from across the world who came to witness South Africa's peaceful transition to democracy.
Q5: What does Mandela mean by 'a long walk to freedom'?
Answer: Mandela uses this phrase to describe his personal and his nation's prolonged, difficult journey towards liberation from apartheid, while also suggesting that true freedom is an ongoing pursuit that continues even after formal, legal equality is achieved.

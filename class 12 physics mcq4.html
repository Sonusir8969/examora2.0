Ch-4: Moving Charges and Magnetism 25 Questions

1. What is the SI unit of Magnetic Field (B)?

  • A. Weber
  • B. Tesla
  • C. Gauss
  • D. Ampere/meter

Answer: B. Tesla

Explanation: The SI unit of magnetic field strength (or magnetic flux density) is Tesla (T), where 1 Tesla = 1 Newton / (Ampere · meter).

2. Oersted's experiment demonstrated that:

  • A. Changing magnetic fields induce electric current
  • B. An electric current produces a magnetic field around it
  • C. Magnetic monopoles exist
  • D. Light is an electromagnetic wave

Answer: B. An electric current produces a magnetic field around it

Explanation: Hans Christian Oersted discovered in 1820 that a magnetic needle deflects near a current-carrying wire, proving electric current creates a magnetic field.

3. A charge 'q' moving with velocity 'v' in a magnetic field 'B' experiences a Lorentz magnetic force given by:

  • A. F = q (v × B)
  • B. F = q (v · B)
  • C. F = (v × B) / q
  • D. F = q v B cosθ

Answer: A. F = q (v × B)

Explanation: Magnetic force on a moving charge is a vector cross product: F = q (v × B), with magnitude F = q v B sinθ.

4. What is the magnetic force on a charge moving parallel (θ = 0°) to the direction of a magnetic field?

  • A. Maximum (q v B)
  • B. Zero
  • C. q v B / 2
  • D. Infinite

Answer: B. Zero

Explanation: Since F = q v B sinθ and θ = 0°, sin 0° = 0, no magnetic force acts on a charge moving parallel or antiparallel to the field.

5. The path of a charged particle projected perpendicularly (θ = 90°) into a uniform magnetic field is a:

  • A. Straight line
  • B. Circle
  • C. Helix
  • D. Parabola

Answer: B. Circle

Explanation: When v is perpendicular to B, the magnetic force acts as a centripetal force (F = q v B = m v² / r), forcing the particle into a circular path.

6. If a charged particle enters a uniform magnetic field at an arbitrary angle θ (other than 0°, 90°, 180°), its path is:

  • A. Circular
  • B. Helical
  • C. Elliptical
  • D. Straight line

Answer: B. Helical

Explanation: The velocity component parallel to field causes linear motion while perpendicular component causes circular motion, producing a helical path.

7. Work done by a magnetic field on a moving charged particle is always:

  • A. Positive
  • B. Negative
  • C. Zero
  • D. Dependent on charge magnitude

Answer: C. Zero

Explanation: The magnetic force F is always perpendicular to velocity v (displacement). Work done W = F · d = 0, so magnetic fields do no work and kinetic energy remains constant.

8. According to Biot-Savart Law, the magnetic field dB due to a current element I dl at distance r varies as:

  • A. dB ∝ 1/r
  • B. dB ∝ 1/r²
  • C. dB ∝ r²
  • D. dB ∝ 1/r³

Answer: B. dB ∝ 1/r²

Explanation: Biot-Savart Law states dB = (μ₀ / 4π) * (I dl sinθ / r²), obeying an inverse-square distance dependence.

9. What is the value of permeability of free space (μ₀)?

  • A. 4π × 10^-7 T m A^-1
  • B. 9 × 10^9 N m² C^-2
  • C. 8.85 × 10^-12 C² N^-1 m^-2
  • D. 1.6 × 10^-19 C

Answer: A. 4π × 10^-7 T m A^-1

Explanation: The magnetic permeability constant of free space μ₀ is exactly 4π × 10^-7 T m A^-1 (or N/A²).

10. The magnetic field at the center of a circular current-carrying loop of radius R and N turns is:

  • A. B = μ₀ N I / (2 R)
  • B. B = μ₀ N I / (4π R)
  • C. B = μ₀ N I / (2π R)
  • D. B = μ₀ N I / R²

Answer: A. B = μ₀ N I / (2 R)

Explanation: Using Biot-Savart Law, magnetic field at center of a circular loop is B = μ₀ I / (2R); for N turns, B = μ₀ N I / (2R).

11. Ampere’s Circuital Law is expressed mathematically as:

  • A. ∮ B · dl = μ₀ I_enclosed
  • B. ∮ E · dl = -dΦ/dt
  • C. ∮ B · dA = 0
  • D. ∮ E · dA = Q/ε₀

Answer: A. ∮ B · dl = μ₀ I_enclosed

Explanation: Ampere's Law states line integral of magnetic field along any closed loop equals μ₀ times net current passing through loop area.

12. Magnetic field at distance 'r' from a long straight current-carrying wire carrying current 'I' is:

  • A. B = μ₀ I / (2π r)
  • B. B = μ₀ I / (4π r)
  • C. B = μ₀ I / (2 r)
  • D. B = 2 μ₀ I / r

Answer: A. B = μ₀ I / (2π r)

Explanation: Applying Ampere's Law around a circular path gives B(2πr) = μ₀ I, hence B = μ₀ I / (2π r).

13. Magnetic field inside a long straight ideal solenoid with 'n' turns per unit length carrying current 'I' is:

  • A. B = μ₀ n I
  • B. B = μ₀ N I / (2 L)
  • C. B = 0
  • D. B = μ₀ n I / 2

Answer: A. B = μ₀ n I

Explanation: Magnetic field inside a long tight solenoid is uniform and given by B = μ₀ n I, where n = N/L is turns per unit length.

14. What is the magnetic field outside an ideal long solenoid?

  • A. Maximum
  • B. Zero
  • C. μ₀ n I
  • D. Infinite

Answer: B. Zero

Explanation: For an ideal, infinitely long solenoid, field lines cancel outside, resulting in negligible (zero) magnetic field.

15. Force per unit length between two parallel straight current-carrying conductors separated by distance 'd' carrying currents I₁ and I₂ is:

  • A. F/L = (μ₀ I₁ I₂) / (2π d)
  • B. F/L = (μ₀ I₁ I₂) / (4π d²)
  • C. F/L = (μ₀ I₁ I₂) / (2 d)
  • D. F/L = μ₀ (I₁ + I₂) / d

Answer: A. F/L = (μ₀ I₁ I₂) / (2π d)

Explanation: Force per unit length between two long parallel wires is given by F/L = (μ₀ I₁ I₂) / (2π d).

16. Two parallel wires carrying current in the SAME direction will:

  • A. Repel each other
  • B. Attract each other
  • C. Neither attract nor repel
  • D. Rotate around each other

Answer: B. Attract each other

Explanation: Like currents (in same direction) attract each other, whereas opposite (antiparallel) currents repel each other due to magnetic forces.

17. Torque (τ) experienced by a current loop of magnetic dipole moment 'M' in a uniform magnetic field 'B' is:

  • A. τ = M × B
  • B. τ = M · B
  • C. τ = B × M
  • D. τ = M / B

Answer: A. τ = M × B

Explanation: Torque on a magnetic dipole in a magnetic field is τ = M × B, with magnitude τ = M B sinθ.

18. Magnetic dipole moment (M) of a current loop of N turns, carrying current I and enclosing area A is:

  • A. M = N I A
  • B. M = N I / A
  • C. M = I A / N
  • D. M = N A / I

Answer: A. M = N I A

Explanation: Magnetic moment of planar loop is vector product M = N I A, with direction given by right-hand thumb rule.

19. What is the gyromagnetic ratio of an electron revolving in an atom?

  • A. e / (2 m_e)
  • B. e / m_e
  • C. 2 e / m_e
  • D. e m_e / 2

Answer: A. e / (2 m_e)

Explanation: Gyromagnetic ratio is ratio of orbital magnetic moment to orbital angular momentum: μ_l / L = e / (2 m_e) ≈ 8.8 × 10^10 C/kg.

20. What is a Moving Coil Galvanometer used to measure or detect?

  • A. High voltage
  • B. Small electric currents
  • C. Resistance only
  • D. Magnetic field directly

Answer: B. Small electric currents

Explanation: Moving coil galvanometer is a sensitive instrument designed to detect and measure small currents in a circuit.

21. To convert a galvanometer into an ammeter, we connect:

  • A. Low resistance (shunt) in parallel
  • B. High resistance in series
  • C. High resistance in parallel
  • D. Low resistance in series

Answer: A. Low resistance (shunt) in parallel

Explanation: Connecting a low resistance (shunt) in parallel allows most current to bypass coil, converting galvanometer to ammeter.

22. To convert a galvanometer into a voltmeter, we connect:

  • A. High resistance in series
  • B. Low resistance in parallel
  • C. Low resistance in series
  • D. High resistance in parallel

Answer: A. High resistance in series

Explanation: A high resistance connected in series limits current through galvanometer when connected across potential difference, acting as a voltmeter.

23. What is the resistance of an ideal ammeter and an ideal voltmeter respectively?

  • A. Zero and Infinite
  • B. Infinite and Zero
  • C. Zero and Zero
  • D. Infinite and Infinite

Answer: A. Zero and Infinite

Explanation: Ideal ammeter has zero resistance to avoid altering circuit current; ideal voltmeter has infinite resistance so it draws no current.

24. What is current sensitivity of a galvanometer?

  • A. Deflection per unit current (θ / I = NBA / k)
  • B. Current per unit deflection
  • C. Deflection per unit voltage
  • D. Resistance per unit deflection

Answer: A. Deflection per unit current (θ / I = NBA / k)

Explanation: Current sensitivity is deflection produced per unit current: I_s = θ / I = (N B A) / k, where k is torsional constant.

25. In a Velocity Selector, charged particles pass undeflected when electric (E) and magnetic (B) fields satisfy:

  • A. v = E / B
  • B. v = B / E
  • C. v = E × B
  • D. v = E² / B

Answer: A. v = E / B

Explanation: Electric force qE equals magnetic force qvB when fields are mutually perpendicular and velocity v = E / B, allowing particle to pass un-deflected.

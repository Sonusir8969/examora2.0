Chem-12: Environmental Chemistry 30 Questions

1. The branch of chemistry that studies the chemical processes occurring in the environment is called:

  • A. Environmental chemistry
  • B. Organic chemistry
  • C. Analytical chemistry only
  • D. Nuclear chemistry only

Answer: A. Environmental chemistry

Explanation: Environmental chemistry specifically studies the chemical reactions and processes occurring in air, water and soil within the environment.

2. The layer of gases surrounding the Earth is called the:

  • A. Atmosphere
  • B. Hydrosphere
  • C. Lithosphere
  • D. Biosphere

Answer: A. Atmosphere

Explanation: The atmosphere refers to the layer of gases enveloping the Earth, essential for supporting life and regulating climate.

3. The region of the atmosphere closest to the Earth's surface, where weather phenomena occur, is called the:

  • A. Troposphere
  • B. Stratosphere
  • C. Mesosphere
  • D. Thermosphere

Answer: A. Troposphere

Explanation: The troposphere is the lowest atmospheric layer, extending from the Earth's surface up to about 10-15 km, where most weather occurs.

4. The atmospheric layer containing the protective ozone layer is called the:

  • A. Stratosphere
  • B. Troposphere
  • C. Mesosphere
  • D. Thermosphere

Answer: A. Stratosphere

Explanation: The stratosphere, located above the troposphere, contains the ozone layer that absorbs harmful ultraviolet radiation from the sun.

5. The undesirable change in the natural composition of air, water or soil due to human activities is called:

  • A. Pollution
  • B. Photosynthesis
  • C. Respiration
  • D. Precipitation

Answer: A. Pollution

Explanation: Pollution refers to the introduction of harmful substances or contaminants into the environment, causing adverse changes.

6. A substance that causes pollution, present in the environment in harmful concentrations, is called a:

  • A. Pollutant
  • B. Catalyst
  • C. Nutrient
  • D. Reagent

Answer: A. Pollutant

Explanation: A pollutant is any substance present in the environment at concentrations high enough to cause harmful effects.

7. The phenomenon in which certain gases trap heat in the Earth's atmosphere, warming the planet, is called the:

  • A. Greenhouse effect
  • B. Ozone depletion
  • C. Acid rain
  • D. Photochemical smog

Answer: A. Greenhouse effect

Explanation: The greenhouse effect describes how certain atmospheric gases trap outgoing infrared radiation, keeping the Earth's surface warm.

8. Which of the following is considered a major greenhouse gas contributing to global warming?

  • A. Carbon dioxide
  • B. Nitrogen gas
  • C. Oxygen gas
  • D. Argon

Answer: A. Carbon dioxide

Explanation: Carbon dioxide is one of the primary greenhouse gases, significantly contributing to the trapping of heat and subsequent global warming.

9. The depletion of the protective ozone layer is primarily caused by:

  • A. Chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs)
  • B. Carbon dioxide only
  • C. Nitrogen gas only
  • D. Water vapour only

Answer: A. Chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs)

Explanation: Chlorofluorocarbons, once widely used in refrigerants and aerosols, release chlorine atoms that catalytically destroy stratospheric ozone molecules.

10. Acid rain is primarily caused by atmospheric pollution from oxides of:

  • A. Sulphur and nitrogen
  • B. Carbon only
  • C. Oxygen only
  • D. Hydrogen only

Answer: A. Sulphur and nitrogen

Explanation: Sulphur dioxide and nitrogen oxides released into the atmosphere react with water vapour to form sulphuric and nitric acids, resulting in acid rain.

11. The presence of excessive smoke combined with fog, particularly in urban and industrial areas, is called:

  • A. Smog
  • B. Acid rain
  • C. Ozone hole
  • D. Eutrophication

Answer: A. Smog

Explanation: Smog is a form of air pollution resulting from the combination of smoke and fog, often intensified by vehicle and industrial emissions.

12. Photochemical smog is formed through reactions triggered by sunlight involving hydrocarbons and:

  • A. Nitrogen oxides
  • B. Carbon dioxide only
  • C. Water vapour only
  • D. Oxygen only

Answer: A. Nitrogen oxides

Explanation: Photochemical smog forms when sunlight drives reactions between hydrocarbons and nitrogen oxides, producing secondary pollutants like ozone at ground level.

13. The excessive growth of algae in water bodies due to nutrient enrichment, often from fertiliser runoff, is called:

  • A. Eutrophication
  • B. Photosynthesis
  • C. Biomagnification
  • D. Bioremediation

Answer: A. Eutrophication

Explanation: Eutrophication describes the process where excess nutrients, particularly nitrogen and phosphorus, cause excessive algal growth in aquatic ecosystems.

14. The measure of the amount of oxygen required by microorganisms to decompose organic matter in water is called:

  • A. Biochemical Oxygen Demand (BOD)
  • B. Chemical Oxygen Demand only
  • C. Dissolved oxygen content only
  • D. pH of water only

Answer: A. Biochemical Oxygen Demand (BOD)

Explanation: BOD specifically measures the oxygen consumed by microorganisms during the decomposition of organic matter, indicating the level of organic pollution in water.

15. A high BOD value in water generally indicates:

  • A. High levels of organic pollution
  • B. Very clean, pure water
  • C. No microbial activity
  • D. High oxygen content

Answer: A. High levels of organic pollution

Explanation: A high BOD value signifies substantial organic pollution, as more oxygen is being consumed by microorganisms breaking down the excess organic material.

16. The accumulation and increasing concentration of harmful substances at successive levels of a food chain is called:

  • A. Biomagnification
  • B. Eutrophication
  • C. Photosynthesis
  • D. Precipitation

Answer: A. Biomagnification

Explanation: Biomagnification refers to the increasing concentration of toxic substances as they move up through successive trophic levels in a food chain.

17. Pesticides like DDT are known for causing environmental harm primarily due to their:

  • A. Persistence and tendency to biomagnify in food chains
  • B. Complete and rapid biodegradability
  • C. Total lack of toxicity
  • D. Instant evaporation into the atmosphere

Answer: A. Persistence and tendency to biomagnify in food chains

Explanation: DDT and similar pesticides persist in the environment for long periods and accumulate in increasing concentrations up the food chain, causing significant ecological harm.

18. The primary greenhouse gas released from the combustion of fossil fuels is:

  • A. Carbon dioxide
  • B. Nitrogen
  • C. Argon
  • D. Helium

Answer: A. Carbon dioxide

Explanation: Burning fossil fuels such as coal, oil and natural gas releases substantial amounts of carbon dioxide, a major contributor to the greenhouse effect.

19. Particulate matter pollution in the atmosphere consists of tiny solid or liquid particles that can cause:

  • A. Respiratory health problems
  • B. Improved air quality
  • C. Ozone layer thickening
  • D. Reduced global warming entirely

Answer: A. Respiratory health problems

Explanation: Fine particulate matter suspended in air can penetrate deep into the lungs, causing significant respiratory and cardiovascular health problems.

20. The process of treating pollutants using biological organisms, such as bacteria, to break them down is called:

  • A. Bioremediation
  • B. Eutrophication
  • C. Biomagnification
  • D. Photosynthesis

Answer: A. Bioremediation

Explanation: Bioremediation utilises living organisms, particularly microorganisms, to degrade and remove pollutants from a contaminated environment.

21. Green chemistry aims to design chemical processes and products that:

  • A. Reduce or eliminate the use and generation of hazardous substances
  • B. Maximise the use of toxic chemicals
  • C. Ignore environmental impact entirely
  • D. Increase waste production

Answer: A. Reduce or eliminate the use and generation of hazardous substances

Explanation: Green chemistry focuses on designing safer, more sustainable chemical processes that minimise environmental harm and hazardous waste generation.

22. Which of the following is a major source of indoor air pollution?

  • A. Burning of biomass fuels for cooking
  • B. Fresh outdoor air
  • C. Sunlight entering through windows
  • D. Rainwater collection

Answer: A. Burning of biomass fuels for cooking

Explanation: The burning of biomass fuels like wood or dung for cooking, particularly in poorly ventilated spaces, is a significant contributor to indoor air pollution.

23. The hole in the ozone layer, observed primarily over Antarctica, results in increased exposure to:

  • A. Harmful ultraviolet radiation
  • B. Visible light only
  • C. Infrared radiation only
  • D. Radio waves

Answer: A. Harmful ultraviolet radiation

Explanation: Depletion of the ozone layer over Antarctica allows greater penetration of harmful ultraviolet radiation, posing risks to living organisms.

24. Which international agreement was established to phase out the production and use of ozone-depleting substances?

  • A. Montreal Protocol
  • B. Kyoto Protocol only
  • C. Paris Agreement only
  • D. Rio Declaration only

Answer: A. Montreal Protocol

Explanation: The Montreal Protocol is the landmark international treaty specifically aimed at phasing out substances responsible for depleting the ozone layer.

25. Water pollution can result from the discharge of untreated industrial waste, sewage and:

  • A. Agricultural runoff containing fertilisers and pesticides
  • B. Only clean rainwater
  • C. Only purified drinking water
  • D. Only distilled water

Answer: A. Agricultural runoff containing fertilisers and pesticides

Explanation: Agricultural runoff, carrying excess fertilisers and pesticides into water bodies, is a significant contributor to water pollution alongside industrial and sewage discharge.

26. The term 'sustainable development' refers to development that meets present needs without:

  • A. Compromising the ability of future generations to meet their own needs
  • B. Using any natural resources at all
  • C. Focusing only on economic growth
  • D. Ignoring environmental concerns entirely

Answer: A. Compromising the ability of future generations to meet their own needs

Explanation: Sustainable development balances current human needs with the responsibility of preserving resources and environmental quality for future generations.

27. Reducing, reusing and recycling materials is a strategy primarily aimed at:

  • A. Minimising waste generation and environmental impact
  • B. Increasing overall consumption
  • C. Eliminating all industrial activity
  • D. Producing more plastic waste

Answer: A. Minimising waste generation and environmental impact

Explanation: The 'reduce, reuse, recycle' approach is a fundamental waste management strategy designed to lessen the environmental burden of waste generation.

28. Carbon monoxide is a particularly dangerous air pollutant because it:

  • A. Binds strongly to haemoglobin, reducing oxygen transport in blood
  • B. Has no effect on the human body
  • C. Is beneficial for respiration
  • D. Only affects plants, not animals

Answer: A. Binds strongly to haemoglobin, reducing oxygen transport in blood

Explanation: Carbon monoxide binds to haemoglobin far more strongly than oxygen does, severely impairing the blood's ability to transport oxygen throughout the body.

29. Which layer of soil, water and rock forming the Earth's outer solid layer, is often affected by pollution from waste disposal?

  • A. Lithosphere
  • B. Atmosphere
  • C. Hydrosphere only
  • D. Ionosphere

Answer: A. Lithosphere

Explanation: The lithosphere, comprising the Earth's crust and soil, is significantly impacted by pollution resulting from improper waste disposal and industrial activity.

30. Environmental chemistry emphasises understanding the sources, reactions, and effects of pollutants primarily to:

  • A. Develop effective strategies for pollution control and prevention
  • B. Increase industrial pollution output
  • C. Ignore the impact of human activity
  • D. Promote the unchecked use of hazardous chemicals

Answer: A. Develop effective strategies for pollution control and prevention

Explanation: A core goal of environmental chemistry is to understand pollutant behaviour thoroughly enough to design effective methods for controlling and preventing environmental damage.

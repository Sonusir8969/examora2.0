Ch-3: Classification of Elements and Periodicity 30 Questions

1. The modern periodic law was given by:

  • A. Henry Moseley
  • B. Dmitri Mendeleev
  • C. John Newlands
  • D. Johann Dobereiner

Answer: A. Henry Moseley

Explanation: Henry Moseley showed that atomic number is a more fundamental property of an element than its atomic mass.

2. Modern periodic law states that physical and chemical properties of elements are periodic functions of their:

  • A. Atomic numbers
  • B. Atomic masses
  • C. Mass numbers
  • D. Densities

Answer: A. Atomic numbers

Explanation: Modern periodic law arranges elements in order of increasing atomic numbers.

3. How many periods and groups are present in the long form of the periodic table?

  • A. 7 periods and 18 groups
  • B. 6 periods and 16 groups
  • C. 7 periods and 8 groups
  • D. 8 periods and 18 groups

Answer: A. 7 periods and 18 groups

Explanation: The modern periodic table consists of 7 horizontal rows called periods and 18 vertical columns called groups.

4. Which of the following element blocks contains metals, non-metals, and metalloids?

  • A. p-block
  • B. s-block
  • C. d-block
  • D. f-block

Answer: A. p-block

Explanation: The p-block is the only region in the periodic table that contains metals, non-metals, and metalloids.

5. Elements present in Group 17 of the periodic table are commonly known as:

  • A. Halogens
  • B. Alkali metals
  • C. Chalcogens
  • D. Noble gases

Answer: A. Halogens

Explanation: Group 17 elements (F, Cl, Br, I, At) are salt formers, called halogens.

6. General electronic configuration of d-block elements is:

  • A. (n-1)d¹⁻¹⁰ ns¹⁻²
  • B. (n-1)d¹⁻¹⁰ ns² np⁶
  • C. ns¹⁻² np¹⁻⁶
  • D. (n-2)f¹⁻¹⁴ ns²

Answer: A. (n-1)d¹⁻¹⁰ ns¹⁻²

Explanation: Transition elements have incompletely filled d-orbitals in their penultimate shell with outer configuration (n-1)d¹⁻¹⁰ ns¹⁻².

7. Which of the following element has the highest electronegativity?

  • A. Fluorine (F)
  • B. Chlorine (Cl)
  • C. Oxygen (O)
  • D. Nitrogen (N)

Answer: A. Fluorine (F)

Explanation: Fluorine is the most electronegative element with a value of 4.0 on Pauling's scale.

8. Which element has the maximum negative electron gain enthalpy?

  • A. Chlorine (Cl)
  • B. Fluorine (F)
  • C. Bromine (Br)
  • D. Oxygen (O)

Answer: A. Chlorine (Cl)

Explanation: Chlorine has a higher negative electron gain enthalpy than fluorine due to weaker inter-electronic repulsions in its larger 3p orbital.

9. Moving down a group in the periodic table, atomic radius generally:

  • A. Increases
  • B. Decreases
  • C. Remains constant
  • D. First decreases then increases

Answer: A. Increases

Explanation: As you move down a group, new principal energy shells are added, increasing atomic size.

10. Across a period from left to right, ionization enthalpy generally:

  • A. Increases
  • B. Decreases
  • C. Remains same
  • D. First increases then decreases

Answer: A. Increases

Explanation: Effective nuclear charge increases across a period, attracting valence electrons more strongly and raising ionization enthalpy.

11. What is the IUPAC official name for the element with atomic number 104?

  • A. Rutherfordium
  • B. Dubnium
  • C. Seaborgium
  • D. Bohrium

Answer: A. Rutherfordium

Explanation: Element 104 (Unnilquadium) is officially named Rutherfordium (Rf).

12. Which element belongs to the 3d transition series?

  • A. Iron (Fe)
  • B. Silver (Ag)
  • C. Gold (Au)
  • D. Mercury (Hg)

Answer: A. Iron (Fe)

Explanation: Iron (Z=26) belongs to the 3d transition series spanning Scandium (Z=21) to Zinc (Z=30).

13. Which of the following is an amphoteric oxide?

  • A. Al₂O₃
  • B. Na₂O
  • C. SO₃
  • D. CaO

Answer: A. Al₂O₃

Explanation: Aluminium oxide (Al₂O₃) reacts with both acids and bases, making it an amphoteric oxide.

14. The diagonal relationship is observed between elements of:

  • A. 2nd and 3rd period
  • B. 1st and 2nd period
  • C. 3rd and 4th period
  • D. 4th and 5th period

Answer: A. 2nd and 3rd period

Explanation: Certain period 2 elements (e.g., Li, Be, B) show similar properties to period 3 elements located diagonally to them (e.g., Mg, Al, Si).

15. Among the following species, which one has the smallest ionic size?

  • A. Al³⁺
  • B. Mg²⁺
  • C. Na⁺
  • D. F⁻

Answer: A. Al³⁺

Explanation: Among isoelectronic ions, the ion with the highest positive nuclear charge (Al³⁺, Z=13) has the smallest size.

16. The screening effect of d-electrons is:

  • A. Less than p-electrons
  • B. Equal to s-electrons
  • C. Greater than s-electrons
  • D. Equal to p-electrons

Answer: A. Less than p-electrons

Explanation: The shielding or screening effect follows the order: s > p > d > f.

17. Which group elements are called Alkali Metals?

  • A. Group 1
  • B. Group 2
  • C. Group 16
  • D. Group 18

Answer: A. Group 1

Explanation: Group 1 elements (excluding Hydrogen) form hydroxides that are strongly alkaline, so they are named alkali metals.

18. Lanthanoids and Actinoids are placed in which block of the periodic table?

  • A. f-block
  • B. d-block
  • C. p-block
  • D. s-block

Answer: A. f-block

Explanation: Elements filling (n-2)f orbitals are grouped at the bottom of the periodic table in the f-block.

19. The IUPAC temporary symbol for element with atomic number 120 is:

  • A. Ubn
  • B. Unn
  • C. Ube
  • D. Ubo

Answer: A. Ubn

Explanation: 1 = un, 2 = bi, 0 = nil -> Unbinilium (Symbol: Ubn).

20. The first ionization enthalpy of Nitrogen is higher than Oxygen because:

  • A. Nitrogen has extra stability due to half-filled p-subshell
  • B. Nitrogen is smaller in size than oxygen
  • C. Oxygen has more nuclear charge
  • D. Nitrogen is a gas

Answer: A. Nitrogen has extra stability due to half-filled p-subshell

Explanation: Nitrogen has a 2p³ configuration (half-filled), which requires higher energy to remove an electron compared to Oxygen (2p⁴).

21. Which element has the highest first ionization enthalpy in the periodic table?

  • A. Helium (He)
  • B. Fluorine (F)
  • C. Neon (Ne)
  • D. Hydrogen (H)

Answer: A. Helium (He)

Explanation: Helium has the smallest atomic size and a stable 1s² completely filled duplet configuration.

22. Which of the following oxides is most basic in nature?

  • A. Na₂O
  • B. MgO
  • C. Al₂O₃
  • D. SiO₂

Answer: A. Na₂O

Explanation: Basic character of oxides increases as metallic character increases. Sodium is the most metallic among the given options.

23. Dobereiner's Law of Triads was based on:

  • A. Atomic mass
  • B. Atomic number
  • C. Valency
  • D. Number of electrons

Answer: A. Atomic mass

Explanation: Dobereiner arranged elements in groups of three (triads) where the middle element's atomic mass was roughly the average of the other two.

24. Newlands' Law of Octaves was applicable up to which element?

  • A. Calcium
  • B. Potassium
  • C. Iron
  • D. Cobalt

Answer: A. Calcium

Explanation: The octave pattern worked only for lighter elements up to Calcium (Z=20).

25. Valence electron configuration of alkali metals is:

  • A. ns¹
  • B. ns²
  • C. ns² np¹
  • D. ns² np⁵

Answer: A. ns¹

Explanation: All Group 1 elements possess a single electron in their outermost s-subshell.

26. Which element exhibits diagonal relationship with Beryllium (Be)?

  • A. Aluminium (Al)
  • B. Lithium (Li)
  • C. Magnesium (Mg)
  • D. Silicon (Si)

Answer: A. Aluminium (Al)

Explanation: Beryllium (Period 2, Group 2) shares similar chemical properties with Aluminium (Period 3, Group 13).

27. An element with atomic number 17 belongs to which period and group?

  • A. Period 3, Group 17
  • B. Period 2, Group 17
  • C. Period 3, Group 7
  • D. Period 4, Group 17

Answer: A. Period 3, Group 17

Explanation: Z = 17 is Chlorine ([Ne] 3s² 3p⁵), placing it in the 3rd period and 17th group.

28. Which of the following properties generally decreases across a period from left to right?

  • A. Metallic character
  • B. Ionization enthalpy
  • C. Electronegativity
  • D. Non-metallic character

Answer: A. Metallic character

Explanation: Elements lose electron-donating ability across a period, leading to a decrease in metallic character.

29. The size of an anion is always:

  • A. Larger than its parent atom
  • B. Smaller than its parent atom
  • C. Equal to its parent atom
  • D. Half of its parent atom

Answer: A. Larger than its parent atom

Explanation: Addition of electrons increases inter-electronic repulsion and lowers effective nuclear charge per electron, expanding the electron cloud.

30. Noble gases have zero or positive electron gain enthalpy because of their:

  • A. Completely filled stable electronic configuration
  • B. Small size
  • C. Low ionization enthalpy
  • D. High electronegativity

Answer: A. Completely filled stable electronic configuration

Explanation: Noble gases have fully filled shells (ns² np⁶), making incoming electron addition endothermic.

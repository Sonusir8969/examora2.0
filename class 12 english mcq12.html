Ch-12: A Roadside Stand 5 Questions

1. Who is the poet of 'A Roadside Stand'?

  • A. Robert Frost
  • B. Pablo Neruda
  • C. John Keats
  • D. Kamala Das

Answer: A. Robert Frost

Explanation: 'A Roadside Stand' is a poem written by the American poet Robert Frost, addressing themes of rural poverty and urban indifference.

2. What does the roadside stand in the poem represent for the poor rural family who built it?

  • A. A permanent government shelter
  • B. A small, desperate hope of earning some money from passing city travellers
  • C. A tourist attraction built by the government
  • D. An abandoned building

Answer: B. A small, desperate hope of earning some money from passing city travellers

Explanation: The roadside stand symbolizes the impoverished rural family's meager, hopeful attempt to earn a little extra money by selling their produce or wares to travellers speeding along the highway.

3. How does the poet describe the attitude of the wealthy city folk who pass by the stand?

  • A. They stop frequently to help and shop at the stand
  • B. They usually speed past without stopping, showing indifference to the family's hopes and needs
  • C. They donate generously every time
  • D. They wish to buy the land immediately

Answer: B. They usually speed past without stopping, showing indifference to the family's hopes and needs

Explanation: Frost portrays the passing city travellers as largely indifferent and self-absorbed, rarely stopping to actually help or purchase from the roadside stand despite the family's evident need.

4. What does the poet criticize through the phrase referring to 'greedy good-doers' and 'beneficent beasts of prey'?

  • A. Genuine charitable organizations
  • B. Government and social planners whose self-serving development schemes exploit and displace the rural poor under the guise of help
  • C. The rural family itself
  • D. Foreign tourists

Answer: B. Government and social planners whose self-serving development schemes exploit and displace the rural poor under the guise of help

Explanation: Frost sharply criticizes hypocritical reformers and planners who claim to help the rural poor (e.g., through relocation schemes) but are, in reality, more concerned with their own interests, at the cost of the poor's dignity and land.

5. What is the overall theme of 'A Roadside Stand'?

  • A. The beauty of highway travel
  • B. The contrast between rural poverty and urban insensitivity, and criticism of exploitative 'development' policies
  • C. The importance of building better roads
  • D. A celebration of city life

Answer: B. The contrast between rural poverty and urban insensitivity, and criticism of exploitative 'development' policies

Explanation: The poem highlights the stark disparity between struggling rural communities and indifferent urban society, while critiquing so-called development schemes that fail to genuinely serve the poor.

NCERT Solutions for Class 9 English Poem 10 A Slumber Did My Spirit Seal

NCERT Solutions for Class 9 English Beehive Poem 10 A Slumber Did My Spirit Seal by William Wordsworth covers the theme of loss and mortality, poetic devices, and important questions and answers.

1. About the Poet and Poem

Short Note:

• William Wordsworth was a leading English Romantic poet, and this short, deeply moving poem is part of his 'Lucy poems', a series about a girl named Lucy who has died.

• In the first stanza, the poet recalls a time when he was in a dreamlike, unaware state, believing that Lucy seemed untouched by the passage of time or the possibility of death.

• In the second stanza, the poet's tone changes sharply as he confronts the reality that Lucy is now dead, reduced to a lifeless state, moved only passively by the earth's natural rotation, alongside rocks, stones and trees.

2. Theme & Central Idea

Short Note:

• The poem's central theme is the shock and grief of loss, particularly the sudden realisation of a loved one's mortality after a period of naive belief in their permanence.

• It reflects on how death reduces a once vibrant, living person to mere physical matter, subject to the same natural, unfeeling forces as inanimate objects.

• The poem's brevity and simple language intensify its emotional impact, contrasting the poet's earlier peaceful unawareness with his later stark grief.

3. Poetic Devices

Short Note:

• Contrast: The poem contrasts the poet's earlier illusion of Lucy's timelessness with the harsh truth of her death.

• Imagery: Natural imagery of rocks, stones, trees and the earth's diurnal motion conveys the finality and passivity of death.

• Simplicity of Structure: The poem's two short stanzas and plain language reflect the restrained yet profound expression of grief typical of Wordsworth's style.

Reading With Insight (Textbook Questions)

Q1: What does the poet mean by 'a slumber did my spirit seal'?
Answer: The poet means that his mind was in a dreamlike, unaware state, as though sealed by sleep, during which he had no real fear or awareness that Lucy could ever be affected by the ordinary passage of time or by death.
Q2: How does the poet describe Lucy in the first stanza?
Answer: In the first stanza, the poet describes Lucy as seeming almost untouched by earthly years, as if she were a being beyond the reach of time and the usual human fear of death.
Q3: How does the second stanza reveal the truth about Lucy's condition?
Answer: The second stanza reveals that Lucy has died and lost all motion and force of life; she now moves only passively along with the earth's daily rotation, treated no differently from lifeless rocks, stones and trees.

Extract-Based / Short Answer Questions

Q4: What is the significance of comparing Lucy to 'rocks, and stones, and trees'?
Answer: This comparison emphasises the completeness of Lucy's death, showing that she has become as inert and passive as inanimate natural objects, stripped entirely of the life and consciousness she once had.
Q5: What emotional shift takes place between the two stanzas of the poem?
Answer: The poem shifts from a peaceful, almost naive sense of security in the first stanza to a stark, grief-stricken realisation of loss and mortality in the second stanza, capturing the poet's emotional journey in just eight lines.

NCERT Solutions for Class 12 Biology Chapter 10 Biotechnology and its Applications

NCERT Solutions for Class 12 Biology Chapter 10 Biotechnology and its Applications covers applications in agriculture (Bt crops), medicine (recombinant insulin, gene therapy), transgenic animals, and ethical issues like biopiracy and GM debates.

1. Biotechnological Applications in Agriculture

Short Note:

• Genetically Modified (GM) crops have been developed to improve yield, provide resistance to pests, herbicides, or environmental stress, and enhance nutritional value; for example, Bt cotton and Bt brinjal contain genes from the bacterium Bacillus thuringiensis that produce toxins effective against specific insect pests without harming other organisms.

• RNA interference (RNAi) technology has been used to develop pest-resistant plants, such as tobacco plants resistant to nematodes, by silencing specific genes essential to the pest's survival.

2. Biotechnological Applications in Medicine

Short Note:

• Recombinant DNA technology has enabled the large-scale, safe production of therapeutic proteins, such as human insulin (produced using genetically engineered E. coli, marketed as Humulin), overcoming earlier issues with insulin extracted from animal sources.

• Gene therapy is an experimental technique used to correct genetic defects by inserting a normal, functional gene into an individual's cells, as demonstrated in the treatment of Adenosine Deaminase (ADA) deficiency, an immune disorder.

• Molecular diagnosis techniques such as PCR and ELISA allow for the early and accurate detection of diseases, including genetic disorders and infections like HIV, often before symptoms even appear.

3. Transgenic Animals and Ethical Issues

Short Note:

• Transgenic animals, which have foreign DNA incorporated into their genome, are used in biological research to study gene regulation, disease models, and the production of biological products such as therapeutic proteins in milk.

• Key ethical and biosafety concerns surrounding biotechnology include the long-term environmental impact of GM crops, the need for regulation by bodies like the GEAC (Genetic Engineering Appraisal Committee) in India, and issues of biopiracy, where bioresources and traditional knowledge of a country are exploited or patented by other nations or corporations without fair compensation.

NCERT Textbook Exercise Questions

Q1: What is Bt cotton, and how does it provide resistance to pests?
Answer: Bt cotton is a genetically modified variety of cotton that contains a gene from the bacterium Bacillus thuringiensis, which produces crystal (Cry) proteins toxic to specific insect pests, such as bollworms. When a susceptible insect feeds on the Bt cotton plant, the toxin binds to specific receptors in its gut, causing cell lysis and eventual death of the insect, while remaining harmless to humans and non-target organisms.
Q2: Explain how recombinant DNA technology is used to produce human insulin.
Answer: To produce recombinant human insulin, the gene sequences encoding the two polypeptide chains (A and B) of human insulin are inserted separately into plasmid vectors, which are then introduced into E. coli bacteria. The genetically modified bacteria produce the individual insulin chains, which are subsequently extracted, purified and combined by forming the necessary disulfide bonds to produce mature, biologically active human insulin, marketed under the name Humulin.
Q3: What is gene therapy, and how was it used to treat ADA deficiency?
Answer: Gene therapy is a technique used to correct a genetic defect diagnosed in a child or embryo by inserting normal, functional genes into the individual's cells to compensate for the defective or missing gene. In the case of ADA deficiency, an immune disorder, lymphocytes from the patient's blood are grown in culture, a functional ADA cDNA is introduced into these lymphocytes using a retroviral vector, and the modified cells are then reintroduced into the patient's body, though such treatment historically required periodic repetition since these cells are not permanent.
Q4: What is biopiracy, and why is it a matter of ethical concern?
Answer: Biopiracy refers to the unauthorised and uncompensated use or exploitation of a country's bioresources, genetic material, and traditional knowledge by other nations, organisations, or corporations, often through patenting these resources without recognising or compensating the country or community of origin. It is a matter of ethical concern because it can deprive developing, biodiversity-rich nations of the economic and intellectual benefits rightfully derived from their own natural resources and traditional practices.

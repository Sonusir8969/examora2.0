NCERT Solutions for Class 10 English Chapter 4 From the Diary of Anne Frank

NCERT Solutions for Class 10 English First Flight Chapter 4 From the Diary of Anne Frank covers Anne's reflections while in hiding during World War II, her character and observations, and important questions and answers.

  • 1. About the Chapter and Context
  • 2. Character Sketch
  • 3. Theme & Message

1. About the Chapter and Context

Short Note:

• This chapter is an excerpt from the diary of Anne Frank, a young Jewish girl who, along with her family, hid in a secret annexe in Amsterdam during the Nazi occupation in World War II.

• Anne addresses her diary as 'Kitty', treating it like a close friend to whom she can express her innermost thoughts, fears, and daily observations during this difficult period of confinement.

• The excerpt describes her school days before hiding, her friendships, her teachers, and her lively, talkative personality, alongside her growing self-reflection during confinement.

2. Character Sketch

Short Note:

• Anne Frank: An intelligent, imaginative and expressive girl who uses writing as an emotional outlet, showing remarkable maturity and self-awareness for her age despite the fear and confinement she experiences.

• Mr. Keesing: Anne's strict mathematics teacher who punishes her talkativeness by assigning essays, which unexpectedly reveal Anne's wit and cleverness through her humorous, well-argued responses.

3. Theme & Message

Short Note:

• The chapter reflects themes of hope, resilience and the human need for companionship and self-expression, even amid the horrors of war and persecution.

• It shows how Anne finds comfort and identity through her diary, treating it as a confidant during a time when trust in the outside world was dangerous and limited.

Reading With Insight (Textbook Questions)

Q1: Why does Anne Frank call her diary 'Kitty' and treat it as a friend?
Answer: Anne feels that she does not have a close friend with whom she can share her deepest thoughts and feelings, so she personifies her diary as 'Kitty', treating it as an understanding companion to whom she can confide freely and honestly.
Q2: How did Mr. Keesing punish Anne for talking too much in class, and how did she respond?
Answer: Mr. Keesing repeatedly assigned Anne essays on topics like 'Chatterbox' as punishment for her talkativeness, but Anne cleverly and humorously defended her habit of talking in her essays, eventually winning her teacher's respect and even a light-hearted, comic poem in response to her essay on 'Quack, Quack, Quack, said Mistress Chatterback'.
Q3: What does the excerpt reveal about Anne's relationships with her friends and family before going into hiding?
Answer: The excerpt reveals that Anne had a wide circle of friends and admirers at school, was known for her humour and lively personality, but also often felt slightly superficial in some of these friendships, lacking one true, deep confidante before she began writing to her diary.

Extract-Based / Short Answer Questions

Q4: What does Anne's description of her school days suggest about her personality?
Answer: Anne's description suggests she was bright, sociable, humorous and a little mischievous, popular among both students and teachers despite frequent scoldings for talking too much in class.
Q5: Why is Anne's diary considered an important historical and literary document?
Answer: Anne's diary offers a deeply personal, first-hand account of the experience of hiding from persecution during the Holocaust, providing valuable historical insight while also showcasing profound emotional and literary expression from a young girl's perspective.

Chem-5: Thermodynamics 30 Questions

1. The branch of science dealing with the interconversion of heat and other forms of energy is called:

  • A. Thermodynamics
  • B. Electrochemistry
  • C. Kinetics
  • D. Stoichiometry

Answer: A. Thermodynamics

Explanation: Thermodynamics specifically studies the relationships between heat, work and other forms of energy transfer.

2. The part of the universe under study, separated from its surroundings, is called the:

  • A. System
  • B. Boundary
  • C. Surroundings
  • D. Universe as a whole

Answer: A. System

Explanation: In thermodynamics, the system refers to the specific portion of matter or space being investigated, distinct from everything else around it.

3. Everything outside the system that can exchange energy or matter with it is called the:

  • A. Surroundings
  • B. System
  • C. Boundary only
  • D. Universe only

Answer: A. Surroundings

Explanation: The surroundings encompass everything external to the system that may interact with it through energy or matter exchange.

4. A system that can exchange both matter and energy with its surroundings is called a/an:

  • A. Open system
  • B. Closed system
  • C. Isolated system
  • D. Adiabatic system

Answer: A. Open system

Explanation: An open system permits the free exchange of both matter and energy across its boundary with the surroundings.

5. A system that can exchange energy but not matter with its surroundings is called a/an:

  • A. Closed system
  • B. Open system
  • C. Isolated system
  • D. Adiabatic system only

Answer: A. Closed system

Explanation: A closed system allows energy transfer across its boundary, but the boundary prevents any matter from entering or leaving.

6. A system that can exchange neither matter nor energy with its surroundings is called a/an:

  • A. Isolated system
  • B. Open system
  • C. Closed system
  • D. Adiabatic system only

Answer: A. Isolated system

Explanation: An isolated system is completely sealed off, with no exchange of either matter or energy occurring across its boundary.

7. The First Law of Thermodynamics is essentially a statement of the law of conservation of:

  • A. Energy
  • B. Mass only
  • C. Momentum
  • D. Charge

Answer: A. Energy

Explanation: The First Law of Thermodynamics states that energy can neither be created nor destroyed, only transformed or transferred, in accordance with energy conservation.

8. The mathematical expression of the First Law of Thermodynamics is commonly written as:

  • A. ΔU = q + w
  • B. ΔU = q - w only
  • C. ΔU = qw
  • D. ΔU = q/w

Answer: A. ΔU = q + w

Explanation: The First Law states that the change in internal energy equals the heat added to the system plus the work done on the system.

9. A process occurring at constant pressure is called:

  • A. An isobaric process
  • B. An isothermal process
  • C. An isochoric process
  • D. An adiabatic process

Answer: A. An isobaric process

Explanation: An isobaric process is defined as one that takes place under conditions of constant pressure throughout.

10. A process occurring at constant temperature is called:

  • A. An isothermal process
  • B. An isobaric process
  • C. An isochoric process
  • D. An adiabatic process

Answer: A. An isothermal process

Explanation: An isothermal process is one in which the temperature remains constant throughout the entire process.

11. A process occurring at constant volume is called:

  • A. An isochoric process
  • B. An isobaric process
  • C. An isothermal process
  • D. An adiabatic process

Answer: A. An isochoric process

Explanation: An isochoric process takes place under conditions of constant volume, with no change in the system's volume.

12. A process in which no heat is exchanged between the system and surroundings is called:

  • A. An adiabatic process
  • B. An isothermal process
  • C. An isobaric process
  • D. An isochoric process

Answer: A. An adiabatic process

Explanation: An adiabatic process occurs with zero heat transfer between the system and its surroundings.

13. The heat absorbed or released by a system at constant pressure is called the change in:

  • A. Enthalpy
  • B. Entropy
  • C. Internal energy only
  • D. Free energy only

Answer: A. Enthalpy

Explanation: Enthalpy change (ΔH) specifically represents the heat absorbed or released during a process occurring at constant pressure.

14. A measure of the degree of randomness or disorder of a system is called its:

  • A. Entropy
  • B. Enthalpy
  • C. Internal energy
  • D. Free energy

Answer: A. Entropy

Explanation: Entropy quantifies the level of disorder or randomness present within a thermodynamic system.

15. The Second Law of Thermodynamics states that the total entropy of an isolated system tends to:

  • A. Increase over time
  • B. Decrease over time
  • C. Remain exactly constant always
  • D. Become zero immediately

Answer: A. Increase over time

Explanation: The Second Law states that natural processes tend to increase the overall disorder (entropy) of an isolated system over time.

16. A reaction that releases heat to the surroundings is called:

  • A. Exothermic
  • B. Endothermic
  • C. Isothermal only
  • D. Adiabatic only

Answer: A. Exothermic

Explanation: Exothermic reactions release energy in the form of heat to the surrounding environment, resulting in negative enthalpy change.

17. A reaction that absorbs heat from the surroundings is called:

  • A. Endothermic
  • B. Exothermic
  • C. Isobaric only
  • D. Isochoric only

Answer: A. Endothermic

Explanation: Endothermic reactions absorb heat energy from the surroundings, resulting in a positive enthalpy change.

18. The thermodynamic quantity that combines enthalpy and entropy to predict the spontaneity of a process is called:

  • A. Gibbs free energy
  • B. Internal energy
  • C. Heat capacity
  • D. Work done

Answer: A. Gibbs free energy

Explanation: Gibbs free energy (G) integrates both enthalpy and entropy changes to determine whether a process will occur spontaneously.

19. According to Gibbs free energy criteria, a process is spontaneous if the change in free energy (ΔG) is:

  • A. Negative
  • B. Positive
  • C. Zero always
  • D. Undefined

Answer: A. Negative

Explanation: A negative value of ΔG indicates that a process is thermodynamically spontaneous under the given conditions.

20. The Third Law of Thermodynamics states that the entropy of a perfect crystal at absolute zero temperature is:

  • A. Zero
  • B. Maximum
  • C. Infinite
  • D. Negative

Answer: A. Zero

Explanation: The Third Law asserts that a perfectly ordered crystalline substance at absolute zero has an entropy value of exactly zero.

21. The enthalpy change associated with the formation of one mole of a compound from its elements in their standard states is called the:

  • A. Standard enthalpy of formation
  • B. Standard enthalpy of combustion
  • C. Standard enthalpy of neutralisation
  • D. Standard enthalpy of solution

Answer: A. Standard enthalpy of formation

Explanation: Standard enthalpy of formation specifically refers to the heat change when one mole of a compound forms from its constituent elements under standard conditions.

22. Hess's Law states that the total enthalpy change of a reaction is:

  • A. Independent of the pathway taken, depending only on initial and final states
  • B. Dependent only on the fastest reaction pathway
  • C. Always zero regardless of the reaction
  • D. Only applicable to gaseous reactions

Answer: A. Independent of the pathway taken, depending only on initial and final states

Explanation: Hess's Law states that enthalpy change is a state function, meaning the total change depends only on initial and final states, not the specific route taken.

23. The enthalpy change when one mole of a substance is completely burned in oxygen is called the enthalpy of:

  • A. Combustion
  • B. Formation
  • C. Neutralisation
  • D. Solution

Answer: A. Combustion

Explanation: Enthalpy of combustion specifically measures the heat released when one mole of a substance undergoes complete combustion in oxygen.

24. A function whose value depends only on the current state of the system, not on how that state was reached, is called a:

  • A. State function
  • B. Path function
  • C. Extensive property only
  • D. Intensive property only

Answer: A. State function

Explanation: State functions, such as enthalpy and internal energy, depend solely on the current state of the system, independent of the path taken to reach it.

25. Quantities like heat and work, which depend on the path taken by the system, are called:

  • A. Path functions
  • B. State functions
  • C. Extensive properties only
  • D. Conserved quantities

Answer: A. Path functions

Explanation: Unlike state functions, path functions such as heat and work depend on the specific route or process followed by the system.

26. Internal energy of a system is classified as a:

  • A. State function
  • B. Path function
  • C. Constant value always
  • D. Undefined quantity

Answer: A. State function

Explanation: Internal energy depends only on the current state of the system (such as temperature and composition), classifying it as a state function.

27. A spontaneous process is one that occurs:

  • A. Naturally, without continuous external intervention
  • B. Only with constant external energy input
  • C. Never in nature
  • D. Only in living systems

Answer: A. Naturally, without continuous external intervention

Explanation: Spontaneous processes proceed naturally in a given direction without requiring ongoing external energy input to sustain them.

28. The relationship between Gibbs free energy, enthalpy and entropy is given by the equation:

  • A. ΔG = ΔH - TΔS
  • B. ΔG = ΔH + TΔS
  • C. ΔG = ΔH × TΔS
  • D. ΔG = ΔH / TΔS

Answer: A. ΔG = ΔH - TΔS

Explanation: This fundamental thermodynamic equation relates Gibbs free energy change to enthalpy change, temperature and entropy change.

29. Work done by a gas during expansion against constant external pressure is calculated as:

  • A. w = -PΔV
  • B. w = PΔV
  • C. w = P/ΔV
  • D. w = P + ΔV

Answer: A. w = -PΔV

Explanation: For expansion against constant external pressure, the work done by the system is calculated as negative pressure multiplied by the change in volume.

30. The branch of thermodynamics known as chemical thermodynamics specifically deals with energy changes occurring during:

  • A. Chemical reactions and physical transformations of matter
  • B. Only nuclear reactions
  • C. Only biological processes
  • D. Only mechanical work

Answer: A. Chemical reactions and physical transformations of matter

Explanation: Chemical thermodynamics focuses specifically on the energy changes, such as heat and work, that accompany chemical reactions and phase transformations.

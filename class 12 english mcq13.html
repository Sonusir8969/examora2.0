Ch-13: Aunt Jennifer's Tigers 4 Questions

1. Who is the poet of 'Aunt Jennifer's Tigers'?

  • A. Adrienne Rich
  • B. Emily Dickinson
  • C. Sylvia Plath
  • D. Maya Angelou

Answer: A. Adrienne Rich

Explanation: 'Aunt Jennifer's Tigers' is a poem by the American poet Adrienne Rich, often read as a commentary on the constraints faced by women, particularly within marriage.

2. What does Aunt Jennifer create in her embroidery, described vividly in the poem?

  • A. Flowers and birds
  • B. Bold, fearless tigers prancing confidently across a screen
  • C. Portraits of her family
  • D. Abstract patterns only

Answer: B. Bold, fearless tigers prancing confidently across a screen

Explanation: Aunt Jennifer embroiders a panel depicting tigers that are bold, confident, and fearless, striding proudly, unlike Aunt Jennifer herself who lives a constrained life.

3. What does Aunt Jennifer's 'massive weight' of her wedding band symbolize in the poem?

  • A. Her wealth and social status
  • B. The heavy burden and oppression of her marriage that she has endured
  • C. Her love for her husband
  • D. Her physical fitness

Answer: B. The heavy burden and oppression of her marriage that she has endured

Explanation: The wedding ring, described as weighing heavily on Aunt Jennifer's hand, symbolizes the oppressive weight of marital duties and societal constraints that have burdened her throughout her life.

4. What does the poet suggest will happen to the embroidered tigers even after Aunt Jennifer's death?

  • A. They will also die with her
  • B. They will continue to prance on fearlessly, symbolizing an enduring freedom and spirit that outlives her own restricted life
  • C. They will be destroyed
  • D. They will be forgotten completely

Answer: B. They will continue to prance on fearlessly, symbolizing an enduring freedom and spirit that outlives her own restricted life

Explanation: The poem concludes that even after Aunt Jennifer passes away, the tigers she created will continue to prance proudly and fearlessly, representing an idea of freedom and spirit that outlasts her own constrained, timid existence.

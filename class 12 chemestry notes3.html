NCERT Solutions for Class 12 Chemistry Chapter 3 Chemical Kinetics

NCERT Solutions for Class 12 Chemistry Chapter 3 Chemical Kinetics provides comprehensive coverage of reaction rates, rate laws, order and molecularity of reactions, integrated rate equations, half-life periods, Arrhenius equation, and activation energy.

Overview of Topics & Subtopics (Click to Jump)

  • 1. Rate of Reaction, Factors & Order/Molecularity
  • 2. Integrated Rate Equations, Half-Life & Pseudo First Order
  • 3. Temperature Dependence, Arrhenius Equation & Collision Theory

1. Rate of Reaction, Factors & Order/Molecularity

Short Note:

• Rate of Reaction: Change in concentration of reactants or products per unit time ($r = -\frac{\Delta [R]}{\Delta t} = +\frac{\Delta [P]}{\Delta t}$). Unit: $\text{mol L}^{-1}\text{s}^{-1}$.

• Rate Law & Rate Constant ($k$): Expression relating reaction rate to concentration of reactants: $r = k[A]^x [B]^y$. $k$ is characteristic for a specific reaction at a given temperature.

• Order of Reaction ($n = x + y$): Sum of powers of reactant concentrations in experimentally determined rate law. Can be 0, fractional, or integer.

• Molecularity: Number of reacting species taking part in an elementary reaction step simultaneously. Always a positive whole integer ($1, 2, 3$). Cannot be zero or fractional.

2. Integrated Rate Equations, Half-Life & Pseudo First Order

Short Note:

• Zero Order Reaction: Rate is independent of concentration.

  - Integrated equation: $k = \frac{[R]_0 - [R]}{t}$

  - Half-life: $t_{1/2} = \frac{[R]_0}{2k}$ (Directly proportional to initial concentration $[R]_0$).

• First Order Reaction: Rate depends linearly on reactant concentration.

  - Integrated equation: $k = \frac{2.303}{t} \log \frac{[R]_0}{[R]}$

  - Half-life: $t_{1/2} = \frac{0.693}{k}$ (Independent of initial concentration $[R]_0$).

• Pseudo First Order Reaction: A higher-order reaction that behaves as a first-order reaction because one reactant is in excess (e.g., acid hydrolysis of ethyl acetate, inversion of cane sugar).

3. Temperature Dependence, Arrhenius Equation & Collision Theory

Short Note:

• Temperature Coefficient: Most chemical reactions double their rate for every $10^\circ\text{C}$ rise in temperature.

• Arrhenius Equation: $k = A e^{-E_a / RT}$

  - Logarithmic Form: $\log k = \log A - \frac{E_a}{2.303 R T}$

  - Two-Temperature Form: $\log \frac{k_2}{k_1} = \frac{E_a}{2.303 R} \left[ \frac{T_2 - T_1}{T_1 T_2} \right]$

• Activation Energy ($E_a$): Minimum energy required by reactant molecules to form an activated complex and turn into products.

• Collision Theory: Effective collisions depend on two factors: Energetic factor (Energy $\ge E_a$) and Orientational factor (Proper spatial alignment of molecules during collision).

Intext & Conceptual Questions

Q1: Differentiate between Order and Molecularity of a chemical reaction.
Answer:
Feature Order of Reaction Molecularity of Reaction
Definition Sum of exponents of concentration terms in experimental rate law expression. Number of species colliding simultaneously in an elementary step.
Nature Experimental value. Theoretical value.
Values Possible Can be zero, integer, or fractional. Always a positive whole integer ($1, 2, 3$). Never zero or fractional.
Applicability Applicable to elementary as well as complex reactions. Applicable only to elementary steps of a reaction.
Q2: A reaction is first order in A and second order in B. How is the rate affected when the concentration of B is tripled?
Answer:
• Rate equation for the given reaction: $r_1 = k [A]^1 [B]^2$
• When concentration of B is tripled ($[B'] = 3[B]$):
  $r_2 = k [A]^1 (3[B])^2 = k [A]^1 \times 9[B]^2 = 9 \times r_1$

• Therefore, the rate of reaction increases by 9 times.

Textbook Chapter End Exercise Questions

Q1: A first order reaction has a rate constant k = 1.15 × 10⁻³ s⁻¹. How long will 5 g of this reactant take to reduce to 3 g?
Answer:
Given:
• Rate constant $k = 1.15 \times 10^{-3}\text{ s}^{-1}$
• Initial concentration $[R]_0 = 5\text{ g}$
• Remaining concentration $[R] = 3\text{ g}$

Step 1: Apply First-Order Integrated Rate Formula
$t = \frac{2.303}{k} \log \frac{[R]_0}{[R]}$

Step 2: Substitute Values
$t = \frac{2.303}{1.15 \times 10^{-3}} \log \left(\frac{5}{3}\right)$
$t = 2002.6 \times \log(1.667)$
$t = 2002.6 \times 0.2219 \approx 444.38\text{ s}$

• The time required for reduction is 444.4 seconds.
Q2: The rate constants of a reaction at 500 K and 700 K are 0.02 s⁻¹ and 0.07 s⁻¹ respectively. Calculate the values of activation energy (Eₐ) and pre-exponential factor (A). (R = 8.314 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹)
Answer:
Given:
• $T_1 = 500\text{ K}$, $k_1 = 0.02\text{ s}^{-1}$
• $T_2 = 700\text{ K}$, $k_2 = 0.07\text{ s}^{-1}$
• $R = 8.314\text{ J K}^{-1}\text{ mol}^{-1}$

Step 1: Calculate Activation Energy ($E_a$)
$\log \frac{k_2}{k_1} = \frac{E_a}{2.303 R} \left[ \frac{T_2 - T_1}{T_1 T_2} \right]$
$\log \left(\frac{0.07}{0.02}\right) = \frac{E_a}{2.303 \times 8.314} \left[ \frac{700 - 500}{500 \times 700} \right]$
$\log(3.5) = \frac{E_a}{19.147} \times \left[ \frac{200}{350000} \right]$
$0.5441 = \frac{E_a}{19.147} \times \left(5.714 \times 10^{-4}\right)$
$E_a = \frac{0.5441 \times 19.147}{5.714 \times 10^{-4}} \approx 18231\text{ J mol}^{-1} = \mathbf{18.23\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}}$

Step 2: Calculate Frequency Factor ($A$) using $T_1 = 500\text{ K}$
$\log k_1 = \log A - \frac{E_a}{2.303 R T_1}$
$\log(0.02) = \log A - \frac{18231}{2.303 \times 8.314 \times 500}$
$-1.6990 = \log A - 1.9043$
$\log A = -1.6990 + 1.9043 = 0.2053$
$A = 10^{0.2053} \approx \mathbf{1.603\text{ s}^{-1}}$

• Activation Energy ($E_a$) = 18.23 kJ mol⁻¹
• Pre-exponential factor ($A$) = 1.60 s⁻¹
